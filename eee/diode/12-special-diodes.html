Special Diodes and Thyristors

Short-answer theory. Special diodes show up in class tests (Schottky drop, Zener, filter capacitor). SCR and TRIAC questions came in 2011–12 (three parts, 46⅔ marks), 2013–14 and 2014–15, and not once since 2014–15, and Sadat sir did not teach thyristors this semester. Read this chapter once; spend your time on Chapters 8 to 11.

1. Special-purpose diodes

Schottky barrier diode (SBD)
Metal–n-type semiconductor junction. Current is carried by majority carriers only, so there is no minority-charge storage: it switches very fast. Forward drop about 0.3–0.5 V (lower than a pn diode's 0.7 V); I–V shape like a pn junction. Used in fast switching, clamping a BJT out of saturation, low-drop rectifiers.
Varactor
A reverse-biased diode used as a voltage-controlled capacitor: the depletion capacitance \(C_j = \dfrac{C_{j0}}{(1 + V_R/V_0)^m}\) falls as \(V_R\) rises (\(m\) ≈ 1/3 to 1/2, larger for hyper-abrupt junctions). Used for electronic tuning (VCOs, FM tuners).
Photodiode
Reverse-biased junction; light creates electron–hole pairs in the depletion region and a reverse photocurrent proportional to the light intensity. Unbiased and loaded, it delivers power: a solar cell (operates in the fourth quadrant of the I–V).
LED
Forward-biased junction in a direct-band-gap material (GaAs, GaAsP, GaN); recombination emits photons whose colour is set by the band gap. Forward drop about 1.7–2 V (red) to 3–3.5 V (blue/white). Needs a series resistor: \(R = (V_S - V_F)/I_F\). LED + photodiode in one package = optocoupler (electrical isolation).
Zener
Designed for reverse breakdown; see Chapter 7.

2. SCR (not examined since 2014–15)

A pnpn device with anode, cathode and gate (on the inner p layer). Three states: reverse blocking (like a reverse diode), forward blocking (only leakage until the breakover voltage \(V_{BO}\)), and forward conduction (about 1–2 V drop). A gate pulse switches it on at a much lower anode voltage; once on it latches and stays on until the anode current falls below the holding current \(I_H\) (natural commutation at the ac zero crossing).

Two-transistor model (2011–12, 20 marks). Split p1-n1-p2-n2 into a pnp Q1 (p1 n1 p2) and an npn Q2 (n1 p2 n2), with \(I_{B1} = I_{C2}\) and \(I_{B2} = I_{C1} + I_G\):

\[ I_{C1} = \alpha_1I_A + I_{CBO1},\quad I_{C2} = \alpha_2I_K + I_{CBO2},\quad I_K = I_A + I_G \;\Rightarrow\; I_A = \frac{\alpha_2I_G + I_{CBO1} + I_{CBO2}}{1 - (\alpha_1 + \alpha_2)} \]

Each transistor feeds the other's base (positive feedback). The α's rise with current, so a gate current pushes \(\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 \to 1\), the denominator goes to zero and the current runs away (regeneration): only the external circuit limits it. Removing the gate current does not stop it.

Phase-controlled rectifier. Firing at angle α, resistive load: half-wave \(V_{dc} = \dfrac{V_m}{2\pi}(1 + \cos\alpha)\); full-wave (2 SCRs with a centre tap, or a 4-SCR bridge, gates fired at α and π + α) \(V_{dc} = \dfrac{V_m}{\pi}(1 + \cos\alpha)\).

TRIAC. Two SCRs in anti-parallel with a common gate: conducts in both directions, triggered by either gate polarity (quadrants I+ and III− most sensitive). Uses: lamp dimmers, fan regulators (ac power control); SCR: controlled rectifiers, motor drives.

Traps

  • A gate pulse arriving while the anode is negative does nothing (2011–12).
  • An SCR is turned off by the anode current, not by the gate.
  • Schottky: majority-carrier device; its low drop and speed are the two points to write.

Exam questions

2014–15 · Sec B · Q8(c) · 11⅔ marks

Draw the circuit diagram of a controlled full-wave rectifier using SCRs. Draw the waveforms of the sinusoidal input, gate pulses and rectified output.

Show worked answer

Centre-tapped transformer with SCR1 and SCR2 in place of D1 and D2 (or a bridge of four SCRs). Gate pulses: SCR1 at α, 2π + α, …; SCR2 at π + α, 3π + α, …. Output (resistive load): zero from 0 to α, follows \(|v_s|\) from α to π, repeating every half-cycle; each SCR turns off at the current zero. \(V_{dc} = \frac{V_m}{\pi}(1 + \cos\alpha)\).

2013–14 · Sec B · Q8(c) · 15 marks

Explain SCR and TRIAC operation with suitable examples.

Show worked answer

Section 2: structure, three states, gate triggering, latching and \(I_H\), two-transistor model; TRIAC as anti-parallel SCRs with one gate. Example numbers: 120 V rms (\(V_m\) = 169.7 V), α = 60°, 10 Ω: half-wave SCR \(V_{dc} = \frac{169.7}{2\pi}(1.5) = 40.5\) V; a TRIAC dimmer at the same α delivers twice the power of the half-wave SCR (1158 W vs 579 W).

Practice

Key terms

Quick check

Summary: Schottky = metal–semiconductor, majority carriers, fast, 0.3–0.5 V. Varactor = reverse-biased variable capacitor. Photodiode = reverse-biased, current ∝ light; solar cell in quadrant IV. LED = forward-biased, 1.7–3.5 V, needs \(R = (V_S - V_F)/I_F\). SCR = pnpn latch, fired by the gate, off below \(I_H\); \(V_{dc} = \frac{V_m}{2\pi}(1 + \cos\alpha)\) half-wave, double for full-wave. TRIAC = bidirectional SCR. Thyristors: not examined since 2014–15.
