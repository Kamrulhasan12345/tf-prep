Zener Diodes and Shunt Regulators

A Zener question appeared in 2014–15 to 2017–18, 2020–21, 2022–23, 2023–24 and 2024–25: almost every year, usually 10 to 20 marks, and mostly Sedra's own example with the numbers changed. There are only three kinds: the Zener model, analysing a regulator (line and load regulation, minimum load), and designing or finding ranges. Each has a five-step recipe.

1. What a Zener does

A Zener is a diode built to be used in reverse breakdown. Once the reverse voltage reaches about \(V_Z\), the current can rise a lot while the voltage barely changes: the diode behaves like a battery of value \(V_Z\). In a circuit the cathode faces the positive side (current flows cathode → anode inside the Zener).

\(V_Z\), \(I_{ZT}\)
Nominal Zener voltage, specified at a test current \(I_{ZT}\).
\(r_z\)
Incremental (dynamic) resistance in breakdown, a few ohms to tens of ohms. Smaller is a better regulator.
\(I_{ZK}\)
Knee current: below it the Zener leaves breakdown and stops regulating. Keep \(I_Z \ge I_{ZK}\).
\(I_{ZM}\), \(P_{ZM}\)
Maximum current and power: \(P_{ZM} = V_Z I_{ZM}\). Keep \(I_Z \le I_{ZM}\).

2. The Zener model (type D-09, part 1)

In breakdown, replace the Zener by a battery \(V_{Z0}\) in series with \(r_z\). \(V_{Z0}\) is where the straight-line approximation meets the voltage axis:

\[ V_Z = V_{Z0} + r_z I_Z, \qquad V_{Z0} = V_{ZT} - r_z I_{ZT} \]

Method · Zener at another current

  1. \(V_{Z0} = V_{ZT} - r_z I_{ZT}\) (use the test point given).
  2. \(V_Z = V_{Z0} + r_z I_Z\) at each new current.
  3. Comment (asked in 2023–24): at lower current the dissipation is smaller but the operating point nears the knee, where \(r_z\) grows and regulation worsens; at higher current \(r_z\) is small and steady but \(P = V_Z I_Z\) heats the diode and \(V_Z\) drifts with temperature.

3. Shunt regulator analysis (type D-09)

The standard regulator: supply \(V^+\) → resistor \(R\) → output node, Zener from the output to ground, load \(R_L\) across the Zener.

Method · regulator analysis (5 steps)

  1. Model: \(V_{Z0} = V_{ZT} - r_zI_{ZT}\).
  2. ON check first (whenever a load is present): remove the Zener, \(V_{TH} = V^+\dfrac{R_L}{R+R_L}\). If \(V_{TH} < V_{Z0}\) the Zener is off and \(V_o = V_{TH}\). Stop.
  3. No load: \(I_Z = \dfrac{V^+ - V_{Z0}}{R + r_z}\), \(V_o = V_{Z0} + r_zI_Z\).
  4. Line regulation: \(\dfrac{\Delta V_o}{\Delta V^+} = \dfrac{r_z}{R + r_z}\) (a divider of \(r_z\) and \(R\)).
  5. Load regulation: \(\Delta V_o = -\Delta I_L\,(r_z\parallel R)\) with \(I_L \approx V_o/R_L\). Exact: node equation \(V_o = \dfrac{V^+/R + V_{Z0}/r_z}{1/R + 1/r_z + 1/R_L}\).
  6. Minimum \(R_L\) (still in breakdown): set \(I_Z = I_{ZK}\) at the lowest supply: \(V_o = V_{Z0} + r_zI_{ZK}\), \(I_R = (V^+_{min} - V_o)/R\), \(I_L = I_R - I_{ZK}\), \(R_{L,min} = V_o/I_L\).

Worked example: 2024–25 Sec B Q8(a), the Sedra regulator

Ideal Zener with a divider (2015–16)

With an ideal Zener (\(r_z = 0\)) the ON check is the whole question: find the Thevenin voltage the Zener sees. If it exceeds \(V_Z\), the Zener clamps the node at \(V_Z\) and takes the surplus current; otherwise it is open.

4. Ranges and design (type D-10)

Method · load range with fixed supply (ideal Zener)

  1. While regulating, \(V_o = V_Z\), so \(I_R = (V_i - V_Z)/R\) is constant.
  2. Heaviest load takes everything except \(I_{ZK}\): \(I_{L,max} = I_R - I_{ZK}\), so \(R_{L,min} = V_Z/I_{L,max}\). Cross-check: \(V_iR_L/(R+R_L) \ge V_Z\).
  3. Lightest load leaves the Zener with its maximum: \(I_{L,min} = I_R - I_{ZM}\), so \(R_{L,max} = V_Z/I_{L,min}\) (if \(I_{L,min} \le 0\), any load up to open circuit is fine).
  4. \(P_{ZM} = V_ZI_{ZM}\).

Method · choose R for a supply range and a load range

  1. Regulation worst case = lowest supply with the heaviest load: \(R = \dfrac{V_{S,min} - V_Z}{I_{L,max} + I_{Z,min}}\).
  2. Dissipation worst case = highest supply with the lightest load: \(I_{Z,max} = \dfrac{V_{S,max} - V_Z}{R} - I_{L,min}\), \(P_Z = V_ZI_{Z,max}\).
  3. State your \(I_{Z,min}\). If none is given, either \(I_{Z,min} = 0\) (simplest) or Neamen's rule \(I_{Z,min} = 0.1\,I_{Z,max}\), which gives \(I_{Z,max} = \dfrac{I_{L,max}(V_{S,max}-V_Z) - I_{L,min}(V_{S,min}-V_Z)}{V_{S,min} - 0.9V_Z - 0.1V_{S,max}}\).

Zener + BJT shunt regulator (2014–15)

The Zener sits between the output and the base of an npn whose collector is the output and emitter is ground. The transistor multiplies the Zener current by β, so the Zener carries only a tiny current:

\[ V_L = V_Z + V_{BE},\quad I_S = \frac{V_{in} - V_L}{R_S},\quad I_L = \frac{V_L}{R_L},\quad I_Z = I_B = \frac{I_S - I_L}{\beta + 1} \]

Traps (where marks go)

  • Using \(V_{ZT}\) (6.8 V) instead of \(V_{Z0}\) (6.7 V) in the KVL.
  • Skipping the ON check: with a heavy load the Zener can be off, and then \(V_o\) is just the divider voltage (2024–25: \(R_L\) = 0.5 kΩ gives 5 V).
  • Minimum load at the nominal supply instead of the lowest supply (2024–25 gives ±1 V: use 9 V).
  • Mixing the two design worst cases: R from (lowest supply, heaviest load); Zener power from (highest supply, lightest load).
  • Drawing the Zener the wrong way round: cathode to the positive node.

Exam questions

2024–25 · Sec B · Q8(a) · 20 marks  |  also 2020–21 Sec B Q6(b) (parts i, iv, 23⅓ marks) · Sedra Example 4.x

A 6.8 V Zener diode has its nominal voltage at a test current of 5 mA; there \(r_z\) = 20 Ω, and the knee current is 0.2 mA. The cathode is connected to \(V^+\) = 10 V (±1 V) through a 0.5 kΩ resistor; the anode is grounded. (i) Find \(V_o\) with no load at the nominal supply. (ii) Find the change in \(V_o\) for a ±1 V change in supply. (iii) Find the change in \(V_o\) when \(R_L\) = 2 kΩ is connected. What happens with \(R_L\) = 0.5 kΩ? (iv) Find the minimum \(R_L\) for which the Zener stays in breakdown.

Show worked answer

Model: \(V_{Z0} = 6.8 - 0.005\times20 = 6.7\) V.

(i) \(I_Z = \dfrac{10 - 6.7}{0.5 + 0.02}\) mA \(= 6.35\) mA; \(V_o = 6.7 + 0.02\times6.35 = 6.83\) V.

(ii) \(\Delta V_o = \pm1\times\dfrac{20}{500+20} = \pm38.5\) mV (line regulation 38.5 mV/V).

(iii) ON check: \(V_{TH} = 10\times\frac{2}{2.5} = 8\) V \(> 6.7\) V, so the Zener stays on. \(I_L \approx 6.8/2 = 3.4\) mA, \(\Delta V_o = -3.4\text{ mA}\times(20\parallel500) = -3.4\times19.2\text{ mV} = -65\) mV. (Exact node equation: \(V_o = 6.762\) V, \(\Delta V_o = -64.9\) mV.)

With \(R_L\) = 0.5 kΩ: \(V_{TH} = 10\times\frac{0.5}{1} = 5\) V \(< V_{Z0}\), so the Zener is off and \(V_o = 5\) V: the circuit no longer regulates.

(iv) Worst case is the lowest supply, 9 V, with \(I_Z = I_{ZK}\): \(V_o = 6.7 + 0.2\times0.02 = 6.704\) V; \(I_R = (9 - 6.704)/0.5 = 4.59\) mA; \(I_L = 4.59 - 0.2 = 4.39\) mA; \(R_{L,min} = 6.704/4.39 = 1.53\) kΩ. (2020–21 gives a fixed 10 V supply: then \(I_R = 6.59\) mA, \(I_L = 6.39\) mA, \(R_{L,min} = 1.05\) kΩ.)

2023–24 · Sec A · Q2(b) · 10 marks  |  also CT-1 (batch 23)

A 9.1 V Zener diode exhibits its nominal voltage at a test current of 28 mA. At this current the incremental resistance is 5 Ω. (i) Find \(V_{Z0}\) of the Zener model. (ii) Find the Zener voltage at 10 mA and at 100 mA. (iii) Comment on running the Zener at currents other than the nominal value.

Show worked answer

(i) \(V_{Z0} = 9.1 - 0.028\times5 = 8.96\) V.

(ii) \(V_Z(10\text{ mA}) = 8.96 + 0.010\times5 = 9.01\) V; \(V_Z(100\text{ mA}) = 8.96 + 0.100\times5 = 9.46\) V.

(iii) At 10 mA the dissipation is lower (91 mW) but the point is closer to the knee, where \(r_z\) rises and regulation is poorer. At 100 mA the voltage is 0.36 V above nominal, the dissipation is 0.95 W (heating, so \(V_Z\) drifts with its temperature coefficient), though \(r_z\) is small and steady. Nominal current is the designed compromise.

2022–23 · Sec A · Q3(a) · 18 marks  |  Boylestad example

For the Zener regulator with \(V_i\) = 50 V, \(R\) = 1 kΩ, \(V_Z\) = 10 V and \(I_{ZM}\) = 32 mA, find the range of \(R_L\) and \(I_L\) that keeps \(V_{RL}\) = 10 V. Also find the maximum wattage rating of the diode.

Show worked answer

Ideal Zener, \(I_{ZK} \approx 0\). While regulating, \(I_R = (50 - 10)/1 = 40\) mA (constant).

Smallest \(R_L\) (Zener just in breakdown, \(I_Z \approx 0\)): \(I_{L,max} = 40\) mA, \(R_{L,min} = 10/40 = 0.25\) kΩ. Check with the divider: \(50R_L/(1 + R_L) = 10 \Rightarrow R_L = 0.25\) kΩ ✓.

Largest \(R_L\) (Zener at \(I_{ZM}\)): \(I_{L,min} = 40 - 32 = 8\) mA, \(R_{L,max} = 10/8 = 1.25\) kΩ.

Ranges: 250 Ω ≤ \(R_L\) ≤ 1.25 kΩ; 8 mA ≤ \(I_L\) ≤ 40 mA. Rating: \(P_{ZM} = 10\times32\text{ mA} = 320\) mW.

2017–18 · Sec A · Q4(c) · 18 marks  |  Neamen example

A Zener regulator powers a car radio at \(V_L\) = 9 V from a battery that varies between 11 and 13.6 V. The radio current varies from 0 (off) to 100 mA (full volume). Find the current-limiting resistor \(R_i\) and the maximum Zener power dissipation.

Show worked answer

State the assumption. Textbook rule \(I_{Z,min} = 0.1I_{Z,max}\):

\(I_{Z,max} = \dfrac{100(13.6 - 9) - 0\,(11 - 9)}{11 - 0.9\times9 - 0.1\times13.6} = \dfrac{460}{1.54} = 298.7\) mA.

\(R_i = \dfrac{11 - 9}{100 + 29.87}\) kΩ \(= 15.4\) Ω; \(P_{Z,max} = 9\times0.2987 = 2.69\) W.

Simpler assumption \(I_{Z,min} = 0\): \(R_i = (11 - 9)/0.1 = 20\) Ω; \(I_{Z,max} = (13.6 - 9)/20 = 230\) mA; \(P_Z = 2.07\) W. Accept either if the assumption is written; the first keeps a regulation margin at the worst case.

2015–16 · Sec A · Q1(c) · 15 marks

A 10 V source feeds node a through \(R_1\) = 2 kΩ; a 5 V Zener (cathode at a) and \(R_2\) = 3 kΩ both go from a to ground. Find \(V_a\) and \(I_Z\). Then swap \(R_1\) and \(R_2\) and repeat.

Show worked answer

Original: without the Zener, \(V_{TH} = 10\times3/5 = 6\) V \(> 5\) V, so the Zener breaks down: \(V_a = 5\) V. \(I_{R1} = (10-5)/2 = 2.5\) mA, \(I_{R2} = 5/3 = 1.667\) mA, \(I_Z = 2.5 - 1.667 = 0.833\) mA.

Swapped (\(R_1\) = 3 kΩ, \(R_2\) = 2 kΩ): \(V_{TH} = 10\times2/5 = 4\) V \(< 5\) V, so the Zener is off: \(V_a = 4\) V, \(I_Z = 0\).

2014–15 · Sec A · Q2(b) · 16 marks  |  Boylestad transistor shunt regulator

A 30 V supply feeds the output node L through \(R_S\) = 1.5 kΩ. A 9.3 V Zener has its cathode at L and its anode at the base of an npn transistor (β = 100, \(V_{BE}\) = 0.7 V) whose collector is at L and emitter at ground. \(R_L\) = 1 kΩ from L to ground. Find the regulated load voltage and the Zener current.

Show worked answer

\(V_L = V_Z + V_{BE} = 9.3 + 0.7 = 10\) V. \(I_S = (30 - 10)/1.5 = 13.33\) mA; \(I_L = 10/1 = 10\) mA.

The remaining 3.33 mA splits into the collector and the Zener (= base) current: \(I_C + I_Z = 3.33\) mA with \(I_C = \beta I_Z\), so \(I_Z = 3.333/101 = 33.0\) µA and \(I_C = 3.30\) mA.

Practice

Key terms

Quick check

Summary: model the Zener as \(V_{Z0} = V_{ZT} - r_zI_{ZT}\) plus \(r_z\). For a regulator: ON check first (divider voltage vs \(V_{Z0}\)); line regulation \(r_z/(R + r_z)\); load regulation \(-\Delta I_L(r_z\parallel R)\); minimum load at the lowest supply with \(I_Z = I_{ZK}\). For design: R from (lowest supply, heaviest load), Zener power from (highest supply, lightest load), and always state \(I_{Z,min}\).
