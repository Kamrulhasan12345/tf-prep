Clippers and Transfer Characteristics

A clipper question appeared in 2011–12, 2015–16, 2016–17 (two), 2017–18, 2018–19, 2021–22 and 2022–23 (compulsory Q1(a), 14 marks), 10 to 18 marks each. It was absent in 2023–24 and 2024–25, but Sadat sir taught it in depth (L9) and asked you to practise designing a clipper from a given \(v_o\)–\(v_i\) curve ("no need to memorise generalised formulas"). Everything here is one idea: find where each diode turns on, then solve a divider in each region.

1. Vocabulary and the two rules

Limiter / clipper
A circuit whose transfer characteristic is linear (gain \(K\)) between two levels and flat (hard) or less steep (soft) outside: \(v_O = Kv_I\) for \(L_-/K \le v_I \le L_+/K\).
Shunt (parallel) clipper
Series \(R\) from input to output, diode branches from output to ground. The common exam form.
Series clipper
Diode in series between input and load; the output follows the input only while the diode conducts (Khairul s.46–49). A half-wave rectifier is a series clipper.
Hard / soft
Branch without resistor: output held at the threshold (slope 0). Branch with \(R_k\): output keeps rising with slope \(R_k/(R + R_k)\).

Sadat sir's two rules (notes p.34, boxed)

  1. Diode pointing down from \(v_o\) (anode at \(v_o\)) clips the top: it conducts when \(v_o > V_{ref} + V_\gamma\). Diode pointing up (cathode at \(v_o\)) clips the bottom: conducts when \(v_o < V_{ref} - V_\gamma\).
  2. \(V_{ref}\) is the battery voltage at the diode's far end, with sign: battery + toward the diode shifts the level up, flipped battery shifts it down. A Zener in breakdown acts as a battery \(V_Z\) (Zener clipper: \(\pm(V_Z + V_\gamma)\)).

2. Analysis: breakpoints and slopes (type D-13)

Method · transfer characteristic

  1. For every branch write its threshold in \(v_o\): \(E = V_{ref} + V_\gamma\) (clips top) or \(V_{ref} - V_\gamma\) (clips bottom). Ideal diode: \(V_\gamma = 0\). PWL diode: also an \(r_D\) in the branch.
  2. Middle region (all off): \(v_o = v_i\), or \(v_o = v_i\dfrac{R_L}{R + R_L}\) with a load.
  3. Breakpoint in \(v_i\): where the middle-region \(v_o\) reaches \(E\): \(v_i = E\) (no load) or \(E(R + R_L)/R_L\).
  4. Beyond it: replace the on diode by \(V_\gamma\) (and \(r_D\)); hard clip: \(v_o = E\); soft clip: \(v_o = E + (v_i - E)\dfrac{R_k}{R + R_k}\) (Sadat p.36: \(y = mx + c\)). With a load, use the nodal form \(v_o = \dfrac{v_i/R + E/R_k}{1/R + 1/R_k + 1/R_L}\).
  5. Check continuity at each breakpoint and that the on diode's current is positive.
  6. Waveform: output peaks from \(v_i = \pm V_m\); times of the breakpoints \(t = \sin^{-1}(v_{i,bp}/V_m)/\omega\) (and \(T/2 - t\), etc.).

Worked example: 2022–23 Sec A Q1(a)

Other forms you must recognise

  • Zener clipper (Sadat p.35, Khairul s.55–59): back-to-back Zeners across the output limit at \(+(V_{Z2} + 0.7)\) and \(-(V_{Z1} + 0.7)\). A single Zener: \(+V_Z\) and \(-0.7\) V.
  • Series clipper (Khairul s.46–49): diode in series, load \(R_L\) (and maybe a battery in series with \(R_L\)). Output = the part of the input that forward-biases the diode, e.g. "series negative clipper with positive bias" passes \(v_i\) above \(+V_b\).
  • Output across the series resistor (Sadat p.82): it is the complement of the normal clipper: \(v_R = v_i - v_o\), zero in the middle region and sloped outside.
  • Diode-bridge limiter (2016–17): all four diodes on while the bias current exceeds the load current; the output then follows the input.

3. The piecewise-linear model (teacher's rewrites of P4.87, P4.91, P4.95)

The teacher crossed out the exponential model in the highlighted Sedra limiter problems and asked for PWL or CVD. A PWL diode is a battery \(V_{D0}\) plus \(r_D\). From two points: \(r_D = \Delta V/\Delta I\) (P4.87: conduction starts at 0.5 V, 0.7 V at 1 mA, so \(r_D\) = 200 Ω). In the on region the diode branch has \(R_k = r_D\), so the slope is \(r_D/(R + r_D)\): \(0.2/1.2 = 1/6\) in P4.87 and \(0.1/1.1 = 1/11\) in P4.91.

4. Design from a required curve (type D-14)

Method · design a shunt clipper

  1. Middle slope 1 → unloaded shunt clipper: series \(R\), branches to ground. (Middle slope 0 → output taken across the series resistor, Sadat p.82.)
  2. Upper breakpoint \(L_+\): diode with anode toward \(v_o\), battery \(V = L_+ - V_\gamma\) (+ toward the diode). Lower breakpoint \(L_-\): diode with cathode toward \(v_o\), reference \(V = L_- + V_\gamma\) (a negative value means battery + at ground).
  3. Flat beyond the breakpoint → no resistor in that branch. Slope \(s\) → branch resistor \(R_k = R\dfrac{s}{1 - s}\) (\(s = 1/2\): \(R_k = R\); \(1/3\): \(R/2\)).
  4. Only diodes allowed (no batteries): use \(n = |L|/V_\gamma\) diodes in series (2.1/0.7 = 3).
  5. Two corners on one side (soft, then hard): two branches, the hard one with the higher threshold.
  6. Re-analyse your circuit to check every corner.

5. Graphical I–V and waveform construction (type D-12)

Old papers (2012–13, 2013–14) give a drawn diode I–V and an input waveform. Read the model off the graph: turn-on voltage \(V_{on}\), slope (\(r_D\) = inverse slope), reverse breakdown at \(-V_{BR}\). In series with \(R\): \(v_R = (v_{in} - V_{on})\frac{R}{R + r_D}\) above \(V_{on}\), \(v_R = (v_{in} + V_{BR})\frac{R}{R + r_{BR}}\) below \(-V_{BR}\), zero between; \(v_D = v_{in} - v_R\). A diode in parallel with an ideal source just has \(v_D = v_{in}\).

Traps (where marks go)

  • Drawing a flat clip when the branch has a resistor (the 2022–23 student sketch did this: the output keeps rising with slope 2/3 and 1/2).
  • Battery polarity: read the long plate as +. A battery with + at ground gives a negative reference.
  • With a load, the breakpoint in \(v_i\) is not the threshold in \(v_o\): \(v_{i,bp} = E(R + R_L)/R_L\).
  • Model not stated: say "ideal" or "CVD 0.7 V" and use it consistently; "non-ideal" means CVD.
  • Design: forgetting to verify; matching a slope without the branch resistor; wrong sign of the battery for a negative level.

2021–22 · Sec B · Q5(b) · 15 marks

Design a limiter circuit using only diodes and 10 kΩ resistors to provide an output signal limited to the range ±2.1 V. Assume each diode has a 0.7 V drop when conducting.

Show worked answer

No batteries allowed, so build each level from diode drops: \(n = 2.1/0.7 = 3\) diodes in series. Series 10 kΩ from \(v_i\) to \(v_o\); from \(v_o\) to ground one string of 3 diodes pointing down (clips at +2.1 V) and one string of 3 pointing up (clips at −2.1 V).

Transfer: \(v_o = v_i\) for \(|v_i| \le 2.1\) V; \(v_o = +2.1\) V for \(v_i \ge 2.1\); \(v_o = -2.1\) V for \(v_i \le -2.1\). When limiting, the source current is \((v_i \mp 2.1)/10\) kΩ. (If you add a 10 kΩ load the middle slope becomes 1/2 and limiting starts at ±4.2 V; the limits stay ±2.1 V.)

2018–19 · Sec A · Q1(d) · 10 marks

For a sinusoidal input, draw the output waveshape for the circuit: series R from \(v_i\) to \(v_o\); a diode with cathode at \(v_o\) and anode at the + terminal of a battery V whose − terminal is grounded. (No values given.)

Show worked answer

Ideal diode (state it): it conducts when \(v_o < V\), so \(v_o = V\) whenever \(v_i < V\), and \(v_o = v_i\) when \(v_i \ge V\). The output follows the sine above \(+V\) and sits flat at \(+V\) elsewhere (take \(V_m > V\)); the diode is off for \(\sin^{-1}(V/V_m) < \omega t < \pi - \sin^{-1}(V/V_m)\). With CVD the level is \(V - 0.7\).

2017–18 · Sec A · Q4(b) · 18 marks

Design a parallel-based clipper that yields the transfer characteristic shown (\(v_O = -5\) V for \(v_I \le -5\) V; \(v_O = v_I\) for \(-5 \le v_I \le 2.5\) V; slope 1/3 above 2.5 V). Assume 0.7 V cut-in.

Show worked answer

Middle slope 1 → shunt clipper with series R. Upper corner 2.5 V with slope 1/3: branch with diode (anode toward \(v_o\)), battery \(2.5 - 0.7 = 1.8\) V (+ toward the diode) and \(R_1\) with \(\frac{R_1}{R + R_1} = \frac13 \Rightarrow R = 2R_1\): take \(R\) = 20 kΩ, \(R_1\) = 10 kΩ. Lower level −5 V flat: diode with cathode at \(v_o\), anode at \(-5 + 0.7 = -4.3\) V (4.3 V battery, + at ground), no resistor.

Check: \(v_I \ge 2.5\): \(v_O = 1.8 + 0.7 + (v_I - 2.5)\frac{10}{30} = \frac{v_I}{3} + \frac53\) (2.5 V at 2.5 V ✓, 5 V at 10 V); \(v_I \le -5\): \(v_O = -4.3 - 0.7 = -5\) V ✓.

2016–17 · Sec B · Q5(b) · 13 marks

Find and sketch the transfer characteristic of the diode-bridge limiter (CVD 0.7 V): +10 V → 10 kΩ → node T; node B → 10 kΩ → −10 V; D1: T→X, D2: T→O, D3: X→B, D4: O→B (anode→cathode); \(v_i\) at X; load 10 kΩ from O to ground.

Show worked answer

All on (small \(|v_i|\)): \(V_T = v_i + 0.7\), \(V_O = V_T - 0.7 = v_i\): slope 1. Bias currents \(I_T = (10 - v_i - 0.7)/10\) mA and \(I_B = (v_i - 0.7 + 10)/10\) mA.

Upper break: as \(v_i\) rises, D2 must carry the load current \(v_o/10\) while \(I_T\) falls; D1 cuts off when \(I_T = I_L\): \((9.3 - v_i)/10 = v_i/10 \Rightarrow v_i = 4.65\) V. Beyond it D1 and D4 are off and the output is set by +10 V, 10 kΩ, D2 and the 10 kΩ load: \(v_o = (10 - 0.7)\frac{10}{20} = 4.65\) V, flat.

By symmetry \(v_o = -4.65\) V for \(v_i \le -4.65\) V. VTC: flat −4.65 | slope 1 | flat +4.65 V.

2016–17 · Sec B · Q5(c) · 13⅔ marks

Plot \(v_o\) versus \(v_i\) and \(i_1\) versus \(v_i\) for \(-10 \le v_i \le 10\) V; ideal diodes, \(V_Z\) = 3 V.

Show worked answer

\(v_i < 0\): the Zener is forward biased and D conducts, so \(v_o = 0\) (D clamps the output at ground) and \(i_1 = v_i/10\) kΩ (−1 mA at −10 V).

\(0 \le v_i \le 3\) V: the Zener blocks (below \(V_Z\)): \(i_1 = 0\), \(v_o = 0\).

2013–14 · Sec A · Q1(a) 26 marks, Q1(b) 10 marks, Q1(c) 10⅔ marks

A diode in series with a 1 Ω resistor; the diode I–V: zero current for −3 V < V < 1 V, a line of slope 1 A/V above 1 V, vertical breakdown at −3 V. The input is a staircase 0, 1, 2, 3, 2, 1, 0, −1, −2, −3, −2, −1, 0 V (the text says "sinusoidal"). (a) Draw \(V_{out}\) across the resistor. (b) What would \(V_{out}\) be if the diode had a constant 0.7 V drop? (c) What if the diode and resistor were in parallel?

Show worked answer

Model: \(V_{on}\) = 1 V, \(r_D\) = 1 Ω, \(V_{BR}\) = 3 V (vertical).

(a) \(V_{in} > 1\): \(V_{out} = (V_{in} - 1)\frac{1}{1 + 1}\). Steps: 0, 0, 0.5, 1.0, 0.5, 0, 0 V; negative half all 0 (the −3 V step only reaches the breakdown edge, current ≈ 0).

(b) \(V_{out} = V_{in} - 0.7\) for \(V_{in} > 0.7\): 0, 0.3, 1.3, 2.3, 1.3, 0.3, 0, and 0 on the negative half (an ideal CVD diode has no breakdown).

(c) Both are directly across the ideal source: \(V_{out} = V_{in}\) (the diode only draws extra current).

2012–13 · Sec A · Q4(a) 20 marks, Q4(b) 16 marks, Q4(c) 10⅔ marks

An ac source drives a diode and resistor in series. \(V_{ac}\) is a triangle (slope ±1 V/s): 0 → +3 V at 3 s → 0 at 6 s → −3 V at 9 s → 0 at 12 s. \(V_R\) is 0 until 1 s, peaks at 3 s, returns to 0 at 5 s; 0 until 8 s, a negative peak at 9 s, 0 at 10 s. (a) Copy \(V_R\) and draw the diode voltage. (b) Draw the diode I–V to scale. (c) Draw the diode voltage if the diode were in parallel with R.

Show worked answer

Conduction starts at \(V_{ac}\) = +1 V (t = 1 s) and at −2 V (t = 8 s), so the diode turns on at +1 V forward and breaks down at −2 V. With \(V_R\) rising at the same 1 V/s slope, \(r_D \approx 0\): \(V_R\) peaks at +2 V and −1 V. (The printed figure suggests 2.2 V and −1.2 V; read literally that needs a negative-resistance diode, so it is a drawing error. The QBank answer uses those values; do not.)

(a) \(V_D = V_{ac} - V_R\): rises 0 → 1 V (0–1 s), held at 1 V (1–5 s), follows \(V_{ac}\) from 1 V down to −2 V (5–8 s), held at −2 V (8–10 s), then −2 → 0 V (10–12 s).

(b) I–V: zero current between −2 V and +1 V, a vertical line at +1 V (forward) and at −2 V (reverse breakdown). (c) In parallel with an ideal source: \(V_D = V_{ac}\).

Practice

Key terms

Quick check

Summary: anode at \(v_o\) clips the top at \(V_{ref} + V_\gamma\); cathode at \(v_o\) clips the bottom at \(V_{ref} - V_\gamma\). Middle region \(v_o = v_i\) (or the load divider). Beyond a breakpoint: flat if the branch has no resistor, slope \(R_k/(R + R_k)\) if it has one (\(r_D\) counts as \(R_k\) for a PWL diode). Design: level → battery \(L \mp V_\gamma\), slope \(s\) → \(R_k = Rs/(1 - s)\), no batteries → \(|L|/V_\gamma\) diodes in series. Always re-check corners.
