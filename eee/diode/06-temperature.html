Temperature and Thermal Resistance

The same 15 A diode-heating question was set in 2023–24 (10 marks) and 2024–25 (15 marks), and in batch 23's CT-1: three numbers, three lines each. Expect it again. A senior solution of 2023–24 got the subtraction wrong (110 °C instead of 60 °C), so write every line.

1. What temperature does to a diode

Voltage at constant current
Falls by about 2 mV per °C: \(V_D(T_2) = V_D(T_1) - 2\text{ mV}\times(T_2 - T_1)\). A diode run at fixed current is a thermometer.
Saturation current \(I_S\)
About 15 % per °C, i.e. it doubles every 5 °C: \(I_S(T_2) = I_S(T_1)\times2^{(T_2 - T_1)/5}\). The reverse current of a real diode (mostly leakage) roughly doubles every 10 °C.
\(V_T = kT/q\)
Grows with T (25.9 mV at 300 K, 32.2 mV at 100 °C); the \(I_S\) increase wins, which is why \(V_D\) falls.
Thermal resistance \(\theta\) (°C/W)
Temperature rise per watt dissipated: \(T_J = T_A + \theta P_D\), \(\theta = \Delta T/P\).

Method · "current applied, voltage drifts down as it heats"

  1. \(\Delta V = V_{final} - V_{initial}\) (negative). Check the subtraction.
  2. \(\Delta T = |\Delta V|/(2\text{ mV/°C})\) (current is constant, so the whole drift is temperature).
  3. Final power \(P = V_{final}\times I\) (the steady state that produced the rise).
  4. \(\theta = \Delta T/P\) in °C/W. (Initial power \(0.7\times I\) only if asked.)

Worked example: Sedra P4.31 (book numbers)

Traps (where marks go)

  • Arithmetic: 0.58 − 0.70 = −0.12 V, not −0.22 V (the senior solution's slip gave 110 °C and 12.64 °C/W).
  • Using the initial 10.5 W for θ: the rise was produced by the final dissipation.
  • Sign: \(V_D\) falls as T rises; \(I_S\) rises.
  • Doubling per 5 °C means \(2^{\Delta T/5}\), not \(2\Delta T/5\).

Exam questions

2024–25 · Sec B · Q8(b) · 15 marks

When 15 A is applied to a particular diode, the junction voltage immediately becomes 0.7 V; as the diode heats it decreases to 0.6 V. (i) What is the apparent rise in junction temperature? (ii) What is the temperature rise per watt? (iii) What is the final power dissipated?

Show worked answer

Assume −2 mV/°C at constant current. (i) \(\Delta V = 0.6 - 0.7 = -0.1\) V → \(\Delta T = 100/2 = 50\) °C. (iii) \(P = 0.6\times15 = 9.0\) W. (ii) \(\theta = 50/9 = 5.56\) °C/W.

2023–24 · Sec A · Q2(a) · 10 marks  |  also CT-1 Q1, batch 23 (6 marks)

When a 15 A current is applied to a particular diode, the junction voltage immediately becomes 0.7 V. As the power dissipated raises its temperature, the voltage decreases and eventually reaches 0.58 V. (i) What is the apparent rise in junction temperature? (ii) What is the temperature rise per watt of power dissipation? (iii) What is the power dissipated in the diode in its final state?

Show worked answer

(i) \(\Delta V = 0.58 - 0.70 = -0.12\) V → \(\Delta T = 120/2 = 60\) °C. (iii) \(P = 0.58\times15 = 8.7\) W. (ii) \(\theta = 60/8.7 = 6.90\) °C/W. (A senior solution writes ΔV = −0.22 V → 110 °C and 12.64 °C/W: wrong.)

Key terms

Quick check

Summary: at constant current \(V_D\) drops 2 mV/°C, so \(\Delta T = |\Delta V|/2\text{ mV}\); \(P = V_{final}I\); \(\theta = \Delta T/P\). \(I_S\) doubles every 5 °C. \(T_J = T_A + \theta P\).
