Diode Models: Ideal, Constant Voltage Drop, Piecewise Linear

The same diode circuit solved with three models came up in 2011–12 (18 marks), and "describe the different models for the diode forward characteristic" in 2022–23 (7 marks); 2013–14 gave a straight-line I–V graph and asked for the output, then asked again with a 0.7 V drop (36⅔ marks). More important: every diode question in this course starts with choosing a model. Get the three models and the Thevenin reduction automatic and the rest of the diode chapters become KVL.

1. The models

Ideal diode
On (\(i > 0\)): a short, \(v_D = 0\). Off (\(v_D < 0\)): an open, \(i = 0\). No cut-in voltage, no leakage. Good for a first pass and when the circuit voltages are much larger than 0.7 V.
Constant voltage drop (CVD)
On: a battery \(V_{D0}\) = 0.7 V (cut-in \(V_c\) in Sadat sir's notes); on when \(v_D \ge V_{D0}\) with \(i > 0\). Off: open. The default in exams: "if not specified, it is always CVD" (Ikra's notes of Sadat L8).
Piecewise linear (PWL, battery-plus-resistance)
On: battery \(V_{D0}\) in series with \(r_D\): \(v_D = V_{D0} + i_Dr_D\), i–v slope \(1/r_D\). Sedra's fit: \(V_{D0}\) = 0.65 V, \(r_D\) = 20 Ω; the teacher's examples use 0.7 V with \(R_{on}\) = 10 Ω or 100 Ω. Fit from two points: \(r_D = \Delta v/\Delta i\), \(V_{D0}\) where the line meets i = 0.
Exponential
\(i = I_S(e^{v/nV_T} - 1)\). Exact but transcendental: solve by iteration, graphically (load line), or with the calculator's SOLVE (Chapter 4).
Small signal
A resistance \(r_d = nV_T/I_D\) for small changes about a DC point (Chapter 5). It is a model for the change, used on top of one of the others.

2. Which model, when

ModelUse whenConsistency check
Ideal"assume ideal diodes"; quick state finding; logic levels; volts ≫ 0.7on: \(i > 0\); off: \(v_D \le 0\)
CVDdefault; "0.7 V drop model"on: \(i > 0\) (only current, since v is forced); off: \(v_D < 0.7\)
PWL"\(V_{D0}\), \(r_D\) given"; soft limiters; better accuracyon: \(i > 0\) or \(v_D > V_{D0}\); off: \(v_D < V_{D0}\)
Exponential\(I_S\) or "0.7 V at 1 mA" given; diode strings; ratiosalways on in forward bias

Sadat sir's note (L5, notes p.15): with CVD you can check an assumed state only by the current direction, because the voltage was set to 0.7 V by assumption; with PWL you may check either.

3. One loop, every model (type D-03)

Method · single-diode circuit with each model

  1. Thevenin: remove the diode and find \(V_{TH}\) (open-circuit voltage at the anode with respect to the cathode) and \(R_{TH}\). A resistor across the diode halves things: never skip this.
  2. On check: ideal on if \(V_{TH} > 0\); CVD/PWL on if \(V_{TH} > V_{D0}\). Otherwise \(I_D = 0\), \(V_D = V_{TH}\).
  3. Ideal: \(I_D = V_{TH}/R_{TH}\), \(V_D = 0\).
  4. CVD: \(I_D = (V_{TH} - V_{D0})/R_{TH}\), \(V_D = V_{D0}\).
  5. PWL: \(I_D = \dfrac{V_{TH} - V_{D0}}{R_{TH} + r_D}\), \(V_D = V_{D0} + I_Dr_D\) (not 0.7 V).
  6. Comment: the three answers bracket the true one; PWL is closest.

Worked example: Sadat L3, notes p.5–7

Fitting a PWL model to two points

Method · PWL from two measured points

  1. \(r_D = \dfrac{V_2 - V_1}{I_2 - I_1}\).
  2. \(V_{D0} = V_1 - I_1r_D\) (extend the line to zero current).
  3. Use the PWL recipe above.

4. A straight-line I–V graph is a PWL model (type D-12)

If the paper draws the diode I–V as straight segments, read the model off the graph: turn-on voltage, forward slope (\(r_D\) = 1/slope; "1 V/A" means 1 Ω), reverse breakdown voltage. Then the series circuit gives \(v_R = (v_{in} - V_{on})\dfrac{R}{R + r_D}\) above turn-on, zero in the gap, and \((v_{in} + V_{BR})\) in breakdown (Chapter 1, §7). A staircase input is the easiest case: compute each step separately.

Traps (where marks go)

  • Skipping Thevenin when a resistor sits across the diode (2011–12: the 1 kΩ in parallel halves \(V_{TH}\) to 2.5 V and \(R_{TH}\) to 0.5 kΩ).
  • Writing \(V_D\) = 0.7 V for the PWL model. It is \(V_{D0} + I_Dr_D\).
  • Ideal "on" means 0 V across the diode, not 0.7 V.
  • Assuming the diode is on without the check: if \(V_{TH} < V_{D0}\) the CVD and PWL answers are \(I_D = 0\) even though the ideal model conducts.
  • Diode in parallel with R straight across an ideal source: \(v_{out} = v_{in}\).

Exam questions

2022–23 · Sec A · Q4(c) · 7 marks

Describe different models for diode forward characteristics.

Show worked answer

Sketch the four i–v curves (Figure 1) and write, for each, the equation, the equivalent circuit and where it is used.

  1. Exponential model: \(i_D = I_S(e^{v_D/nV_T} - 1)\), with \(I_S\) the saturation current, \(n\) = 1–2, \(V_T = kT/q \approx 25\) mV. Most accurate; the circuit equation becomes transcendental, so it is solved graphically (load line \(i_D = (V_{DD} - v_D)/R\) meets the curve at Q) or by iteration.
  2. Ideal model: forward-biased diode is a short (\(v_D = 0\)), reverse-biased an open (\(i_D = 0\)). Used for first-pass analysis and when the applied voltages are much larger than the diode drop.
  3. Constant-voltage-drop model: when conducting, \(v_D = V_{D0} \approx 0.7\) V (a battery in series with an ideal diode); otherwise open. The steep part of the exponential is nearly vertical at 0.6–0.8 V, so this is accurate enough for most DC work; the commonest model in hand analysis.
  4. Piecewise-linear model: the exponential is replaced by two straight lines: \(i_D = 0\) for \(v_D < V_{D0}\), and \(i_D = (v_D - V_{D0})/r_D\) above it (battery \(V_{D0}\) ≈ 0.65 V in series with \(r_D\) ≈ 20 Ω and an ideal diode). Better accuracy over a chosen current range.
  5. (Also worth a line) Small-signal model: about a bias point \(I_D\), small changes see a resistance \(r_d = nV_T/I_D\), valid for \(|v_d| \ll nV_T\) (5–10 mV).
2013–14 · Sec A · Q1(a)(b)(c) · 26 + 10 + 10⅔ marks

Figure 1(a) shows a diode in series with a 1 Ω resistor across \(V_{in}\), and the diode's I–V characteristic: zero current for −3 V < V < 1 V, a forward line of slope "1 volt/Amp" starting at 1 V, and vertical reverse breakdown at −3 V. (b) What would \(V_{out}\) be if the diode had a constant 0.7 V drop? (c) What would the output be if the diode and resistor were connected in parallel?

Show worked answer

Model from the graph: \(V_{on}\) = 1 V, \(r_D\) = 1 Ω (1 A per volt), breakdown at −3 V with zero slope resistance.

(a) For \(V_{in} > 1\) V: \(I = (V_{in} - 1)/(1 + 1)\), \(V_{out} = I\times1 = (V_{in} - 1)/2\). For −3 V ≤ \(V_{in}\) ≤ 1 V the diode is open and \(V_{out}\) = 0. At the −3 V step the diode is only at the edge of breakdown, so the current is still zero.

2011–12 · Sec B · Q6(b) · 18 marks

Determine \(I_D\) and \(V_D\) using (i) the piecewise-linear model (\(V_{D0}\) = 0.7 V, \(r_D\) = 20 Ω), (ii) the constant-voltage-drop model (0.7 V), (iii) the ideal diode model.

Show worked answer

Thevenin at the diode: \(V_{TH} = 5\times\dfrac{1}{1+1} = 2.5\) V, \(R_{TH} = 1\parallel1 = 0.5\) kΩ. \(V_{TH} > 0.7\) V, so the diode conducts in every model.

(i) PWL: \(I_D = \dfrac{2.5 - 0.7}{0.5 + 0.02} = \dfrac{1.8}{0.52} = 3.462\) mA; \(V_D = 0.7 + 3.462\times0.02 = 0.769\) V.

(ii) CVD: \(I_D = (2.5 - 0.7)/0.5 = 3.6\) mA; \(V_D = 0.7\) V.

(iii) Ideal: \(I_D = 2.5/0.5 = 5\) mA; \(V_D = 0\).

Comment: the ideal model overestimates the current by about 40 %; CVD and PWL agree within 4 %. (An exponential diode with 0.7 V at 1 mA would give 3.54 mA at 0.732 V.)

Highlighted textbook problem

Sedra 7e P4.10 · highlighted by the teacher (he struck out "utilize Thévenin's theorem")

Ideal diodes. Find I and V. (a) +5 V feeds node A through 10 kΩ, 10 kΩ from A to ground; diode from A (anode) to node V; 20 kΩ from V to ground. (b) Divider 10 kΩ/10 kΩ from +5 V gives node B; a second divider 10 kΩ/10 kΩ from +3 V gives node C; diode anode at C, cathode at B; V = \(V_C - V_B\).

Show worked answer

(a) Without Thevenin: assume the diode on, so \(V_A = V\). KCL at A: \(\dfrac{5 - V}{10} = \dfrac{V}{10} + \dfrac{V}{20}\) → \(5 - V = V + 0.5V\) → \(V = 2\) V. \(I = V/20 = 0.1\) mA > 0 ✓. (Thevenin: 2.5 V behind 5 kΩ, \(I = 2.5/25 = 0.1\) mA, same.)

(b) With the diode open, \(V_B = 2.5\) V and \(V_C = 1.5\) V: the anode is 1 V below the cathode, so the diode is off. \(I = 0\), \(V = V_C - V_B = -1\) V.

Practice

Key terms

Quick check

Summary: reduce the circuit seen by the diode to \(V_{TH}\), \(R_{TH}\); check on/off against 0 (ideal) or \(V_{D0}\) (CVD, PWL). Then ideal \(I = V_{TH}/R_{TH}\); CVD \(I = (V_{TH} - V_{D0})/R_{TH}\); PWL \(I = (V_{TH} - V_{D0})/(R_{TH} + r_D)\) and \(V_D = V_{D0} + Ir_D\). A straight-line I–V graph is a PWL model; a staircase input is a set of separate DC problems.
