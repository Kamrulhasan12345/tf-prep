Rectifiers and Battery Chargers

A rectifier without a capacitor appeared in 2011–12, 2018–19, 2019–20, 2020–21, 2021–22, 2022–23 and 2023–24: seven papers, 10 to 23 marks, and the same Sedra numbers (12 V rms, 0.7 V, 100 Ω, or 120 V through 10:1 into 1 kΩ) keep coming back. The battery charger (2011–12, 2015–16) uses the same "fraction of the cycle the diode conducts" idea Sadat sir taught in L6. With a filter capacitor the problem becomes the peak rectifier of Chapter 9.

1. The three rectifiers

All three use the constant-voltage-drop model (Sadat sir: "if not specified, it is always CVD"). \(V_s\) is the peak of the sinusoid driving the diodes: \(V_s = \sqrt2V_{rms}\), after dividing by the transformer turns ratio.

Half-wave (HW)
One diode in series with \(R\). \(v_O = v_S - V_D\) while \(v_S > V_D\), else 0. PIV \(= V_s\).
Centre-tapped full-wave (CT)
Two diodes, each driven by one half of a centre-tapped secondary (peak \(V_s\) each). \(v_O = |v_S| - V_D\). PIV \(= 2V_s - V_D\): when D2 is off its cathode is at \(V_s - V_D\) and its anode at \(-V_s\).
Bridge
Four diodes, ordinary secondary; two diodes conduct in each half-cycle. \(v_O = |v_S| - 2V_D\). PIV \(= V_s - V_D\): the off diode is in parallel with (on diode + load).
Dead zone
The output is zero while \(|v_S|\) is below the drops in the path: conduction starts at \(\theta = \sin^{-1}(V_0/V_s)\) with \(V_0 = V_D\) (HW, CT) or \(2V_D\) (bridge), and ends at \(\pi - \theta\).

Comparison (Sadat L8): centre-tap uses only two diodes and one drop; the bridge needs no centre-tapped transformer and has about half the PIV. "Lower PIV is better": a diode rated 5 V reverse fails in a circuit with PIV 6 V.

2. Average, rms and conduction fraction

Derivation asked in 2011–12 (bridge). Each half-cycle repeats, so average over one half-period, ignoring the tiny dead zone:

\[ V_O = \frac1\pi\int_0^\pi\left(V_s\sin\phi - 2V_{D0}\right)d\phi = \frac{1}{\pi}\Big[-V_s\cos\phi\Big]_0^\pi - 2V_{D0} = \frac{2}{\pi}V_s - 2V_{D0} \]

Exact (with the dead zone). Integrate only from \(\theta\) to \(\pi - \theta\), \(\theta = \sin^{-1}(V_0/V_s)\):

\[ V_{avg,FW} = \frac{2V_s\cos\theta - V_0(\pi - 2\theta)}{\pi},\qquad V_{avg,HW} = \frac{2V_s\cos\theta - V_D(\pi - 2\theta)}{2\pi} \]

rms (Sadat notes p.29: \(V_{rms} = \sqrt{\frac1T\int v_o^2\,dt}\)). Ideal diodes: \(V_{rms} = V_s/2\) (HW), \(V_s/\sqrt2\) (FW). With a drop \(V_0\):

\[ V_{rms,FW}^2 = \frac1\pi\Big[V_s^2\Big(\frac{\pi - 2\theta}{2} + \frac{\sin2\theta}{2}\Big) - 4V_sV_0\cos\theta + V_0^2(\pi - 2\theta)\Big],\qquad V_{rms,HW}^2 = \tfrac12V_{rms,FW}^2 \]

Fraction of the cycle a diode conducts \(= (\theta_2 - \theta_1)/360^\circ = (\pi - 2\theta)/2\pi\). It does not depend on \(R\) (Sadat L6). For a bridge each diode conducts just under half the cycle.

3. Rectifier without capacitor (type D-07)

Method · six lines

  1. \(V_s = \sqrt2V_{rms}\) (divide a mains rms by the turns ratio first).
  2. Peak output \(V_s - V_0\), with \(V_0 = V_D\) (HW, CT) or \(2V_D\) (bridge).
  3. \(\theta = \sin^{-1}(V_0/V_s)\); fraction each diode conducts \(= (180^\circ - 2\theta)/360^\circ\).
  4. \(V_{avg}\): Sedra form \((2/\pi)V_s - V_0\) (or \(V_s/\pi - V_D/2\) HW); write the exact value too if asked "exactly".
  5. \(I_{avg} = V_{avg}/R\); peak diode current \(= (V_s - V_0)/R\).
  6. PIV: HW \(V_s\), CT \(2V_s - V_D\), bridge \(V_s - V_D\).

Worked example: 2023–24 Sec A Q3(a) (also 2022–23 Q4(b))

Variant: two diodes and two resistors (2020–21)

Only one diode conducts in each half-cycle; solve each half by a divider. With \(R_1 = R_2 = R\) and load \(R_L\) the output is \(|v_o| = k|v_i|\), \(k = \dfrac{R\parallel R_L}{R + R\parallel R_L}\): a full-wave rectifier with gain \(k\). The off diode sees the whole input, so PIV \(= V_m\).

4. Battery charger and "fraction of the cycle" (type D-11)

A source \(v_s = V_m\sin\omega t\) charges a battery \(V_B\) through \(R\) and a diode (Figure 7). The diode conducts only while \(v_s > V_B + V_D\).

Method · battery charger

  1. State the model (ideal unless a drop is given; CVD alternative is a bonus line).
  2. \(\theta_1 = \sin^{-1}\dfrac{V_B + V_D}{V_m}\); conducts from \(\theta_1\) to \(180^\circ - \theta_1\); fraction \(= \dfrac{180^\circ - 2\theta_1}{360^\circ}\).
  3. Peak current at \(\omega t = 90^\circ\): \(I_p = (V_m - V_B - V_D)/R\). Draw \(i_D = (v_s - V_B - V_D)/R\) as a cap.
  4. Maximum reverse voltage at the negative peak: \(V_m + V_B\) (the battery adds).
  5. Average charging current, if asked: \(\dfrac{2V_m\cos\theta_1 - (V_B + V_D)(\pi - 2\theta_1)}{2\pi R}\).

Worked example: Sadat L6, slide 14 #4

Traps (where marks go)

  • One \(V_D\) instead of two for a bridge.
  • Average = \(2V_{o,peak}/\pi\) = 9.91 V (Gemini's answer for 2021–22): wrong. It is \((2/\pi)V_s - 2V_D\) = 9.40 V (exact 9.44 V).
  • Centre-tap PIV is \(2V_s - V_D\), not \(V_s\); bridge PIV is \(V_s - V_D\).
  • Forgetting the turns ratio, or using the rms value as the peak.
  • Battery charger PIV is \(V_m + V_B\), not \(V_m\).
  • Writing the conduction angle (170.5°) when the question asks for a fraction (47.4 %).

Exam questions

2023–24 · Sec A · Q3(a) · 10 marks  |  also 2022–23 Sec A Q4(b) · 16 marks (same question)

A full-wave bridge rectifier with a 1 kΩ load operates from a 120 V (rms), 60 Hz household supply through a 10:1 step-down transformer with a single secondary winding. It uses four diodes, each modelled as a 0.7 V drop at any current. (i) What is the peak value of the rectified voltage across the load? (ii) For what fraction of a cycle does each diode conduct? (iii) What are the average voltage across and the average current through the load?

Show worked answer

Secondary 12 V rms: \(V_s = 12\sqrt2 = 16.97\) V.

(i) \(V_{o,p} = 16.97 - 2(0.7) = 15.57\) V.

(ii) Conduction starts when \(v_s = 2V_D\): \(\theta = \sin^{-1}(1.4/16.97) = 4.73^\circ\), ends at \(175.27^\circ\). Each diode conducts \(170.54^\circ\) per cycle, i.e. \(170.54/360 = 47.4\%\).

(iii) \(V_{avg} \approx \frac{2}{\pi}(16.97) - 1.4 = 9.40\) V (exact with the dead zone: 9.44 V). \(I_{avg} = 9.40/1 = 9.4\) mA (9.44 mA).

One senior solution stops before (iii); the other gives 9.4 V / 9.4 mA, correct.

2021–22 · Sec B · Q5(c) · 15 marks  |  also 2018–19 Sec A Q1(b) · 10 marks (peak current and PIV only) · Sadat slide 22

For the bridge rectifier (Sedra Fig. 4.24), \(v_s\) is a 12 V (rms) sinusoid, \(V_D\) = 0.7 V and R = 100 Ω. Find the average of the output voltage \(v_o\), the peak diode current and the PIV.

Show worked answer

\(V_s = 16.97\) V; \(V_{o,p} = 16.97 - 1.4 = 15.57\) V.

\(V_{avg} = \frac2\pi(16.97) - 2(0.7) = 10.80 - 1.40 = 9.40\) V. (Exact: \(\theta = 4.73^\circ\), \(V_{avg} = [2(16.97)\cos4.73^\circ - 1.4(\pi - 0.165)]/\pi = 9.44\) V.)

Peak diode current \(= 15.57/100 = 155.7\) mA. PIV \(= V_s - V_D = 16.27\) V.

The Gemini question bank answers 9.91 V (\(2V_{o,p}/\pi\)): that ignores where the drop is subtracted and is wrong.

2020–21 · Sec B · Q6(a) · 23⅓ marks

Draw the transfer curve of the circuit (Figure 6), assuming ideal diodes, \(R_1 = R_2\) = 20 kΩ and \(R_L\) = 10 kΩ. The input is a ±10 V sinusoid (period about 2 ms). (i) What is the maximum peak voltage of the output? (ii) What is the PIV? (iii) What is the average current through the load?

Show worked answer

\(v_i > 0\): the bottom diode conducts, so the bottom end of the source sits at ground; the top diode is reverse biased by \(v_i\). \(R_2\parallel R_L = 6.67\) kΩ, so \(v_o = v_i\dfrac{6.67}{20 + 6.67} = v_i/4\).

\(v_i < 0\): by symmetry the top diode conducts and \(v_o = |v_i|/4\). Transfer curve: \(v_o = |v_i|/4\), a V of slopes ±1/4 through the origin (full-wave rectifier with gain 1/4).

(i) \(V_{o,max} = 10/4 = 2.5\) V. (ii) PIV = 10 V (the off diode sees the full input). (iii) \(V_{avg} = \frac{2}{\pi}(2.5) = 1.59\) V, \(I_{L,avg} = 1.59/10 = 0.159\) mA.

The question-bank reading of the figure has the diodes reversed, giving \(v_o = -|v_i|/4\); magnitudes are identical. Say which way you read the diodes.

2019–20 (paper dated "January 2021") · Sec A · Q1(a) · 20 marks

Draw the circuit of a full-wave rectifier with a centre-tapped secondary winding and the input and output voltage waveshapes. Comment on the values of the RC time constant for a rectifier circuit.

Show worked answer

Draw Figure 2. Waveforms: input sine of peak \(V_s\) on each half winding; output = \(|v_s| - V_D\), humps of peak \(V_s - V_D\) at twice the input frequency, zero for \(|v_s| < V_D\) (dead zone of \(2\sin^{-1}(V_D/V_s)\) around each zero crossing). State: D1 conducts in positive half-cycles, D2 in negative; PIV \(= 2V_s - V_D\).

RC comment: add \(C\) across \(R\); need \(RC \gg T/2\) so \(V_r = V_p/(2fRC)\) is small and \(V_{dc} = V_p - V_r/2\); larger \(RC\) shrinks the ripple but shortens the conduction angle \(\sqrt{2V_r/V_p}\) and raises the peak diode current \(I_L(1 + 2\pi\sqrt{V_p/2V_r})\). Full answer in Chapter 9, section 7.

2015–16 · Sec A · Q1(a) · 15 marks

The circuit (Figure 7, R = 100 Ω) charges a 5 V battery. If \(v_s = 10\sin(100\pi t)\), find (i) the duty cycle (fraction of each cycle the diode conducts); (ii) draw the diode current waveform indicating the peak value; (iii) the maximum reverse-bias voltage across the diode.

Show worked answer

Ideal diode (none specified). \(f = 50\) Hz, \(T\) = 20 ms.

(i) Conducts when \(10\sin\theta > 5\): \(\theta_1 = 30^\circ\), \(\theta_2 = 150^\circ\). Fraction \(= 120/360 = 1/3\) (6.67 ms of every 20 ms).

(ii) \(i_D = (10\sin\omega t - 5)/100\) A between 30° and 150° (1.67 ms to 8.33 ms), zero otherwise (Figure 8). Peak \(= (10 - 5)/100 = 50\) mA at 5 ms.

(iii) At the negative peak: \(10 + 5 = 15\) V.

With a 0.7 V drop: \(\theta_1 = \sin^{-1}0.57 = 34.75^\circ\), fraction 30.7 %, peak 43 mA, PIV still 15 V.

2013–14 · Sec A · Q2(d) 5 marks + Q2(e) 10⅔ marks

(d) Write a script in C that takes a sinusoidal input and provides a full-wave rectified output. (e) How does your program rectify the input? Is there any diode inside the processor? If not, what makes rectification possible?

Show worked answer
#include <stdio.h>
#include <math.h>
int main(void) {
    const double A = 10, f = 50, fs = 5000;      /* amplitude, signal and sample rate */
    for (int n = 0; n < 200; n++) {
        double vin = A * sin(2 * M_PI * f * n / fs);
        double vout = fabs(vin);                  /* full-wave; bridge-like: fmax(fabs(vin) - 1.4, 0) */
        printf("%d %f %f\n", n, vin, vout);
    }
    return 0;
}

(e) The input is sampled into numbers (ADC); rectification is the arithmetic operation |x| (test the sign bit and negate negative samples). No rectifying diode is used in the data path: transistors acting as switches implement the logic (compare, conditional negate) in the ALU. A DAC turns the numbers back into a voltage if an analogue output is needed.

2011–12 · Sec B · Q5(c) · 15⅔ marks

A circuit for charging a 10 V battery (Figure 7, R = 100 Ω): \(v_s\) is a sinusoid of 20 V peak. Find the fraction of each cycle during which the (ideal) diode conducts, the peak diode current and the maximum reverse-bias voltage across the diode.

Key terms

Quick check

Summary: \(V_s = \sqrt2V_{rms}\)/turns ratio. Peak output \(V_s - V_D\) (HW, CT) or \(V_s - 2V_D\) (bridge). \(V_{avg} = \frac2\pi V_s - V_0\) for full-wave (\(\frac{V_s}{\pi} - \frac{V_D}{2}\) HW). Conduction fraction \((180^\circ - 2\theta)/360^\circ\), \(\theta = \sin^{-1}(\text{threshold}/V_s)\), independent of R. PIV: \(V_s\), \(2V_s - V_D\), \(V_s - V_D\). Battery charger: threshold \(V_B + V_D\), peak \((V_m - V_B - V_D)/R\), PIV \(V_m + V_B\).
