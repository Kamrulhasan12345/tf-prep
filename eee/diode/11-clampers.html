Clampers and Voltage Doublers

A clamper question appeared in 2011–12, 2013–14, 2014–15 (clipper followed by clamper, 20 marks), 2015–16, 2019–20 and 2022–23 (design, 12 marks): six papers out of the last eleven. It skipped 2023–24 and 2024–25, but Sadat sir spent a whole lecture (L10) on it, gave a handout combining a clamper with a clipper, and asked for design from a given graph. One idea solves every question: the capacitor charges once to whatever value makes the diode just touch its clamp level, and then the whole waveform is shifted by that value.

1. How a clamper works

A capacitor \(C\) in series, a diode (maybe with a battery) across the output. On the first extreme of the input that forward-biases the diode, \(C\) charges until the diode is at its threshold. After that the diode is off (with \(RC \gg T\) there is no discharge path), \(C\) keeps its voltage and \(v_o = v_i + V_C\): the same waveform, shifted. The peak-to-peak value is unchanged, so the transfer characteristic is a line of slope 1 that does not pass through the origin (Sadat: "a straight line with slope 1 not through the origin means a clamper").

Positive clamper
Diode cathode at \(v_o\), anode at \(V_{ref}\). It pins the minimum: \(v_{o,min} = V_{ref} - V_\gamma\). Shift \(= V_{ref} - V_\gamma - v_{i,min}\).
Negative clamper
Diode anode at \(v_o\), cathode at \(V_{ref}\). It pins the maximum: \(v_{o,max} = V_{ref} + V_\gamma\). Shift \(= V_{ref} + V_\gamma - v_{i,max}\).
\(V_C\)
The capacitor voltage equals the shift (taking + on the output side). It is the same for a square wave or a sine of the same peaks: "as long as the peak-to-peak voltages are the same, the clamping will be the same; just evaluate the peak values" (Sadat's handout).
Condition
\(RC \gg T\) (Khairul boxes it on every slide). The diode must actually conduct: for a positive clamper \(v_{i,min} < V_{ref} - V_\gamma\) (Sadat slide 31).

Sadat's warning (p.38): a positive clamper with a large negative bias can shift downwards, but a big reverse voltage may break the diode down. For a downward shift use a negative clamper.

2. Output levels of a biased clamper

Method · four lines

  1. Orientation: cathode at \(v_o\) → positive clamper (pins the minimum); anode at \(v_o\) → negative clamper (pins the maximum).
  2. Clamp level: \(V_{ref} - V_\gamma\) (positive) or \(V_{ref} + V_\gamma\) (negative), \(V_{ref}\) signed by the battery polarity (long plate +).
  3. Shift \(V_C\) = clamp level − \(v_{i,min}\) (positive) or clamp level − \(v_{i,max}\) (negative). Check the diode does conduct.
  4. \(v_o = v_i + V_C\): both levels; draw the shifted waveform and the transfer line \(v_o = v_i + V_C\).

3. Design a clamper for a target waveform

Method · design (Sadat notes p.40–41)

  1. Check the peak-to-peak values are equal; if not, a clipper is needed as well.
  2. Shift \(= v_{o,max} - v_{i,max}\). Upward → positive clamper; downward → negative clamper.
  3. Positive: \(V_{ref} = v_{o,min} + V_\gamma\). Negative: \(V_{ref} = v_{o,max} - V_\gamma\). Draw the battery with the right polarity (a negative \(V_{ref}\): + at ground).
  4. State \(RC \gg T\) and pick values (e.g. \(R\) = 100 kΩ, \(C\) = 1 µF for a 1 kHz input).

4. Finite RC: tilt, and the clamper with source and load resistors

With a load \(R\), \(C\) discharges while the diode is off: \(V_C(t) = V_{C0}e^{-t/RC}\), so the flat top droops by about \(\Delta V \approx V\frac{T/2}{RC}\), and at the next edge the diode recharges \(C\) almost instantly (a small spike past the clamp level, Sadat p.42: top 8 → 7.5 V, bottom kicks to −2.5 V and returns to −2 V). Large \(RC\) → ideal clamper.

With a source resistor \(R_s\) as well (2015–16), the capacitor current is no longer an instant spike: find \(V_C\) by charge balance. In steady state the average capacitor current is zero, so for equal half-periods (\(V_C\) = input side minus output side, positive clamper, ±\(V_m\) square):

\[ \frac{V_m - V_C}{R_s + R_L} + \frac{-V_m + V_\gamma - V_C}{R_s} = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; V_C = \frac{V_mR_s + (V_\gamma - V_m)(R_s + R_L)}{2R_s + R_L} \]

Diode-off half: \(v_o = (V_m - V_C)\dfrac{R_L}{R_s + R_L}\). Diode-on half: \(v_o = -V_\gamma\). Check the diode current is positive, and give the tilt from \(\tau = (R_s + R_L)C\).

5. Combinations and the voltage doubler

Clipper then clamper (2014–15): clip first, then clamp the clipped waveform's extreme. Clamper then clipper (Sadat's handout): find the shifted waveform \(v_x\), then clip it; the breakpoint in \(v_i\) is the clip level minus the shift.

Voltage doubler (Sedra Fig. 4.31): a clamper followed by a peak rectifier. \(C_1\) and \(D_1\) clamp the input so it swings from 0 to \(-2V_p\); \(D_2\) and \(C_2\) hold its negative peak. Output \(= -2V_p\) (ideal), \(-2(V_p - V_D)\) with CVD diodes. Flip both diodes for \(+2V_p\).

Traps (where marks go)

  • Forgetting \(V_\gamma\) or using the wrong sign: positive clamper level is \(V_{ref} - V_\gamma\), negative clamper \(V_{ref} + V_\gamma\). (The question-bank note on 2019–20 says 5.7 V with CVD; it is 4.3 V.)
  • Not stating \(RC \gg T\) (or an ideal diode) when the question gives no values.
  • Clamping the raw input instead of the clipped waveform in a clipper–clamper chain.
  • The sign of \(V_C\): say which side is +.
  • A clamper changes only the DC level; if the target peak-to-peak differs, you also need a clipper.

Exam questions

2022–23 · Sec A · Q4(a) · 12 marks

Design a clamper circuit to perform the function shown: input \(v_i\) a ±20 V square wave, output \(v_o\) a square wave between +30 V and −10 V. Assume ideal diodes.

Show worked answer

Both waves are 40 V peak-to-peak, so a clamper alone works. Shift \(= 30 - 20 = +10\) V (upward) → positive clamper. The minimum is pinned at −10 V: diode cathode at \(v_o\), anode at \(V_{ref} = -10\) V (ideal), i.e. a 10 V battery with + at ground.

During the −20 V half the diode conducts and \(C\) charges to 10 V (+ on the output side); then \(v_o = v_i + 10\): +30 / −10 V. Choose \(RC \gg T\), e.g. 1 µF with a 100 kΩ load at 1 kHz. (With 0.7 V diodes use a 9.3 V battery.)

2019–20 (paper dated "January 2021") · Sec A · Q1(b) · 16 marks

Draw the output waveshape for the circuit with the given square input (+10 V / −20 V): series C, diode with cathode at \(v_o\) and anode at the + terminal of a 5 V battery (− at ground), load R across the output.

Show worked answer

Assume an ideal diode and \(RC \gg T\). Positive clamper with \(V_{ref}\) = +5 V: the minimum of \(v_o\) is pinned at +5 V. During the −20 V half the diode conducts: \(V_C = 5 - (-20) = 25\) V (+ on the output side).

\(v_o = v_i + 25\): a square wave between +35 V and +5 V (peak-to-peak 30 V kept).

With 0.7 V diodes the clamp level is \(5 - 0.7 = 4.3\) V, so +34.3 / +4.3 V.

2015–16 · Sec A · Q2(b) · 15 marks

Draw the steady-state output for the ±5 V square input (period 1 ms): 1 kΩ in series, then 10 µF in series, diode with anode at ground and cathode at \(v_o\), 2 kΩ load. The diode is non-ideal (0.7 V).

Show worked answer

Let \(V_C\) = (resistor side) − (output side). Positive half: diode off, current \((5 - V_C)/3\) mA. Negative half: diode on, \(v_o = -0.7\) V, current \((-5 + 0.7 - V_C)/1\) mA. Charge balance (equal halves):

\(\dfrac{5 - V_C}{3} + (-4.3 - V_C) = 0 \Rightarrow 5 - V_C - 12.9 - 3V_C = 0 \Rightarrow V_C = -1.975\) V.

Positive half: \(v_o = (5 + 1.975)\frac{2}{3} = +4.65\) V. Negative half: \(v_o = -0.7\) V; diode current \(= 2.325 - 0.35 = 1.975\) mA > 0 ✓.

Tilt: \(\tau_{off} = (1 + 2)\text{ kΩ}\times10\ \mu\text{F} = 30\) ms over 0.5 ms, so the top droops by about \(4.65\times0.5/30 = 0.08\) V (exact steady state: 4.69 → 4.61 V); the bottom is flat at −0.7 V.

2014–15 · Sec A · Q2(a) · 20 marks

Assuming ideal diodes, find and draw \(V_o\). Input: triangle, 0 → +10 V at 2.5 ms → −10 V at 7.5 ms → 0 at 10 ms. Circuit: 10 Ω from \(v_{in}\) to node A; D1 from A (anode) to +6 V; D2 from −4 V (anode) to A; C from A to the output; D3 from ground (anode) to the output (cathode).

Show worked answer

Clipper: \(v_A = v_{in}\) clipped to the range −4 V … +6 V. With slope 4 V/ms: \(v_A\) reaches 6 V at 1.5 ms, stays until 3.5 ms; reaches −4 V at 6.0 ms, stays until 9.0 ms.

Clamper: D3 (cathode at the output) is a positive clamper with \(V_{ref}\) = 0: it pins the minimum of \(v_o\) at 0. The minimum of \(v_A\) is −4 V, so \(V_C\) = 4 V and \(V_o = v_A + 4\).

\(V_o\) is the clipped triangle shifted up: 0 to +10 V, flat at 10 V from 1.5 to 3.5 ms and flat at 0 V from 6.0 to 9.0 ms.

2013–14 · Sec A · Q2(a) · 20 marks

Draw the output \(v_o\) and the \(v_i\)–\(v_o\) graph: \(v_i = 3\sin\omega t\), a series loop of C (with \(v_o\) defined across C), a diode and a 1 V battery; constant 0.7 V drop; C initially uncharged.

Show worked answer

There is no resistor, so the capacitor charges on the first quarter-cycle and can never discharge.

Question-bank reading (diode anode toward C, cathode to +1 V): the diode conducts when \(v_i - v_o \ge 1.7\) V, first at \(\omega t = \sin^{-1}(1.7/3)\). Then \(v_o = v_i - 1.7\) until the peak: \(v_o = 3 - 1.7 = 1.3\) V at \(T/4\). After that the diode stays off and \(v_o = 1.3\) V DC for ever; the \(v_i\)–\(v_o\) graph is a horizontal line at 1.3 V. The voltage across the diode + battery branch is \(v_i - 1.3\): a clamped sine from −4.3 V to +1.7 V (probably what the figure's "\(V_L\)" means).

As drawn in the scan (cathode toward C, anode at −1 V): the same reasoning gives \(v_o = -1.3\) V DC and the branch voltage \(v_i + 1.3\) (−1.7 V to +4.3 V). State which reading you use.

2011–12 · Sec B · Q6(c) · 12 marks

Design a clamper circuit to perform the indicated function using an ideal diode: input ±10 V square, output 0 / +20 V square.

Show worked answer

Peak-to-peak 20 V on both, shift +10 V with the minimum at 0: a positive clamper with no battery. Series C; diode with anode at ground and cathode at \(v_o\); load R with \(RC \gg T\). \(V_C\) = 10 V (+ on the output side), \(v_o = v_i + 10\).

Class examples and exercises (Sadat L10, handout, Ikra p.34–36)

Sadat notes p.40 · Ikra p.34

Design a clamper with \(v_o = v_i + 3\) for an input from −5 V to +5 V (0.7 V diode).

Show worked answer

Upward shift → positive clamper. Minimum output \(-5 + 3 = -2\) V \(= V_{ref} - 0.7\) → \(V_{ref} = -1.3\) V (1.3 V battery, + at ground). Output +8 / −2 V.

Sadat notes p.41 · Ikra p.35 (left unsolved in Ikra's notes)

Design a clamper from the graph: a line of slope 1 crossing the \(v_o\) axis at −4 V (\(v_o = v_i - 4\)); the input goes from −8 V to +10 V.

Show worked answer

Intercept negative → negative clamper (diode anode at \(v_o\)). Maximum output \(10 - 4 = 6\) V \(= V_{ref} + 0.7\) → \(V_{ref}\) = 5.3 V (battery + toward the diode cathode). Output +6 / −12 V. With \(RC \gg T\) "the capacitor decays extremely slowly" (Ikra's red note), so the levels hold.

Sadat sir's Clipper-Clamper Note (handout; Ikra p.36) · exercise (i) and (ii)

For a 10 V p-p input (−5 to +5 V) and 0.7 V diodes, design circuits that give: (i) \(v_o = v_i + 2\), flat below \(v_i\) = −3 V and above \(v_i\) = 4 V; (ii) \(v_o = v_i - 1\), flat below \(v_i\) = −2 V and above \(v_i\) = 2 V.

Show worked answer

(i) Clamper then clipper. Shift +2: positive clamper, \(v_{x,min} = -5 + 2 = -3 = V_{ref} - 0.7\) → anode at −2.3 V; \(v_x\) runs −3 … 7 V. Clip at \(v_o = -3 + 2 = -1\) V: diode cathode at \(v_o\), anode at −0.3 V. Clip at \(v_o = 4 + 2 = 6\) V: diode anode at \(v_o\), cathode at +5.3 V. Output −1 … 6 V.

(ii) Shift −1: negative clamper, \(v_{x,max} = 5 - 1 = 4 = V_{ref} + 0.7\) → cathode at +3.3 V; \(v_x\) runs −6 … 4 V. Clip at \(2 - 1 = 1\) V: diode anode at \(v_o\), cathode at +0.3 V. Clip at \(-2 - 1 = -3\) V: diode cathode at \(v_o\), anode at −2.3 V. Output −3 … 1 V.

Both verified by tracing the peaks: each clipper branch sits after a series R from \(v_x\).

Practice

Key terms

Quick check

Summary: cathode at \(v_o\) → positive clamper, minimum pinned at \(V_{ref} - V_\gamma\); anode at \(v_o\) → negative clamper, maximum pinned at \(V_{ref} + V_\gamma\). Shift = pinned level − the input's matching extreme; \(v_o = v_i + V_C\), peak-to-peak unchanged; needs \(RC \gg T\). Finite \(R_s\), \(R_L\): charge balance. Chains: process stage by stage. Doubler: clamper + peak rectifier, \(-2V_p\).
