The Exponential Model and Diode Strings

The exponential law was asked in 2024–25 (diode-string reference, 15 marks), 2021–22 (two identical diodes, 16⅔ marks) and 2015–16 (string design, 15 marks), and in the 2022 class test. Sadat sir starred his exponential-model example in class. There are only three moves: \(i = I_Se^{v/nV_T}\), the two-point form \(V_2 - V_1 = nV_T\ln(I_2/I_1)\), and solving a transcendental equation with SOLVE or iteration.

1. The law and its two-point form

\[ i_D = I_S\left(e^{v_D/nV_T} - 1\right) \approx I_Se^{v_D/nV_T}\ (v_D \gg V_T),\qquad v_D = nV_T\ln\frac{i_D}{I_S} \]
\[ V_2 - V_1 = nV_T\ln\frac{I_2}{I_1} = 2.3\,nV_T\log_{10}\frac{I_2}{I_1} \]
  • With \(V_T\) = 25 mV and n = 1: 57.5 mV per decade of current (Sedra rounds to 60 mV/decade); 17.3 mV per doubling.
  • "0.7 V at 1 mA" replaces \(I_S\): \(i = 1\text{ mA}\times e^{(v - 0.7)/nV_T}\). You never need \(I_S\) itself.
  • \(V_T\): 25 mV in Sedra and the slides, 25.9 mV (T = 300 K) in Sadat sir's notes. Both are accepted; state yours. Because \(V_T\) sits in an exponent, mixing them changes currents a lot (2024–25: 7.90 mA with 25 mV, 2.75 mA with 25.85 mV).

2. Solving a circuit with an exponential diode

Method · operating point (\(V_{DD}\), R, diode)

  1. KVL: \(I_D = (V_{DD} - V_D)/R\). Diode: \(I_D = I_Se^{V_D/nV_T}\). Combine into one equation in \(V_D\), e.g. \(V_{DD} - V_D - RI_Se^{V_D/nV_T} = 0\): transcendental, no closed form.
  2. Calculator (fx-991: SHIFT CALC), start at 0.7: gives \(V_D\) directly. Write the equation in the script, then the answer.
  3. Iteration (Sedra): guess \(V_D\) = 0.7 V → \(I_D = (V_{DD} - V_D)/R\) → \(V_D = nV_T\ln(I_D/I_S)\) (or \(V_1 + nV_T\ln(I_D/I_1)\)) → repeat; two or three rounds converge to 1 mV.
  4. Graphical: the load line from \((0, V_{DD}/R)\) to \((V_{DD}, 0)\) meets the diode curve at Q.

Worked example: Sadat L4, notes p.9 (starred in class)

3. Diode strings: voltage references

Method · string of N identical diodes fed through R

  1. Each diode takes \(V_o/N\).
  2. \(I = I_Se^{V_o/(NnV_T)}\) (or \(I_1e^{(V_o/N - V_1)/nV_T}\)).
  3. \(R = (V_{CC} - V_o)/I\) (no load).
  4. Load current \(I_L\) drawn: small-signal \(\Delta V_o = -I_L\,(Nr_d\parallel R)\), \(r_d = nV_T/I\) (Chapter 5), or exactly: \(V_o = NnV_T\ln\frac{(V_{CC} - V_o)/R - I_L}{I_S}\), solved by SOLVE.

4. Two identical diodes, different voltages

Identical diodes share \(I_S\), so their current ratio depends only on their voltage difference: \(I_2/I_1 = e^{(V_{D2} - V_{D1})/nV_T}\). In 2021–22 (Sedra P4.28) a current source feeds D2 directly to ground and D1 in series with R; the voltage across R is exactly \(V_{D2} - V_{D1}\).

Method · current-ratio problems

  1. Identify which diode has the extra voltage V: \(I_2/I_1 = e^{V/nV_T}\).
  2. KCL: \(I_1 + I_2 = I\) → \(I_1 = I/(1 + e^{V/nV_T})\).
  3. R is in series with the smaller-current diode: \(R = V/I_1\).

Traps (where marks go)

  • Forgetting to divide the string voltage by N.
  • Mixing 25 mV and 25.85/26 mV inside one answer; or using \(\log_{10}\) where ln is needed.
  • Putting R in series with the wrong diode in the two-diode problem.
  • Rounding \(V_D\) to 0.7 V and then using it in the exponent: keep 4 significant figures in \(V_D\).
  • For a 1 mA load on a 7.9 mA string the small-signal answer (−9.4 mV) is fine; for a load comparable to the bias current (CT 2022) it is not: solve exactly.

Exam questions

2024–25 · Sec B · Q7(b) · 15 marks

I is the current through each diode and R is unknown. The diodes are identical with \(I_S = 10^{-16}\) A and n = 1. (i) Find I and R such that \(V_o\) = 2.4 V. (ii) If 1 mA is drawn from the output by a load, what is the change in output voltage?

Show worked answer

\(V_T\) = 25 mV. (i) Each diode: \(V_D = 2.4/3 = 0.8\) V. \(I = 10^{-16}e^{0.8/0.025} = 10^{-16}\times7.896\times10^{13} = 7.896\) mA. \(R = (10 - 2.4)/7.896 = 0.9625\) kΩ = 962.5 Ω.

(ii) Small signal: \(r_d = V_T/I = 25/7.896 = 3.166\) Ω per diode, \(3r_d = 9.50\) Ω. Looking into the output the string is in parallel with R: \(R_o = 9.50\parallel962.5 = 9.405\) Ω. \(\Delta V_o = -1\text{ mA}\times9.405\text{ Ω} = -9.41\) mV (−9.50 mV if R is ignored, Sedra's style). Exact check with SOLVE: −10.0 mV, so the small-signal estimate is good.

2021–22 · Sec B · Q5(a) · 16⅔ marks  |  = Sedra 7e P4.28 (highlighted by the teacher)

Both diodes are identical. Find the value of R for which V = 50 mV.

Show worked answer

D2 is across node a to ground; D1 plus R is across the same nodes, so \(V_{D2} = V_{D1} + V\). Identical diodes: \(\dfrac{I_{D2}}{I_{D1}} = e^{V/V_T} = e^{0.05/0.025} = e^2 = 7.389\).

KCL: \(I_{D1} + I_{D2} = 10\) mA → \(I_{D1} = 10/(1 + 7.389) = 1.192\) mA, \(I_{D2} = 8.808\) mA.

\(R = V/I_{D1} = 50\text{ mV}/1.192\text{ mA} = 41.9\) Ω (book: ≈ 42 Ω).

2015–16 · Sec A · Q2(c) · 15 marks

Design the circuit (find R) to provide \(v_o\) = 3 V. The diodes have 0.7 V drop at 1 mA, \(V_T\) = 25 mV, n = 1.

Show worked answer

Four diodes: \(V_D = 3/4 = 0.75\) V each. \(I_D = 1\text{ mA}\times e^{(0.75 - 0.7)/0.025} = e^2 = 7.389\) mA. \(R = (10 - 3)/7.389 = 0.947\) kΩ = 947 Ω.

Class tests and assignment

CT-1(C) 2022 (earlier batch)

+5 V feeds \(V_o\) through 180 Ω; two diodes in series from \(V_o\) to ground, each 0.7 V at 10 mA. Find \(V_o\) and I, then \(V_o\) with a 150 Ω load.

Show worked answer

\(V_T\) = 25 mV. Equation: \(\dfrac{5 - V_o}{0.18} = 10e^{(V_o/2 - 0.7)/0.025}\) (mA). SOLVE: \(V_o\) = 1.434 V, I = (5 − 1.434)/0.18 = 19.8 mA (each diode 0.717 V).

With 150 Ω: \(\dfrac{5 - V_o}{0.18} - \dfrac{V_o}{0.15} = 10e^{(V_o/2 - 0.7)/0.025}\) → \(V_o\) = 1.403 V (ΔV = −31 mV). The small-signal estimate, −23.8 mV, is poor here because the load takes about half the current: solve exactly. (Line regulation and \(R_{out}\) of this circuit: Chapter 5.)

Assignment · Q4 (posted solution uses \(V_T\) = 26 mV)

+5 V — 200 Ω — \(v_o\); two diodes in series to ground (0.7 V at 10 mA). (i) \(v_o\) at no load; (ii) \(v_o\) with \(R_L\) = 150 Ω; (iii) the supply needed for \(v_o\) = 1.293 V with that load.

Show worked answer

(i) \(5 = 0.2\times10e^{(V_D - 0.7)/0.026} + 2V_D\) → \(V_D\) = 0.7151 V, \(v_o\) = 1.430 V. (ii) \(10e^{(V_D - 0.7)/0.026} = 25 - (70/3)V_D\) → \(v_o\) = 1.393 V. (iii) \(V_D\) = 0.6465 V: \(I_D = 10e^{-0.0535/0.026} = 1.277\) mA, \(I_L = 1.293/0.15 = 8.62\) mA, \(V_S = 1.293 + 0.2\times9.897 = 3.27\) V.

Highlighted textbook problems

Sedra 7e Exercises 4.6 and 4.7 · highlighted by the teacher

(4.6) Find the change in diode voltage when the current changes from 0.1 mA to 10 mA. (4.7) A diode has 0.7 V at 1 mA; find its voltage at 0.1 mA and 10 mA.

Show worked answer

(4.6) Two decades: \(\Delta V = 2\times2.3\times25 = 115\) mV (book: 120 mV, with 60 mV/decade).

(4.7) \(V = 0.7 + 0.025\ln(0.1) = 0.642\) V ≈ 0.64 V; \(V = 0.7 + 0.025\ln10 = 0.758\) V ≈ 0.76 V.

Key terms

Quick check

Summary: \(i = I_Se^{v/nV_T}\); \(\Delta V = nV_T\ln(I_2/I_1)\), about 60 mV per decade. Strings: \(V_D = V_o/N\), \(I\) from the law, \(R = (V_{CC} - V_o)/I\). Two identical diodes: ratio \(e^{V/nV_T}\), split by KCL. Anything with R and the law together is transcendental: SOLVE or iterate, and keep \(V_D\) to 4 figures.
