Exam Format and Strategy

The fourteen past term finals tell you a lot about the next one: the format, what is always asked, what has dropped out, and which textbook examples keep coming back with new numbers. This page is the summary; each year's paper is under Past papers.

Each compulsory question pairs an "explain / derive" part (tagged to a course outcome) with a design or analysis numerical. Examples: 2022–23 Q1 = clipper + CB derivation + NMOS I–V; 2023–24 Q1 = diode small-signal phase shifter + bridge rectifier with C; 2024–25 Q1 = Colpitts + op-amp ODE solver; 2024–25 Q5 = bootstrapped follower + half-wave peak rectifier.

2. The two sections swap

Neither section owns a topic. In most years one section is devices (diodes, BJT, MOSFET; DC and small-signal) and the other is linear ICs (op-amps, oscillators, 555). Devices were in Section A in 2014–15, 2015–16, 2017–18, 2018–19, 2019–20, 2022–23 and 2023–24, and in Section B in 2016–17, 2021–22 and 2024–25. You cannot drop half the syllabus.

3. What one current paper contains

Across 2022–23, 2023–24 and 2024–25, a paper has roughly:

  • Diodes (2–3 parts): a rectifier numerical (every year), a Zener question (every year), plus diode thermal (2023–24, 2024–25) or a clipper/clamper (2022–23).
  • BJT (2–3 parts): DC node voltages (every year), one amplifier derivation with the T model (every year: CB, CE with ro, CE with Early effect), saturation or Early-effect numericals.
  • MOSFET (2–3 parts): a common-source amplifier numerical (every paper since 2019–20), DC sizing / differential pair / follower, a short theory part (regions, gm and the small-signal condition, RS bias stability).
  • Oscillators: one derivation, Colpitts and Wien alternating (Colpitts 2018–19, 2020–21, 2022–23, 2024–25; Wien 2023–24).
  • 555 timer: one or two designs, every year since 2018–19 (astable for a given f and duty; monostable in 2021–22 and 2023–24).
  • Op-amps (1–2 parts): an ODE solver or expression synthesis, plus one of T-network, Howland V→I, Schmitt trigger, subtractor.

5. Repeats: textbook examples that keep coming back

Most questions are Sedra & Smith examples or exercises with changed numbers, and some repeat almost exactly:

  • Diode thermal: 15 A, 0.7 V falling to 0.58 V (2023–24) and to 0.6 V (2024–25); also the batch-23 CT-1.
  • Zener 6.8 V / 5 mA / 20 Ω regulator (2020–21, 2024–25); 9.1 V / 28 mA / 5 Ω model (2023–24, CT).
  • 555 astable, 10 ms period and 75 % duty (2021–22 and 2022–23).
  • 120 V rms, 10:1 transformer rectifiers (several years).
  • Diode-connected NMOS sizing (2012–13, 2013–14, 2014–15, 2019–20, 2023–24 twice).
  • Bootstrapped follower; pnp with measured VE = 1.7 V; three-diode string reference.

So the Sedra end-of-section examples, and the problems your teacher highlighted in the book (diodes, MOSFETs, BJTs, amplifiers: Ch 4–7), are the best extra practice. They are included in the chapters.

6. Choosing and timing questions

Method · in the exam hall

  1. First 5 minutes: read all eight questions. Mark each optional one by how many parts you can name the type of. Pick the two per section with the most recognised parts.
  2. Start with your strongest compulsory question, not with Q1 by default. Confidence early saves time later.
  3. 30 minutes per question (35 marks). Write the time you must move on in the margin. A half-finished part with a correct method collects most of its marks; a blank part collects none.
  4. Derivation parts: draw the small-signal circuit first, then write Rin, Ro, Avo, Av, Gv in that order. Examiners mark each expression.
  5. Last 10 minutes: fill in final answers with units, box them, and add any missing verification lines.

7. How to write an answer that gets full marks

  • Redraw the circuit with node names and current directions; for amplifiers draw the DC circuit and the small-signal circuit separately.
  • State every assumption: "Assume D₁ on, D₂ off", "Assume Q₁ active (VCE ≥ 0.3 V)", "Assume saturation", "VT = 25 mV".
  • Equation in symbols, then numbers, then result with unit. Three significant figures (Sadat sir keeps 3 decimal places).
  • Verification line after every assumed state; reason for rejecting a quadratic root.
  • When a figure leaves something out (a missing VCC or β), state the value you assume and continue.

Summary: 210 marks, 3 h, Q1 and Q5 compulsory, 30 minutes per 35-mark question. Every paper has a rectifier, a Zener, BJT DC, a BJT amplifier derivation, a CS amplifier, a 555 design, an oscillator derivation and an op-amp ODE or synthesis. Sections swap, so prepare everything; filters, log amps, digital and converters have dropped out. Write assumptions, verification lines and units on every part.
