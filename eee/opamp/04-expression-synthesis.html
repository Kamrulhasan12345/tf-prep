Building a Given Expression

"Design an op-amp circuit that gives \(v_{out} = 5\sin40t - 4\cos100t + 4\)" was the compulsory 2023–24 Q5(b) (18 marks); the same type came in 2021–22, 2020–21, 2012–13 and 2011–12 (94 marks in all). It is pure bookkeeping: one inverting summer at the end, one small stage per term, and careful signs. This chapter gives a procedure that always works, plus the one trap that cost the circulating solutions their marks: the constant an uncharged integrator adds.

2. The procedure

Method · synthesis with a final inverting summer

  1. Rewrite every term in terms of the available signals. \(\cos\omega t = \frac{1}{\omega}\frac{d}{dt}\sin\omega t\); \(\sin^2\theta = \tfrac12 - \tfrac12\cos2\theta\); a constant comes from a source or a rail.
  2. Choose the final stage: an inverting summer, \(v_{out} = -\sum (R_f/R_i)\,x_i\). So each summer input must be minus the wanted term's shape: for a term \(+a\,g(t)\) feed \(x = -g\); for \(-a\,g(t)\) feed \(x = +g\). Branch resistor \(R_i = R_f/|a|\).
  3. Build each \(x_i\) from the signals with the fewest stages: direct wire, inverter, differentiator (output \(-RC\,\dot v\)), integrator (output \(-\frac{1}{RC}\int v\)). Count every inversion.
  4. Pick values: \(R_f\) = 100 kΩ (or 1 MΩ), C = 1 µF, so R lands in 1 kΩ–1 MΩ. Differentiator \(RC = 1/\omega\) gives a unit-amplitude cosine.
  5. Verify: substitute back stage by stage; check the peak \(|a_1| + |a_2| + |K| \lt L\) (about 13 V with a 15 V supply).
  6. Integrators: state the initial condition. Prefer a differentiator for \(\cos\) (no constant appears).

3. The integrator constant (the trap)

An integrator that starts with an uncharged capacitor computes a definite integral from 0:

\[ -100\int_0^t \sin(100\tau)\,d\tau = \cos(100t) - 1 \quad\text{not}\quad \cos(100t) \]

So building \(\cos\) with an integrator adds a DC offset. In 2023–24 this turns the intended "+4" into "+8"; in the 2021–22 \(\sin^2\) question it adds +0.5 V. Two clean fixes: use a differentiator (\(-RC\frac{d}{dt}\sin\omega t = -RC\omega\cos\omega t\), no constant), or say explicitly that the integrator capacitor is pre-charged to the right initial value. Practical note: a real differentiator needs a small series resistor (noise) and a real integrator a large resistor across C (drift); mention it in one line.

4. Worked example: the 2023–24 compulsory question

5. Terms with derivatives and integrals of different inputs

Khairul s.19–20 realise \(V_O = 10V_1 - 5\dot V_2 + 2\int V_3\,dt\): differentiator (1 µF, 1 MΩ, RC = 1 s) + inverter → \(+\dot V_2\); integrator (1 MΩ, 1 µF) → \(-\int V_3\); inverter → \(-V_1\); summer \(R_f\) = 1 MΩ with 100 kΩ (from \(-V_1\), gain 10), 200 kΩ (from \(+\dot V_2\), gain 5), 500 kΩ (from \(-\int V_3\), gain 2). Six op-amps. The inverter after the differentiator is needed because the term is \(-5\dot V_2\): the summer must receive \(+\dot V_2\), and a differentiator only gives \(-\dot V_2\). For each term ask: "what sign must enter the summer, and what sign does my stage give?"

Traps

  • Uncharged integrator for a cosine: adds a constant (the 2023–24 and 2021–22 senior answers are off by +4 V and +0.5 V).
  • Sign bookkeeping: the final summer inverts; every differentiator and integrator inverts too.
  • The summer's \(R_f/R_i\) multiplies the whole branch, including any gain already built into a pre-stage.
  • Differentiator for a unit cosine needs \(RC = 1/\omega\) with ω in rad/s (\(\sin40t\): ω = 40, not \(2\pi\cdot40\)).
  • Check the output peak against the supply: a design whose sum exceeds about 13 V clips.
  • "Minimum number of op-amps" questions: every inversion you can absorb into the summer saves one stage.

Exam questions

2023–24 · Sec B · Q5(b) · 18 marks · compulsory

You have three voltage sources: \(V_1 = \sin(40t)\), \(V_2 = \sin(100t)\), \(V_3\) = 1 V. Design a circuit using op-amps to generate \(V_{out} = 5\sin(40t) - 4\cos(100t) + 4\). Draw the overall connection diagram with element values. The op-amps are biased from a 15 V source.

Show worked answer

Plan: final inverting summer, \(R_f\) = 100 kΩ. Its inputs must be \(-\sin40t\) (×5), \(+\cos100t\) (×4) and \(-1\) V (×4).

  1. \(-\sin40t\): inverter on \(V_1\) (10 kΩ/10 kΩ). Summer resistor \(100/5 = 20\) kΩ.
  2. \(+\cos100t\): inverter on \(V_2\) gives \(-\sin100t\); differentiator with C = 1 µF, R = 10 kΩ (RC = 0.01 s = 1/100): \(-0.01\frac{d}{dt}(-\sin100t) = +\cos100t\). Summer resistor \(100/4 = 25\) kΩ.
  3. \(-1\) V: inverter on \(V_3\). Summer resistor 25 kΩ. (Or, saving an op-amp, the −15 V rail through \(100\times15/4 = 375\) kΩ.)
  4. Summer: \(V_{out} = -100\left(\frac{-\sin40t}{20} + \frac{\cos100t}{25} + \frac{-1}{25}\right) = 5\sin40t - 4\cos100t + 4\) ✓.

Peak check: \(|V_{out}| \le 5 + 4 + 4 = 13\) V \(< 15\) V ✓.

Note on the circulating solutions: building \(\cos100t\) with an integrator (\(R_iC_i\) = 0.01 s) from an uncharged capacitor gives \(\cos100t - 1\), so their circuit outputs \(5\sin40t - 4\cos100t + 8\). Use the differentiator, or state that the integrator capacitor is pre-charged to give an initial output of +1 V.

2021–22 · Sec A · Q1(a) · 20 marks

Sources \(v_1 = \sin(100t)\) V and \(v_2\) = 1 V are available. Using op-amps, produce \(v_o = \sin^2(50t)\) and show that your circuit works.

Show worked answer

\(\sin^2(50t) = \tfrac12 - \tfrac12\cos(100t)\).

  1. Differentiator on \(v_1\) with RC = 0.01 s (C = 1 µF, R = 10 kΩ): \(v_A = -0.01\times100\cos100t = -\cos100t\).
  2. Non-inverting averager: \(v_A\) and \(v_2\) each through 10 kΩ to the + input of a follower: \(v_o = \frac{v_A + v_2}{2} = \tfrac12 - \tfrac12\cos100t = \sin^2(50t)\) ✓.

Two op-amps. Check at t = 0: \(\sin^20 = 0\) and \(\tfrac12 - \tfrac12 = 0\) ✓. (An integrator route gives \(1 - \tfrac12\cos100t\) with an uncharged capacitor, 0.5 V too high; that is the error in the circulating answer.)

2020–21 · Sec B · Q7(a) · 20 marks

Design an op-amp circuit whose output is proportional to \(\int(v_1 + v_2)\,dt\), and express \(v_o\) in terms of the components.

Show worked answer

Summing integrator: \(R_1\) from \(v_1\) and \(R_2\) from \(v_2\) to the inverting input, C from the inverting input to the output, + grounded. KCL at the virtual ground: \(\frac{v_1}{R_1} + \frac{v_2}{R_2} = -C\frac{dv_o}{dt}\), so

\(v_o = -\dfrac{1}{C}\displaystyle\int\left(\frac{v_1}{R_1} + \frac{v_2}{R_2}\right)dt + v_o(0)\). With \(R_1 = R_2 = R\): \(v_o = -\dfrac{1}{RC}\displaystyle\int(v_1 + v_2)\,dt\). Add an inverter if a positive constant of proportionality is required.

2012–13 · Sec B · Q8(b) · 16 marks

Design an op-amp circuit that performs \(v_{out} = 5v_1 - 2\,\dfrac{dv_2}{dt} + 6\displaystyle\int v_3\,dt\).

Show worked answer

Final summer with \(R_f\) = 100 kΩ; its inputs must be \(-v_1\) (×5), \(+\dot v_2\) (×2), \(-\int v_3\) (×6).

  1. \(-v_1\): inverter. Summer resistor 20 kΩ.
  2. \(+\dot v_2\): differentiator (C = 10 µF, R = 100 kΩ, RC = 1 s) gives \(-\dot v_2\); inverter → \(+\dot v_2\). Summer resistor 50 kΩ.
  3. \(-\int v_3\): integrator (R = 100 kΩ, C = 10 µF, RC = 1 s). Summer resistor 100/6 = 16.7 kΩ.

\(v_{out} = -100\left(\frac{-v_1}{20} + \frac{\dot v_2}{50} + \frac{-\int v_3}{16.7}\right) = 5v_1 - 2\dot v_2 + 6\int v_3\) ✓. Five op-amps. (Equivalent: put the gains in the pre-stages and use a unity summer.)

2011–12 · Sec A · Q2(c) · 20⅔ marks

Propose an op-amp circuit producing \(V_{out} = -4V_1 + 2V_2 + 7\dfrac{dV_1}{dt} - 8\displaystyle\int V_2\,dt\), where \(V_1\) and \(V_2\) are two sources.

Show worked answer

Summer \(R_F\) = 100 kΩ; inputs needed: \(+V_1\) (×4), \(-V_2\) (×2), \(-\dot V_1\) (×7), \(+\int V_2\) (×8).

  1. \(+V_1\): direct, 25 kΩ.
  2. \(-V_2\): inverter on \(V_2\); 50 kΩ.
  3. \(-\dot V_1\): differentiator on \(V_1\), RC = 1 s (C = 10 µF, R = 100 kΩ); summer resistor 100/7 = 14.3 kΩ. (Or RC = 7 s with R = 700 kΩ and a 100 kΩ summer resistor.)
  4. \(+\int V_2\): integrator fed from \(-V_2\) (the inverter output): \(-\frac{1}{RC}\int(-V_2) = +\int V_2\) with RC = 1 s; summer resistor 12.5 kΩ.

Check: \(-100\left(\frac{V_1}{25} + \frac{-V_2}{50} + \frac{-\dot V_1}{14.3} + \frac{\int V_2}{12.5}\right) = -4V_1 + 2V_2 + 7\dot V_1 - 8\int V_2\) ✓. Four op-amps.

Practice

Key terms

Quick check

Summary: end with an inverting summer; each branch must carry minus the wanted term's shape, through \(R_i = R_f/|a_i|\). Build each branch with inverters, a differentiator (\(-RC\,\dot v\); cos from sin with RC = 1/ω) or an integrator (\(-\frac{1}{RC}\int v\)). Count inversions, check the peak against the rails, and never let an uncharged integrator add a constant you did not ask for.
