MOSFET Structure and Operation

Theory only, but it is asked almost every year: "why does the current saturate?" (18⅔ marks, 2019–20), enhancement versus depletion (26⅔ marks, 2018–19), BJT versus MOSFET (twice: 2013–14, 2012–13), and threshold voltage with the effect of oxide thickness (20⅔ marks, 2012–13). The same physics also gives the one numerical you can be asked here: oxide capacitance and \(k_n'\) from \(\mu_n\) and \(t_{ox}\) (Sedra Example 5.1). Read it once, then use the model answers at the bottom as the shape of a full-marks script.

1. Structure of the enhancement NMOS

Terminals
Gate (G), source (S), drain (D), body or substrate (B). When B is wired to S there are three terminals.
NMOS
p-type substrate, n⁺ source and drain, current carried by electrons. PMOS is the complement: n-type substrate, p⁺ source and drain, holes.
Gate stack
Metal (or poly-Si) gate over a thin SiO₂ film of thickness \(t_{ox}\): a parallel-plate capacitor with \(C_{ox} = \varepsilon_{ox}/t_{ox}\) per unit area, \(\varepsilon_{ox} = 3.9\varepsilon_0 = 3.45\times10^{-11}\) F/m.
Dimensions
Channel length \(L\) (source to drain), width \(W\). The aspect ratio \(W/L\) is the designer's knob.

2. Creating the channel

  1. \(v_{GS} = 0\): source and drain are two back-to-back p-n junctions. No current for any \(v_{DS}\) (resistance about 10¹² Ω).
  2. Small positive \(v_{GS}\): the field in the oxide repels holes from the surface and leaves a depletion region of fixed negative ions.
  3. \(v_{GS} \ge V_t\): electrons are attracted to the surface and form a thin n-type layer, the inversion layer, joining source to drain. \(V_t\) is the threshold voltage at which the conducting channel forms.
  4. The channel charge per unit area is \(C_{ox}(v_{GS} - V_t) = C_{ox}v_{OV}\), where \(v_{OV} = v_{GS} - V_t\) is the overdrive voltage. More gate voltage, more electrons, more conductance.
\[ C_{ox} = \frac{\varepsilon_{ox}}{t_{ox}},\quad k_n' = \mu_nC_{ox}\ \ (\text{process}),\quad k_n = k_n'\frac{W}{L}\ \ (\text{device}),\quad |Q_{ch}| = C_{ox}(WL)\,v_{OV} \]

Units: \(C_{ox}\) in F/m² (1 F/m² = 10³ fF/µm²), \(\mu_n\) in cm²/V·s (×10⁻⁴ for m²/V·s), \(k_n'\) in A/V² (quoted in µA/V²).

3. Operation as \(v_{DS}\) rises (the derivation the exam asks for)

Take \(v_{GS} > V_t\) fixed and apply \(v_{DS}\). The channel voltage \(v(x)\) rises from 0 at the source (\(x = 0\)) to \(v_{DS}\) at the drain (\(x = L\)); the gate-to-channel voltage at \(x\) is \(v_{GS} - v(x)\), so the local charge is \(C_{ox}W[v_{OV} - v(x)]\) per unit length. With \(i_G = 0\), the same current flows at every \(x\):

\[ i_D = \mu_nC_{ox}W\,[v_{OV} - v(x)]\frac{dv}{dx} \;\Rightarrow\; i_D\!\int_0^L\!dx = \mu_nC_{ox}W\!\int_0^{v_{DS}}\![v_{OV} - v]\,dv \;\Rightarrow\; i_D = k_n'\frac{W}{L}\Big[v_{OV}v_{DS} - \tfrac12v_{DS}^2\Big] \]
StageChannel\(i_D\)
(a) very small \(v_{DS}\)uniform depth, a resistor\(i_D \approx k_nv_{OV}v_{DS}\), \(r_{DS} = 1/(k_nv_{OV})\)
(b) larger \(v_{DS}\) (\(< v_{OV}\))tapered: deeper at the source, shallower at the drain, because the gate-to-channel voltage falls to \(v_{GS} - v_{DS}\) at the draincurve bends over: \(k_n[v_{OV}v_{DS} - \tfrac12v_{DS}^2]\)
(c) \(v_{DS} = v_{OV}\)pinch-off: depth is zero at the drain end, \(v_{GD} = V_t\)maximum of the parabola: \(\tfrac12k_nv_{OV}^2\)
(d) \(v_{DS} > v_{OV}\)pinch-off point moves slightly towards the source; the extra \(v_{DS} - v_{OV}\) is dropped across a depletion region at the drain, where the field sweeps electrons acrossstays \(\tfrac12k_nv_{OV}^2\) (flat): saturation

Why the current saturates (this is the 18⅔-mark answer): once the channel is pinched off at the drain, the voltage across the channel proper stays fixed at \(v_{OV}\) (source to pinch-off point). The current is set by that fixed voltage over the channel, so it no longer depends on \(v_{DS}\). Extra drain voltage appears across the short depletion region and not across the channel. With channel-length modulation the pinch-off point creeps towards the source, \(L\) shrinks by \(\Delta L\), and \(i_D\) rises slightly: \(i_D = \tfrac12k_nv_{OV}^2(1 + \lambda v_{DS})\), \(V_A = 1/\lambda\), \(r_o = 1/(\lambda I_D')\) (chapter 2).

5. Body effect

If the body is not tied to the source, \(V_{SB} > 0\) (reverse bias) widens the depletion region, so more gate voltage is needed to form the channel: \(V_t\) rises (Sadat sir: "body more negative, channel forms with more difficulty"). It is modelled as a threshold shift:

\[ V_t = V_{t0} + \gamma\left[\sqrt{2\phi_f + V_{SB}} - \sqrt{2\phi_f}\right],\quad \gamma = \frac{\sqrt{2qN_A\varepsilon_s}}{C_{ox}}\ \ [\mathrm{V}^{1/2}],\quad 2\phi_f \approx 0.6\ \mathrm{V} \]

\(V_{t0}\) is the threshold at \(V_{SB} = 0\). Most exam circuits have B tied to S (or ignore the body, "body effect ignored"), so this appears as a theory point and a small numerical.

6. Oxide capacitance and \(k_n'\) (Sedra Example 5.1)

Method · process numbers to a bias point

  1. \(C_{ox} = 3.45\times10^{-11}/t_{ox}\) F/m². (Convert: \(t_{ox}\) in nm ×10⁻⁹; result in F/m²; 1 F/m² = 10³ fF/µm².)
  2. \(k_n' = \mu_nC_{ox}\) with \(\mu_n\) in m²/V·s (cm²/V·s × 10⁻⁴).
  3. \(k_n = k_n'(W/L)\). In saturation \(V_{OV} = \sqrt{2I_D/k_n}\), \(V_{GS} = V_t + V_{OV}\), \(V_{DS,min} = V_{OV}\).
  4. As a resistor at small \(v_{DS}\): \(r_{DS} = 1/(k_nV_{OV})\), so \(V_{OV} = 1/(k_nr_{DS})\).

Traps (where marks go)

  • Saying the channel "disappears" at saturation. It is pinched off at the drain end; the channel still exists and still carries the current.
  • Boundary written as \(v_{DS} = V_t\). It is \(v_{DS} = v_{OV} = v_{GS} - V_t\), equivalently \(v_{GD} = V_t\).
  • Unit slips in Example 5.1: cm²/V·s to m²/V·s is ×10⁻⁴, and 1 F/m² is 10³ fF/µm² (not 10⁶).
  • Thicker oxide raises \(V_t\) (smaller \(C_{ox}\)); high-k at the same thickness lowers it. Students reverse these.
  • Depletion NMOS: \(V_t\) is negative, and "no channel at \(v_{GS} = 0\)" is the enhancement statement.

Exam questions

2019–20 (held Jan 2021) · Sec A · Q4(a) · 18⅔ marks

Showing different operating regions, describe the output characteristics of a MOSFET. Why does current saturation occur in a MOSFET as \(V_{DS}\) is increased?

Show worked answer

Output characteristics (sketch \(i_D\) against \(v_{DS}\), one curve per \(v_{GS}\)): for \(v_{GS} \le V_t\) the curve is the axis (cut-off, \(i_D = 0\)). For \(v_{GS} = V_t + v_{OV}\) the curve rises from the origin.

  • Triode (\(v_{DS} < v_{OV}\)): \(i_D = k_n'(W/L)[v_{OV}v_{DS} - \tfrac12v_{DS}^2]\). Near the origin it is a straight line of slope \(k_nv_{OV}\), a voltage-controlled resistor \(r_{DS} = 1/(k_nv_{OV})\); it then bends because the channel resistance grows with \(v_{DS}\).
  • Saturation (\(v_{DS} \ge v_{OV}\)): \(i_D = \tfrac12k_n'(W/L)v_{OV}^2\), flat, with a slight upward slope from channel-length modulation, \(1/r_o\).
  • The boundary is the parabola \(i_D = \tfrac12k_nv_{DS}^2\) (it joins the points \(v_{DS} = v_{OV}\)).

Why it saturates. The channel voltage rises from 0 at the source to \(v_{DS}\) at the drain, so the gate-to-channel voltage falls from \(v_{GS}\) to \(v_{GS} - v_{DS}\) and the channel tapers towards the drain. At \(v_{DS} = v_{OV}\) it reaches \(v_{GD} = V_t\): the channel depth is zero at the drain, pinch-off. For larger \(v_{DS}\) the pinch-off point moves slightly towards the source and the excess \(v_{DS} - v_{OV}\) falls across the depletion region next to the drain. The voltage across the conducting channel stays \(v_{OV}\), the charge profile is unchanged, so \(i_D\) stays \(\tfrac12k_nv_{OV}^2\). Because the pinch-off point moves, \(L\) shrinks by \(\Delta L\) and \(i_D\) rises a little: \(i_D = \tfrac12k_nv_{OV}^2(1 + \lambda v_{DS})\).

2018–19 · Sec A · Q4(a) · 26⅔ marks

What is the difference between enhancement type and depletion type MOSFETs? Draw \(I_D\) versus \(V_{GS}\) characteristics and \(I_D\) versus \(V_{DS}\) characteristics for different values of \(V_{GS}\) for enhancement type and depletion type MOSFETs.

Show worked answer

Difference. An enhancement MOSFET has no channel at \(v_{GS} = 0\); \(V_t > 0\) (NMOS) and it conducts only when \(v_{GS} > V_t\), the gate "enhances" a channel into existence. A depletion MOSFET has a channel built in by implantation between source and drain; \(V_t < 0\) (NMOS). It carries \(I_{DSS}\) at \(v_{GS} = 0\); a negative \(v_{GS}\) depletes (shrinks) the channel and a positive \(v_{GS}\) enhances it.

Same square law for both: \(i_D = \tfrac12k_n(v_{GS} - V_t)^2\) in saturation (\(v_{DS} \ge v_{GS} - V_t\)); for the depletion device \(I_{DSS} = \tfrac12k_nV_t^2\).

\(I_D\)–\(V_{GS}\) (transfer, saturation): enhancement: parabola starting at \(v_{GS} = V_t\) on the axis (e.g. +1 V), zero below. Depletion: the same parabola shifted left, crossing \(v_{GS} = 0\) at \(I_{DSS}\) and reaching zero at \(v_{GS} = V_t\) (e.g. −2 V).

\(I_D\)–\(V_{DS}\) (output): both give a family of curves, each rising in triode and flat in saturation (boundary parabola \(i_D = \tfrac12k_nv_{DS}^2\)). Enhancement: curves exist only for \(v_{GS} > V_t\) (> 0). Depletion: curves exist for \(v_{GS} \le 0\) too, including \(v_{GS} = 0\) (the \(I_{DSS}\) curve) and negative values below it, as well as positive ones above it. Label each curve with its \(v_{GS}\).

2013–14 · Sec A · Q4(c) · 10⅔ marks  |  also 2012–13 Sec B Q5(a), 12 marks ("mutual advantages and disadvantages")

Compare MOSFET and BJT in terms of their operating principles. Mention the advantages and disadvantages of each.

Show worked answer

Operating principle. BJT: bipolar; a small base current (forward-biased B–E junction) controls a much larger collector current, \(i_C = \beta i_B = I_Se^{v_{BE}/V_T}\) (current-controlled, exponential). MOSFET: unipolar (one carrier type); the gate voltage sets up an electric field across the oxide that induces a channel, and the drain current is controlled by that voltage with no gate current, \(i_D = \tfrac12k_n(v_{GS} - V_t)^2\) (voltage-controlled, square law).

MOSFET advantages: \(i_G = 0\) (extremely high input resistance), very small area and easy scaling, low static power, works as an almost ideal switch, basis of CMOS and VLSI. Disadvantages: lower transconductance \(g_m = 2I_D/V_{OV}\) than the BJT's \(I_C/V_T\) at the same current, so lower gain; gate oxide is destroyed by electrostatic discharge.

BJT advantages: high \(g_m\) and gain per unit current, good high-frequency analog performance, low noise. Disadvantages: base current loads the source (low input resistance), larger area, more static power, thermal runaway.

2012–13 · Sec B · Q5(b) · 20⅔ marks

Explain the concept of threshold voltage (\(V_t\)) of a MOSFET. What happens to \(V_t\) and the oxide capacitance (\(C_{ox}\)) if (i) the dielectric thickness is increased or decreased, (ii) the gate insulator is replaced with a high-k dielectric of the same thickness?

Show worked answer

Threshold voltage. \(V_t\) is the gate-to-source voltage at which the induced n-type layer (inversion layer) just forms, i.e. the surface electron density equals the substrate hole density. Below it the device is off. Three terms make it up: the voltage that cancels the work-function and charge difference (flat-band voltage \(V_{FB}\)), the surface potential to invert the surface \(2\phi_F\), and the voltage across the oxide to support the depletion charge:

\[ V_t = V_{FB} + 2\phi_F + \frac{Q_{dep}}{C_{ox}},\qquad C_{ox} = \frac{\varepsilon_{ox}}{t_{ox}} = \frac{k\,\varepsilon_0}{t_{ox}} \]

(i) Thicker oxide: \(C_{ox}\) falls, \(Q_{dep}/C_{ox}\) grows, so \(V_t\) rises (and \(k_n'\) falls, so the current drive drops). Thinner oxide: \(C_{ox}\) rises, \(V_t\) falls, drive rises (limited by oxide breakdown and tunnelling leakage).

(ii) High-k at the same thickness: \(C_{ox}\) rises in proportion to \(k\) (relative permittivity), so \(V_t\) falls and \(k_n'\) rises. Alternatively the oxide can be made physically thicker for the same \(C_{ox}\), which cuts gate tunnelling leakage.

Highlighted textbook example

Sedra Example 5.1 · Khairul s.14–15 (worked in lecture)

A process has \(L_{min}\) = 0.4 µm, \(t_{ox}\) = 8 nm, \(\mu_n\) = 450 cm²/V·s, \(V_t\) = 0.7 V. (a) Find \(C_{ox}\), \(k_n'\). (b) For \(W/L\) = 8 µm/0.8 µm find \(V_{OV}\), \(V_{GS}\) and \(V_{DS,min}\) for saturation at \(I_D\) = 100 µA. (c) For this device find \(V_{OV}\) and \(V_{GS}\) that make it a 1000 Ω resistor at very small \(v_{DS}\).

Show worked answer

(a) \(C_{ox} = 3.45\times10^{-11}/(8\times10^{-9}) = 4.31\times10^{-3}\) F/m² = 4.31 fF/µm². \(k_n' = 450\times10^{-4}\times4.31\times10^{-3} = 1.94\times10^{-4}\) = 194 µA/V².

(b) \(k_n = 0.194\times10 = 1.94\) mA/V². \(0.1 = \tfrac12(1.94)V_{OV}^2\) so \(V_{OV}\) = 0.321 V, \(V_{GS}\) = 1.021 V, \(V_{DS,min} = V_{OV}\) = 0.321 V.

(c) \(r_{DS} = 1/(k_nV_{OV})\): \(V_{OV} = 1/(1.94\times1) = 0.515\) V (kΩ × mA/V² = 1), \(V_{GS}\) = 1.215 V.

Key terms

Quick check

Summary: \(V_t\) forms the inversion channel; \(v_{OV} = v_{GS} - V_t\) sets its charge, \(k_n = \mu_nC_{ox}W/L\) its conductance. Triode: continuous channel (\(v_{DS} < v_{OV}\)), \(i_D = k_n[v_{OV}v_{DS} - \tfrac12v_{DS}^2]\), resistor at small \(v_{DS}\). Pinch-off at \(v_{DS} = v_{OV}\) (\(v_{GD} = V_t\)); beyond it the channel voltage is fixed, so \(i_D = \tfrac12k_nv_{OV}^2\) (saturation, with a slight rise from CLM). Enhancement: \(V_t > 0\), no channel at zero bias; depletion: \(V_t < 0\), \(I_{DSS} = \tfrac12k_nV_t^2\). Body effect: \(V_{SB} > 0\) raises \(V_t\). MOSFET: voltage-controlled, \(i_G = 0\), low \(g_m\); BJT: current-controlled, high \(g_m\).
