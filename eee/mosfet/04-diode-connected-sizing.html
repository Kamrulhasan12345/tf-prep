Diode-Connected Transistors and Sizing

"Find W (and R) for the indicated voltages" is the most repeated MOSFET DC question: 2012–13, 2013–14, 2014–15 and twice in 2023–24 (15 + 15 marks), plus two class tests. It is also the easiest: a diode-connected MOSFET is always saturated, so there is no region to guess, and each device is one line of algebra.

1. Why a diode-connected MOSFET is always saturated

Tie the gate to the drain. Then \(V_{DS} = V_{GS}\), and since \(V_{GS} - V_t < V_{GS}\):

\[ V_{DS} = V_{GS} > V_{GS} - V_t = V_{OV} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{saturation (whenever } V_{GS} > V_t) \]

Equivalently \(V_{GD} = 0 < V_t\): the channel is always pinched off at the drain. Sadat sir's box (L20, notes p.91): "MOSFETs with G and D shorted can either be in (1) saturation or (2) cut-off. No triode mode." Write this one line in every answer; it replaces the region check. The same holds for a PMOS with gate tied to drain (\(V_{SD} = V_{SG} > |V_{OV}|\)).

So the device is a two-terminal element with a square-law i–v curve, a "diode" with threshold \(V_t\) (CT-2 2022 asked exactly this):

\[ i = \tfrac12k_n'(W/L)(v - V_t)^2 \;\;(v \ge V_t), \qquad i = 0 \;\;(v < V_t) \]

2. Sizing for given voltages and current

Method · find W and R (5 steps)

  1. State: gate tied to drain ⇒ \(V_{GD} = 0 < V_t\) ⇒ saturation.
  2. For each device, \(V_{GS} = V_{\text{its drain}} - V_{\text{its source}}\). In a stack the source of the upper device is the drain of the lower one. PMOS: \(V_{SG} = V_S - V_D\).
  3. \(V_{OV} = V_{GS} - V_t\) (PMOS: \(V_{SG} - |V_{tp}|\)).
  4. \(\dfrac{W}{L} = \dfrac{2I}{k_n'V_{OV}^2}\), then \(W = (W/L)\times L\). Devices in series carry the same \(I\).
  5. Resistors by Ohm's law: \(R = \Delta V/I\) (µA × kΩ: 120 µA × 12.5 kΩ = 1.5 V).

3. Analysis: R and W known, find the voltage

The reverse question (and the first step of every current mirror in the next chapter): a resistor \(R\) from \(V_{DD}\) feeds a diode-connected NMOS. KVL \(V_{DD} = IR + V_t + V_{OV}\) with \(I = \tfrac12k_nV_{OV}^2\) gives the same quadratic as the source-resistor bias of chapter 3:

\[ \tfrac12k_nR\,V_{OV}^2 + V_{OV} - (V_{DD} - V_t) = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; V_{OV} = \frac{-1 + \sqrt{1 + 2k_nR(V_{DD} - V_t)}}{k_nR} \]

Reject the negative root (\(V_{GS} < V_t\): no current). For a PMOS with R to ground, replace \(V_{DD} - V_t\) by \(V_{DD} - |V_{tp}|\) and \(V_{GS}\) by \(V_{SG}\).

Parallel devices and unequal devices

Two identical diode-connected devices in parallel behave like one device with twice the \(k_n\) (twice the width). Two unequal devices in series carry the same current, so \(\sqrt{k_1}\,V_{OV1} = \sqrt{k_2}\,V_{OV2}\): the narrow device needs the larger overdrive. Both ideas appear in the assignment and in Sedra P5.59 below.

Traps (where marks go)

  • Using the node voltage instead of the device's own \(V_{GS}\): in a stack, Q1's \(V_{GS}\) is \(3.5 - 1.5 = 2\) V, not 3.5 V.
  • Forgetting the ½ (doubles W) or mixing µm with W/L (answer W in µm, not W/L).
  • Labels swapped between papers: 2014–15 numbers the bottom device Q1, 2023–24 numbers the top device Q1. Read the figure.
  • PMOS signs: use \(V_{SG}\) and \(|V_{tp}|\); \(V_{tp} = -0.7\) V means \(|V_{tp}| = 0.7\) V.
  • Bringing λ into a bias calculation: bias normally ignores it (2013–14 gives \(V_A\) = 50 V; the textbook answer is 25 kΩ without it).
  • Writing a region check "assume saturation … verify". Not wrong, but the one-line reason (\(V_{GD} = 0 < V_t\)) is better and faster.

Exam questions

2023–24 · Sec A · Q2(c) · 15 marks

The NMOS transistors in the circuit (Figure 2) have \(V_t\) = 1 V, \(\mu_nC_{ox}\) = 120 µA/V², λ = 0 and \(L_1 = L_2\) = 1 µm. Find the required gate widths of Q1 and Q2 and the value of R to obtain the indicated voltages (3.5 V at Q1's drain, 1.5 V at the Q1–Q2 junction). The current in the resistance is 120 µA.

Show worked answer

Both transistors have gate tied to drain: \(V_{GD} = 0 < V_t\), so both are saturated. Series connection: both carry 120 µA.

Q2 (bottom): \(V_{GS2} = 1.5 - 0 = 1.5\) V, \(V_{OV2} = 0.5\) V. \(120 = \tfrac12(120)(W_2/1)(0.5)^2 \Rightarrow W_2/L = 8\), \(W_2 = 8\) µm.

Q1 (top): \(V_{GS1} = 3.5 - 1.5 = 2\) V, \(V_{OV1} = 1\) V. \(120 = \tfrac12(120)(W_1/1)(1)^2 \Rightarrow\) \(W_1 = 2\) µm.

R \(= (5 - 3.5)/0.12 = 12.5\) kΩ.

2023–24 · Sec A · Q4(c) · 15 marks

The PMOS transistor (source at +5 V, gate tied to drain, R from drain to ground) has \(V_t\) = −0.7 V, \(\mu_pC_{ox}\) = 60 µA/V², L = 0.8 µm, λ = 0. Find W and R to establish a drain current of 115 µA and \(V_D\) = 3.5 V.

Show worked answer

Gate tied to drain: \(V_{SD} = V_{SG} > |V_{OV}|\), so saturated.

\(V_{SG} = 5 - 3.5 = 1.5\) V, \(|V_{OV}| = 1.5 - 0.7 = 0.8\) V.

\(115 = \tfrac12(60)(W/L)(0.8)^2 = 19.2\,(W/L) \Rightarrow W/L = 5.99\), \(W = 5.99\times0.8 = 4.79\) µm.

R \(= 3.5/0.115 = 30.43\) kΩ.

2014–15 · Sec A · Q4(b) · 16 marks

The NMOS transistors have \(V_t\) = 1 V, \(\mu_nC_{ox}\) = 120 µA/V², \(L_1 = L_2\) = 1 µm. R from +5 V to the drain of Q2 (at +3.5 V); Q2 diode-connected down to Q1's drain (+1.5 V); Q1 diode-connected to ground. The current is 120 µA. Find \(W_1\), \(W_2\) and R.

Show worked answer

Same circuit as 2023–24 Q2(c) with the labels swapped: here Q1 is the bottom device. Both diode-connected ⇒ saturated.

Q1: \(V_{GS} = 1.5\) V, \(V_{OV} = 0.5\) V ⇒ \(W_1 = \dfrac{2\times120\times1}{120\times0.25} = 8\) µm. Q2: \(V_{GS} = 2\) V, \(V_{OV} = 1\) V ⇒ \(W_2 = 2\) µm. R \(= 1.5/0.12 = 12.5\) kΩ.

2013–14 · Sec B · Q5(a) · 16⅔ marks  |  Sedra Example 5.x

Design the circuit (R from +3 V to the drain of a diode-connected NMOS, source grounded) to obtain \(I_D\) = 80 µA. \(V_t\) = 0.6 V, \(\mu_nC_{ox}\) = 200 µA/V², L = 0.8 µm, W = 4 µm, \(V_A\) = 50 V.

Show worked answer

Diode-connected ⇒ saturated. \(k_n = 0.2\times4/0.8 = 1\) mA/V².

Neglecting λ (normal for bias): \(0.08 = \tfrac12(1)V_{OV}^2 \Rightarrow V_{OV} = 0.4\) V, \(V_{GS} = V_D = 1.0\) V. R = (3 − 1)/0.08 = 25 kΩ.

With λ = 1/\(V_A\) = 0.02 V⁻¹ and \(V_{DS} = V_{GS}\): \(0.08 = \tfrac12V_{OV}^2(1 + 0.02V_{GS})\); iterating gives \(V_{GS}\) = 0.996 V and R = 25.05 kΩ. The difference is 0.2 %, which is why bias work drops λ.

2012–13 · Sec B · Q5(c) · 14 marks

Three diode-connected NMOS in series from +5 V to ground (Q3 on top, then Q2, then Q1), with the Q3–Q2 junction at 3.5 V and the Q2–Q1 junction at 1.5 V; I = 120 µA. \(V_t\) = 1 V, \(\mu_nC_{ox}\) = 120 µA/V², \(L_1 = L_2 = L_3\) = 1 µm. Find \(W_1\), \(W_2\), \(W_3\).

Show worked answer

All three are diode-connected ⇒ saturated; all carry 120 µA. \(W = \dfrac{2IL}{k_n'V_{OV}^2} = \dfrac{2}{V_{OV}^2}\) µm here.

Q1: \(V_{GS} = 1.5\), \(V_{OV} = 0.5\) ⇒ \(W_1\) = 8 µm. Q2: \(V_{GS} = 2\), \(V_{OV} = 1\) ⇒ \(W_2\) = 2 µm. Q3: \(V_{GS} = 5 - 3.5 = 1.5\), \(V_{OV} = 0.5\) ⇒ \(W_3\) = 8 µm. (Body effect ignored.)

Class tests

CT-2 2022 · 10 marks

Find the i–v relationship of the two-terminal device formed by an NMOS with its gate connected to its drain (+ terminal) and its source as the − terminal. Parameters \(V_t\), \(k_n'(W/L)\).

Show worked answer

\(v = v_{GS} = v_{DS}\). For \(v < V_t\) there is no channel: \(i = 0\). For \(v \ge V_t\): \(v_{DS} = v_{GS} > v_{GS} - V_t\), so the device is saturated and \(i = \tfrac12k_n'(W/L)(v - V_t)^2\). It never enters triode. Sketch: zero up to \(V_t\), then a parabola (Figure 1).

CT-4 2018 · 8 marks  |  the same CT also set Sedra Ex D5.9 (highlighted)

Diode-connected NMOS fed from 1.8 V through R. \(V_t\) = 0.5 V, \(\mu_nC_{ox}\) = 0.4 mA/V², W/L = 0.72 µm/0.18 µm. Find \(I_D\) and R for \(V_D\) = 0.8 V. (D5.9: the same with \(V_D\) = 0.7 V.)

Show worked answer

Saturated (gate tied to drain). W/L = 4, \(k_n\) = 1.6 mA/V².

\(V_D\) = 0.8 V: \(V_{OV}\) = 0.3 V, \(I_D = \tfrac12(1.6)(0.09) = 72\) µA, R = (1.8 − 0.8)/0.072 = 13.9 kΩ.

D5.9, \(V_D\) = 0.7 V: \(V_{OV}\) = 0.2 V, \(I_D\) = 32 µA, R = 1.1/0.032 = 34.4 kΩ.

Sedra 7e P5.50 · highlighted by the teacher

+2.5 V → R → 1.8 V node; Q2 diode-connected from 1.8 V down to 1.0 V; Q1 diode-connected from 1.0 V to ground; current 0.5 mA. \(V_t\) = 0.5 V, \(\mu_nC_{ox}\) = 250 µA/V², L = 0.25 µm. Find R, \(W_1\), \(W_2\).

Sedra 7e P5.51 · highlighted by the teacher

Three diode-connected NMOS stacked from 2.5 V to ground with junctions at 1.5 V and 0.8 V, carrying 90 µA. \(V_t\) = 0.5 V, \(\mu_nC_{ox}\) = 90 µA/V², L = 0.5 µm. Find \(W_1\) (bottom), \(W_2\), \(W_3\) (top).

Show worked answer

\(W = \dfrac{2IL}{k_n'V_{OV}^2} = \dfrac{2\times90\times0.5}{90\,V_{OV}^2} = \dfrac{1}{V_{OV}^2}\) µm.

Q1: \(V_{GS}\) = 0.8, \(V_{OV}\) = 0.3 ⇒ 11.1 µm. Q2: \(V_{GS}\) = 0.7, \(V_{OV}\) = 0.2 ⇒ 25 µm. Q3: \(V_{GS}\) = 1.0, \(V_{OV}\) = 0.5 ⇒ 4 µm.

Sedra 7e P5.49 · highlighted by the teacher

Diode-connected PMOS, source at 1.8 V, R from \(V_D\) to ground. \(V_t\) = −0.5 V, \(\mu_pC_{ox}\) = 100 µA/V², L = 0.18 µm. Find W and R for \(I_D\) = 180 µA and \(V_D\) = 1 V.

Show worked answer

\(V_{SG}\) = 0.8 V, \(|V_{OV}|\) = 0.3 V. W/L = \(2\times180/(100\times0.09) = 40\), W = 7.2 µm. R = 1/0.18 = 5.56 kΩ.

Sedra 7e P5.59(b), (c) · highlighted by the teacher

\(\mu_nC_{ox} = 3\mu_pC_{ox}\) = 270 µA/V², \(|V_t|\) = 0.5 V, W/L = 3 µm/1 µm for both devices. (b) A diode-connected PMOS (source at the supply) sits on top of a diode-connected NMOS (source grounded); find the middle node \(V_4\) and the current. (c) Repeat with \(W_p\) = 9 µm. (The supply is +3 V: the book answers below need it.)

Show worked answer

\(k_n = 0.27\times3 = 0.81\) mA/V², \(k_p = 0.09\times3 = 0.27\) mA/V². Same current: \(\sqrt{k_p}\,(3 - V_4 - 0.5) = \sqrt{k_n}\,(V_4 - 0.5)\).

(b) \(0.5196(2.5 - V_4) = 0.9(V_4 - 0.5) \Rightarrow\) \(V_4\) = 1.232 V, \(I = \tfrac12(0.81)(0.732)^2 = 0.217\) mA.

(c) \(W_p\) = 9 µm makes \(k_p = k_n\): symmetric, \(V_5\) = 1.5 V, \(I = \tfrac12(0.81)(1)^2 = 0.405\) mA. Widening the PMOS by \(\mu_n/\mu_p\) = 3 balances the pair, the idea behind CMOS inverter sizing.

Assignment Q6 (EEE 263 assignment set)

Three diode-connected NMOS, \(k_n\) = 1 mA/V², \(V_t\) = 2 V. M1: drain and gate at +5 V, source at \(v_o\). M2 and M3 in parallel from \(v_o\) to ground (each diode-connected). Find \(v_o\) and the current.

Show worked answer

All saturated. KCL at \(v_o\): \(\tfrac12(5 - v_o - 2)^2 = 2\times\tfrac12(v_o - 2)^2\) (M2 ∥ M3 = double width).

\((3 - v_o)^2 = 2(v_o - 2)^2 \Rightarrow v_o^2 - 2v_o - 1 = 0 \Rightarrow\) \(v_o = 1 + \sqrt2 = 2.414\) V (reject \(1 - \sqrt2\): below \(V_t\)). Current in M1: \(\tfrac12(0.586)^2 = 0.172\) mA (= \(3 - 2\sqrt2\)).

Practice

Key terms

Quick check

Summary: gate tied to drain ⇒ \(V_{GD} = 0 < V_t\) ⇒ always saturated (or off). Each device: \(V_{GS}\) from its own two terminals, \(V_{OV} = V_{GS} - V_t\), \(W = 2IL/(k'V_{OV}^2)\). Series devices share the current; resistors follow from Ohm's law. In reverse (R known), solve \(\tfrac12k_nRV_{OV}^2 + V_{OV} - (V_{DD} - V_t) = 0\) and keep the positive root.
