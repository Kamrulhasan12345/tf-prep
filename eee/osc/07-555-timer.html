The 555 Timer: Monostable and Astable

A 555 question has appeared in every paper since 2018–19: 2018–19, 2019–20 (29⅔ marks), 2020–21, 2021–22 (two parts), 2022–23, 2023–24 (two questions, 30 marks) and 2024–25. It is the highest-frequency oscillator type of the last four exams, and almost always a design: "f and duty cycle given, find \(R_A\), \(R_B\)". Two constants do everything: \(\ln 2 = 0.693\) (astable) and \(\ln 3 \approx 1.1\) (monostable). The one idea that separates full marks from half: a duty cycle below 50% needs a steering diode.

1. Inside the 555

Comparator 1
+ = Threshold (pin 6), − = \(\tfrac23V_{CC}\). Output high (resets the flip-flop) when the threshold voltage exceeds \(\tfrac23V_{CC}\).
Comparator 2
+ = \(\tfrac13V_{CC}\), − = Trigger (pin 2). Output high (sets the flip-flop) when the trigger falls below \(\tfrac13V_{CC}\).
SR flip-flop
S = 1 → Q = 1 (output high, discharge transistor off); R = 1 → Q = 0 (output low, \(\bar Q\) = 1 turns the discharge transistor \(Q_1\) on, shorting pin 7 to ground). R = S = 0 holds.
Pins
1 ground, 2 trigger, 3 output, 4 reset (tie to \(V_{CC}\)), 5 control voltage (0.01 µF to ground for noise), 6 threshold, 7 discharge, 8 \(V_{CC}\).

Every timing result comes from one capacitor law: a capacitor heading from \(V_0\) toward \(V_\infty\) through \(R\):

\[ v_C(t) = V_\infty - (V_\infty - V_0)\,e^{-t/RC} \;\Rightarrow\; t = RC\,\ln\frac{V_\infty - V_0}{V_\infty - V_{end}} \]

2. Monostable (one-shot): T = RC ln 3 ≈ 1.1RC (type S-07)

Operation. Stable state: Q = 0, the discharge transistor is on, so C is held at 0 V and the output is low. A negative trigger pulse below \(\tfrac13V_{CC}\) sets the flip-flop: output goes high, the transistor turns off, and C charges through R toward \(V_{CC}\). When \(v_C\) reaches \(\tfrac23V_{CC}\), comparator 1 resets the flip-flop: output low, C is discharged quickly. The pulse width is the charging time from 0 to \(\tfrac23V_{CC}\):

\[ v_C = V_{CC}\big(1 - e^{-t/RC}\big) = \tfrac23V_{CC} \;\Rightarrow\; T = RC\ln 3 = 1.0986\,RC \approx 1.1\,RC \]

Method · monostable design

  1. \(T = 1.1RC\) (or \(RC\ln3\); say which).
  2. Pick C (often given: 1 µF, 10 µF). \(R = T/(1.1C)\); keep R between 1 kΩ and 1 MΩ, otherwise change C.
  3. Draw the connection diagram (pins 4, 8 to \(V_{CC}\); 5 via 0.01 µF; 6 and 7 together at the R–C node; trigger into 2) and the waveforms. The trigger pulse must be shorter than T.

3. Astable: free-running square wave (type S-06)

Operation. Pins 2 and 6 both watch \(v_C\), so the capacitor swings between \(\tfrac13V_{CC}\) and \(\tfrac23V_{CC}\):

  • Output high (charging): transistor off; C charges toward \(V_{CC}\) through \(R_A + R_B\), from \(\tfrac13\) to \(\tfrac23V_{CC}\): \(t_H = (R_A + R_B)C\ln\dfrac{V_{CC} - V_{CC}/3}{V_{CC} - 2V_{CC}/3} = (R_A + R_B)C\ln 2\).
  • Output low (discharging): at \(\tfrac23V_{CC}\) the flip-flop resets; pin 7 is grounded, so C discharges toward 0 through \(R_B\) only, from \(\tfrac23\) to \(\tfrac13V_{CC}\): \(t_L = R_BC\ln 2\).
\[ \begin{gathered} t_H = 0.693(R_A + R_B)C,\quad t_L = 0.693R_BC,\quad T = 0.693(R_A + 2R_B)C \\ f = \frac{1.44}{(R_A + 2R_B)C},\quad D = \frac{R_A + R_B}{R_A + 2R_B} \end{gathered} \]

D is always above 50% for this circuit (charging goes through more resistance than discharging). It approaches 50% only when \(R_A \ll R_B\) (Khairul s.16).

Method · astable analysis

  1. \(t_H = 0.693(R_A + R_B)C\) (output high, charging), \(t_L = 0.693R_BC\).
  2. \(T = t_H + t_L\), \(f = 1/T\), \(D = t_H/T\).
  3. \(v_C\) limits: \(V_{CC}/3\) and \(2V_{CC}/3\) (independent of \(V_{CC}\) in timing: f does not depend on the supply).
  4. "How does f depend on C?": \(f = 1.44/((R_A + 2R_B)C) \propto 1/C\), a rectangular hyperbola.

4. Astable design for given f and D

Method · design (decide the circuit from D first)

  1. \(T = 1/f\), \(t_H = D\,T\), \(t_L = T - t_H\). Pick C (given, or so that the resistors are 1–100 kΩ).
  2. D > 50%: standard circuit. \(R_B = \dfrac{t_L}{0.693C}\), then \(R_A = \dfrac{t_H}{0.693C} - R_B\). (Shortcut: \(R_A/R_B = (2D - 1)/(1 - D)\); D = 75% gives \(R_A = 2R_B\), D = 60% gives \(R_B = 2R_A\).)
  3. D ≤ 50%: steering diode across \(R_B\) (anode at pin 7, cathode at the capacitor). Charging now goes through \(R_A\) and the diode only; discharging through \(R_B\) into pin 7 (diode reverse biased). With an ideal diode: \(t_H = 0.693R_AC\), \(t_L = 0.693R_BC\), so \(R_A = \dfrac{t_H}{0.693C}\), \(R_B = \dfrac{t_L}{0.693C}\), \(D = \dfrac{R_A}{R_A + R_B}\).
  4. Alternative for D < 50%: design a standard astable for \(1 - D\) and invert the output (e.g. a transistor or logic inverter).
  5. Check: recompute f and D. Draw the circuit with values and the output waveform (high for \(t_H\), low for \(t_L\)).

The standard formula forced onto D < 50% gives a negative \(R_A\): that is the signal you need the diode.

Traps (where marks go)

  • Using 1.1 (monostable) in an astable or 0.693 in a monostable.
  • Swapping \(t_H\) and \(t_L\): the output is high while charging, and charging goes through \(R_A + R_B\).
  • Using the standard circuit for D ≤ 50% (negative \(R_A\)). Say "steering diode across \(R_B\)" and draw it.
  • Forgetting the connection details in the drawing: pins 4 and 8 to \(V_{CC}\), 5 through 0.01 µF, 2 and 6 tied together.
  • Answering "frequency" with \(1/t_H\). \(f = 1/(t_H + t_L)\).

Exam questions

2024–25 · Sec A · Q4(a) · 15 marks

The internal circuit of a 555 timer is shown (Figure 1). Design a circuit using a 555 to generate a 100 kHz square wave with duty cycle 40%. Draw the final connection diagram with component values.

Show worked answer

\(T = 1/100\text{ kHz} = 10\) µs; \(t_H = 0.4T = 4\) µs, \(t_L = 6\) µs.

D < 50% is impossible with the standard astable (\(D = (R_A + R_B)/(R_A + 2R_B) > 0.5\)). Use a steering diode across \(R_B\) (anode at pin 7): charging through \(R_A\) only, discharging through \(R_B\).

Choose C = 1 nF: \(R_A = \dfrac{t_H}{0.693C} = \dfrac{4\times10^{-6}}{0.693\times10^{-9}} = 5.77\) kΩ; \(R_B = \dfrac{6\times10^{-6}}{0.693\times10^{-9}} = 8.66\) kΩ.

Check: \(D = 5.77/(5.77 + 8.66) = 0.40\), \(T = 0.693\times14.43\text{k}\times1\text{n} = 10\) µs.

Connection diagram (Figure 4 with the diode): \(V_{CC}\) to pins 8 and 4; \(R_A\) = 5.77 kΩ from \(V_{CC}\) to pin 7; \(R_B\) = 8.66 kΩ from pin 7 to pins 2–6; diode across \(R_B\), anode at pin 7; C = 1 nF from pins 2–6 to ground; 0.01 µF from pin 5 to ground; output at pin 3 (high 4 µs, low 6 µs).

2023–24 · Sec B · Q8(b) · 12 marks

A 555 timer is connected as in the figure (standard astable, internal diagram drawn). The capacitor is 1 nF. Find \(R_A\) and \(R_B\) for an oscillation frequency of 50 kHz and duty cycle 60%. What measures can be taken to achieve duty cycle below 50%?

Show worked answer

\(T = 1/50\text{ kHz} = 20\) µs; \(t_H = 0.6\times20 = 12\) µs, \(t_L = 8\) µs.

\(t_L = 0.693R_BC \Rightarrow R_B = \dfrac{8\times10^{-6}}{0.693\times10^{-9}} = 11.54\) kΩ.

\(t_H = 0.693(R_A + R_B)C \Rightarrow R_A + R_B = \dfrac{12\times10^{-6}}{0.693\times10^{-9}} = 17.31\) kΩ, so \(R_A = 5.77\) kΩ. (Check: \(R_B = 2R_A\) for 60%.)

D < 50%: (1) connect a diode across \(R_B\) (anode at the discharge pin, cathode at the capacitor) so C charges through \(R_A\) only: \(D = R_A/(R_A + R_B)\), then choose \(R_A < R_B\); or (2) design for \(1 - D\) and invert the output.

2023–24 · Sec B · Q6(a) · 18 marks

With proper connection diagram, explain the monostable mode of operation of a 555 timer. Design a monostable multivibrator using a 555 timer that generates an output pulse of duration T = 100 ms. Draw the waveforms of output voltage and trigger voltage of your design.

Show worked answer

Draw Figure 2 (with the internal blocks of Figure 1 if time allows). Operation: in the stable state Q = 0, the discharge transistor holds C at 0 V, output low. A negative trigger below \(V_{CC}/3\) makes comparator 2 set the flip-flop: output high, transistor off, C charges through R toward \(V_{CC}\): \(v_C = V_{CC}(1 - e^{-t/RC})\). At \(v_C = \tfrac23V_{CC}\) comparator 1 resets the flip-flop: output low, C discharged. The circuit waits for the next trigger.

Pulse width: \(\tfrac23 = 1 - e^{-T/RC} \Rightarrow T = RC\ln3 = 1.1RC\).

Design: choose C = 1 µF: \(R = \dfrac{0.1}{1.0986\times10^{-6}} = 91.0\) kΩ (91 kΩ standard; with 1.1, 90.9 kΩ). (Equally valid: C = 10 µF, R = 9.1 kΩ.)

Waveforms (Figure 3): trigger normally at \(V_{CC}\), a short negative spike below \(V_{CC}/3\) at t = 0 (narrower than 100 ms); output high from 0 to 100 ms, then low; \(v_C\) rises exponentially from 0 to \(2V_{CC}/3\) at 100 ms, then drops to 0.

2022–23 · Sec B · Q7(b) · 15 marks  |  same question 2021–22 Sec A Q2(b)(ii) (10 marks)

Design a square wave generator with 10 ms period and 75% duty cycle with 555 timer.

Show worked answer

\(t_H = 7.5\) ms, \(t_L = 2.5\) ms. D > 50%: standard astable.

Choose C = 1 µF: \(R_B = \dfrac{2.5\times10^{-3}}{0.693\times10^{-6}} = 3.61\) kΩ; \(R_A + R_B = \dfrac{7.5\times10^{-3}}{0.693\times10^{-6}} = 10.82\) kΩ, so \(R_A = 7.21\) kΩ (= \(2R_B\), as expected for 75%).

Draw Figure 4 without the diode: \(R_A\) = 7.21 kΩ, \(R_B\) = 3.61 kΩ, C = 1 µF, 0.01 µF on pin 5. Output: high 7.5 ms, low 2.5 ms.

2021–22 · Sec A · Q2(b)(i) · 10 marks

To get an output pulse of 10 ms width when trigger is applied, an external resistor and a 1 µF capacitor are connected with a 555 timer. What should be the value of the resistor?

Show worked answer

Monostable: \(T = 1.1RC\). \(R = \dfrac{10\times10^{-3}}{1.1\times10^{-6}} = 9.09\) kΩ (9.10 kΩ with \(\ln3\)); use 9.1 kΩ. Connect R from \(V_{CC}\) to pins 6–7, C from pins 6–7 to ground, trigger into pin 2.

2020–21 · Sec A · Q3(a) · 20 marks

Design an oscillator circuit using a 555 timer IC which has a square-wave output with oscillation frequency 20 kHz and duty cycle 25%. Provide the necessary illustration of the designed circuit and draw the wave shape.

Show worked answer

\(T = 50\) µs, \(t_H = 12.5\) µs, \(t_L = 37.5\) µs. D < 50%: steering diode across \(R_B\).

C = 1 nF: \(R_A = \dfrac{12.5\times10^{-6}}{0.693\times10^{-9}} = 18.0\) kΩ; \(R_B = \dfrac{37.5\times10^{-6}}{0.693\times10^{-9}} = 54.1\) kΩ. Check \(D = 18.0/72.1 = 0.25\).

Alternative: standard astable for 75% (\(R_A = 2R_B\): \(R_B\) = 18.0 kΩ, \(R_A\) = 36.1 kΩ with 1 nF) followed by an inverter.

Draw Figure 4 with the diode and values; wave shape: output high 12.5 µs, low 37.5 µs; \(v_C\) between \(V_{CC}/3\) and \(2V_{CC}/3\), rising faster (through \(R_A\)) than it falls (through \(R_B\)).

2019–20 (exam held January 2021) · Sec B · Q6(b) · 29⅔ marks

Consider the 555 circuit with \(R_A\) = 7 kΩ (\(V_{CC}\) to pin 7), \(R_B\) = 3 kΩ (pin 7 to pins 2, 6), C = 0.5 µF, \(V_{CC}\) = 10 V, 0.01 µF on pin 5. (i) Draw the waveshapes of \(V_C\) and \(V_O\). (ii) Determine output low time \(t_{low}\) and output high time \(t_{high}\). (iii) Find the duty cycle and free-running frequency. (iv) How does the free-running frequency depend on C? Discuss with a graph. (v) Design the resistors and capacitor so that the duty cycle becomes 0.40.

Show worked answer

(i) Figure 5: \(V_C\) swings exponentially between 3.33 V and 6.67 V (rising through \(R_A + R_B\), falling through \(R_B\)); \(V_O\) ≈ 10 V while \(V_C\) rises, ≈ 0 V while it falls.

(ii) \(t_{high} = 0.693\times(7 + 3)\text{k}\times0.5\text{µ} = 3.47\) ms; \(t_{low} = 0.693\times3\text{k}\times0.5\text{µ} = 1.04\) ms.

(iii) \(T = 4.51\) ms, \(f = 222\) Hz, \(D = 3.47/4.51 = 0.769\) (76.9%).

(iv) \(f = \dfrac{1.44}{(R_A + 2R_B)C} = \dfrac{1.44}{13\text{k}\cdot C}\): inversely proportional to C (graph of f against C is a rectangular hyperbola; f against 1/C is a straight line through the origin). Doubling C halves f; D does not change with C.

(v) D = 0.40 < 0.5 needs a diode across \(R_B\): \(D = R_A/(R_A + R_B) = 0.4 \Rightarrow R_B = 1.5R_A\). Keeping the same frequency (T = 4.51 ms) and C = 0.5 µF: \(R_A = \dfrac{0.4\times4.51\text{ ms}}{0.693\times0.5\text{ µF}} = 5.20\) kΩ, \(R_B = 7.80\) kΩ. (Any pair with \(R_B = 1.5R_A\) works, e.g. 10 kΩ, 15 kΩ, 0.1 µF giving 577 Hz.)

Design a circuit using 555 IC which results in a square wave with oscillation frequency of 100 kHz and 75% duty cycle. Also draw your designed circuit and output wave shape.

Practice

Key terms

Quick check

Summary: thresholds \(V_{CC}/3\) and \(2V_{CC}/3\). Monostable: \(T = RC\ln3 \approx 1.1RC\). Astable: \(t_H = 0.693(R_A + R_B)C\) (output high, charging), \(t_L = 0.693R_BC\), \(f = 1.44/((R_A + 2R_B)C)\), \(D = (R_A + R_B)/(R_A + 2R_B) > 50\%\). Design: \(t_H = DT\), \(t_L = (1 - D)T\); D > 50%: \(R_B\) from \(t_L\), then \(R_A\); D ≤ 50%: diode across \(R_B\), \(R_A = t_H/(0.693C)\), \(R_B = t_L/(0.693C)\).
