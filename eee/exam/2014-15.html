2014–15 · Sec A · Q1(b) · 16 marks

The small-signal model of a diode is said to be valid for voltage variations of about 10 mV. To what percentage current change does this correspond for \(n = 1\) (consider both positive and negative signals)? What is the maximum allowable voltage signal (positive or negative) if the current change is limited to 10%?

Answer key

Exact: \(i_D/I_D = e^{v_d/V_T}\), \(V_T\) = 25 mV.

  • \(v_d = +10\) mV: \(e^{0.4} - 1 = +49.2\%\).
  • \(v_d = -10\) mV: \(e^{-0.4} - 1 = -33.0\%\). (The linear model would predict ±40%.)
  • 10% limit: \(v_d = V_T\ln 1.1 = +2.38\) mV; \(v_d = V_T\ln 0.9 = -2.63\) mV. So the signal must stay within about 2.4 mV (if the 10% is applied to the linear model, \(v_d = 0.1V_T = 2.5\) mV).

Full method: Small-signal model

2014–15 · Sec A · Q1(c) · 20 marks

A peak rectifier circuit is operated with a 1 kΩ load and a diode that can be modelled to have a 0.7 V drop at any current. It is fed by a 50 Hz sinusoid and the capacitor is chosen to provide a peak-to-peak ripple voltage of 10% of the peak output voltage. (i) What is the value of the capacitor? (ii) What fraction of the cycle does the diode conduct? (iii) What is the average diode current? (iv) What is the peak diode current? Consider the load current \(I_L\) to be 15 mA.

(No figure: standard half-wave peak rectifier, source → diode → C ∥ R.)

Answer key

\(V_p = I_L R = 15\) V (input peak 15.7 V), \(V_r = 1.5\) V.

  • (i) \(C = V_p/(fRV_r) = 15/(50 \times 1000 \times 1.5) = \) 200 µF.
  • (ii) \(\omega\Delta t = \sqrt{2V_r/V_p} = \sqrt{0.2} = 0.447\) rad (25.6°), fraction \(= 0.447/2\pi = \) 7.12% (Δt = 1.42 ms).
  • (iii) \(i_{D,av} = I_L(1 + \pi\sqrt{2V_p/V_r}) = 15(1 + \pi\sqrt{20}) = \) 225.7 mA.
  • (iv) \(i_{D,max} = I_L(1 + 2\pi\sqrt{2V_p/V_r}) = \) 436.5 mA.

Full method: Peak rectifier: ripple, conduction angle, diode current

Question 2

2014–15 · Sec A · Q2(a) · 20 marks

Assuming that the diodes are ideal, find the output voltage \(V_o\) for the circuit given in the figure. Draw the wave shape clearly.

Input \(v_{in}\): triangular, period 10 ms, ±10 V peak: 0 V at \(t = 0\), +10 V at 2.5 ms, 0 at 5 ms, −10 V at 7.5 ms, 0 at 10 ms (slope ±4 V/ms).

Answer key

Clipper: \(v_x = v_{in}\) for \(-4 < v_{in} < 6\) V; \(v_x = 6\) V when D₁ conducts, \(-4\) V when D₂ conducts. With slope 4 V/ms: \(v_x\) reaches 6 V at 1.5 ms, stays flat to 3.5 ms; reaches −4 V at 6.0 ms, stays flat to 9.0 ms.

Clamper: D₃ conducts at the most negative \(v_x = -4\) V and charges C to 4 V (output side +). Then \(V_o = v_x + 4\).

\(V_o\): the same clipped triangle shifted up 4 V: swings from 0 V to +10 V, flat at 10 V for 1.5–3.5 ms and flat at 0 V for 6–9 ms.

2014–15 · Sec A · Q2(c) · 10⅔ marks

The input–output characteristic of the circuit is given: \(v_o = V_{CC}\) for \(v_{in} < 0.5\) V, then \(v_o\) falls linearly with \(v_{in}\) until it saturates at \(v_o\) = 0.2 V. What will happen (i) if \(V_{CC}\) is increased, (ii) if \(R_C\) is increased? Explain with the characteristic curve.

Answer key
  • Regions: cut-off (\(v_o = V_{CC}\)), active (slope \(-g_mR_C\)), saturation (\(v_o = V_{CE,sat}\) = 0.2 V). Saturation starts when \(I_C = (V_{CC} - 0.2)/R_C\).
  • (i) \(V_{CC}\) up: the top plateau rises to the new \(V_{CC}\); turn-on \(v_{in}\) (≈ 0.5 V) is unchanged; \(I_{C,sat}\) is larger, so saturation starts at a higher \(v_{in}\); the floor stays at 0.2 V. Larger output swing.
  • (ii) \(R_C\) up: plateau unchanged; the active slope is steeper (\(|A_v| = g_mR_C\) larger); \(I_{C,sat}\) smaller, so saturation starts at a lower \(v_{in}\); floor still 0.2 V. Narrower input range for linear operation.

Full method: Load lines, transfer curves, switching

Question 3

2014–15 · Sec A · Q3(a) · 16 marks

Find the expression for the emitter current in the circuit of the figure. What are the conditions that should be satisfied? How is the feedback operation of \(R_E\) performed?

Answer key
  • Thevenin at the base: \(V_{BB} = V_{CC}R_2/(R_1+R_2)\), \(R_B = R_1\parallel R_2\).
  • KVL: \(V_{BB} = I_B R_B + V_{BE} + I_E R_E\) with \(I_B = I_E/(\beta+1)\) ⇒ \(\displaystyle I_E = \frac{V_{BB} - V_{BE}}{R_E + R_B/(\beta+1)}\).
  • Conditions for \(I_E\) insensitive to β and \(V_{BE}\): \(V_{BB} \gg V_{BE}\) and \(R_E \gg R_B/(\beta+1)\) (in practice divider current ≈ 0.1 \(I_E\) or more).
  • Feedback: if \(I_C\) rises (temperature, β), \(V_E = I_ER_E\) rises; with \(V_B\) fixed by the divider, \(V_{BE}\) falls, \(I_B\) falls, and \(I_C\) is pulled back. This is negative feedback.

2014–15 · Sec A · Q3(b) · 10⅔ marks

In the figure of Q3(a), if \(R_1\) = 40 kΩ, \(R_2\) = 10 kΩ, \(R_C\) = 1.5 kΩ, \(R_E\) = 1 kΩ, \(V_{CC}\) = 12 V and β = 1000, calculate the biasing point. How can the values of \(R_1\) and \(R_2\) be changed for a good biasing design?

Answer key

\(V_{BB} = 2.4\) V, \(R_B = 8\) kΩ. \(I_E = 1.7/(1 + 8/1001) = 1.687\) mA, \(I_C\) = 1.685 mA, \(I_B\) = 1.68 µA, \(V_C = 9.47\) V, \(V_E = 1.69\) V, \(V_{CE}\) = 7.79 V (active ✓). (If β = 1000 is a misprint for 100: \(I_C\) = 1.560 mA, \(V_{CE}\) = 8.09 V.)

  • Good design: keep the ratio \(R_1:R_2\) (so \(V_{BB}\) is unchanged) but make both smaller so that \(R_B/(\beta+1) \ll R_E\) for the lowest β expected and the divider current is ≫ \(I_B\). Cost: more supply current, lower input resistance.
  • Sedra's rule of thumb (\(V_{BB} \approx V_{CC}/3\), divider current \(0.1I_E\)) for the same \(I_E\) ≈ 1.68 mA: \(R_2 = 4/0.168 = 23.8\) kΩ, \(R_1 = 8/0.168 = 47.6\) kΩ, and \(R_E\) must become \((4-0.7)/1.685 = 1.96\) kΩ.

The NMOS and PMOS transistors in the circuit have \(k_n'(W_n/L_n) = k_p'(W_p/L_p)\) = 1 mA/V² and \(V_{tn} = -V_{tp}\) = 1 V. Assume λ = 0. Find \(i_{DN}\), \(i_{DP}\) and \(v_o\) for \(v_i\) = +2.5 V and −2.5 V.

Check: \(V_{DS} = 1.46\) V \(> V_{OV} = 0.457\) V ✓.

\(v_i = -2.5\) V: mirror image: \(Q_N\) off, \(v_o\) = −1.043 V, \(i_{DP}\) = 0.104 mA, \(i_{DN}\) = 0.

2014–15 · Sec A · Q4(c) · 10⅔ marks

How can the MOSFET transconductance \(g_m\) be varied with the design parameters \(W/L\) and overdrive voltage \(V_{OV}\)? Explain.

Answer key
  • \(g_m = k_n'(W/L)V_{OV}\): at fixed \(V_{OV}\), \(g_m \propto W/L\); at fixed \(W/L\), \(g_m \propto V_{OV}\).
  • \(g_m = \sqrt{2k_n'(W/L)I_D}\): at fixed bias current, \(g_m \propto \sqrt{W/L}\).
  • \(g_m = 2I_D/V_{OV}\): at fixed current, a smaller \(V_{OV}\) (wider device) gives more \(g_m\).
  • Trade-offs: larger \(W/L\) costs area and capacitance (speed); larger \(V_{OV}\) costs output swing and power.

2014–15 · Sec B · Q5(b) · 15 marks

Design a Schmitt trigger that triggers from +16 V to −16 V at an input voltage of 12 V and triggers from −16 V to +16 V at an input voltage of 8 V. Specify all the values of resistors used and the reference DC voltage.

Answer key

Inverting Schmitt: \(v_{in}\) to the − input; \(R_2\) from \(v_o\) to the + input; \(R_1\) from \(V_{REF}\) to the + input. \(v_+ = (v_oR_1 + V_{REF}R_2)/(R_1+R_2)\).

\(12(R_1+R_2) = 16R_1 + V_{REF}R_2\) and \(8(R_1+R_2) = -16R_1 + V_{REF}R_2\). Subtract: \(4(R_1+R_2) = 32R_1\) ⇒ \(R_2 = 7R_1\), then \(V_{REF} = 80/7 = 11.43\) V. E.g. \(R_1\) = 10 kΩ, \(R_2\) = 70 kΩ. Check: \(V_{TH} = (16 + 80)/8 = 12\) V, \(V_{TL} = (-16 + 80)/8 = 8\) V ✓. Output ±16 V needs rails a little above 16 V.

Full method: Bistables and Schmitt triggers

Design a circuit using op-amps that gives the output waveform shown from a 1 V DC input. Specify all values of resistors and capacitor used. Use +15 V and −15 V for DC biasing.

Waveform: \(v_o\) rises linearly from 0 V at \(t = 0\) to +15 V at \(t\) = 5 s, then stays at +15 V (saturation) through 10 s and beyond.

Question 6

2014–15 · Sec B · Q6(a) · 20 marks

Design a circuit using op-amps that can solve the differential equation \(3\,\dfrac{d^2x(t)}{dt^2} + 4x(t) = 0\). Also point out the node that represents the solution \(x(t)\). Specify all values of resistors and capacitors used.

Answer key
  • \(\ddot x = -\tfrac43 x\).
  • Integrator 1 (1 MΩ, 1 µF, RC = 1 s): input \(\ddot x\) → output \(-\dot x\).
  • Integrator 2 (1 MΩ, 1 µF): input \(-\dot x\) → output \(x\). This node is the solution \(x(t)\).
  • Inverting amplifier with \(R_f\) = 100 kΩ, \(R_1\) = 75 kΩ: input \(x\) → output \(-\tfrac43x = \ddot x\), fed back to integrator 1. Three op-amps.
  • Set a non-zero initial condition (pre-charge integrator 2 to \(x(0)\), or integrator 1 to \(-\dot x(0)\)); then \(x = x(0)\cos(1.155t)\), ω = \(\sqrt{4/3}\) = 1.155 rad/s.

Question-bank answer incomplete

The Gemini question-bank answer gives the right loop but no initial condition. With uncharged capacitors the circuit sits at \(x \equiv 0\) forever; say how the oscillation is started.

2014–15 · Sec B · Q6(b) · 26⅔ marks

Suppose you have two input voltages \(X_1\) and \(X_2\). Design a circuit using op-amps that gives output \(V_o = 10X_1X_2\). The diodes available for the design obey \(I_D = I_Se^{V_D/nV_T}\), where \(I_S = 10^{-6}\) A. Specify all values of resistors used.

  • Unity inverter: \(V_o = +10X_1X_2\). Check: \(I_S(1/(I_SR))^2R_f = 10\) ✓. Works for \(X_1, X_2 > 0\) only (one quadrant); same diodes at the same temperature.

Question 7

2014–15 · Sec B · Q7(a) · 20 marks (3 + 12 + 5)

Write the equation of the magnitude of gain of a −20 dB/decade low-pass filter. Why is it called a −20 dB/decade filter? Can it also be called a −6 dB/octave filter?

Answer key
  • \(|H(f)| = \dfrac{A_0}{\sqrt{1 + (f/f_c)^2}}\); in dB: \(20\log A_0 - 10\log[1 + (f/f_c)^2]\).
  • For \(f \gg f_c\), \(|H| \approx A_0f_c/f\): each ×10 in frequency divides the gain by 10, i.e. −20 dB per decade.
  • Each ×2 in frequency: \(-20\log 2 = -6.02\) dB. So yes, −6 dB/octave is the same slope.
  • At \(f = f_c\): −3 dB, phase −45°.

Full method: Active filters

2014–15 · Sec B · Q7(b) · 20 marks

Design a notch filter that eliminates any signal between 8 kHz and 12 kHz using op-amps. Specify all values of resistors and capacitors used.

Answer key

Band-stop = input minus band-pass. \(f_0 = \sqrt{8 \times 12} = 9.80\) kHz, BW = 4 kHz, Q = 2.45.

  • Multiple-feedback band-pass, \(C_1 = C_2\) = 1 nF, centre gain −1: \(R_3 = Q/(\pi f_0C) = 79.6\) kΩ (feedback), \(R_1 = R_3/2 = 39.8\) kΩ (input), \(R_2 = R_1/(2Q^2 - 1) = 3.62\) kΩ (to ground).
  • Inverting summer (10 kΩ from \(v_{in}\), 10 kΩ from the band-pass output, 10 kΩ feedback): \(v_o = -(1 + H_{BP})v_{in}\). At 9.80 kHz \(H_{BP} = -1\), output 0; at 8 kHz and 12 kHz \(|v_o/v_{in}|\) = 0.707 (−3 dB edges); far away the gain is 1.

Common answer that does not work

"First-order LPF at 8 kHz in parallel with first-order HPF at 12 kHz, then add" is a frequent answer. With first-order sections the sum only dips to 0.80 (−1.9 dB) at 9.8 kHz: nothing is eliminated. If you use the LPF + HPF idea, use higher-order sections and say so; the band-pass subtraction above is the safe design.

Full method: Active filters

2014–15 · Sec B · Q7(c) · 6⅔ marks

Write down the Barkhausen stability criterion for an oscillation in a linear electronic circuit to sustain.

Answer key
  • For a loop with amplifier A and positive feedback β, at the oscillation frequency \(\omega_0\): \(|A\beta| = 1\) and \(\angle A\beta = 0°\) (or 360°).
  • Equivalently the characteristic equation \(1 - A(s)\beta(s) = 0\) has poles on the jω axis at \(\pm j\omega_0\).
  • In practice \(|A\beta|\) is made slightly greater than 1 for start-up, and a non-linear limiter brings it back to 1.

Full method: Barkhausen criterion and amplitude limiters

Question 8

2014–15 · Sec B · Q8(a) · 20 marks (17 + 3)

Design a Wien-bridge oscillator with an oscillation frequency of 1 kHz. What is the gain of the feedback network of your design? Specify all values of resistors and capacitors used.

Answer key

2014–15 · Sec B · Q8(b) · 15 marks (10 + 5)

Derive the expression of desensitivity. Explain positive and negative feedback from desensitivity.

Answer key
  • \(A_f = A/(1 + A\beta)\) ⇒ \(dA_f/dA = 1/(1 + A\beta)^2\) ⇒ \(\dfrac{dA_f}{A_f} = \dfrac{1}{1 + A\beta}\dfrac{dA}{A}\).
  • Desensitivity factor \(D = 1 + A\beta\) (the amount by which the relative change of gain is reduced).
  • Negative feedback: \(A\beta > 0\), D > 1: gain less sensitive to A, wider bandwidth, less distortion, at the cost of gain.
  • Positive feedback: \(-1 < A\beta < 0\), D < 1: gain more sensitive; at \(A\beta = -1\), D = 0 and \(A_f \to \infty\): the oscillation condition.

Full method: Barkhausen criterion and amplitude limiters

2014–15 · Sec B · Q8(c) · 11⅔ marks · no longer examined

Draw the circuit diagram of a controlled full-wave rectifier using SCR. Draw the waveforms of sinusoidal input, gate pulses and rectified output.

Answer key

Centre-tapped transformer with two SCRs (or a bridge of four), load R. Gate pulses once per half-cycle, delayed by the firing angle α (T1 at α, T2 at π + α). Output is zero from 0 to α and follows \(|v_s|\) from α to π, each half-cycle; each SCR turns off at the current zero. \(V_{dc} = (V_m/\pi)(1 + \cos\alpha)\).
