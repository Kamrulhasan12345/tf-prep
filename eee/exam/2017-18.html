Term Final 2017–18

280 marks, 3 hours, two sections (A: Q1–Q4, B: Q5–Q8), use separate scripts for each section. Answer any THREE of the four questions in each section; every question carries 46⅔ marks. Section A is diodes, BJT and MOSFET; Section B is op-amps, filters, an oscillator and (Q7–Q8) digital logic, most of which is no longer examined.

What this paper tests

  • Q1: pnp common-base amplifier gain; CE amplifier with \(r_o\): \(R_{in}\), \(R_o\), \(A_{vo}\), \(A_v\), \(G_v\), signal limits.
  • Q2: CS amplifier with drain-to-gate \(R_G\) bias; why bias a MOSFET (fixed \(V_G\) + \(R_S\)); MOSFET as a small-signal amplifier and its VTC.
  • Q3: collector-feedback bias with \(R_E\); npn stage driving a pnp stage (\(I_{C1}\), \(I_{C2}\)).
  • Q4: peak rectifier (C, conduction fraction, diode current); shunt clipper design from a VTC; Zener regulator for a car radio.
  • Q5: op-amp with a MOSFET in the feedback path; integrator with a square-wave input; comparator + T flip-flop + integrator system.
  • Q6: identify an active filter (band-pass); design a band-pass filter; buffered RC phase-shift oscillator.
  • Q7: CMOS complex gate (examinable); PLA and LUT (no longer examined).
  • Q8: priority encoder, mod-12 counter, shift register (all no longer examined).

Question 1

2017–18 · Sec A · Q1(a) · 26⅔ marks

Determine the voltage gain \(v_o/v_i\) of the transistor amplifier shown in Fig. for Q. 1(a). Assume \(\beta\) = 100. (A pnp transistor with its base grounded; \(R_E\) = 10 kΩ from +10 V to the emitter, \(R_C\) = 5 kΩ from the collector to −10 V; \(v_i\) is coupled into the emitter and \(v_o\) taken from the collector through large capacitors.)

Answer key

DC (capacitors open, \(V_{EB}\) = 0.7 V): base at 0, so \(V_E\) = +0.7 V; \(I_E = (10 - 0.7)/10 = 0.93\) mA; \(I_C = \alpha I_E = 0.921\) mA; \(V_C = -10 + 5\times0.921 = -5.40\) V. \(V_{EC} = 6.1\) V, \(V_{CB} = -5.4\) V (CB reverse biased): active.

Small signal (common base): \(r_e = V_T/I_E = 25/0.93 = 26.9\) Ω, \(g_m = I_C/V_T = 36.8\) mA/V.

\(A_v = \dfrac{v_o}{v_i} = \dfrac{\alpha R_C}{r_e} = g_mR_C = 36.8\times5 = \mathbf{+184}\) V/V (non-inverting). \(R_{in} = r_e\parallel R_E \approx 26.8\) Ω, irrelevant here because \(v_i\) is an ideal source.

Full method: Common base and emitter follower

2017–18 · Sec A · Q1(b) · 20 marks · Sedra example

A CE amplifier utilizes a BJT with \(\beta\) = 100 and \(V_A\) = 100 V, is biased at \(I_C\) = 1 mA and has a collector resistance \(R_C\) = 5 kΩ. Find \(R_{in}\), \(R_o\) and \(A_{vo}\). If the amplifier is fed with a signal source having a resistance of 5 kΩ, and a load resistance \(R_L\) = 5 kΩ is connected to the output terminal, find the resulting \(A_v\) and \(G_v\). If \(v_\pi\) is to be limited to 5 mV, what are the corresponding \(v_{sig}\) and \(v_o\) with the load connected?

Answer key

\(g_m = 1/0.025 = 40\) mA/V, \(r_\pi = \beta/g_m = 2.5\) kΩ, \(r_o = V_A/I_C = 100\) kΩ.

\(R_{in} = r_\pi = 2.5\) kΩ; \(R_o = R_C\parallel r_o = 4.76\) kΩ; \(A_{vo} = -g_m(R_C\parallel r_o) = -190.5\) V/V.

\(A_v = -g_m(R_C\parallel r_o\parallel R_L) = -40\times2.439 = -97.6\) V/V; \(G_v = \dfrac{R_{in}}{R_{in}+R_{sig}}A_v = \dfrac{2.5}{7.5}(-97.6) = -32.5\) V/V.

\(v_\pi\) = 5 mV: \(v_{sig} = v_\pi(R_{in}+R_{sig})/R_{in} = 15\) mV; \(v_o = A_v v_\pi = -0.488\) V (peak).

One senior file drops the minus sign on \(A_{vo}\); keep it (the CE stage inverts).

Full method: Common-emitter amplifier

Question 2

2017–18 · Sec A · Q2(a) · 20 marks · Sedra example  |  also 2015–16 Sec A Q3(b)

Determine the voltage gain \(v_o/v_i\) of the common-source MOSFET amplifier shown in Fig. for Q. 2(a). The transistor has \(V_t\) = 1.5 V, \(k_n'(W/L)\) = 0.25 mA/V² and \(V_A\) = 50 V. (\(R_D\) = 10 kΩ from +15 V to the drain, \(R_G\) = 10 MΩ from drain to gate, source grounded; \(v_i\) coupled to the gate and \(R_L\) = 10 kΩ coupled to the drain through large capacitors.)

Answer key

DC: no gate current, so \(V_G = V_D\) and the MOSFET is saturated (\(V_{DS} = V_{GS} > V_{GS} - V_t\)). \(I_D = (15 - V_D)/10 = 0.125(V_D - 1.5)^2\) gives \(1.25V_D^2 - 2.75V_D - 12.1875 = 0\), so \(V_D = V_{GS} = 4.41\) V (the other root is negative: reject), \(I_D = 1.06\) mA.

\(g_m = k_n V_{OV} = 0.25\times2.91 = 0.728\) mA/V; \(r_o = V_A/I_D = 47.2\) kΩ.

Gain (\(R_G\) carries negligible signal current): \(A_v \approx -g_m(R_D\parallel R_L\parallel r_o) = -0.728\times4.52 = \mathbf{-3.29}\) V/V (Sedra: −3.3).

Full method: Feedback-biased CS, CG and CD

2017–18 · Sec A · Q2(b) · 11⅔ marks

What is the reason for biasing a MOSFET? Write a short note on the biasing technique of a discrete MOSFET using a fixed voltage at the gate and a resistance in the source, \(R_S\).

Answer key
  • Biasing sets a stable dc operating point in saturation, which fixes \(g_m\), the gain, and the allowed output swing; the signal then rides on that point.
  • Circuit: divider \(R_1\), \(R_2\) gives \(V_G = V_{DD}R_2/(R_1+R_2)\) (gate current zero, so large resistors keep \(R_{in}\) high); \(R_S\) from source to ground.
  • \(V_G = V_{GS} + I_DR_S\). If \(I_D\) rises (higher \(k_n\), lower \(V_t\), temperature), \(V_S\) rises, \(V_{GS}\) falls and pulls \(I_D\) back: negative feedback.
  • With \(I_DR_S \gg V_{GS}\), \(I_D \approx (V_G - V_{GS})/R_S\) is nearly independent of device parameters; sketch two \(i_D\)–\(v_{GS}\) curves and the bias line of slope \(-1/R_S\) to show the small spread in \(I_D\).
  • Bypass \(R_S\) with a capacitor to recover the ac gain; a fixed \(V_{GS}\) without \(R_S\) is poor because \(I_D\) varies with \(V_{OV}^2\).

Full method: Small-signal model and biasing

2017–18 · Sec A · Q2(c) · 15 marks

Briefly explain the operation of a MOSFET as a small-signal amplifier and also deduce its VTC from the \(I_D\)–\(V_{DS}\) graph.

Answer key
  • CS circuit: \(v_{GS} = V_{GS} + v_{gs}\), \(R_D\) to \(V_{DD}\), \(v_O = V_{DD} - i_DR_D\).
  • Draw the \(i_D\)–\(v_{DS}\) curves and the load line \(i_D = (V_{DD} - v_{DS})/R_D\). Each \(v_{GS}\) curve meets the line at one point, giving one \((v_I, v_O)\) pair of the VTC.
  • VTC: \(v_O = V_{DD}\) for \(v_I < V_t\) (cutoff); a steep falling saturation segment \(v_O = V_{DD} - \tfrac12k_nR_D(v_I - V_t)^2\); triode once \(v_O < v_I - V_t\), where \(v_O\) flattens toward 0.
  • Bias at Q in the middle of the saturation segment; a small \(v_{gs}\) moves the point along the line and \(v_o = A_v v_{gs}\) with \(A_v = -g_mR_D\), the slope of the VTC at Q.
  • Small-signal condition: \(i_D = \tfrac12k_n(V_{OV}+v_{gs})^2\) is linear in \(v_{gs}\) only if \(v_{gs} \ll 2V_{OV}\); then \(g_m = k_nV_{OV}\).

Full method: Small-signal model and biasing

2017–18 · Sec A · Q4(a) · 10⅔ marks · Sedra example

Consider a peak rectifier fed by a 60 Hz sinusoid having a peak value \(V_p\) = 100 V. Let the load resistance \(R\) = 10 kΩ. Find the value of the capacitance \(C\) that will result in a peak-to-peak ripple of 2 V. Also calculate the fraction of the cycle during which the diode is conducting and the average and peak values of the diode current. (Standard half-wave circuit: source, diode, then \(C \parallel R\); ideal diode.)

Answer key

\(V_r = V_p/(fRC)\) → \(C = \dfrac{100}{60\times10^4\times2} = 83.3\) µF.

\(\omega\Delta t = \sqrt{2V_r/V_p} = \sqrt{0.04} = 0.2\) rad → fraction \(0.2/2\pi = 3.18\,\%\).

\(I_L \approx V_p/R = 10\) mA; \(i_{D,av} = I_L(1 + \pi\sqrt{2V_p/V_r}) = 10(1 + 10\pi) = 324\) mA; \(i_{D,max} = I_L(1 + 2\pi\sqrt{2V_p/V_r}) = 638\) mA.

Full method: Peak rectifier: ripple, conduction angle, diode current

2017–18 · Sec A · Q4(b) · 18 marks

Design a parallel-based clipper that will yield the voltage transfer function shown in Fig. for Q. 4(b). Assume 0.7 V as diode cut-in voltage.

The VTC (described): \(v_O = -5\) V (flat) for \(v_I \le -5\) V; \(v_O = v_I\) (slope 1) for \(-5 \le v_I \le 2.5\) V; slope \(1/3\) for \(v_I > 2.5\) V, starting from the break point (2.5 V, 2.5 V).

Answer key
  • Series resistor \(R\) from \(v_I\) to \(v_O\); two shunt branches from \(v_O\) to ground; no load.
  • Soft clip above 2.5 V: \(R_1\) in series with \(D_1\) (anode toward \(v_O\)) and a battery \(V_{1}\) (+ at the diode's cathode side). Break: \(V_1 + 0.7 = 2.5\) → \(V_1 = 1.8\) V. Slope \(R_1/(R + R_1) = 1/3\) → \(R = 2R_1\), e.g. \(R_1\) = 10 kΩ, \(R\) = 20 kΩ.

  • Check: for \(v_I \ge 2.5\), \(v_O = v_I/3 + 5/3\), which equals 2.5 V at \(v_I\) = 2.5 V ✓. Any \(R = 2R_1\) pair is acceptable.

A voltage regulator circuit is shown in Fig. for Q. 4(c). The regulator has to power a car radio at \(V_L\) = 9 V from an automobile battery whose voltage may vary between 11 and 13.6 V. The current in the radio will vary between 0 (off) and 100 mA (full volume). Determine the value of the current-limiting resistor \(R_i\) and the maximum power dissipated in the Zener diode.

Answer key

State the assumption. Textbook rule \(I_{Z,min} = 0.1I_{Z,max}\):

\(I_{Z,max} = \dfrac{100(13.6 - 9) - 0\,(11 - 9)}{11 - 0.9\times9 - 0.1\times13.6} = \dfrac{460}{1.54} = 298.7\) mA; \(R_i = \dfrac{11 - 9}{100 + 29.87}\) kΩ \(= 15.4\) Ω; \(P_{Z,max} = 9\times0.2987 = 2.69\) W.

2017–18 · Sec B · Q5(c) · 20⅔ marks

Design a circuit using op-amp and digital circuits for the given input wave shape \(v_{in}\) and output wave shape \(v_{out}\) in Fig. for Q. 5(c). Assume a 5 V level to be digital 1 and −5 V level to be digital 0.

Waveforms (described): \(v_{in}\) is a 5 V-peak sinusoid with period 10 ms, rising through zero at \(t\) = 0 (zero crossings at 0, 5, 10, 15, 20 ms). \(v_{out}\) is a triangle that starts at 0 V at \(t\) = 0, rises linearly to +2 V at 10 ms, falls to 0 at 20 ms and repeats (period 20 ms, half the input frequency).

Answer key

Question 6

2017–18 · Sec B · Q6(a) · 18 marks  |  also 2016–17 Sec A Q2(a)

Determine the type of filter shown in the circuit in Fig. for Q. No. 6(a) by computing the transfer function in the frequency domain. (\(R_1\) in series with \(C_1\) from \(v_{in}\) to the inverting input; \(R_2 \parallel C_2\) from the inverting input to \(v_o\); + input grounded.)

Answer key

\(Z_1 = R_1 + \dfrac{1}{sC_1} = \dfrac{1 + sR_1C_1}{sC_1}\), \(Z_2 = R_2\parallel\dfrac{1}{sC_2} = \dfrac{R_2}{1 + sR_2C_2}\).

\(H(s) = -\dfrac{Z_2}{Z_1} = -\dfrac{sR_2C_1}{(1 + sR_1C_1)(1 + sR_2C_2)}\).

\(|H| \to 0\) as \(\omega \to 0\) (zero at the origin) and as \(\omega \to \infty\) (two poles): a band-pass filter (inverting, second order overall). With \(R_1C_1 \gg R_2C_2\): \(\omega_L = 1/R_1C_1\), \(\omega_H = 1/R_2C_2\), midband gain \(-R_2/R_1\).

Full method: Active filters

2017–18 · Sec B · Q6(c) · 18 marks

For the oscillator circuit shown in Fig. for Q. 6(c), find the oscillation frequency (\(\omega\)), \(\beta\)-network gain (\(\beta\)), and amplifier gain (\(A\)). Also design this oscillator for an oscillation frequency of 1 kHz. Note: each stage is separated by a source-follower (buffer) circuit, hence each stage gives a phase shift of 60° and three stages give a total of 180°. Here \(\beta = V_2/V_1\).

Answer key

Each buffered CR section: \(\dfrac{sRC}{1 + sRC}\), so \(\beta = \left(\dfrac{j\omega RC}{1 + j\omega RC}\right)^3\).

Each section must give 60° (total 180°): \(\tan^{-1}(1/\omega RC) = 60°\) → \(\omega RC = 1/\sqrt3\), \(\mathbf{\omega_0 = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt3\,RC}}\).

\(|\beta|\) per section \(= \cos60° = 1/2\), so \(\beta = -1/8\). Barkhausen \(A\beta = 1\) → \(A = -8\), i.e. \(R_f/R = 8\) (use \(R_f\) slightly larger to start).

Design for 1 kHz: \(RC = 1/(2\pi\sqrt3\times1000) = 91.9\) µs; \(C\) = 10 nF → \(R\) = 9.19 kΩ, \(R_f \ge 73.5\) kΩ.

Full method: RC phase-shift oscillator

2017–18 · Sec B · Q7(a) · 15 marks

Draw a CMOS complex gate for the logic function \(f(a,b,c,d) = \Sigma m(0,1,2,4,6,8,10,12,14)\). Use as few transistors as possible.

Answer key

All even minterms plus \(m_1\): \(f = \bar d + \bar a\bar b\bar c\), so \(\bar f = d(a + b + c)\), which uses only true inputs.

  • PDN (conducts when \(f\) = 0): NMOS \(d\) in series with the parallel group \(a \parallel b \parallel c\).
  • PUN (dual): PMOS \(d\) in parallel with the series chain \(a\)–\(b\)–\(c\).
  • Output \(= \overline{d(a+b+c)} = f\) directly: 4 NMOS + 4 PMOS = 8 transistors, no inverters.

Design a modulo-12 up counter with synchronous reset.
