Question 1 COMPULSORY

2024–25 · Sec A · Q1(a) · 18 marks · compulsory · CO3

With an appropriate circuit diagram, describe the operation of an op-amp based Colpitts oscillator. Derive the frequency of oscillation and explain how this circuit satisfies the conditions for sustained oscillation.

Answer key

Circuit (no figure is given: draw one). Non-inverting amplifier: \(R_1\) from the inverting input to ground, \(R_f\) from the output to the inverting input, gain \(A = 1 + R_f/R_1\). Feedback network: a series \(R\) from the op-amp output to node A, \(L\) from A to ground, and \(C_1\) from A to node B with \(C_2\) from B to ground; B drives the non-inverting input. (The other accepted drawing is an inverting amplifier with a π network: \(C_1\) at the output, \(L\) in series, \(C_2\) at the input; same \(\omega_0\), gain condition \(R_f/R_i \ge C_2/C_1\).)

Loop gain: with \(C_{eq} = C_1C_2/(C_1+C_2)\), the tank (L in parallel with \(C_1\) in series with \(C_2\)) gives \[ \beta(s) = \frac{V_B}{V_o} = \frac{sL}{R(1 + s^2LC_{eq}) + sL}\cdot\frac{C_1}{C_1 + C_2}. \]

Frequency (phase condition): \(\beta\) is real and positive when \(1 - \omega^2LC_{eq} = 0\): \[ f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{L\,\dfrac{C_1C_2}{C_1 + C_2}}}. \]

Magnitude condition: at \(\omega_0\) the tank is resistive-infinite, so \(\beta = C_1/(C_1+C_2)\). Barkhausen \(A\beta = 1\) needs \(1 + R_f/R_1 = 1 + C_2/C_1\), i.e. \(R_f/R_1 \ge C_2/C_1\) (slightly larger to start; amplitude is then limited by op-amp saturation or a diode limiter).

Operation: noise contains all frequencies; only \(f_0\) returns in phase with loop gain ≥ 1, so it grows; nonlinearity reduces the effective gain until \(A\beta = 1\) exactly and a steady sinusoid remains.

Full method: Colpitts, Hartley and crystal oscillators · background: Barkhausen criterion and amplitude limiters

2024–25 · Sec A · Q1(b) · 17 marks · compulsory · CO4

Design a circuit using op-amps and other passive elements that can solve \[ 2\frac{d^2v}{dt^2} - 3\frac{dv}{dt} + 4v + 5 = 0. \] Draw the overall connection diagram with proper element values. The op-amps are biased with ±15 V. All initial conditions are zero.

Answer key

Isolate the highest derivative: \(\dfrac{d^2v}{dt^2} = 1.5\dfrac{dv}{dt} - 2v - 2.5\).

One valid design (three op-amps, integrators with \(RC\) = 1 s: R = 1 MΩ, C = 1 µF):

  1. Integrator 1 (inverting): input \(v'' \) → output \(-v'\).
  2. Integrator 2 (inverting): input \(-v'\) → output \(+v\).
  3. Inverting summer, \(R_f\) = 100 kΩ, output \(= -\left(\dfrac{R_f}{R_a}(-v') + \dfrac{R_f}{R_b}v + \dfrac{R_f}{R_c}(+15\text{ V})\right)\). Set \(R_a\) = 66.7 kΩ (gain 1.5), \(R_b\) = 50 kΩ (gain 2), \(R_c\) = 600 kΩ (15 × 100/600 = 2.5). The output is then \(1.5v' - 2v - 2.5 = v''\); wire it back to the input of integrator 1 to close the loop.
  4. Zero initial conditions: a reset switch across each integrator capacitor, opened at t = 0. Output \(v(t)\) is taken from integrator 2.

Check: the equation's roots are \(s = 0.75 \pm j1.20\) (from \(2s^2 - 3s + 4 = 0\)), so the solution is a growing oscillation about the particular value \(v = -1.25\) V; in practice the outputs grow until an op-amp saturates at about ±15 V. Mention this; it is not a design error. Any equivalent arrangement (e.g. a non-inverting summer, or integrators with different RC and compensating gains) earns full marks if the signs close the loop correctly.

Full method: Analog computer for differential equations

Question 2

2024–25 · Sec A · Q2(i)–(v) · 5 × 7 = 35 marks

The circuit in the figure is a common-source amplifier. All capacitors are open at DC and short at the signal frequency. \(V_t\) = 1 V, \(\mu_nC_{ox}(W/L)\) = 4 mA/V², \(V_A\) = 100 V. (i) Find the DC drain voltage \(V_D\) and drain current \(I_D\) (ignore the Early effect for bias). (ii) Find \(g_m\) and \(r_o\), and draw the small-signal equivalent circuit. (iii) Determine expressions and values for \(R_{in}\), \(R_o\) and the overall gain \(v_o/v_{sig}\). (iv) Calculate the maximum peak \(v_{sig}\) while keeping \(v_{gs} \le 0.1V_{OV}\). (v) Suggest a circuit modification that allows the maximum \(v_{sig}\) of (iv) to be doubled while keeping the small-signal approximation, without changing the DC bias point.

Answer key

(i) \(V_G = 15\times\dfrac{5}{10+5} = 5\) V. With \(k_n\) = 4 mA/V²: \(I_D = \tfrac12\cdot4\,(V_{GS}-1)^2\), \(V_{GS} = 5 - 7I_D\) ⇒ \(98I_D^2 - 113I_D + 32 = 0\) ⇒ \(I_D\) = 0.5 mA or 0.653 mA. Reject 0.653 mA (it gives \(V_{GS}\) = 0.43 V < \(V_t\)). So \(I_D\) = 0.5 mA, \(V_{GS}\) = 1.5 V, \(V_{OV}\) = 0.5 V, \(V_D = 15 - 16\times0.5\) = 7 V. Saturation check: \(V_{DS} = 7 - 3.5 = 3.5\) V > 0.5 V ✓.

(ii) \(g_m = 2I_D/V_{OV}\) = 2 mA/V; \(r_o = V_A/I_D\) = 200 kΩ. Model: \(R_{G1}\parallel R_{G2}\) from gate to ground, \(g_mv_{gs}\) from drain to ground in parallel with \(r_o\), source grounded (bypassed), then \(R_D\) and \(R_L\) from drain to ground.

(iii) \(R_{in} = R_{G1}\parallel R_{G2}\) = 3.33 MΩ. \(R_o = R_D\parallel r_o = 16\parallel200\) = 14.8 kΩ. \(\dfrac{v_o}{v_{sig}} = \dfrac{R_{in}}{R_{in}+R_{sig}}\cdot\left[-g_m(R_D\parallel r_o\parallel R_L)\right] = 0.943\times(-2\times7.69)\) = −14.5 V/V.

(iv) \(v_{gs} = 0.943\,v_{sig} \le 0.1\times0.5 = 50\) mV ⇒ \(\hat v_{sig}\) = 53 mV.

(v) Leave part of the source resistance unbypassed: split \(R_S\) = 7 kΩ into \(R_s\) = 0.5 kΩ (unbypassed, next to the source) + 6.5 kΩ (bypassed). DC is unchanged (still 7 kΩ). Now \(v_{gs} = v_i/(1 + g_mR_s) = v_i/2\), so \(v_{sig}\) can be doubled to 106 mV. The price: the gain roughly halves (≈ −7.3 V/V, ignoring \(r_o\)).

Full method: Common-source amplifier (type M-11)

The circuit in the figure works as a voltage-to-current converter. The op-amp is biased with ±20 V and stays linear while \(V_{out}\) is within the rails (so the virtual short \(V_1 = V_2\) holds). \(R_1 = R_2\) = 10 kΩ, \(R_3 = R_4\) = 1 kΩ, \(R_L\) = 100 Ω. Question 4

2024–25 · Sec A · Q4(a) · 15 marks

The paper shows the internal circuit of a 555 timer (pins Threshold, Trigger, Discharge, Output, \(V_{CC}\), Ground; a three-resistor divider setting \(\tfrac23V_{CC}\) and \(\tfrac13V_{CC}\); comparator 1 on Threshold, comparator 2 on Trigger; an SR flip-flop; a discharge transistor Q1 driven from \(\bar Q\) through 100 Ω). Design a circuit using a 555 to generate a 100 kHz square wave with 40 % duty cycle. Draw the final connection diagram with component values.

The internal block diagram is the standard one and is described in words here; see the 555 chapter for the drawing.

Answer key

\(T = 1/100\text{ kHz} = 10\) µs, \(T_H = 0.4T = 4\) µs, \(T_L = 6\) µs. A standard astable has \(T_H = 0.693(R_A+R_B)C > T_L = 0.693R_BC\), so it cannot go below 50 %. Add a steering diode across \(R_B\) (anode at the discharge pin): C charges through \(R_A\) only and discharges through \(R_B\) only.

Choose C = 1 nF: \(R_A = \dfrac{4\text{ µs}}{0.693\times1\text{ nF}}\) = 5.77 kΩ, \(R_B = \dfrac{6\text{ µs}}{0.693\times1\text{ nF}}\) = 8.66 kΩ (diode drop neglected). Pins 4 and 8 to \(V_{CC}\), pin 5 to ground through 10 nF, pins 2 and 6 tied to C.

Alternative accepted answer: design a 60 % astable (\(R_A\) = 2.89 kΩ, \(R_B\) = 5.77 kΩ with 1 nF; check: \(T_H = 0.693\times8.66\times1 = 6\) µs, \(T_L\) = 4 µs) and invert the output.

Full method: The 555 timer (type S-06)

2024–25 · Sec A · Q4(b) · 12 marks

Explain why adding a source resistor \(R_S\) to a CS MOSFET amplifier improves its bias stability compared with grounding the source. Detail how \(R_S\) compensates for manufacturing variations in \(V_t\) and \(k_n\).

Answer key
  • Grounded source with fixed \(V_{GS}\): \(I_D = \tfrac12k_n(V_{GS}-V_t)^2\) depends directly (quadratically) on \(V_t\) and linearly on \(k_n\); both spread widely between devices and with temperature, so \(I_D\) and \(V_D\) can move enough to push the device into triode.
  • With \(R_S\): \(V_{GS} = V_G - I_DR_S\). If \(I_D\) tries to rise (lower \(V_t\) or higher \(k_n\)), \(V_S\) rises, \(V_{GS}\) falls and pulls \(I_D\) back: negative feedback (degeneration).
  • Large \(R_S\) (with \(V_G \gg V_{GS}\)): \(I_D \approx (V_G - V_{GS})/R_S\), nearly independent of the device. Sketch: two \(I_D\)–\(V_{GS}\) curves (devices 1, 2) cut by the bias line \(I_D = (V_G - V_{GS})/R_S\); the steeper the line (small \(R_S\)) the larger the spread \(\Delta I_D\).
  • Cost: the drop across \(R_S\) eats headroom; AC gain is preserved by bypassing \(R_S\) with a capacitor.

Full method: Small-signal model and biasing

2024–25 · Sec A · Q4(c) · 8 marks

Explain the operating regions of an enhancement PMOS with properly labelled \(I_D\)–\(V_{SD}\) plots. Derive the threshold conditions for operation in these regions.

Answer key
  • Cutoff: \(V_{SG} < |V_{tp}|\): no inversion layer, \(I_D \approx 0\).
  • Triode: \(V_{SG} \ge |V_{tp}|\) and \(V_{SD} < V_{SG} - |V_{tp}|\): channel continuous; \(I_D = k_p'(W/L)\left[(V_{SG}-|V_{tp}|)V_{SD} - \tfrac12V_{SD}^2\right]\).
  • Saturation: \(V_{SD} \ge V_{SG} - |V_{tp}| = |V_{OV}|\): channel pinched off at the drain; \(I_D = \tfrac12k_p'(W/L)(V_{SG}-|V_{tp}|)^2(1 + |\lambda|V_{SD})\).
  • Boundary derivation: the hole channel exists where the gate-to-channel voltage exceeds \(|V_{tp}|\). At the source end this is \(V_{SG} > |V_{tp}|\); at the drain end, \(V_{DG} > |V_{tp}|\) is lost when \(V_{SD} = V_{SG} - |V_{tp}|\). Plot: a family of curves for increasing \(V_{SG}\), parabolic rise, flat beyond the dashed locus \(I_D = \tfrac12k_p'(W/L)V_{SD}^2\).

Full method: I–V, regions, channel-length modulation

Question 6

2024–25 · Sec B · Q6(a) · 20 marks

The small-signal gain of a BJT CE amplifier is \(A_v = -I_CR_C/V_T\) with \(i_C = I_Se^{v_{BE}/V_T}\) (no Early effect). Show that with \(i_C = I_Se^{v_{BE}/V_T}(1 + v_{CE}/V_A)\) the gain becomes \[ A_v = -\frac{I_CR_C/V_T}{1 + \dfrac{I_CR_C}{V_A + V_{CE}}} = -\frac{(V_{CC} - V_{CE})/V_T}{1 + \dfrac{V_{CC} - V_{CE}}{V_A + V_{CE}}}. \] For \(V_{CC}\) = 5 V and \(V_{CE}\) = 2.5 V, find the gain with and without the Early effect (\(V_A\) = 100 V).

Answer key

\(g_m = \partial i_C/\partial v_{BE} = I_C/V_T\) (unchanged). Output conductance: \(\dfrac{1}{r_o} = \dfrac{\partial i_C}{\partial v_{CE}} = \dfrac{I_Se^{V_{BE}/V_T}}{V_A} = \dfrac{I_C}{V_A + V_{CE}}\), so \(r_o = (V_A + V_{CE})/I_C\).

\(A_v = -g_m(R_C\parallel r_o) = -\dfrac{g_mR_C}{1 + R_C/r_o} = -\dfrac{I_CR_C/V_T}{1 + I_CR_C/(V_A + V_{CE})}\), and KVL on the collector gives \(I_CR_C = V_{CC} - V_{CE}\). ∎

Numbers: without Early: \(A_v = -2.5/0.025\) = −100 V/V. With: \(-100/(1 + 2.5/102.5)\) = −97.6 V/V (2.4 % lower).

Full method: I–V characteristics and the Early effect (type B-12)

(ii) What is the temperature rise per watt (thermal resistance)? (iii) What is the final power dissipated?

Answer key

(i) At constant current, \(V_D\) falls about 2 mV/°C: \(\Delta T = 0.1/0.002\) = 50 °C.

(iii) \(P = 15\times0.6\) = 9 W (final, steady state).

(ii) \(\theta = 50/9\) = 5.56 °C/W. Use the final power, which is the steady state that produced the 50 °C rise. (Using the initial 10.5 W would give 4.76 °C/W; the same choice tripped a senior solution of the 2023–24 version of this question.)
