Question 1

2015–16 · Sec A · Q1(a) · 15 marks

The circuit shown in Fig. for Q. No. 1(a) is a circuit for charging a 5 V battery. If \(v_s = 10\sin(100\pi t)\), find (i) the duty cycle (fraction of each cycle during which the diode conducts); (ii) draw the diode current waveform indicating the peak value; (iii) the maximum reverse-bias voltage that appears across the diode.

Answer key

Ideal diode: it conducts when \(v_s > 5\) V. \(f = 50\) Hz, \(T = 20\) ms.

  • (i) \(\sin\theta_1 = 5/10\): \(\theta_1 = 30°\), conduction from 30° to 150°, i.e. 120° of 360°: duty cycle = 1/3 (6.67 ms, from 1.67 ms to 8.33 ms in each period).
  • (ii) \(i_D = \dfrac{10\sin\omega t - 5}{100}\) during conduction: a pulse starting at 1.67 ms, peaking at \(i_{D,peak} = (10 - 5)/100 = 50\) mA at 5 ms, back to 0 at 8.33 ms; zero for the rest of the cycle.
  • (iii) The diode is reversed when \(v_s\) is most negative: \(V_{R,max} = 10 + 5 = \mathbf{15}\) V.

(With a 0.7 V drop: conduction 30.7 % of the cycle, peak 43 mA, PIV still 15 V.)

Full method: Rectifiers and battery chargers

2015–16 · Sec A · Q1(b) · 16⅔ marks

Draw the circuit diagram of a full-wave rectifier with a filter capacitor. Assume the input is a sinusoidal signal with 10 kHz frequency and 7.07 V rms voltage, the load is a 1 kΩ resistor and the capacitor used is 1 µF. The diode has a forward voltage drop of 0.7 V. Find (i) ripple voltage; (ii) PIV and duty cycle; (iii) peak output voltage.

Answer key

Circuit: bridge of four diodes fed by the source, with the capacitor C = 1 µF in parallel with \(R_L\) = 1 kΩ across the bridge output. \(V_m = 7.07\sqrt2 = 10\) V.

  • (iii) Peak output (two diodes in the path): \(V_p = 10 - 2(0.7) = \mathbf{8.6}\) V.
  • (i) Ripple for full wave: \(V_r = \dfrac{V_p}{2fRC} = \dfrac{8.6}{2\times10^4\times10^3\times10^{-6}} = \mathbf{0.43}\) V (exponential discharge: 0.419 V).
  • (ii) PIV (bridge) \(= V_m - V_D = \mathbf{9.3}\) V. Conduction angle \(\omega\Delta t = \sqrt{2V_r/V_p} = \sqrt{0.1} = 0.316\) rad, so each pair conducts for 5.03 % of a cycle per charging pulse (two pulses per cycle: duty cycle 5.03 % per diode, the capacitor is recharged for 10.1 % of the time).

If a centre-tapped transformer is drawn instead (10 V peak per half-winding, one diode drop): \(V_p = 9.3\) V, \(V_r = 0.465\) V, PIV = \(2V_m - V_D = 19.3\) V, same 5.03 % duty. State which circuit you drew.

Full method: Peak rectifier: ripple, conduction angle, diode current

2015–16 · Sec A · Q2(a) · 16⅔ marks

For the circuit shown in Fig. for Q. No. 2(a), neatly draw the output (\(v_o\)) waveform corresponding to the input (\(v_{in}\)). Here \(v_{in} = 10\sin(200\pi t)\) and the diodes are non-ideal.

2015–16 · Sec A · Q2(b) · 15 marks

For the circuit shown in Fig. for Q. No. 2(b), neatly draw the output waveform (\(v_o\)) corresponding to the input waveform (\(v_{in}\)) in steady state. Assume the diode is non-ideal.

\(v_{in}\): square wave ±5 V, period about 1 ms.

Answer key

Positive clamper. Time constants: diode on, \(\tau = RC = 10\) ms; diode off, \((R + R_L)C = 30\) ms; both ≫ T = 1 ms, so the capacitor voltage is nearly constant and the steady state follows from charge balance.

Let \(V_C\) be the capacitor voltage (input side minus output side). Negative half (\(v_{in} = -5\) V): the diode conducts, \(v_o = -0.7\) V, \(i = (-5 - V_C + 0.7)/R\). Positive half (\(v_{in} = +5\) V): the diode is off, \(i = (5 - V_C)/(R + R_L)\). Average capacitor current zero:

\[ \frac{5 - V_C}{3} + \frac{-4.3 - V_C}{1} = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; V_C = -1.975\ \text{V}. \]
  • Positive half: \(v_o = (5 + 1.975)\dfrac{2}{3} = \mathbf{+4.65}\) V (diode off; checks: reverse biased).
  • Negative half: \(v_o = \mathbf{-0.7}\) V, and the diode current is \(1.975\) mA \(> 0\) ✓.

So \(v_o\) is a square wave between −0.7 V and +4.65 V. The exact exponential steady state gives a tiny droop on the top (4.69 V to 4.61 V). The output swing (5.35 V) is smaller than 10 V because of the divider R, RL.

Full method: Clampers and voltage doublers

2015–16 · Sec A · Q3(a) · 15 marks

The CMOS inverter circuit shown in Fig. for Q. No. 3(a) has \(k_n'(W_n/L_n) = k_p'(W_p/L_p) = 1\) mA/V² and \(V_{tn} = -V_{tp} = 1\) V. Find (i) \(i_{DN}\), (ii) \(i_{DP}\), (iii) \(v_o\) for \(v_{in} = -2.5\) V.

Answer key

\(v_{in} = -2.5\) V: QN has \(v_{GS} = 0\): off, \(i_{DN} = 0\). QP: \(v_{SG} = 2.5 - (-2.5) = 5\) V, \(V_{OV} = 4\) V.

Try saturation: \(i_{DP} = \tfrac12(1)(4)^2 = 8\) mA, which would need \(v_o = 80\) V: impossible. So QP is in triode, with \(x = v_{SD} = 2.5 - v_o\):

\[ i_{DP} = 1\left[4x - \tfrac12x^2\right] = \frac{2.5 - x}{10} \;\Rightarrow\; 5x^2 - 41x + 2.5 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 0.0614\ \text{V}. \]

(The other root, 8.14 V, exceeds the 5 V available.) \(v_o = 2.5 - 0.0614 = \mathbf{2.439}\) V, \(i_{DP} = 2.439/10 = \mathbf{0.244}\) mA, \(i_{DN} = \mathbf{0}\). Check: \(x = 0.061 < V_{OV} = 4\), triode ✓.

2015–16 · Sec A · Q3(b) · 31⅔ marks

For the circuit shown in Fig. for Q. No. 3(b): (i) calculate the DC bias point (\(I_D\) and \(V_D\)); (ii) draw the small-signal equivalent circuit; (iii) calculate the small-signal voltage gain; (iv) find the input resistance; (v) determine the largest allowable input signal voltage. The transistor has \(V_t = 1.5\) V, \(k_n'(W/L) = 0.25\) mA/V² and \(V_A = 50\) V.

Answer key

(i) Bias. No DC gate current, so \(V_{GS} = V_D = 15 - 10I_D\) (λ ignored in DC). \(I_D = \tfrac12(0.25)(V_{GS} - 1.5)^2 = 0.125(13.5 - 10I_D)^2\) ⇒ \(12.5I_D^2 - 34.75I_D + 22.78 = 0\) ⇒ \(I_D = \mathbf{1.06}\) mA (the other root, 1.72 mA, gives \(V_{GS} < V_t\)). \(\mathbf{V_D = 4.41}\) V. \(V_{OV} = 2.91\) V, \(V_{DS} = 4.41 > V_{OV}\): saturation ✓.

(ii) Model. \(g_m = k_n'(W/L)V_{OV} = 0.25\times2.91 = 0.728\) mA/V, \(r_o = V_A/I_D = 50/1.06 = 47.2\) kΩ. Small-signal circuit: \(v_{gs} = v_i\) at the gate, \(g_mv_{gs}\) from drain to source in parallel with \(r_o\), \(R_D\), \(R_L\) at the drain, and \(R_G\) bridging gate and drain.

(iii) Gain. \(A_v = \dfrac{v_o}{v_i} \approx -g_m(r_o\parallel R_D\parallel R_L) = -0.728\times4.52 = \mathbf{-3.29}\) V/V (the exact expression \(-(g_m - 1/R_G)/(1/r_o + 1/R_D + 1/R_L + 1/R_G)\) differs in the fourth digit).

(iv) Input resistance (Miller, \(R_G\) bridges input and output): \(R_{in} = \dfrac{R_G}{1 - A_v} = \dfrac{10}{4.29} = \mathbf{2.33}\) MΩ.

(v) Largest input. Saturation needs \(v_{DS} \ge v_{GS} - V_t\), i.e. \(v_o - v_i \ge -V_t\) (the DC parts cancel). With \(v_o = A_vv_i\): \(\hat v_i(1 + |A_v|) \le V_t\) ⇒ \(\hat v_i \le \dfrac{1.5}{4.29} = \mathbf{0.35}\) V peak (Sedra quotes 0.34 V).

Full method: Feedback-biased CS, CG and CD

Question 5

2015–16 · Sec B · Q5(a) · 20⅔ marks

For the circuit shown in Fig. for Q. No. 5(a), (i) determine the output voltage \(V_{out}\) in terms of the input voltage \(V_{in}\); (ii) using this circuit perform the operation \(V_{out} = \sqrt{V_{in}}\). Use additional op-amps and DC voltage sources if necessary. Specify the values of all parameters used for this design. (Threshold voltage \(V_{TH}\) is 0.5 V for the MOSFET M₁.)

Answer key

(i) The − input is a virtual ground, so the current \(V_{in}/R\) (for \(V_{in} > 0\)) flows into M₁'s drain. \(V_{GD} = 0 - 0 = 0 < V_{TH}\): M₁ is always saturated. \(V_{GS} = 0 - V_{out} = -V_{out}\). With \(i_D = K(V_{GS} - V_{TH})^2\), \(K = \tfrac12k_n'W/L\):

\[ \frac{V_{in}}{R} = K(-V_{out} - 0.5)^2 \;\Rightarrow\; V_{out} = -0.5 - \sqrt{\frac{V_{in}}{KR}}. \]

A square-root amplifier with an offset of −0.5 V (\(V_{out}\) must be ≤ −0.5 V, so \(V_{GS} > V_{TH}\)).

(ii) Choose \(K = 1\) mA/V² (assumed) and \(R = 1\) kΩ so that \(KR = 1\) V⁻¹: \(V_{out1} = -0.5 - \sqrt{V_{in}}\). Add a second op-amp, an inverting summer with all resistors 10 kΩ, whose inputs are \(V_{out1}\) and a +0.5 V DC source:

\[ V_{out} = -(V_{out1} + 0.5) = \sqrt{V_{in}}. \]

Valid for \(0 < V_{in}\) up to the point where \(V_{out1}\) reaches −15 V (\(V_{in} \approx 210\) V²). If K differs, choose \(R = 1/K\) (in V⁻¹) to keep \(KR = 1\).

Full method: Log, antilog and nonlinear circuits

2015–16 · Sec B · Q5(b) · 14 marks

Design a circuit using a single op-amp that performs \(V_o = 3(V_1 - V_2)\), where \(V_1\) and \(V_2\) are input signals (AC or DC). Specify values of all resistors used.

Answer key

Difference amplifier: \(V_2\) through \(R_1\) to the − input, \(R_2\) from output to −, \(V_1\) through \(R_3\) to +, \(R_4\) from + to ground. \(V_o = -\dfrac{R_2}{R_1}V_2 + \left(1 + \dfrac{R_2}{R_1}\right)\dfrac{R_4}{R_3 + R_4}V_1\). Gain 3: \(R_1 = R_3 = 10\) kΩ, \(R_2 = R_4 = 30\) kΩ. Check: \(\left(1 + 3\right)\dfrac{30}{10 + 30} = 3\) ✓.

2015–16 · Sec B · Q5(c) · 12 marks

Input signal \(V_{in}\) and output signal \(V_{out}\) of a circuit are given in Fig. for Q. No. 5(c). Design the circuit using op-amps (show necessary calculations).

\(V_{in}\): triangle between 0 and 4 V, rising 0 → 4 V in 2 ms and falling 4 → 0 V in 2 ms (period 4 ms). \(V_{out}\): square wave, +2 V for 0–2 ms, −2 V for 2–4 ms, repeating.

Answer key

The output switches at the triangle's peaks and is +2 V while the input rises and −2 V while it falls, so it is proportional to the slope: \(\dfrac{dV_{in}}{dt} = \pm\dfrac{4\text{ V}}{2\text{ ms}} = \pm2\) V/ms = ±2000 V/s. A differentiator gives \(-RC\,dV_{in}/dt\); we need magnitude 2 V: \(RC\times2000 = 2\) ⇒ \(RC = 1\) ms.

  • Differentiator: C = 0.1 µF, R = 10 kΩ (with a small series resistor at the input and a small capacitor across R for stability): output −2 V while rising, +2 V while falling.
  • Unity inverter (10 kΩ / 10 kΩ): output +2 V while rising and −2 V while falling ✓. (Or feed the differentiator from an inverted input.)

A comparator at 2 V would not work: it switches when \(V_{in}\) crosses 2 V (at 1 ms), not at the peak.

Full method: Integrator and differentiator

Question 6

2015–16 · Sec B · Q6(a) · 26⅔ marks

You have a sinusoidal input voltage \(V_{in}(t) = \sin(4000\pi t)\) V. Design a frequency-multiplier circuit using op-amps that multiplies the input frequency by 2 to produce \(V_{out}(t) = \sin(8000\pi t)\) V. Use resistors, capacitors, inductors and diodes (reverse saturation current \(10^{-6}\) A) if necessary. Show necessary calculations and specify all design parameters.

Answer key

Idea. \(\sin^2\theta = \tfrac12(1 - \cos2\theta)\) with \(\theta = 4000\pi t\). Square the input, remove the DC, then turn \(\cos\) into \(\sin\) with an integrator. A diode log amp accepts only positive input, but squaring does not care about sign, so rectify first.

  1. Precision full-wave rectifier (two op-amps, diodes, 10 kΩ resistors): \(x = |\sin\theta|\).
  2. Log amp (R = 100 kΩ, diode in feedback): \(v_L = -V_T\ln\dfrac{x}{I_SR} = -V_T\ln\dfrac{x}{0.1}\).
  3. Inverting amplifier, gain 2 (10 kΩ, 20 kΩ): \(v_A = -2v_L = V_T\ln\dfrac{x^2}{(I_SR)^2}\).
  4. Antilog amp (diode at the input, \(R_f\)): \(v_B = -R_fI_Se^{v_A/V_T} = -\dfrac{R_f}{I_SR^2}x^2\). Choose \(R_f = I_SR^2 = 10^{-6}\times10^{10} = 10\) kΩ: \(v_B = -x^2 = -\sin^2\theta = -\tfrac12 + \tfrac12\cos2\theta\).
  5. Summing integrator, equal input resistors Ra = 19.9 kΩ for \(v_B\) and for a +0.5 V DC source, C = 1 nF (\(RC = 19.9\ \mu\)s): input \(v_B + 0.5 = \tfrac12\cos 2\theta\), \(\omega_2 = 8000\pi\). \(v_I = -\dfrac{1}{RC}\int\tfrac12\cos(\omega_2t)\,dt = -\dfrac{1}{2RC\,\omega_2}\sin\omega_2t\). With \(RC = \dfrac{1}{2\omega_2} = \dfrac{1}{16000\pi}\): \(v_I = -\sin(8000\pi t)\).
  6. Unity inverter (10 kΩ/10 kΩ): \(V_{out} = +\sin(8000\pi t)\) ✓.

Seven op-amps (2 in the rectifier, log, ×2 amplifier, antilog, integrator, inverter). Practical notes: a large resistor across the integrator capacitor stops DC drift, and the DC input must cancel the 0.5 V exactly; the diode circuits need a fixed temperature.

Question-bank answer incomplete

The Gemini question-bank answer multiplies sin and cos with log amps without rectifying (a diode log amp fails for negative input) and takes cos from an uncharged integrator, which gives cos − 1, a spurious DC term. Use the squaring route above, or a differentiator for the cos.

2015–16 · Sec B · Q6(b) · 20 marks

Design an analog computer circuit to solve, for \(t > 0\): \(\dfrac{d^2v}{dt^2} + 3\dfrac{dv}{dt} + 2v + 4\cos10t = 0\), with initial conditions \(v(0) = 2\), \(v'(0) = 0\). An oscillator is available to provide a \(\sin10t\) signal.

Answer key

\(v'' = -(3v' + 2v + 4\cos10t)\). Time scale 1 (integrators with \(RC = 1\) s: 1 MΩ, 1 µF).

  • Summer (\(R_f = 100\) kΩ): inputs \(+v'\) through 33.3 kΩ (×3), \(+v\) through 50 kΩ (×2), \(+\cos10t\) through 25 kΩ (×4). Output \(= -(3v' + 2v + 4\cos10t) = v''\).
  • Integrator 1: input \(v''\) → output \(-v'\). Capacitor initially 0, so \(v'(0) = 0\).
  • Inverter (10 kΩ/10 kΩ): \(-v' \to +v'\), fed back to the summer.
  • Integrator 2: input \(-v'\) → output \(+v\). Its capacitor is pre-charged so that its output at \(t = 0\) is +2 V: \(v(0) = 2\).
  • cos 10t from the oscillator: differentiator on \(\sin10t\) with \(RC = 0.1\) s (C = 1 µF, R = 100 kΩ): output \(-RC\cdot10\cos10t = -\cos10t\). A unity inverter makes it \(+\cos10t\), which goes to the 25 kΩ input. (For an oscillator amplitude of 1 V.)

Six op-amps (summer, 2 integrators, 2 inverters, differentiator). Output \(v(t)\) is at integrator 2. Switch closed on all initial-condition switches for \(t < 0\), released at \(t = 0\).

The Gemini answer makes cos10t from sin10t with an integrator, which carries a constant of integration: an uncharged auxiliary integrator gives \(\cos10t - 1\) and adds a false forcing of −4. Pre-charge it to +1 V, or use the differentiator above.

Question 7

2015–16 · Sec B · Q7(a) · 26 marks

For the oscillator circuit given in Fig. for Q. No. 7(a): (i) derive the expression for the oscillation frequency \(f_0\); (ii) obtain the relationship between \(R_1\) and \(R_2\) for sustained oscillation; (iii) specify the bias conditions and values of L, \(R_1\), \(R_2\) to obtain a 20 V peak-to-peak, 500 kHz sinusoidal signal from this oscillator.

Answer key

Feedback network \(\beta = v_P/v_O\): \(Z_X = R_1\parallel(R_2 + sL)\) (from X to ground); \(\dfrac{v_X}{v_O} = \dfrac{Z_X}{sL + Z_X}\); \(\dfrac{v_P}{v_X} = \dfrac{sL}{R_2 + sL}\). Multiplying:

\[ \beta(s) = \frac{sLR_1}{s^2L^2 + sL(2R_1 + R_2) + R_1R_2} \;\Rightarrow\; \frac1\beta = 2 + \frac{R_2}{R_1} + j\left(\frac{\omega L}{R_1} - \frac{R_2}{\omega L}\right). \]
  • (i) Phase = 0: \(\omega_0L/R_1 = R_2/(\omega_0L)\) ⇒ \(\omega_0 = \dfrac{\sqrt{R_1R_2}}{L}\), \(f_0 = \dfrac{\sqrt{R_1R_2}}{2\pi L}\).
  • (ii) Magnitude: \(\beta(\omega_0) = \dfrac{1}{2 + R_2/R_1}\). Amplifier gain \(A = 1 + 40/20 = 3\). \(A\beta \ge 1\) ⇒ \(3 \ge 2 + R_2/R_1\) ⇒ \(R_1 \ge R_2\) (equality for sustained oscillation, slightly above for start-up; a limiter then sets the amplitude).
  • (iii) Design. 20 V p-p means 10 V peak, so the op-amp supplies must be at least ±12 V (use \(\pm V_{bias} = \pm15\) V) and the op-amp's gain–bandwidth well above 500 kHz × 3. Choose L = 1 mH: for \(f_0 = 500\) kHz, \(\sqrt{R_1R_2} = 2\pi f_0L = 3.14\) kΩ. Take \(R_2 = 3\) kΩ, \(R_1 = 3.3\) kΩ: \(\sqrt{R_1R_2} = 3.146\) kΩ, \(f_0 = 500.7\) kHz, \(\beta = 1/2.909\) so \(A\beta = 1.03 > 1\) ✓ (start-up margin 3 %).

For the RC phase-shift oscillator shown in Fig. for Q. No. 7(b): (i) determine the oscillation frequency \(f_0\) and obtain the relationship between \(R_i\) and \(R_f\) for sustained oscillation; (ii) how can you generate a 100 kHz signal from this oscillator?

Fig. for Q. 7(b): A1 (±15 V) is an inverting amplifier (Rf from output to the − input, Ri from the feedback node Vf to the − input, + input grounded). The output Vo feeds three RC low-pass sections (R in series, C to ground), each followed by a voltage follower; the third section's node Vf returns through Ri. All R equal, all C equal.

Answer key

Ideal reading (followers isolate the sections, last section unloaded, \(R_i \gg R\)): each section \(\dfrac{1}{1 + sRC}\). At \(\omega RC = \sqrt3\) each section gives −60° and magnitude \(1/2\); three give −180° and \(1/8\); the inverting amplifier adds 180°.

  • (i) \(f_0 = \dfrac{\sqrt3}{2\pi RC}\); \(|A\beta| = \dfrac{R_f}{R_i}\cdot\dfrac18 \ge 1\) ⇒ \(R_f \ge 8R_i\).
  • Exact with the figure (the last section is loaded by \(R_i\) into the virtual ground): \(L(s) = -\dfrac{R_f}{R_i}\cdot\dfrac{1}{(1 + sRC)^2\,(1 + R/R_i + sRC)}\). Phase 180° at \(f_0 = \dfrac{\sqrt{3 + 2R/R_i}}{2\pi RC}\); sustain condition \(R_f = 8R_i + 8R + \dfrac{2R^2}{R_i}\). For \(R_i = R\): \(f_0 = \dfrac{\sqrt5}{2\pi RC}\), \(R_f = 18R\).

With \(R_i = R\): \(R = \dfrac{\sqrt5}{2\pi\times10^5\times10^{-10}} = 35.6\) kΩ, \(R_i = 35.6\) kΩ, \(R_f = 640\) kΩ. The op-amp's gain–bandwidth must be well above 100 kHz (a few MHz or more).

2015–16 · Sec B · Q8(a) · 23⅔ marks

For the filter circuit given in Fig. for Q. No. 8(a), determine (i) its type and comment on its order; derive the transfer function of the circuit and find the cutoff frequency; (ii) if R = 10 kΩ and C = 0.1 µF, would it be possible to pass a 100 Hz signal through this filter without distortion? Justify. Note that the first op-amp block performs summation of the input and output signals.

Answer key

A1 (inverting summer, feedback 2R): \(x = -2R\left[\dfrac{v_{in}}{1/sC} + \dfrac{V_o}{5R}\right] = -2R\left[sC\,v_{in} + \dfrac{V_o}{5R}\right]\).

A2 (inverting integrator, input R, feedback 2C): \(V_o = -\dfrac{x}{sR(2C)} = \dfrac{2R\,[sCv_{in} + V_o/(5R)]}{2sRC} = v_{in} + \dfrac{V_o}{5sRC}\).

\[ H(s) = \frac{V_o}{v_{in}} = \frac{1}{1 - \dfrac{1}{5sRC}} = \frac{s}{s - \dfrac{1}{5RC}} \]
  • (i) Type: first order (one pole), high-pass form: \(H \to 0\) as \(s \to 0\) and \(H \to 1\) as \(s \to \infty\). Corner: \(\omega_c = \dfrac{1}{5RC}\), \(f_c = \dfrac{1}{10\pi RC}\).
  • (ii) With R = 10 kΩ, C = 0.1 µF: \(RC = 1\) ms, \(\omega_c = 200\) rad/s, \(f_c = 31.8\) Hz. Reading it as a first-order high-pass filter, 100 Hz is above \(f_c\): \(|H| = \dfrac{\omega}{\sqrt{\omega^2 + \omega_c^2}} = \dfrac{628.3}{\sqrt{628.3^2 + 200^2}} = 0.953\) (−0.42 dB), phase lead about 17.7°. A single tone passes with small attenuation and a phase shift, and no waveform distortion (a pure sine stays a sine). Yes (for a multi-tone signal the different phase shifts would change its shape).

Remark on the circuit as drawn: the pole is at \(s = +1/(5RC)\), in the right half-plane (two inverting stages plus the 5R path give positive feedback), so the real circuit is unstable and latches to a rail. The intended answer is the stable first-order high-pass reading; mention both.

In the circuit shown in Fig. for Q. No. 8(b), input voltage \(v_{in}(t) = 5 + 3\cos(300\pi t - 30°) + 8\sin(1000\pi t) + 7\sin(1600\pi t + 45°)\) V. Design the active filter blocks and summing amplifier to obtain output \(v_{out}(t) = 7 + 12\sin(1000\pi t)\) V. Sketch the frequency response and specify the cutoff frequency of each filter. Also specify all values of resistors, capacitors/inductors used.

Fig. for Q. 8(b): block diagram: vin feeds Filter 1 and Filter 2 in parallel; both feed a summing amplifier, which gives vout.

Answer key

Components of \(v_{in}\): DC (5 V), 150 Hz (3 V), 500 Hz (8 V), 800 Hz (7 V). Keep DC with gain \(7/5 = 1.4\) and 500 Hz with gain \(12/8 = 1.5\); remove 150 Hz and 800 Hz. Use inverting filters and an inverting unity summer (the two inversions cancel).

  • Filter 1: low-pass, \(|A| = 1.4\): \(R_i = 10\) kΩ, \(R_f = 14\) kΩ in parallel with \(C_f = 1\ \mu\)F. \(f_c = \dfrac{1}{2\pi R_fC_f} = 11.4\) Hz (well below 150 Hz). Output for the DC part: −1.4 × 5 = −7 V.
  • Filter 2: band-pass at 500 Hz, \(|A_0| = 1.5\) (multiple-feedback), \(C_1 = C_2 = 0.1\ \mu\)F, \(Q = 5\) (band 452–552 Hz): \(R_3 = \dfrac{2Q}{\omega_0C} = 31.8\) kΩ, \(R_1 = \dfrac{R_3}{2A_0} = 10.6\) kΩ, \(R_2 = \dfrac{Q}{(2Q^2 - A_0)\omega_0C} = 328\ \Omega\) (\(\omega_0 = 1000\pi\)). Output: −1.5 × 8 sin = −12 sin(1000πt).
  • Summer: inverting, all resistors 10 kΩ: \(v_{out} = 7 + 12\sin(1000\pi t)\) ✓.

Responses: LPF flat at gain 1.4 (3 dB at 11.4 Hz, −20 dB/dec above); BPF peak 1.5 at 500 Hz, half-power points 452 and 552 Hz.

Residuals: first-order and Q = 5 filters leave a little unwanted signal (about 0.3 V of 150 Hz through each filter and 2 V of 800 Hz through the band-pass). Use higher-order filters or a higher Q to reduce this; first-order idealised filters are what is expected at exam level.
