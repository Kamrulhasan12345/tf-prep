Question 1

2022–23 · Sec A · Q1(a) · 14 marks · compulsory · CO1

For the circuit given in Fig. for Q. 1(a), plot \(v_o\) vs time. The input \(v_I\) is a sine wave with 5 V amplitude and 1 kHz frequency. Assume 0.7 V voltage drop across diodes in forward bias.

Answer key

Breakpoints. D₁ conducts when \(v_o > 1 + 0.7 = 1.7\) V. D₂ conducts when \(v_o < -2 - 0.7 = -2.7\) V. Between them no diode current flows, so no drop in the 5 kΩ: \(v_o = v_I\).

D₁ on (\(v_I > 1.7\) V): D₁ branch = 1.7 V battery behind 10 kΩ. Divider: \(v_o = 1.7 + (v_I - 1.7)\dfrac{10}{5+10} = \tfrac{2}{3}v_I + 0.567\). At the peak \(v_I = 5\) V: \(v_o = 3.9\) V.

D₂ on (\(v_I < -2.7\) V): −2.7 V behind 5 kΩ. \(v_o = -2.7 + (v_I + 2.7)\dfrac{5}{5+5} = 0.5v_I - 1.35\). At \(v_I = -5\) V: \(v_o = -3.85\) V.

Plot (one period, T = 1 ms): \(v_o\) follows the sine up to 1.7 V at t = 0.055 ms, rises more slowly (slope 2/3) to a rounded top of 3.9 V at 0.25 ms, and comes back to 1.7 V at 0.445 ms. It follows the sine down to −2.7 V at t = 0.591 ms, flattens (slope 1/2) to a bottom of −3.85 V at 0.75 ms, and rejoins the sine at 0.909 ms. These are soft limits, not flat clipping.

Common mark-loser

A circulating scan has a pencil sketch with vo flat at +1.7 V and −2.7 V. That ignores the 10 kΩ and 5 kΩ branch resistors. The correct peaks are +3.9 V and −3.85 V.

2022–23 · Sec A · Q1(b) · 14 marks · compulsory · CO2

For a Common Base Amplifier, using appropriate small signal equivalent model, derive the expressions of input resistance, output resistance, open circuit voltage gain and overall voltage gain.

Answer key

Draw the CB stage: \(v_{sig}\) with \(R_{sig}\) into the emitter (through a coupling capacitor), base at signal ground, \(R_C\) from collector to AC ground, load \(R_L\) coupled to the collector. Replace the BJT by its T model (\(r_o\) neglected):

  • Input resistance. The base is grounded, so \(r_e\) is connected between E and ground: \(i_e = -v_i/r_e\). \(R_{in} = v_i/(-i_e) = r_e\) (with an emitter bias resistor: \(R_E \parallel r_e \approx r_e\)). Low, tens of ohms.
  • Open-circuit voltage gain. \(i_c = \alpha i_e = -\alpha v_i/r_e\); \(v_o = -i_cR_C\), so \(A_{vo} = \dfrac{v_o}{v_i} = \dfrac{\alpha R_C}{r_e} = g_mR_C\). Positive (non-inverting).
  • Output resistance. Set \(v_{sig} = 0\): \(i_e = 0\), so \(\alpha i_e = 0\) (open). \(R_o = R_C\) (\(R_C \parallel r_o\) if \(r_o\) is kept).
  • Loaded gain. \(A_v = \dfrac{\alpha (R_C\parallel R_L)}{r_e} = g_m(R_C\parallel R_L)\).
  • Overall gain. \(v_i = v_{sig}\dfrac{r_e}{R_{sig}+r_e}\), so \(G_v = \dfrac{r_e}{R_{sig}+r_e}\cdot\dfrac{\alpha(R_C\parallel R_L)}{r_e} = \dfrac{\alpha(R_C\parallel R_L)}{R_{sig}+r_e}\).
  • Comment: current gain ≈ α ≈ 1; because \(R_{in}\) is tiny, \(G_v\) is small unless \(R_{sig}\) is small. Used as a current buffer and at high frequency (no Miller effect).

Full method: Common base and emitter follower

2022–23 · Sec A · Q1(c) · 7 marks · compulsory · CO1

Briefly explain the I-V characteristics of an n-channel enhancement MOSFET with necessary plots.

Answer key
  • Transfer curve \(i_D\)–\(v_{GS}\) (in saturation): \(i_D = 0\) for \(v_{GS} < V_t\) (no channel); above \(V_t\), \(i_D = \tfrac12 k_n'(W/L)(v_{GS}-V_t)^2\), a parabola.
  • Output curves \(i_D\)–\(v_{DS}\), one per \(v_{GS}\): triode for \(v_{DS} < v_{OV}\): \(i_D = k_n'(W/L)[(v_{GS}-V_t)v_{DS} - \tfrac12 v_{DS}^2]\); near the origin it is a resistor \(r_{DS} = 1/[k_n'(W/L)V_{OV}]\).
  • Saturation for \(v_{DS} \ge v_{OV}\): the channel pinches off at the drain end and \(i_D\) stops rising (flat lines). The boundary is the parabola \(i_D = \tfrac12 k_n'(W/L)v_{DS}^2\).
  • Channel-length modulation: real saturation lines slope upward, \(i_D \propto (1+\lambda v_{DS})\); extended back they meet at \(v_{DS} = -V_A = -1/\lambda\); \(r_o = V_A/I_D\).
  • Plots: the parabola-shaped transfer curve starting at \(V_t\), and the family of output curves with the dashed triode/saturation boundary.

Full method: I–V, regions, channel-length modulation

The NMOS and PMOS transistors in the circuit of Fig. for Q. 2(a) are matched with \(k_n = k_p\) = 1 mA/V² and \(V_{tn} = -V_{tp}\) = 1 V. Assuming λ = 0 for both MOSFETs, find the drain currents \(i_{DN}\) and \(i_{DP}\) and the voltage \(v_O\) for \(v_I\) = 0 V and +2.5 V.

2022–23 · Sec A · Q3(b) · 17 marks

In the circuit shown in Fig. for Q. 3(b), the NMOS transistor has \(|V_t|\) = 0.9 V and \(V_A\) = 50 V and operated with \(V_D\) = 2 V. What is the voltage gain \(v_o/v_i\)? What do \(V_D\) and the gain become for I = 1 mA?

Answer key

Bias at I = 0.5 mA. No DC gate current, so \(V_{GS} = V_D = 2\) V, \(V_{OV} = 1.1\) V. \(k_n = 2I/V_{OV}^2 = 1/1.21 = 0.826\) mA/V² (CLM ignored in DC, as in Sedra). \(g_m = 2I/V_{OV} = 0.909\) mA/V, \(r_o = V_A/I = 100\) kΩ.

Gain. KCL at the drain: \(\dfrac{v_i - v_o}{R_G} = g_mv_i + v_o\left(\dfrac1{r_o} + \dfrac1{R_L}\right)\) ⇒ \(\dfrac{v_o}{v_i} = -\dfrac{g_m - 1/R_G}{1/r_o + 1/R_L + 1/R_G} \approx -g_m(r_o\parallel R_L) = -0.909\times 9.09 = \mathbf{-8.26}\) V/V (Sedra: −8.3).

At I = 1 mA. Same device: \(V_{OV} = \sqrt{2\times1/0.826} = 1.556\) V, so \(\mathbf{V_D = V_{GS} = 0.9 + 1.556 = 2.46}\) V. \(g_m = 2/1.556 = 1.286\) mA/V, \(r_o = 50/1 = 50\) kΩ, \(r_o\parallel R_L = 8.33\) kΩ. Gain \(\approx -1.286\times 8.33 = \mathbf{-10.7}\) V/V.

Correct: VD = 2.46 V, ro = 50 kΩ, Av ≈ −10.7. Its I = 0.5 mA answer of −7.95 comes from kn = 0.795 (with the (1 + VDS/VA) factor) but gm = knVOV without it. That is inconsistent: gm = 2I/VOV = 0.909 mA/V, which gives −8.26.

A full-wave bridge rectifier circuit with a 1 kΩ load operates from a 120 V (rms) 60 Hz household supply through a 10-to-1 step-down transformer having a single secondary winding. It uses four diodes, each of which can be modeled to have a 0.7 V drop in forward bias. What is the peak value of the rectified voltage across the load? Answer key

Secondary: 12 V rms, \(V_s = 12\sqrt2 = 16.97\) V. Two diodes conduct in series in each half-cycle.

2022–23 · Sec A · Q4(c) · 7 marks

Describe different models for diode forward characteristics.

Answer key
  • Exponential model: \(i_D = I_S(e^{v_D/nV_T} - 1)\). Most accurate; needs iteration or a graph to solve circuits.
  • Ideal diode: short when forward biased (\(v_D = 0\), \(i_D > 0\)), open when reverse biased. Good when circuit voltages ≫ 0.7 V.
  • Constant-voltage-drop (CVD): \(v_D = V_{D0} \approx 0.7\) V when on, open when off. The usual exam model.
  • Piecewise-linear (battery plus resistance): \(v_D = V_{D0} + i_Dr_D\) for \(v_D \ge V_{D0}\) (e.g. \(V_{D0}\) = 0.65 V, \(r_D\) = 20 Ω): a straight-line fit to the exponential.
  • Small-signal model: about a bias point \(I_D\), \(r_d = nV_T/I_D\), valid for signals of a few mV.
  • Sketch each one's \(i_D\)–\(v_D\) line next to the exponential curve.

Full method: Diode models: ideal, CVD, piecewise linear

2022–23 · Sec B · Q5(b) · 20 marks · compulsory · CO3

Draw and explain the operation of a triangular wave generator circuit using OP Amps and Schmitt trigger. Derive the frequency of oscillation for the circuit.

Answer key

Circuit: a non-inverting Schmitt trigger (A1: R₂ from the triangle output to node P, R₃ from the square output to P, − input grounded) drives an inverting integrator (A2: R₁, C). The integrator output feeds back to the Schmitt.

  • Thresholds: A1 switches when \(v_P = 0\): \(v_{tri}/R_2 + v_{sq}/R_3 = 0\). With \(v_{sq} = \pm L\): \(V_{TH} = +L\dfrac{R_2}{R_3}\), \(V_{TL} = -L\dfrac{R_2}{R_3}\); triangle peak-to-peak \(= 2L R_2/R_3\).
  • Operation: with \(v_{sq} = +L\) the integrator output ramps down at slope \(L/(R_1C)\) until it reaches \(V_{TL}\); A1 flips to −L and the ramp reverses upward until \(V_{TH}\); and so on. A1 gives the square wave, A2 the triangle.
  • Half period: \(\dfrac{T}{2} = \dfrac{V_{TH}-V_{TL}}{L/(R_1C)} = 2R_1C\dfrac{R_2}{R_3}\).
  • Frequency: \(T = 4R_1C\dfrac{R_2}{R_3}\), \(f_0 = \dfrac{R_3}{4R_1R_2C}\) (independent of L).
  • Draw the two waveforms one under the other: square ±L, triangle between ±LR₂/R₃, flips at the triangle peaks.

Full method: Astable, monostable, square–triangle generators

Question 7

2022–23 · Sec B · Q7(a) · 20 marks

Derive the expression of oscillation frequency for a Colpitts oscillator. Then derive the conditions for Barkhausen criteria to be satisfied.

Answer key

Circuit: a CS (or CE) amplifier with load R (R includes \(r_o\) and the load); tank: C₁ from drain to ground, C₂ from gate to ground, L from drain to gate. Small-signal model: \(g_mV_{gs}\) source in parallel with R; the gate draws no current.

  • Gate node: current in C₂ is \(sC_2V_{gs}\); the same current flows through L, so \(V_d = V_{gs}(1 + s^2LC_2)\).
  • Drain KCL: \(sC_2V_{gs} + g_mV_{gs} + \left(\dfrac1R + sC_1\right)V_d = 0\).
  • Substitute (\(V_{gs} \ne 0\) for oscillation): \(s^3LC_1C_2 + s^2\dfrac{LC_2}{R} + s(C_1 + C_2) + \left(g_m + \dfrac1R\right) = 0\).
  • Put \(s = j\omega\). Imaginary part = 0: \(\omega(C_1 + C_2) - \omega^3LC_1C_2 = 0\) ⇒ \(\omega_0 = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{L\,\dfrac{C_1C_2}{C_1 + C_2}}}\).
  • Real part = 0: \(g_m + \dfrac1R - \omega_0^2\dfrac{LC_2}{R} = 0\). With \(\omega_0^2LC_2 = 1 + C_2/C_1\): \(\dfrac{C_2}{C_1} = g_mR\).
  • Barkhausen: loop gain \(A\beta = 1\) at \(\omega_0\) (phase 360°, magnitude 1). To start, make \(g_mR\) slightly larger than \(C_2/C_1\); the amplitude then grows until nonlinearity reduces the gain to exactly \(C_2/C_1\).

Full method: Colpitts, Hartley and crystal oscillators

Question 8

2022–23 · Sec B · Q8(a) · 20 marks

Using op amps, design a circuit to solve the following differential equation: \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dt^2} - 2\dfrac{dy}{dt} + 2y = 8x\), where x is input and y is output.

Answer key

Rearrange: \(y'' = 2y' - 2y + 8x\). Use integrators with RC = 1 s (1 MΩ, 1 µF), so each gives \(-\int(\cdot)\,dt\).

  • Summer (A0, \(R_f\) = 100 kΩ): inputs \(V_1 = y'\) through 50 kΩ (×2), \(V_2 = -y\) through 50 kΩ (×2), x through 12.5 kΩ (×8). Output \(V_0 = -(2y' - 2y + 8x) = -y''\) ✓.
  • Integrator 1 (A1): input \(-y''\) ⇒ output \(+y' = V_1\).
  • Integrator 2 (A2): input \(y'\) ⇒ output \(-y = V_2\).
  • Inverter (A3, 10 kΩ/10 kΩ): output \(+y\).

Note: the characteristic roots are 1 ± j, so the equation is unstable and the real circuit's output grows until the op-amps saturate. The design still implements the equation as asked.

2022–23 · Sec B · Q8(b) · 15 marks

Suppose you have two inputs \(v_1\) and \(v_2\). Design a subtractor circuit using op amps, so the output voltage is \(v_o = v_1 - v_2\).

Answer key

Difference amplifier: \(v_2\) through \(R_1\) to the − input, \(R_2\) from − to the output, \(v_1\) through \(R_3\) to the + input, \(R_4\) from + to ground.

Superposition: \(v_o = -\dfrac{R_2}{R_1}v_2 + \left(1 + \dfrac{R_2}{R_1}\right)\dfrac{R_4}{R_3 + R_4}v_1\). With \(R_4/R_3 = R_2/R_1\): \(v_o = \dfrac{R_2}{R_1}(v_1 - v_2)\). Choose all four equal (10 kΩ): \(v_o = v_1 - v_2\) ✓.

Full method: Summers, difference amplifier, V-to-I
