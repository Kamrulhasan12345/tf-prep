Structure and Modes of Operation

Every BJT number in this course rests on one page of facts: the structure, the three modes, \(i_C = I_Se^{v_{BE}/V_T}\) and the α–β relations. Pure theory questions on it appear too: the pnp energy-band diagram (2018–19, 23 marks; repeated 2020–21, 16 marks) and "why a third terminal" (2019–20, 10⅔ marks). Learn the mode table here; Chapter 3 turns it into the assume-and-verify method.

1. Structure

An npn transistor is an n-type emitter, a thin p-type base and an n-type collector, giving two junctions: the emitter–base junction (EBJ) and the collector–base junction (CBJ). A pnp is the mirror (p–n–p). Symbol: the arrow is on the emitter and points in the direction of conventional current (out of an npn, into a pnp).

Doping
Emitter > Collector > Base. A heavily doped emitter injects many carriers; a lightly doped base keeps the reverse (base→emitter) injection and recombination small.
Width
Collector > Emitter > Base. The base is very thin so injected carriers cross it before recombining. The collector is large to dissipate power.
Not symmetric
Emitter and collector cannot be swapped. Used backwards ("reverse-active") the device has a tiny \(\beta_R\) and no practical use.

2. Active mode: how the current flows

npn, EBJ forward biased (\(v_{BE} \approx 0.7\) V), CBJ reverse biased. Electrons are injected from the emitter into the base, diffuse across the thin base, and are swept into the collector by the CBJ field: this is \(i_C\), and it depends only on \(v_{BE}\), not on \(v_{CB}\). The base current has two small parts: holes injected from the base into the emitter (\(i_{B1}\)) and holes supplied to replace electrons that recombine in the base (\(i_{B2}\)).

\[ i_C = I_Se^{v_{BE}/V_T},\quad i_B = \frac{i_C}{\beta},\quad i_E = i_C + i_B = \frac{i_C}{\alpha},\quad \alpha = \frac{\beta}{\beta+1},\quad \beta = \frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha},\quad i_E = (\beta+1)i_B \]

\(V_T = kT/q \approx 25\) mV at room temperature (Sadat sir's notes use 25.9 mV). \(I_S\) is proportional to the EBJ area. For a pnp replace \(v_{BE}\) by \(v_{EB}\); currents flow into the emitter and out of the base and collector. Two consequences of the exponential: \(v_{BE}\) changes by \(V_T\ln 10 \approx 57.6\) mV per decade of current, and between two currents \(V_{BE2} = V_{BE1} + V_T\ln(I_{C2}/I_{C1})\). At constant current \(v_{BE}\) falls about 2 mV/°C.

The CBJ is a larger junction and only conducts significantly beyond about 0.4 V, so the boundary is \(V_{CE} = 0.7 - 0.4 = 0.3\) V: the edge of saturation (active-mode equations still hold there). Inside saturation take \(V_{CE,sat} = 0.2\) V (slides: 0.2 V "deep"; Sadat sir's notes: 0.2 V normal, 0.1 V deep if asked). β is defined only in active mode; in saturation \(\beta_{forced} = I_C/I_B < \beta\), and \(\beta/\beta_{forced}\) is the overdrive factor. For a pnp use \(V_{EB}\), \(V_{CB}\), \(V_{EC}\) in the same table.

5. Energy-band diagram of a pnp

Plot electron energy against position. In p regions \(E_F\) lies near \(E_V\); in the n base it lies near \(E_C\). At zero bias \(E_F\) is flat and the base bands sit lower by the built-in barrier \(qV_0\) at both junctions (for holes, which float up in this picture, the base is a barrier). In forward-active bias the EBJ is forward biased by \(V_{EB}\): the emitter bands drop, so the EB barrier shrinks to \(q(V_0 - V_{EB})\) and its depletion layer narrows; holes are injected into the base. The CBJ is reverse biased by \(V_{BC}\): the collector bands rise, the CB step grows to \(q(V_0 + V_{BC})\) and its depletion layer widens; holes reaching it are swept into the collector. The Fermi level splits: \(E_{F,E}\) lies \(qV_{EB}\) below \(E_{F,B}\), and \(E_{F,C}\) lies \(qV_{BC}\) above it.

Traps (where marks go)

  • Writing β in saturation: in saturation only \(\beta_{forced} = I_C/I_B\) exists, and it is smaller than β.
  • pnp signs: \(V_{EB}\) = 0.7 V and current out of the base.
  • Band diagram: drawing the forward-active emitter barrier taller instead of shorter, or keeping one flat \(E_F\) under bias.
  • Calling the boundary of saturation \(V_{CE}\) = 0.2 V: 0.2 V is inside saturation; the edge is 0.3 V.

Draw the energy band profile of a pnp transistor under zero-bias and forward-active mode bias conditions.

Show worked answer

Draw Figure 2 (both panels, same horizontal axis). Points to state on the script:

  • Structure p⁺ emitter (heavy), narrow lightly doped n base, p collector. \(E_F\) near \(E_V\) in the p regions, near \(E_C\) in the base.
  • Zero bias: single flat \(E_F\); built-in barrier \(qV_0\) at both junctions; no net current.
  • Forward active: \(V_{EB} > 0\) lowers the EB barrier to \(q(V_0 - V_{EB})\), narrowing its depletion layer, so holes are injected from the emitter into the base; \(V_{BC} > 0\) (CBJ reverse) raises the CB step to \(q(V_0 + V_{BC})\), widening its depletion layer. Injected holes diffuse across the thin base and "fall" (rise, in electron-energy terms) over the large CB step into the collector: \(i_C \approx \alpha i_E\). Quasi-Fermi levels split by \(qV_{EB}\) and \(qV_{BC}\).
  • A few electrons go from base to emitter and a few holes recombine in the base: these make \(i_B\).
2019–20 (held Jan 2021) · Sec A · Q1(c) · 10⅔ marks

Why is the third terminal (base or gate) used in a transistor?

Show worked answer

A two-terminal device has one current set by one voltage; it cannot use a small signal to control a large one. The third terminal separates the control port (B–E or G–S) from the power port (C–E or D–S). In a BJT \(v_{BE}\) (and a small \(i_B\)) sets \(i_C = I_Se^{v_{BE}/V_T} = \beta i_B\) while \(i_C\) is almost independent of \(v_{CE}\): the device acts as a voltage- or current-controlled valve. In a MOSFET the insulated gate draws no current and \(v_{GS}\) sets the channel, \(i_D = \tfrac12k_n(v_{GS} - V_t)^2\). This control gives amplification (a small input power controls a large output power, with little loading of the source) and switching (cutoff/saturation, or cutoff/triode) for logic.
