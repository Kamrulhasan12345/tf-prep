I–V Characteristics and the Early Effect

The Early effect is in both of the last two papers: 2023–24 Q4(a) (10 marks: \(r_o\) and \(V_A\) from a slope) and 2024–25 Q6(a) (20 marks: derive the CE gain with the Early effect, then evaluate it). As theory it was asked in 2011–12, 2019–20 and 2021–22. Three formulas cover all of it: \(i_C = I_Se^{v_{BE}/V_T}(1 + v_{CE}/V_A)\), \(r_o = V_A/I_C\), and \(A_v = -g_m(R_C\parallel r_o)\).

1. The characteristic curves

\(i_C\)–\(v_{BE}\)
Exponential: negligible below about 0.5 V, steep around 0.7 V. Higher temperature shifts it left by about 2 mV/°C at constant current.
Common-base \(i_C\)–\(v_{CB}\)
For a fixed \(i_E\): flat at \(i_C = \alpha i_E\) for \(v_{CB} > -0.4\) V (active), dropping quickly to zero below that (saturation: the CBJ conducts).
Common-emitter \(i_C\)–\(v_{CE}\)
A family of curves, one per \(v_{BE}\) (or \(i_B\)): a steep saturation region for \(v_{CE} < 0.3\) V, then the active region where the curves are almost flat but rise slowly, and cutoff along \(i_B = 0\).

2. The Early effect

Cause: base-width modulation. Raising \(v_{CE}\) at fixed \(v_{BE}\) raises the CBJ reverse bias \(v_{CB}\). The CB depletion region widens, mostly into the lightly doped base, so the effective base width \(W\) shrinks. The injected minority-carrier profile across the base becomes steeper, and since \(I_S \propto 1/W\), \(i_C\) rises (Sadat sir's version: thinner base, less recombination, so more of the emitter current reaches the collector). Ideally the lines are flat (\(V_A = \infty\)); in a real BJT:

\[ i_C = I_Se^{v_{BE}/V_T}\left(1 + \frac{v_{CE}}{V_A}\right),\qquad r_o = \left[\frac{\partial i_C}{\partial v_{CE}}\right]^{-1}_{v_{BE}\text{ const}} = \frac{V_A + V_{CE}}{I_C} \approx \frac{V_A}{I_C'} \]

where \(I_C' = I_Se^{V_{BE}/V_T}\) is the current without the Early factor; Sedra uses \(r_o = V_A/I_C\). In the small-signal model \(r_o\) sits between collector and emitter. Consequences: \(i_C\) is not constant in active mode; finite output resistance \(r_o\); the CE gain falls to \(-g_m(R_C\parallel r_o)\) and the best possible gain is \(g_mr_o = V_A/V_T\) (≈ 4000 for 100 V); β rises slightly with \(v_{CE}\); in the extreme the depletion layer reaches across the base (punch-through breakdown).

Method · ro and VA numerics (type B-12)

  1. Slope given: \(r_o = 1/\text{slope}\).
  2. \(V_A = r_oI_C\) (strictly \(r_oI_C - V_{CE}\)).
  3. At another current: \(r_o' = V_A/I_C'\) (\(r_o\) is inversely proportional to the current).
  4. Two points on one curve: \(r_o = \Delta V_{CE}/\Delta I_C\); \(V_A = \dfrac{I_{C1}V_{CE2} - I_{C2}V_{CE1}}{I_{C2} - I_{C1}}\) (where the line meets the axis, sign reversed).

Worked example: 2023–24 Sec A Q4(a)

3. CE gain with the Early effect (2024–25 Q6(a))

Circuit: \(v_{BE}\) applied to the base, \(R_C\) from \(V_{CC}\) to the collector, emitter grounded, so \(v_{CE} = V_{CC} - i_CR_C\) and \(A_v = dv_{CE}/dv_{BE}\) at the bias point.

Derivation. Without the Early effect: \(A_v = -R_C\,di_C/dv_{BE} = -I_CR_C/V_T\). With it, differentiate \(i_C = I_Se^{v_{BE}/V_T}(1 + v_{CE}/V_A)\), remembering \(v_{CE}\) also changes:

\[ \frac{di_C}{dv_{BE}} = \frac{I_C}{V_T} + \frac{I_C'}{V_A}\,A_v,\qquad A_v = -R_C\frac{di_C}{dv_{BE}} \;\Rightarrow\; A_v\left(1 + \frac{I_C'R_C}{V_A}\right) = -\frac{I_CR_C}{V_T} \]

With \(I_C' = I_C\,V_A/(V_A + V_{CE})\) and \(I_CR_C = V_{CC} - V_{CE}\):

\[ A_v = -\frac{I_CR_C/V_T}{1 + \dfrac{I_CR_C}{V_A + V_{CE}}} = -\frac{(V_{CC} - V_{CE})/V_T}{1 + \dfrac{V_{CC} - V_{CE}}{V_A + V_{CE}}} \]

This is the same as \(-g_m(R_C\parallel r_o)\) with \(r_o = (V_A + V_{CE})/I_C\).

Traps (where marks go)

  • Writing \(r_o = V_A/I_C\) and then reusing the old \(r_o\) at a new current: \(r_o\) scales as \(1/I_C\).
  • Drawing the Early lines converging at \(+V_A\), or on the current axis.
  • In the derivation, treating \(v_{CE}\) as constant: then the Early term never appears.
  • Explaining the Early effect by the EBJ: it is the CBJ depletion layer eating into the base.

Exam questions

2024–25 · Sec B · Q6(a) · 20 marks

The small-signal gain of a CE amplifier is \(A_v = -I_CR_C/V_T\) with \(i_C = I_Se^{v_{BE}/V_T}\) (no Early effect). Show that with \(i_C = I_Se^{v_{BE}/V_T}(1 + v_{CE}/V_A)\) the gain becomes \(A_v = -\dfrac{I_CR_C/V_T}{1 + I_CR_C/(V_A + V_{CE})} = -\dfrac{(V_{CC} - V_{CE})/V_T}{1 + (V_{CC} - V_{CE})/(V_A + V_{CE})}\). For \(V_{CC}\) = 5 V and \(V_{CE}\) = 2.5 V, find the gain with and without the Early effect (\(V_A\) = 100 V).

Show worked answer

Derivation as in section 3: \(v_{CE} = V_{CC} - i_CR_C\), so \(A_v = dv_{CE}/dv_{BE} = -R_C\,di_C/dv_{BE}\); with the Early factor \(di_C/dv_{BE} = I_C/V_T + (I_C'/V_A)A_v\); solve for \(A_v\); substitute \(I_C' = I_CV_A/(V_A + V_{CE})\) and \(I_CR_C = V_{CC} - V_{CE}\).

Numbers (\(V_T\) = 25 mV): \(V_{CC} - V_{CE}\) = 2.5 V. Without: \(A_v = -2.5/0.025 = -100\) V/V. With: \(A_v = -100/(1 + 2.5/102.5) = -100/1.0244 = -97.6\) V/V (2.4 % lower).

2023–24 · Sec A · Q4(a) · 10 marks

For an npn transistor operating at \(v_{BE}\) = 0.67 V and \(I_C\) = 3 mA, the \(i_C\)–\(v_{CE}\) curve has a slope of \(3\times10^{-5}\) S. (i) What output resistance does this correspond to? (ii) What is the Early voltage? (iii) For operation at 30 mA, what would the output resistance become?

Show worked answer

(i) \(r_o = 1/(3\times10^{-5}) = 33.33\) kΩ. (ii) \(V_A = r_oI_C = 33.33\text{ kΩ}\times3\text{ mA} = 100\) V (strictly \(100 - V_{CE}\)). (iii) \(r_o = V_A/I_C = 100/30\text{ mA} = 3.33\) kΩ.

2021–22 · Sec B · Q6(a) · 10⅔ marks  |  also 2019–20 Sec A Q2(a) · 16⅔ marks (Early effect and its consequences)

What is the Early effect in a BJT? Draw the I–V curve of a practical BJT showing the Early effect. (2019–20: What are the consequences of the Early effect on BJT behaviour?)

Show worked answer

Definition and cause: base-width modulation by \(v_{CB}\) (section 2). Draw Figure 1: \(i_C\)–\(v_{CE}\) family, saturation below about 0.3 V, sloped active lines extrapolating to \(-V_A\); write \(i_C = I_Se^{v_{BE}/V_T}(1 + v_{CE}/V_A)\) and \(r_o = V_A/I_C\).

Consequences: finite output resistance \(r_o\) between C and E; \(i_C\) depends on \(v_{CE}\); CE voltage gain limited to \(-g_m(R_C\parallel r_o)\), intrinsic maximum \(V_A/V_T\); output resistance of current sources limited to \(r_o\); β increases with \(v_{CE}\); punch-through at very high \(v_{CB}\). Typical \(V_A\) 50–150 V.

2011–12 · Sec B · Q8(a) · 10⅔ marks

Why is biasing needed for an amplifier using a BJT? Draw the output characteristics of a BJT in CE configuration. Define the Early effect.

Show worked answer

Bias puts the Q-point in the active region, near the middle of the load line, so the signal swings without clipping and \(g_m = I_C/V_T\) is defined and stable (Chapter 4). Output characteristics: Figure 1 with cutoff (\(i_B = 0\)), saturation (\(v_{CE} < 0.3\) V) and active regions labelled. Early effect: the increase of \(i_C\) with \(v_{CE}\) in the active region caused by base-width modulation; the lines meet at \(-V_A\).

Quick check

Summary: Early effect = base-width modulation: \(i_C = I_Se^{v_{BE}/V_T}(1 + v_{CE}/V_A)\), active lines meet at \(-V_A\), \(r_o = V_A/I_C\) = 1/slope, so \(V_A = r_oI_C\) and \(r_o \propto 1/I_C\).
