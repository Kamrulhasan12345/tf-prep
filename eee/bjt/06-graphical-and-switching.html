Load Lines, Transfer Curves and the BJT Switch

Graphical questions appeared in 2011–12, 2012–13, 2013–14, 2014–15 and 2016–17 (10 to 46 marks each) but not in the last eight papers: treat this chapter as low priority and read it after the amplifier chapters. It covers the DC load line and the Q-point, the voltage transfer characteristic (VTC) of the CE stage used as an inverter with its cutoff and saturation breakpoints, how the curves change with \(V_{CC}\) and \(R_C\), the BJT as a switch and, briefly, BJT logic gates (old papers only).

1. DC load line and the Q-point

KVL around the output loop of a CE stage (units mA, kΩ, V): \(V_{CC} = I_CR_C + V_{CE} + I_ER_E\). With \(R_{dc} = R_C + R_E\) (and \(I_E\approx I_C\)) the DC load line is

\[ I_C = \frac{V_{CC} - V_{CE}}{R_{dc}} \]

Intercepts: \(V_{CE} = V_{CC}\) at \(I_C = 0\) (cutoff end) and \(I_{C,max} = V_{CC}/R_{dc}\) at \(V_{CE}\approx 0\) (saturation end; with \(V_{CE,sat}\) = 0.2 V it is \((V_{CC} - 0.2)/R_{dc}\)). The Q-point is where the load line meets the device curve for the chosen \(I_B\) (or \(V_{BE}\)). For symmetric linear amplification put Q in the middle: \(V_{CEQ}\approx V_{CC}/2\), \(I_{CQ}\approx I_{C,max}/2\). A Q-point at the saturation end gives clipping of one half-cycle and a very asymmetric swing; at the cutoff end the other half is clipped. Available swing: down to saturation \(V_{CEQ} - 0.2\), up to cutoff \(V_{CC} - V_{CEQ}\); the smaller one limits a symmetric signal.

Load line against a piecewise-linear device curve

Method · intersection with a PWL characteristic

  1. Write the load line from KVL in the plotted variables (e.g. \(I_C\) against \(V_{CB}\): \(V_{CC} = I_CR_C + V_{CB} + V_{BE}\)).
  2. Write each PWL segment of the device curve as a line \(I_C = I_0 + s(V - V_0)\) (a break point and a slope).
  3. Solve the two lines simultaneously in the region the point must lie in; check the answer lies on the segment you used.
  4. Convert variables if asked (\(V_{CE} = V_{CB} + V_{BE}\)).

2. The CE stage as an inverter: the VTC

Take a CE stage with \(R_C\) to \(V_{CC}\), emitter grounded, input \(V_{in}\) at the base. The output has three regions:

  • Cutoff, \(V_{in} < V_\gamma\) (about 0.5 V): \(I_C = 0\), \(V_o = V_{CC}\).
  • Active: \(V_o = V_{CC} - I_CR_C\) with \(I_C\) rising with \(V_{in}\); the slope of the VTC is \(dV_o/dV_{in} = -g_mR_C\) (the CE gain). In a piecewise-linear model, \(I_C = g_m(V_{in} - V_\gamma)\).
  • Saturation: \(V_o = V_{CE,sat}\approx 0.2\) V (not 0). The knee is where \(I_C\) reaches \(I_{C,sat} = (V_{CC} - 0.2)/R_C\), at \(V_{in,sat} = V_\gamma + I_{C,sat}/g_m\). With a base resistor \(R_B\): \(V_{in,sat} = 0.7 + R_BI_{C,sat}/\beta\) (the knee depends on β).

The \(I_C\)–\(V_{in}\) curve is the mirror image: 0 for \(V_{in} < V_\gamma\), rising with slope \(g_m\), flat at \(I_{C,sat}\) beyond \(V_{in,sat}\).

Method · VTC and what changes (VCC and RC)

  1. From the given Vo–Vin line get the slope \(-g_mR_C\) and so \(g_m\) (it is fixed by the device model, not by \(R_C\) or \(V_{CC}\)).
  2. New \(I_{C,sat} = (V_{CC}' - 0.2)/R_C'\); new knee \(V_{in,sat}' = V_\gamma + I_{C,sat}'/g_m\). Turn-on stays at \(V_\gamma\).
  3. Plateau (cutoff level) moves to \(V_{CC}'\); floor stays at 0.2 V. Draw Vo from \((V_\gamma, V_{CC}')\) to \((V_{in,sat}', 0.2)\) and \(I_C\) from \((V_\gamma, 0)\) to \((V_{in,sat}', I_{C,sat}')\), flat after.
  4. \(R_C\to\infty\): \(I_C = 0\) for all \(V_{in}\) and \(V_o = V_{CC}\) (as a limit; at exactly \(R_C = \infty\) the collector floats). \(R_C\) larger: steeper slope, lower \(I_{C,sat}\), earlier knee. \(V_{CC}\) larger: higher plateau, larger \(I_{C,sat}\), later knee.

Worked example: S3-Q33 (2012–13, 2013–14)

3. The BJT as a switch

As a switch the transistor is driven between cutoff (open, \(V_o = V_{CC}\)) and saturation (closed, \(V_o = V_{CE,sat}\approx0.2\) V). Design for saturation with margin: \(I_{C,sat} = (V_{CC} - 0.2)/R_C\) and force \(I_B \ge I_{C,sat}/\beta_{min}\) (the worst-case, lowest β), so with a base resistor from a high input \(V_H\):

\[ R_B \le \frac{\beta_{min}(V_H - 0.7)}{I_{C,sat}} = \frac{\beta_{min}(V_H - 0.7)R_C}{V_{CC} - 0.2} \]

The ratio \(\beta_{forced} = I_{C,sat}/I_B\) is below β in saturation. The same stage is the basic inverter (NOT gate): \(V_{in}\) low gives \(V_o\) high and vice versa.

BJT logic gates (low priority: 2012–13 only)

Resistor–transistor logic: each input drives its own transistor base through \(R_B\), with a shared \(R_C\). NOR: transistors in parallel (collectors tied, emitters grounded): if any input is high \(Y\) = 0.2 V, otherwise \(Y\) = \(V_{CC}\). NAND: transistors in series (stacked): only when both are on is \(Y\approx 2V_{CE,sat}\approx 0.4\) V (low), otherwise high. Give the truth table and the saturation design \(R_B\) formula above.

Traps (where marks go)

  • Drawing the floor of the VTC at 0 V: it is \(V_{CE,sat}\approx0.2\) V.
  • Using the unchanged knee when \(V_{CC}\) or \(R_C\) changes: \(I_{C,sat}\) and so \(V_{in,sat}\) both change.
  • Reading a load-line intersection off a sketch; solve the two equations and check the segment.
  • With a base resistor the knee is \(0.7 + R_BI_{C,sat}/\beta\), so it moves with β; with the input directly on the base it does not.
  • Putting Q at the saturation end (maximum \(I_C\)) for a linear amplifier: it clips and is asymmetric. Centre it.

Exam questions

2016–17 · Sec · 15 marks · type B-06 (qualitative VTC)  |  question-bank item S3-Q20

Draw the voltage transfer characteristic of the circuit by qualitative analysis. Circuit (simplified TTL inverter): Q1 npn with its emitter at Vin, base through 10 kΩ to VCC, collector to the base of Q2; Q2 emitter grounded, collector at Vout through 1 kΩ to VCC.

Show worked answer

\(V_{in}\) low: Q1 is saturated (its base is pulled up by the 10 kΩ), so \(V_{B2} = V_{in} + V_{CE1,sat}\approx V_{in} + 0.1\) to 0.2 V. Q2 is off while \(V_{B2} < 0.6\) to 0.7 V: \(V_{out} = V_{CC}\).

Transition: Q2 turns on at \(V_{in}\approx0.5\) V. The 10 kΩ supplies much more base current than Q2 needs (\((V_{CC}-0.2)/(1\text{ k}\Omega\cdot\beta)\)), so Q2 saturates almost at once and \(V_{out}\) falls steeply to 0.2 V by \(V_{in}\approx 0.6\) to 0.7 V.

\(V_{in}\) higher: \(V_{B1}\) clamps at about \(V_{BC1} + V_{BE2}\approx1.4\) V and Q1 goes reverse-active (it steers current into Q2's base). \(V_{out}\) stays at \(V_{CE2,sat}\approx0.2\) V. So: flat at \(V_{CC}\), a steep fall near 0.5–0.7 V, flat at 0.2 V. (A sketch with a linear fall from 0.5 V to 1.5 V is imprecise: the fall ends near 0.6–0.7 V; 1.4 V is only where Q1 changes mode.) 2014–15 · Sec · 10 marks

The VTC (\(V_o\) against \(V_{in}\)) of a basic CE switch (\(R_C\) to \(V_{CC}\), emitter grounded) is given: \(V_o = V_{CC}\) below about 0.5 V and the floor is 0.2 V. What happens if (i) \(V_{CC}\) is increased, (ii) \(R_C\) is increased? Explain with the curve.

Show worked answer

Regions: cutoff (\(V_{in} < \sim0.5\) V, \(V_o = V_{CC}\)); active (\(V_o = V_{CC} - R_CI_Se^{V_{in}/V_T}\), slope \(-g_mR_C\)); saturation (\(V_o = 0.2\) V).

(i) \(V_{CC}\) up: the top plateau rises to the new \(V_{CC}\); turn-on stays at 0.5 V; saturation starts later because \(I_{C,sat} = (V_{CC} - 0.2)/R_C\) is larger; the floor stays at 0.2 V. (ii) \(R_C\) up: the plateau is unchanged; the active slope \(-g_mR_C\) is steeper; \(I_{C,sat}\) is smaller so saturation starts at a lower \(V_{in}\); the floor stays at 0.2 V.

2013–14 · (a) 10 marks and 2012–13 · (b)–(e) 41 marks · question-bank item S3-Q33 (51 marks in all)

An npn inverter has the input directly on the base, \(R_C\) = 480 Ω to \(V_{CC}\) = 5 V and the piecewise-linear transfer characteristic \(V_o\) against \(V_{in}\) from (0.5 V, 5 V) to (0.7 V, 0.2 V). (b) Copy the \(V_o\)–\(V_{in}\) curve and draw \(I_C\)–\(V_{in}\) with the break coordinates. (a) Redo the curves for \(V_{CC}\) = 2.5 V. (c) Redo for \(R_C = \infty\). (d) Redo for \(V_{CC}\) = 10 V. (e) With a linear \(I_C\)–\(V_{BE}\) relation in the active mode, does the gain change with \(V_{in}\)? Does the output swing change?

Show worked answer

From the graph: slope \(= -4.8/0.2 = -24 = -g_mR_C\), so \(g_m = 24/0.48 = 50\) mA/V (constant) and \(I_C = 50(V_{in} - 0.5)\) mA in the active region.

(b) \(I_{C,sat} = (5 - 0.2)/0.48 = 10\) mA. \(I_C\): 0 up to 0.5 V, straight to (0.7 V, 10 mA), flat at 10 mA beyond. \(V_o\) as given.

(a) \(V_{CC}\) = 2.5 V: \(I_{C,sat} = 2.3/0.48 = 4.79\) mA, knee at \(V_{in} = 0.5 + 4.79/50 = 0.596\) V: \(V_o\) from (0.5, 2.5) to (0.596, 0.2); \(I_C\) from (0.5, 0) to (0.596, 4.79 mA), flat after.

(d) \(V_{CC}\) = 10 V: \(I_{C,sat} = 9.8/0.48 = 20.4\) mA, knee at \(0.5 + 20.4/50 = 0.908\) V: \(V_o\) from (0.5, 10) to (0.908, 0.2); \(I_C\) up to 20.4 mA, flat after.

(c) \(R_C = \infty\): \(I_C = 0\) for every \(V_{in}\); \(V_o = V_{CC}\) for \(V_{in} < 0.5\) V as the limit of large \(R_C\) (at exactly \(R_C=\infty\) the collector floats), dropping abruptly to about 0.2 V as the device turns on.

(e) With a linear \(I_C\)–\(V_{BE}\) law \(g_m\) is constant, so the gain \(-g_mR_C\) does not depend on \(V_{in}\) (unlike the exponential law, where \(g_m\propto I_C\)). The undistorted output swing does depend on the bias \(V_{in}\) (the distance to cutoff and to saturation).

2012–13 · Sec · 16 + 10 + 10 + 10 = 46 marks · question-bank item S3-Q10

A BJT has a 1 V battery from the base to ground, the emitter grounded, and \(R_C\) = 2 kΩ from +5 V to the collector. (a) Express \(I_C\) as a function of \(V_{CB}\) along the load line. (b) Write the piecewise-linear equation of the given \(I_C\)–\(V_{CB}\) characteristic (break point (−0.5 V, 1 mA), the active-region line at 20° to the horizontal, a steep line below −0.5 V). (c) Plot both and find the intersection (bias point). (d) Find \(V_{CE}\). Axes taken as mA and V so that tan 20° = 0.364 mA/V; state this assumption.

Show worked answer

(a) KVL: \(5 - 2I_C - V_{CB} - 1 = 0\), so \(I_C = 2 - 0.5V_{CB}\) (mA, V).

(b) For \(V_{CB}\ge -0.5\) V: \(I_C = 1 + 0.364(V_{CB} + 0.5)\); below −0.5 V the steep saturation branch.

(c) \(2 - 0.5V_{CB} = 1 + 0.364(V_{CB} + 0.5)\): \(0.818 = 0.864V_{CB}\), \(V_{CB} = \mathbf{0.947}\) V, \(I_C = 2 - 0.4735 = \mathbf{1.527}\) mA (on the active segment, \(V_{CB}\ge-0.5\) ✓).

(d) \(V_{CE} = V_{CB} + V_{BE} = 0.947 + 1 = \mathbf{1.947}\) V (\(V_{BE}\) is fixed at 1 V by the battery).

2011–12 · Sec · 12 marks · question-bank item S3-Q31

From the given DC load line (collector-current intercept 8 mA, \(V_{CE}\) intercept 20 V), find the maximum collector current. What problem arises if the Q-point is set at that maximum for linear amplification?

Show worked answer

\(V_{CC} = 20\) V and \(R_{dc} = 20/8 = 2.5\) kΩ. \(I_{C,max} = V_{CC}/R_{dc} = \mathbf{8}\) mA at \(V_{CE}\approx0\). A Q-point there is at the edge of saturation: the positive half-cycles of the input drive the transistor into saturation and are clipped, so the swing is badly asymmetric and distorted. For linear amplification set Q at the middle of the load line, (10 V, 4 mA).

2012–13 · Sec · 10 marks · question-bank item S3-Q32 (low priority)

Make a NAND and a NOR gate using BJTs.

Show worked answer

Each input goes through a base resistor \(R_B\) to an npn base; a shared \(R_C\) goes to \(V_{CC}\) and the output is the collector node. NOR: transistors in parallel (collectors tied, emitters to ground): any input high gives \(Y\approx0.2\) V (low), both low gives \(V_{CC}\). NAND: transistors in series (the lower emitter grounded): only both high gives \(Y\approx2V_{CE,sat}\approx0.4\) V, otherwise \(Y = V_{CC}\) (high). Truth table: NOR (00 → 1, 01 → 0, 10 → 0, 11 → 0); NAND (00 → 1, 01 → 1, 10 → 1, 11 → 0). Saturation design: \(R_B \le \beta_{min}(V_H - 0.7)R_C/(V_{CC} - 0.2)\).

Key terms

Quick check

Summary: DC load line \(I_C = (V_{CC} - V_{CE})/R_{dc}\); Q at the intersection with the device curve, centred (\(V_{CE}\approx V_{CC}/2\)) for linear amplification. The CE inverter VTC: \(V_o = V_{CC}\) (cutoff) → slope \(-g_mR_C\) (active) → 0.2 V (saturation), knee at \(I_{C,sat} = (V_{CC} - 0.2)/R_C\), \(V_{in,sat} = V_\gamma + I_{C,sat}/g_m\). Raising \(V_{CC}\): higher plateau, later knee; raising \(R_C\): steeper, earlier knee. Switch: \(R_B \le \beta_{min}(V_H - 0.7)/I_{C,sat}\).
