Extended Relational Algebra: Grouping, Sorting, Assignment and Updates

Slide 21 promises "extensions and limitations". Plain RA cannot count, sum or sort, and many exam questions ask for exactly that. This chapter teaches the extended operators (γ, generalized π, τ, δ, assignment), shows how updates are written in RA, states what RA cannot do, and then works the bank's six questions that need them.

1. Why extend the algebra

With σ, π, ×, ∪, −, ρ you cannot write "the number of parts each supplier supplies" or "the average salary per department": the basic operators never compute a new value, they only pick and combine existing ones. "More than 10 parts" can in principle be written by joining 11 copies of Catalog with pairwise-different pids, but nobody would. The textbook (§5.2) adds operators that SQL has anyway:

2. Grouping and aggregation γ

Aggregate function
A function that turns a column of values into one value: SUM, AVG, MIN, MAX, COUNT. COUNT(*) counts tuples; COUNT(A) counts non-null A values; COUNT(DISTINCT A) counts different values.
γL(R)
L is a list of grouping attributes and aggregates f(A)→name. γ partitions R into groups of tuples that agree on all grouping attributes, and outputs one tuple per group: the grouping values plus each aggregate computed over the group. With no grouping attributes, the whole relation is one group and the result is one tuple.

For example γsid, COUNT(pid)→n(Catalog) gives each supplier's number of catalog rows. A HAVING condition is simply a σ on top of γ: σn>3(γsid, COUNT(pid)→n(Catalog)).

A set-semantics trap. Project away the key before aggregating and duplicates merge. πdept, salary(instructor) collapses two instructors of the same department with the same salary into one tuple, so SUM over it is wrong. Keep a key in the input of γ (or aggregate directly on the base relation). The Atkinson question below shows this.

3. Generalized projection, sorting, δ

A generalized projection may compute new attributes from old ones, πname, salary·1.1→raise(instructor). τ sorts; since a set has no order, its result is a list and τ must be the last (outermost) operator. δ removes duplicates from a bag; under the set semantics of these slides it changes nothing, but it matters in the bag algebra that SQL actually uses (textbook §5.1).

4. Assignment and database updates

Assignment names an intermediate result, T := E (Chapter 8). Written as R ← E with a stored relation R on the left, it replaces R's contents, which is how RA expresses updates:

  • Insert: R ← R ∪ E, where E is a constant relation or a query.
  • Delete: R ← R − σc(R), equivalently R ← σ¬c(R).
  • Update: rebuild R with a generalized projection: R ← π…, f(A)→A, …(σc(R)) ∪ σ¬c(R).

5. Limitations (slide 21's last item)

Even extended RA cannot express recursion: "all prerequisites of CS-315, at any depth" (the transitive closure of prereq) needs a join repeated an unknown number of times, and every RA expression has a fixed number of joins. SQL adds recursion for this (WITH … UNION ALL recursive queries; Oracle also has CONNECT BY). RA also has no notion of ordering inside the algebra (τ only formats the final output), and no procedural control flow; those are what stored procedures and triggers provide (Chapter 10).

DB QB · 23-24 4a · 12 marks  |  BUET QBank · CSE 215 | L-2/T-1 | 2023–24 · 25 marks

Suppliers(sid: integer, sname: string, address: string), Parts(pid: integer, pname: string, color: string), Catalog(sid: integer, pid: integer, cost: real). For the schema above, write down the relational algebraic expression for the following:

(i) Find the names of suppliers who supply more than 10 parts.
(ii) Find the sids of suppliers who supply some red or green part.
(iii) Find the sids of suppliers who supply some red part or are located at 221 Packer Street.
(iv) Find the sids of suppliers who supply some red part with each part's cost more than 100 taka.
(v) Find the sids of suppliers who supply every part.

Show worked answer

(i) Counting needs γ. Count each supplier's parts, keep counts above 10, attach names:

πsname(Suppliers ⋈ σn>10(γsid, COUNT(pid)→n(Catalog)))

(If (sid, pid) were not a key of Catalog, count distinct pids: COUNT(DISTINCT pid).) The sample data has no supplier with more than 10 parts, so the answer there is empty; the evaluator below also runs it with 3 instead of 10.

(ii) πsid(σcolor='red' ∨ color='green'(Parts) ⋈ Catalog)

(iii) The two conditions are about different relations, so take the union of two sets of sids:

πsid(σcolor='red'(Parts) ⋈ Catalog) ∪ πsid(σaddress='221 Packer Street'(Suppliers))

The second set includes suppliers that supply nothing, which is right: they are located at 221 Packer Street.

(iv) The wording allows two readings; state the one you use.

Reading 1, "supply some red part whose cost is more than 100": πsid(σcolor='red' ∧ cost>100(Parts ⋈ Catalog)).

Reading 2, "supply some red part, and every red part they supply costs more than 100": all red suppliers minus those with a cheap red part:

πsid(σcolor='red'(Parts) ⋈ Catalog) − πsid(σcolor='red' ∧ cost≤100(Parts ⋈ Catalog))

(v) πsid,pid(Catalog) ÷ πpid(Parts) (Chapter 5).

Sample data results: (i) ∅ (with "> 3": Acme); (ii) {1, 2, 3, 5}; (iii) {1, 2, 4, 5}; (iv) both readings give {1, 5} here (supplier 2's red bolt costs 90); (v) {1}.

DB QB · 17-18 6b · 10 marks  |  BUET QBank · CSE 215 | L-2/T-2 | 2017–18 · 10 marks

Consider the following schemas: Manufacturer(man_id, name, address), Laptop(model, man_id, speed, ram, hd, screen, price), Printer(model, man_id, is_color, type, price). Write expressions of relational algebra to answer the following queries.

(i) Find the model and speeds of each laptop manufactured by "Dell".
(ii) Find the sorted list of names of the manufacturers that produce at least three different models of laptops.

Show worked answer

(i) Manufacturer and Laptop share only man_id:

πmodel, speed(σname='Dell'(Manufacturer) ⋈ Laptop)

(ii) Count models per manufacturer, keep counts ≥ 3, attach names, sort:

τname(πname(Manufacturer ⋈ σn≥3(γman_id, COUNT(model)→n(Laptop))))

Without γ, use three renamed copies with strictly increasing models (which also makes them different): πL1.man_id(σL1.man_id=L2.man_id ∧ L2.man_id=L3.man_id ∧ L1.model<L2.model ∧ L2.model<L3.model(ρL1(Laptop) × ρL2(Laptop) × ρL3(Laptop))), then join with Manufacturer and sort as above. Both forms give Dell and Lenovo on the sample data.

BUET QBank · CSE 303 | L-3/T-1 | 2013–14 · 20 marks

Consider the schema EMPLOYEE(EMPLOYEEID, LASTNAME, FIRSTNAME, PHONE, HIREDATE, JOBID, SALARY, DEPARTMENT), JOB(JOBID, JOBTITLE, MINSALARY, MAXSALARY), DEPARTMENT(DEPARTMENTID, DEPARTMENTNAME, MANAGERID). Write relational algebra expressions for the following:

(a) List the average salary of each department.
(b) List the FIRSTNAME and JOBTITLE of employee 'John'.
(c) Increase all salaries more than 10000 by 5% and less than 10000 by 4%.
(d) Delete all employees from the EMPLOYEE table who earn less than 1000 as salary.

Show worked answer

(a) γDEPARTMENT, AVG(SALARY)→avg_salary(EMPLOYEE). To show department names, join first (EMPLOYEE.DEPARTMENT references DEPARTMENTID): γDEPARTMENTNAME, AVG(SALARY)→avg_salary(EMPLOYEE ⋈DEPARTMENT=DEPARTMENTID DEPARTMENT).

(b) EMPLOYEE and JOB share only JOBID: πFIRSTNAME, JOBTITLE(σFIRSTNAME='John'(EMPLOYEE) ⋈ JOB).

(c) Updates are written as assignment with a generalized projection. Let A stand for EMPLOYEEID, LASTNAME, FIRSTNAME, PHONE, HIREDATE, JOBID (in that order). Three disjoint groups: above 10000, below 10000, and exactly 10000, which the question does not mention and so must stay unchanged:

EMPLOYEE ← πA, SALARY·1.05→SALARY, DEPARTMENT(σSALARY>10000(EMPLOYEE)) ∪ πA, SALARY·1.04→SALARY, DEPARTMENT(σSALARY<10000(EMPLOYEE)) ∪ σSALARY=10000(EMPLOYEE)

Note: the bank's answer gives the 4% raise to salary = 10000 as well; the question only says "less than 10000", so leaving 10000 unchanged is the literal reading. Forgetting the third part would delete those employees.

(d) EMPLOYEE ← EMPLOYEE − σSALARY<1000(EMPLOYEE), or EMPLOYEE ← σSALARY≥1000(EMPLOYEE). (If SALARY could be NULL, only the first form keeps those employees.)

BUET QBank · CSE 303 | L-3/T-1 | 2012–13 · 25 marks

Consider the relational schema employee(emp_no, name, office, age), books(isbn, title, author, publisher), loan(empno, isbn, date). Answer the following queries in relational algebra:

(a) Find the names of employees who have borrowed a book published by McGraw-Hill.
(b) Find the names and ages of employees who have borrowed all books written by Carl Hamacher.
(c) Find the names of employees who have borrowed more than five different books published by Pearson Education.
(d) For each publisher, find the names of employees who have borrowed more than five books of that publisher.
(e) Find the titles and authors of the books that have been borrowed by Peter Hart.

Show worked answer

loan and books share isbn, so ⋈ is safe there; employee's emp_no and loan's empno have different names, so that link is an equi-join.

(a) πname(employee ⋈emp_no=empno (loan ⋈ σpublisher='McGraw-Hill'(books)))

(b) "all books written by Carl Hamacher" is division. Project loan onto (empno, isbn) first, so that date does not take part:

πname, age(employee ⋈emp_no=empno (πempno, isbn(loan) ÷ πisbn(σauthor='Carl Hamacher'(books))))

(c) "different books": project onto (empno, isbn) before counting, so borrowing the same book twice counts once:

P := πempno, isbn(loan ⋈ σpublisher='Pearson Education'(books))
C := σn>5(γempno, COUNT(isbn)→n(P))
Answer := πname(employee ⋈emp_no=empno C)

(d) The same, but grouped by publisher too, and the publisher kept in the output:

B := πempno, isbn, publisher(loan ⋈ books)
C := σn>5(γpublisher, empno, COUNT(isbn)→n(B))
Answer := πpublisher, name(employee ⋈emp_no=empno C)

(Here "more than five books" is read as different books, as in (c).)

(e) πtitle, author(books ⋈ (loan ⋈empno=emp_no σname='Peter Hart'(employee)))

On the sample data below, employee 1 borrowed 6 different Pearson books (one of them twice) and qualifies for (c) and (d); employee 2 has 6 Pearson loans but only 5 different books and correctly does not.

DB QB · 21 CT2 2 · 6 marks

Write the following query using relational algebra. Use the normalized schema you prepared in the previous question [21 CT2 1]. Print the total transaction made by username = "Atkinson" in each year. Print the year and total transaction sorted by the year. You can use TO_YEAR function to get the year of a date.

Show worked answer

The schema. In 21 CT2 1, the unnormalized Sales relation has the dependency Transaction Time, Username, Order Number → Transaction Amount. The BCNF decomposition therefore contains a relation Transaction(TransactionTime, Username, OrderNumber, TransactionAmount) with key (TransactionTime, Username, OrderNumber). (TransactionType goes to a separate relation keyed by TransactionTime, because Transaction Time → Transaction Type.) Only this relation is needed.

T := πTransactionTime, OrderNumber, TO_YEAR(TransactionTime)→year, TransactionAmount(σUsername='Atkinson'(Transaction))
Answer := τyear(γyear, SUM(TransactionAmount)→total(T))

Why T keeps TransactionTime and OrderNumber: if it projected only (year, TransactionAmount), two different transactions in the same year with the same amount would become one tuple (sets have no duplicates), and the sum would be too small. Keeping the key avoids that. In the sample data below Atkinson made two 500-taka transactions in 2020; the evaluator shows the wrong total (one 500 lost) for the naive version.

DB QB · 22-23 6a · 30 marks  |  BUET QBank · CSE 215 | L-2/T-1 | 2022–23 · 30 marks

A school has prepared a database to manage admission tests. The examinees are given roll numbers and assigned to specific rooms for the exam. The test contains 10 questions. Each question is marked by two examiners to ensure fairness and correctness. An absentee list is kept to ensure no script goes missing. The schema: Room(Room_No, Floor, Capacity), Examinee(Roll_No, Name, Address, Date_of_Birth, Room_No), Evaluation(Roll_No, Question_No, Examiner, Marks), Absentee(Roll_No). Write queries using relational algebra to compute the following.

(i) The students should be assigned to the rooms in a manner that all the Roll Nos in the room are sequential (e.g., if a room has 10 examinees and the lowest Roll No in that room is 501, then Roll Nos 502, 503, …, 510 should be assigned to that room). Find the rooms that violate this condition.
(ii) We need to check if all scripts have been correctly evaluated, i.e., all examinees' answers to all questions have been evaluated by exactly two teachers. Find out the Roll No and Question No of the students that have not got correctly evaluated. Please note that only the examinees that are present in the exam should be considered here.
(iii) The marks given by two examiners can only be at most 2 marks apart. Find the Roll No and Question No for which two examiners' marks differ by more than 2.

Show worked answer

Let E := πRoll_No, Room_No(Examinee).

(i) A room's roll numbers are sequential exactly when every examinee in it, except the one with the highest roll, has roll + 1 in the same room. So find the examinees that are not the last in their room, remove those whose successor is in the room, and report the rooms of the rest:

NotLast := πE1.Roll_No, E1.Room_No(σE1.Room_No=E2.Room_No ∧ E1.Roll_No<E2.Roll_No(ρE1(E) × ρE2(E)))
HasNext := πE1.Roll_No, E1.Room_No(σE1.Room_No=E2.Room_No ∧ E2.Roll_No=E1.Roll_No+1(ρE1(E) × ρE2(E)))
Answer  := πRoom_No(NotLast − HasNext)

With aggregation it is shorter: a room is sequential iff max − min + 1 equals the number of examinees (roll numbers are unique):

πRoom_No(σhi−lo+1 ≠ n(γRoom_No, MIN(Roll_No)→lo, MAX(Roll_No)→hi, COUNT(Roll_No)→n(E)))

Note: the bank's answer looks for a roll number between two of the room's rolls that is assigned to a different room. That misses a gap whose roll number is assigned to no room at all (room 103 below has 506 and 508, and nobody has 507). Both answers above catch it.

(ii) Required pairs: every present examinee with every question. Q is the set of the 10 question numbers; it can be written as a constant relation {(1), …, (10)}, or as πQuestion_No(Evaluation) if every question was marked for someone.

Present := πRoll_No(Examinee) − Absentee
Req     := Present × Q
Cnt     := γRoll_No, Question_No, COUNT(Examiner)→n(Evaluation)
Answer  := (Req − πRoll_No, Question_No(Evaluation))           -- never evaluated
           ∪ πRoll_No, Question_No(σn≠2(Cnt) ⋈ Present)    -- evaluated 1 or 3+ times

Without γ: "at least two" is a self-product with different examiners, "at least three" a three-way one; then (Req − AtLeast2) ∪ (AtLeast3 ∩ Req). The bank's version forgets to restrict the "more than two" part to present examinees.

(iii) Pair the two evaluations of the same answer:

πV1.Roll_No, V1.Question_No(σV1.Roll_No=V2.Roll_No ∧ V1.Question_No=V2.Question_No ∧ V1.Examiner≠V2.Examiner ∧ V1.Marks−V2.Marks>2(ρV1(Evaluation) × ρV2(Evaluation)))

No absolute value is needed: the product contains both orders of each pair, and whichever examiner gave more appears as V1 in one of them. (The bank writes the ∨ of the two differences without brackets, which by precedence attaches only to the last conjunct; the version above avoids that.)

Sample data (two questions instead of ten, to stay small): rooms 101 (501, 502, 505) and 103 (506, 508) violate (i); (ii) finds (503, 2) marked once, (504, 1) marked three times, and (506, 2) never marked, while absent 508 is ignored; (iii) finds (502, 1) with 8 vs 4 and (505, 2) with 4 vs 7.

Summary: Extended RA adds γ (GROUP BY with SUM/AVG/MIN/MAX/COUNT; HAVING = σ on top), generalized projection with computed attributes, τ (sorting, outermost only), δ (for bags) and assignment. Updates are assignments: insert R ← R ∪ E, delete R ← R − σ(R), update R ← π…f(A)→A…(σc(R)) ∪ σ¬c(R). Keep a key in the input of γ so set semantics does not merge tuples. RA cannot express recursion.
