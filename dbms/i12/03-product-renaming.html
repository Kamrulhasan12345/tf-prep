Cartesian Product (×) and Renaming (ρ)

The cross product combines two relations by pairing every row of one with every row of the other. Renaming changes names without touching rows. Together they are what lets you compare a relation with itself, which many exam queries need.

1. Cartesian product × (slides 12–13)

Cartesian product R × S
The relation containing every tuple formed by joining one tuple of R with one tuple of S (slide 12: "each tuple in R1 with each tuple in R2"). Its schema is all attributes of R followed by all attributes of S. If both have an attribute called A, the result has two attributes, written R.A and S.A.
Size of a product
|R × S| = |R| · |S| tuples, and arity(R × S) = arity(R) + arity(S).

Slide 12 uses Students(sid, sname, gpa) and People(ssn, pname, address):

SELECT *
FROM Students, People;

Students × People

A comma-separated FROM list is exactly a Cartesian product. The slide's other example is Employee × Dependents, which pairs every employee with every dependent, including other employees' dependents. That is why slide 12 adds that × is "rare in practice; mainly used to express joins": on its own it mostly produces meaningless pairs. Followed by a selection that keeps the sensible pairs (σEmployee.SSN = Dependents.SSN), it becomes a join (Chapter 4).

Slide 13's example

Two people times two students gives 2 × 2 = 4 tuples with 3 + 3 = 6 attributes. One correction: in the slide's result the fourth row shows Bob (ssn 5423341) at "216 Rosse", but People says Bob lives at 217 Rosse. A product never changes values; it only copies them, so the correct fourth row is (5423341, Bob, 217 Rosse, 002, Bob, 1.3). The slide lists People's attributes first, which by the named perspective is the same relation as Students × People.

2. Renaming ρ (slides 14–16)

Renaming ρS(B1,…,Bn)(R)
Returns the same tuples as R, but the relation is called S and its attributes are called B1, …, Bn (in the order of R's attributes). It "changes the schema, not the instance" (slide 14).
Shorthand ρS(A1→B1, …, An→Bn)(R)
Since attributes are identified by name, not order, the proper form says which old name becomes which new name. ρS(R) renames only the relation; ρA→B(R) renames only attribute A.

The slide calls ρ a "special" operator: neither basic nor derived. It cannot be built from σ, π, ×, ∪, −, but it never changes any data either; it only changes how attributes are referred to. In SQL it is the AS keyword (slide 15):

SELECT sid AS studId,
       sname AS name,
       gpa AS gradePtAvg
FROM Students;

ρStudents(studId, name, gradePtAvg)(Students)

Why renaming is needed

  1. Comparing a relation with itself. To compare two tuples of the same relation, you need two copies with different names: ρS1(Students) × ρS2(Students). Without renaming, Students × Students would have two attributes called Students.gpa, and a condition could not say which one it means.
  2. Controlling a natural join. A natural join matches attributes with equal names (Chapter 4). Renaming makes two attributes match (sname → name) or stops an accidental match.
  3. Union compatibility. ∪, ∩ and − need the same attributes on both sides (Chapter 5); ρ can make the names agree.
  4. Naming intermediate results, as in R1 := … on slide 48 (Chapter 8).

A self-product, step by step

"Find pairs of students where the first has a higher gpa than the second." Make two copies, pair them, keep the pairs that satisfy the comparison, and project:

πS1.sname, S2.sname(σS1.gpa > S2.gpa(ρS1(Students) × ρS2(Students)))

Notice that the pair (Rina, Tanvir) appears but (Tanvir, Rina) does not: the strict > already orders each pair. When the condition is symmetric (for example "same gpa"), add S1.sid < S2.sid so each pair is listed once and a student is not paired with itself. Slide 47's "pairs of PC models with the same speed and RAM, listed once" uses exactly this trick (Chapter 8).

Summary: R × S pairs every tuple of R with every tuple of S: |R|·|S| tuples, arity(R)+arity(S) attributes, clashing names qualified as R.A and S.A. It is a comma in FROM. ρS(B1,…,Bn)(R) renames the relation and/or its attributes without changing any tuple (SQL's AS). Renaming is essential for self-products such as ρS1(R) × ρS2(R).
