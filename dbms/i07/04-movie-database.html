Building an ERD Step by Step: the Movie Database

The slides' six-step workflow, applied to the class activity: from a paragraph about actors, directors, films and studios to the finished diagram of slide 41, one decision at a time.

1. A practical ERD workflow (slide 36)

  1. Read the requirement once for the story. Do not draw yet; understand what the organisation does.
  2. Mark important nouns as candidate entity sets.
  3. Mark verbs or verb phrases as candidate relationships.
  4. Attach descriptive facts as attributes.
  5. Add keys and multiplicity constraints.
  6. Test the diagram using small example scenarios.

The order matters. Structure (boxes and diamonds) first, then attributes, then constraints. Deciding multiplicity before the structure is settled wastes effort, because a relationship may still move or split.

2. The class activity (slide 37)

  • For actors and directors, store name, unique ID, address, and date of birth.
  • For films, store title, production year, and type, such as thriller or comedy.
  • Store who directed each film and who acted in each film.
  • Every film has one director.
  • Store each actor's salary for each film.
  • For studios, store name, address, and president. A film is produced by only one studio.

3. Marking the text (slides 38–40)

Slides 38–40 colour the requirement three times. Step through them:

Some judgement is needed at each pass:

  • Entity nouns: actors, directors, films, studios. "Type" and "president" are nouns too, but they are single descriptive values about a film and a studio, so they are attributes (slide 16's question: one value, or an object to describe?).
  • Relationship verbs: directed (Director–Film), acted in (Actor–Film), produced by (Film–Studio). "Every film has one director" is not a new relationship; it is the multiplicity of directs.
  • Attributes: name, unique ID, address and date of birth for actors and for directors; title, production year and type for films; name, address and president for studios; and salary, which is "each actor's salary for each film": it depends on the pair (actor, film), so it goes on actedIn, exactly like grade on enrolls (Chapter 2).

Question (worked answer): "Every film has one director." Does the arrow capture "every"?

Only half of it. The arrow says at most one director per film. "Every film has one" also means at least one: no film without a director. In the slides' notation that second half is written as a design note beside the diagram ("every film has exactly one director"), as on slide 96. Chen's notation draws it as a double line from Film to directs (total participation, Chapter 10). When converted to tables (Chapter 11), it becomes a NOT NULL foreign key column director_id in Film.

Question (worked answer): Actors and directors have the same attributes. Why two entity sets?

The slide keeps them separate because they play different parts: directors are connected to films through directs (one per film) and actors through actedIn (many per film, with a salary). A person who both acts and directs would appear in both sets with the same ID, which duplicates their name and address. A cleaner alternative uses Chapter 7's subclasses: an entity set Person(id, name, address, dateOfBirth) with subclasses Actor and Director, each keeping its own relationship. Both designs are acceptable answers; say which assumption you make.

5. Test the diagram (workflow step 6)

Try concrete scenarios against the finished diagram:

  • Can Tom Hanks act in two films with two different salaries? Yes: two actedIn pairs, each with its own salary.
  • Can a film have two directors? No: the arrow forbids it. If the domain allows co-directors (it does, in reality), the arrow must go; that is a requirement question, not a drawing question.
  • Can a studio produce no films yet? Yes: nothing forces a studio to take part in produces.
  • Can we record a film's studio's president? Yes, through produces: Film → Studio → president. We must not also store a presidentName on Film (Principle 2).

DB QB · Jan 20 13 · 10 marks  ·  BUET QBank · CSE 215 | L-2/T-2 | 2018–19 · 10 marks

Suppose you are the manager of the website development department of your organization. Each website developed by your organization can be uniquely identified by a url. It also has a public host-ip address and a date of going live. Each website is developed by a team of developers. Each developer can be uniquely identified by an employee id. The relevant information for a developer is his/her name, address, highest educational qualification, salary and hire date. Each website is developed by a web-based framework which is associated with a particular programming language. Each framework and its associated programming language have a specific name and the development year. The other information relevant to a web framework is its author name. Now design a Entity Relationship Diagram for your website development department so that you can manage it conveniently.

Show worked answer

Nouns → entity sets: Website, Developer, Framework, Language. Language is an entity set (not an attribute of Framework) because it has its own facts (name, development year) and several frameworks can share one language.

Verbs → relationships: developedBy (Website–Developer), builtWith (Website–Framework), uses (Framework–Language).

Assumptions: (1) a developer may work on several websites over time, so developedBy is many-to-many; (2) a website uses exactly one framework ("developed by a web-based framework"), many websites can use the same framework: arrow into Framework; (3) a framework is associated with exactly one language, a language can have many frameworks: arrow into Language; (4) framework names and language names are unique, so they are keys.

Relational schema (Chapter 11 rules): Website(url, host_ip, live_date, framework_name), Developer(emp_id, name, address, qualification, salary, hire_date), DevelopedBy(url, emp_id), Framework(name, dev_year, author, language_name), Language(name, dev_year). Italic columns are foreign keys; framework_name and language_name are NOT NULL because participation is total. The question bank's answer is the same design.

Summary: read the story, mark nouns (entity sets), verbs (relationships) and descriptive facts (attributes), then add keys and multiplicities, then test with scenarios. In the movie database, salary depends on (actor, film) so it sits on actedIn; "every film has one director" gives an arrow into Director plus a design note for "every"; Film's key is the pair (title, year).
