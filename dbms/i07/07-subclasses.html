Subclasses, isA, and the Limits of Key Notation

Some entities are special cases of others: every software product is a product, with extra facts of its own. This chapter explains subclasses and the isA connection, the two design questions (overlapping or disjoint, total or partial), and what ER notation cannot say about keys.

1. Modeling subclasses (slide 72)

Some entities in an entity set may be special cases: they have everything the general entity has, plus more. A shop sells products; some products are software (with supported platforms), some are educational (with a target age group).

Subclass
An entity set whose entities are all members of another entity set, the superclass (or parent). A subclass inherits every attribute and relationship of its parent and may add its own attributes or relationships.
isA
The connection from a subclass to its superclass. "A isA B" means every A is also a B. The slides draw it as a diamond labelled isA joining the parent (above) to its subclasses (below). The textbook (Garcia-Molina et al. §4.1.11) and many exam papers draw a small triangle labelled isa pointing at the parent; Elmasri–Navathe draw a circle with d (disjoint) or o (overlapping). All mean the same thing.

2. Subclass attributes (slide 73)

Parent attributes apply to every subclass entity. Subclass attributes apply only to entities in that subclass. Every product has name and price; only software products have platforms; only educational products have an ageGroup. The key comes from the parent: a software product is identified by the same key as the product it is. Subclasses therefore normally have no key attribute of their own.

Why not just give Product the attributes platforms and ageGroup? Because most products would have no value for them (a chair has no platform), and because a subclass can also take part in relationships that the general entity set does not, for example SoftwareProduct runsOn OperatingSystem. Slide 76 lists both uses.

3. Subclass design questions (slides 74–75)

Each hierarchy needs answers to three questions, which become constraints in the design:

  1. Disjoint or overlapping? Can an entity belong to several subclasses at once?
  2. Total or partial? Does every parent entity have to belong to some subclass?
  3. Where does each attribute go? On the parent if it applies to every entity; on a subclass if it applies only there.
Overlapping subclasses
An entity may belong to more than one subclass. Slide 75: in ER modeling subclasses may overlap unless the design says otherwise. A product may be both a software product and an educational product.
Disjoint subclasses
An entity belongs to at most one subclass (an account is savings or checking, never both). Must be stated explicitly.
Total (complete) specialization
Every parent entity belongs to at least one subclass. Partial: some parent entities belong to no subclass.

Slide 75's example: KidPix, a drawing program for children, is in Product, in SoftwareProduct and in EducationalProduct. This differs from many object-oriented class systems, where an object belongs to exactly one most-specific class, so the classes are effectively disjoint; in Java a KidPix object could not be both a SoftwareProduct and an EducationalProduct without a third class that extends both.

4. isA review (slide 76)

  • If A isA B, then every A is also a B. (The reverse need not hold.)
  • Use isA to add descriptive attributes to a subclass.
  • Use isA when a subclass connects to a relationship differently (e.g. only Faculty, a subclass of Employee, can advise students).
  • isA is a modeling connection, not an ordinary relationship. It has no multiplicity arrows and no attributes, and it does not pair two different entities: a KidPix entity in SoftwareProduct is the KidPix entity in Product, viewed with more attributes. Chapter 12 shows the three ways to turn it into tables.

5. Keys: limits of ER notation (slide 77)

ER diagrams show keys by underlining attributes, and a key may have one attribute or several. But an entity set can have several candidate keys: a student is identified by student_id and also by email, and also by national ID. Basic ER notation underlines one key (the primary key) and has no clean way to show the others. Record extra candidate-key constraints in accompanying design notes, for example "email is also unique". In Oracle they become UNIQUE constraints (Lec 06):

CREATE TABLE Student (
  student_id NUMBER(9)    PRIMARY KEY,
  email      VARCHAR2(60) NOT NULL UNIQUE,  -- design note: 2nd candidate key
  name       VARCHAR2(40)
);

The same holds for any rule the diagram cannot express (Chapter 5): keep design notes beside the diagram.

BUET QBank · CSE 303 | L-3/T-1 | 2014–15 · 9 marks

Show an example of specialization hierarchy in the context of ERD with disjoint and overlapping subtypes.

Show worked answer

Specialization is the top-down process of defining subclasses (subtypes) of an entity set; each subtype inherits the supertype's attributes and relationships and adds its own. Example, a university:

  • Person(nid, name, address) is the root.
  • Person → {Student(major), Employee(salary)} is overlapping and partial: a teaching assistant is both a student and an employee; an alumnus is a person who is neither.
  • Employee → {Faculty(rank), Staff(position)} is disjoint and total: every employee is exactly one of faculty or staff.

Reading it: nid 101 may appear in Person, Student and Employee at once (overlapping), but under Employee it is in Faculty or in Staff, not both (disjoint), and it must be in one of them (total). Faculty inherits nid, name, address from Person and salary from Employee, and adds rank.

Summary: a subclass (A isA B) contains special cases of its parent: it inherits the parent's attributes, key and relationships and adds its own. Ask whether subclasses are disjoint or overlapping (in ER they overlap unless stated) and whether the specialization is total or partial. isA is a modeling connection, not a relationship. Extra candidate keys and other rules the diagram cannot show go in design notes and become UNIQUE constraints.
