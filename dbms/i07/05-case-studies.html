Five Case Studies, and When a Relationship Becomes an Entity

Five short requirements, each drawn step by step: course registration, departments offering courses, advising, library loans and product reviews. Then the rules for reading multiplicity from sentences, for placing attributes on relationships, and for promoting a relationship to an entity set.

1. Case study 1: course registration (slides 42–47)

A university stores students and courses. Each student has a student ID, name, and department. Each course has a course code, title, and credits. Students enroll in courses each semester and receive a grade.

The slides follow the workflow of Chapter 4 exactly: start with the nouns, then decide which facts describe nouns and which describe associations.

  • Step 1 (slide 43): Student and Course are things we store. Semester may be an attribute or an entity, depending on the domain: if the university only needs the label "January 2025", it is an attribute; if semesters have their own facts (start date, end date, registration deadline), Semester becomes an entity set (Chapter 2, Principle 4).
  • Step 2 (slide 44): the verb phrase "enroll in" connects Student and Course. Name the diamond so it reads naturally: Student enrolls Course.
  • Step 3 (slide 45): student_id, name; course_code, title as entity attributes (department and credits are omitted on the slide for space; they belong to Student and Course). Semester and grade go on enrolls: the grade belongs to the enrollment, not to Student or Course alone.
  • Step 4 (slide 46): one student can enroll in many courses, and one course can have many students, so both connectors stay plain: many-to-many.

Testing the ERD (slide 47)

Try concrete examples. Amina enrolls in CSE101 and CSE103. Rahim also enrolls in CSE101.

  • Can one student connect to multiple courses? Yes: (Amina, CSE101) and (Amina, CSE103) are two pairs.
  • Can one course connect to multiple students? Yes: (Amina, CSE101) and (Rahim, CSE101).
  • Can each enrollment have its own semester and grade? Yes: each pair carries its own values.

Question (worked answer): can Amina take CSE101 twice, failing in January and passing in July?

Not with this diagram. A relationship set is a set of pairs, so the pair (Amina, CSE101) can appear only once, with one semester and one grade. To record retakes we need the semester to be part of what identifies an enrollment. Two fixes: make Semester an entity set and enrolls a three-way relationship (Student, Course, Semester; Chapter 6), or turn the enrollment into an entity set whose key includes the semester (section 6 below). This is precisely the kind of problem step 6, "test with scenarios", is meant to catch.

2. Case study 2: department offers courses (slides 48–49)

Each course is offered by exactly one department. A department can offer many courses. A department has a department ID and name.

Entity sets Department and Course; relationship offeredBy. "Each course is offered by exactly one department": fix a course, at most one department, so the arrow goes into Department. "Exactly" adds that every course has one; that is the participation constraint, recorded as a design note (or a double line from Course in Chen's notation, Chapter 10).

3. Case study 3: advising (slides 50–51)

Students are assigned advisors. An advisor is a faculty member. A student has exactly one advisor, but a faculty member can advise many students. Store the date from which advising started.

"An advisor is a faculty member" tells us that Advisor is not a separate entity set: advisors are entities of Faculty, playing the role of advisor in the relationship advises. The date describes the advising assignment, so start_date is a relationship attribute. The arrow into Faculty says a student has at most one advisor. (Slides 94–96 repeat this case as the practice task; see Chapter 9.)

4. Case study 4: library loans (slides 52–53)

A member can borrow many books. A book can be borrowed by many members over time. For each loan, store issue date, due date, and return date.

"Over time" makes the relationship many-to-many: at any moment a book is with at most one member, but across the years it is borrowed by many. The dates are facts about one loan.

Question (worked answer): the same member borrows the same book twice. What happens?

As with retakes in case study 1, the pair (member, book) can appear only once in the relationship set, so the second loan would overwrite the first. If the library must keep loan history, the loan should become an entity set Loan (with its own loan_id, or with issue_date as part of its identity) connected to Member and Book; see section 6. The slide's simpler diagram is fine for a system that records only the current or latest loan.

5. Case study 5: product reviews (slides 54–55)

Customers review products. A customer may review many products. A product may receive many reviews. A customer can review the same product only once. Each review has rating and comment.

Here the "only once" rule is satisfied automatically: a relationship set contains each (customer, product) pair at most once. The slide still writes it as a design note, because a reader of the diagram might otherwise not realise it is a requirement, and because it will become the primary key (customer_id, product_id) of the Reviews table (Chapter 11). Rating and comment are relationship attributes.

6. Rules distilled from the cases (slides 56–60)

Reading multiplicity from sentences (slide 56)

SentenceERD decision
Each course has one department.Arrow toward Department.
A department has many courses.Plain connector toward Course.
A student enrolls in many courses.Plain connector toward Course.
A passport belongs to one person.Arrow toward Person.

The pattern: "each X has one Y" puts an arrow into Y; "X has many Y" leaves the line into Y plain. Note that every sentence talks about one side only; you need a sentence (or an assumption) for each side.

When to use a relationship attribute (slide 57)

Ask whether the fact describes an entity or an association. grade describes Student taking Course; start_date describes Faculty advising Student; return_date describes Member borrowing Book. Rule of thumb: if the fact only makes sense after two or more entities are connected, put it on the relationship.

When to create a new entity set (slides 58–59)

Create an entity set when the thing has its own identity, when it has many attributes, or when it connects to other relationships. Slide 58's example: if Enrollment has grade, semester, payment status, retake status and approval history, it may deserve its own entity set. Slide 59 shows the change:

After the change, Enrollment is an entity set connected to Student and to Course by two binary relationships. Each enrollment belongs to exactly one student and one course (arrows from Enrollment's relationships into Student and Course), while a student has many enrollments. Unlike the old relationship, two enrollments may now have the same student and course, as long as they differ in semester. This is the same move as converting a multi-way relationship to binary ones (Chapter 6, slides 69–70).

Step-by-step habit (slide 60)

  • Do not try to draw the final ERD immediately.
  • First draw entity sets and relationships only.
  • Add attributes after the structure feels right.
  • Add multiplicity last, after asking concrete domain questions.
  • Keep design notes for rules that the diagram cannot express clearly.

BUET QBank · CSE 303 | L-3/T-1 | 2015–16 · 7 marks

East West Property Development is a construction company of Bashundhara Group with over 1000 employees. A customer can hire the company for more than one project, and employees sometimes work on more than one project at a time. Equipment is assigned to only one project. Draw an E-R diagram for this company indicating cardinality.

Show worked answer

Entity sets: Customer, Project, Employee, Equipment. Relationships from the verbs: hires (Customer–Project), worksOn (Employee–Project), assignedTo (Equipment–Project).

Cardinalities, sentence by sentence (slide 56 method):

  • "A customer can hire the company for more than one project": plain line into Project. Assumption: a project is commissioned by one customer, so an arrow into Customer. hires is one-to-many from Customer to Project (Customer 1 : N Project).
  • "Employees sometimes work on more than one project at a time": plain line into Project. A project obviously needs many employees: plain line into Employee. worksOn is many-to-many (M : N). A useful attribute is hours per (employee, project).
  • "Equipment is assigned to only one project": arrow into Project; a project uses many pieces of equipment. assignedTo is many-to-one from Equipment to Project (N : 1).

Attributes are not given, so the keys are assumed identifiers.

Summary: nouns become entity sets, verbs relationships, and facts that only make sense for a pair (grade, start_date, the loan dates, rating) go on the relationship. Read each multiplicity sentence one side at a time. Keep rules the diagram cannot show as design notes. When an association gains identity, many attributes or relationships of its own, or must repeat for the same pair, promote it to an entity set.
