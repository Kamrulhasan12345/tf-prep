Attribute Kinds, Participation and the Other Notations

Exam papers use more notation than the slides: composite, multivalued and derived attributes, double lines for total participation, 1/N/M labels, (min, max) pairs and crow's feet. This chapter teaches each one from the textbooks (Silberschatz; Elmasri–Navathe) and maps it to the slides' arrows.

The slides' notation (Garcia-Molina–Ullman–Widom) is deliberately small. Many past questions, however, were set from Silberschatz or Elmasri–Navathe and expect the richer Chen-style notation. Everything here extends, and never contradicts, what the slides teach.

1. Kinds of attributes

Simple (atomic) attribute
Cannot be divided into meaningful parts: salary, gender.
Composite attribute
Made of component attributes: name = (first, middle, last); address = (street, city, postal_code). Drawn as an oval with its components hanging off it. Useful when users sometimes need the whole value and sometimes a part ("all customers in Dhaka").
Single-valued vs multivalued attribute
A single-valued attribute has one value per entity (date of birth). A multivalued attribute may have several (phone numbers, email addresses, skills). Drawn as a double oval; in Silberschatz's text written {phone}.
Derived attribute
Its value can be computed from other stored data: age from date of birth, number of employees from the works-in relationship, a total from line items. Drawn as a dashed oval.
Key attribute
Underlined (Chapter 1); partial key of a weak entity: dashed underline (Chapter 8).

How these relate to the slides: a multivalued attribute is a lightweight version of slide 18's "model repeating values as entities". If the values have no structure and nothing else refers to them (phone numbers), a double oval is enough; if they have structure or relationships (addresses shared by several people), make an entity set. Both become a separate table in Chapter 11. A composite attribute keeps the structure of a value without making it an entity.

2. Total and partial participation

Participation constraint
Says whether every entity of a set must take part in a relationship. Total participation: every entity appears in at least one relationship instance (minimum 1); drawn as a double line between the entity set and the diamond. Partial participation: some entities may appear in none (minimum 0); single line.

Participation is independent of multiplicity. Multiplicity bounds the maximum (at most one, or many); participation sets the minimum (zero or one). The slides write the minimum as a design note ("every student has exactly one advisor"); Chen-style diagrams draw it.

3. The four ways to write multiplicity

  1. Slides / Garcia-Molina: arrow into Department (each employee at most one department); "exactly one" and "one or more" as design notes. (The textbook also has a rounded arrow for "exactly one".)
  2. Chen (Silberschatz older editions, Elmasri): labels N next to Employee and 1 next to Department; double lines for total participation.
  3. (min, max) on each line (Elmasri): the pair next to an entity set says how many relationship instances each of its entities takes part in. Employee (1,1): each employee is in exactly one worksIn pair. Department (1,N): each department in one or more. Careful: (min,max) labels sit on the opposite side from Chen's 1/N labels. In Chen, "1" next to Department means "one department per employee"; in (min,max), (1,1) next to Employee means "one pair per employee". Both describe the same constraint.
  4. Crow's foot (many tools and the "production tracking" exam figure): a fork means many, a bar means one, a circle means zero. Read the two marks nearest an entity set as (minimum, maximum) of that side: "|<" = one or many, "||" = exactly one, "o<" = zero or many.

DB QB · 24-25 7d · 10 marks  ·  BUET QBank · CSE 215 | L-2/T-1 | 2024–25 · 10 marks

Explain the concept of total and partial participation of entities of the entity set in the relationship set using an example ER diagram.

Show worked answer

A participation constraint states the minimum number of relationship instances an entity must take part in. Entity set E has total participation in relationship R if every entity of E appears in at least one instance of R (minimum 1; existence dependency). It is drawn as a double line between E and R. E has partial participation if some entities of E may appear in no instance of R (minimum 0); single line.

Example. Employee manages Department (one-to-one). Not every employee manages a department, so Employee's participation is partial. Every department must have a manager, so Department's participation is total. A second relationship, worksIn, has total participation of Employee (every employee works in a department) and partial participation of Department (a newly created department may have no staff yet).

BUET QBank · CSE 303 | L-3/T-1 | 2013–14 · 8 marks

What are the advantages and disadvantages of storing derived attributes in tables?

Show worked answer

A derived attribute (dashed oval) can be computed from other data, e.g. age from dob, total of an order from its line items, totalTeam of a university from its teams (slide 82). Storing it means keeping a physical column with the computed value.

Advantages of storing:

  • Faster reads: no recomputation; especially valuable for expensive aggregates (SUM, COUNT over many rows, joins).
  • Can be indexed and used directly in WHERE / ORDER BY.
  • Simpler queries for users and reports.
  • Can freeze history: an invoice total stored at sale time stays correct even if prices change later (then it is no longer purely derived).

Disadvantages:

  • Redundancy and inconsistency: the same fact is stored twice (Principle 2). If the base data changes and the derived value is not updated, the database contradicts itself (update anomaly).
  • Maintenance cost on writes: every insert/update/delete of the base data must also update the derived value, via triggers or application code, slowing writes.
  • Values that change with time (age) become wrong automatically unless refreshed daily.
  • Extra storage.

Rule of thumb: compute on demand (a view or an Oracle virtual column: age AS (TRUNC(MONTHS_BETWEEN(SYSDATE, dob)/12)) is not allowed because SYSDATE is non-deterministic, so use a view) for cheap or time-dependent values; store, with a trigger or a materialized view, only expensive aggregates that are read far more often than their base data changes.

Summary: composite attributes have components; multivalued attributes (double oval) hold several values; derived attributes (dashed oval) can be computed. Total participation (double line) means every entity must take part (minimum 1); partial means it may not (minimum 0). Multiplicity sets the maximum, participation the minimum. Arrows, Chen's 1/N labels, (min,max) pairs and crow's feet are four spellings of the same constraints.
