Karnaugh maps: PI, EPI, don't-cares, SOP and POS

A K-map question appears in every paper and is often the first thing you do before a circuit. 2017–18 Q1c (12, SOP with don't-cares, all PIs, EPIs and the responsible minterm of each EPI); 2018–19 Q1c (12, the same task); 2020–21 Q2b (15, 5 variables, PIs, EPIs, all minimal sums, responsible minterms); 2019–20 Q6b (20, Σ(0,1,2,5,7,11,13) to a minimal POS with its circuit); 2022–23 Q3b (14, Σ(0,1,2,5,8,9,10) as SOP and POS); 2023–24 Q1c (7, prime implicant against essential prime implicant) and Q2c (16, Σ(0,1,2,4,5,6,8,9,12,13,14) minimised, then NAND-only circuit); 2024–25 Q2c (15, Σ(3,4,6,7,9,11,12,14,15) to POS, plus the purpose of tabulation). CT2 2026 Q1 (12, 5-variable map with don't-cares, all PIs and the minimum SOP). Older papers repeat the same pattern (5-variable, POS with don't-cares, PI/EPI of Σ(0,2,3,5,7,8,9,10,11,13,15)). With the K-map you can answer about 60 marks of Section A in a paper.

1. Her map layouts

Each square is one minterm. Axes are written in Gray order (00, 01, 11, 10) so that neighbouring squares differ in exactly one variable. Adjacent means left–right or up–down, including wrap-around (left edge with right edge, top with bottom). Diagonals are not adjacent. The four corners of a 4-variable map are adjacent to each other.

  • 2 variables: rows A' , A; columns B', B. Cells m₀ m₁ / m₂ m₃.
  • 3 variables (corner label A \ BC): columns 00 01 11 10, rows A', A. Cells m₀ m₁ m₃ m₂ / m₄ m₅ m₇ m₆.
  • 4 variables (corner label AB \ CD, or wx \ yz): columns 00 01 11 10, rows 00 01 11 10. Cells m₀ m₁ m₃ m₂ / m₄ m₅ m₇ m₆ / m₁₂ m₁₃ m₁₅ m₁₄ / m₈ m₉ m₁₁ m₁₀.
  • 5 variables: two 4-variable maps side by side, A = 0 (m₀–m₁₅) on the left and A = 1 (m₁₆–m₃₁) on the right. Each map is labelled BC \ DE. Squares in the same position in the two maps are adjacent (m₅ and m₂₁). Six variables: four maps (heading only in her notes).

2. The procedure and the definitions

Implicant
A group of 1, 2, 4, 8 … adjacent 1-squares (a power of 2; never 3 or 6). It is one product term.
Prime implicant (PI)
An implicant that is not contained in any larger implicant. The biggest possible group.
Essential prime implicant (EPI)
A PI that covers at least one 1-square that no other PI covers. Such a square is its responsible minterm. An EPI must be in the answer.
  1. Fill the map: 1 for each minterm of F, X for each don't-care, 0 elsewhere.
  2. Find every prime implicant: for each 1, look for the largest group it can be in (don't-cares may be used as 1s).
  3. Find the essential ones first: a 1 that can be in only one PI forces that PI. Write the responsible minterm beside it.
  4. Cover the remaining 1s with as few and as large groups as possible. Do not add a group whose 1s are all already covered (redundant).
  5. Read each group: variables that stay the same stay in the term (0 → complemented), variables that change vanish.
  6. OR the terms. Check that every 1 is covered and no 0 is.

Her three rules (p28): maximise group size, minimise the number of groups, avoid redundancy. "Identify the essential prime implicants first to avoid redundancy later."

PI against EPI (2023–24 Q1c, 7 marks · 2011–12)

2023–24 Q1(c) · 7 marks

Differentiate prime implicant and essential prime implicant. (Older: 2014–15 and 2022–23 use F(A,B,C,D) = Σ(0,2,3,5,7,8,9,10,11,13,15) for this.)

Answer

A prime implicant is a group of adjacent 1s that cannot be enlarged, i.e. it is not part of a bigger group. A function usually has more PIs than it needs. An essential prime implicant is a PI that covers at least one minterm (its responsible minterm) that is covered by no other PI. EPIs must appear in every minimal expression; the other PIs are optional and are chosen only to cover what the EPIs leave.

Example, Σ(0,2,3,5,7,8,9,10,11,13,15): there are six PIs, B'D', B'C, CD, BD, AB', AD. Only B'D' (responsible minterm 0) and BD (responsible minterm 5) are essential. Minterms 3, 9, 11 are left, and each of them is in two PIs, so we choose any one of B'C, CD for 3 and one of AB', AD for 9. This gives four equally minimal answers, for example B'D' + BD + CD + AD. A map with no EPI at all is called cyclic.

What to write (PI / EPI, K-map question, 7–12 marks)

  1. The map, drawn with its Gray-order labels and every minterm written as 1 (and X for don't-cares).
  2. All groups drawn and labelled with their product term. List all PIs with the minterms each covers.
  3. The EPI list, with the responsible minterm beside each ("m₇ only in w'xy").
  4. The minimal expression: EPIs plus the extra PIs you chose, and one line saying why those were chosen.

3. Don't-cares: SOP with PIs and EPIs

A don't-care (X) is an input that never occurs, or whose output nobody cares about. Treat each X as 0 or 1, whichever makes a bigger group. Three consequences: (1) X can be in a group but it never has to be covered. (2) A PI made only of X-squares is useless. (3) The responsible minterm must be a real 1, never an X. The answer is called a simplified form even though it may differ from F on the don't-care inputs. Her example: F = Σ(1,3,7,11,15) + d(0,2,5) gives yz + w'z (or yz + w'x'), "not equal for all possible inputs".

2017–18 Q1(c) · 12 marks

Using a K-map minimise f(w,x,y,z) = Σ(1, 2, 4, 7, 9, 10, 12, 14) + d(0, 6, 8, 13). Find the prime implicants and the essential prime implicants. Write down the responsible minterm for each EPI.

Answer

All PIs: w'xy (6, 7), x'y' (0, 1, 8, 9), wy' (8, 9, 12, 13), z' (0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14).

EPIs and responsible minterms: x'y' (m₁), z' (m₂, m₄, m₁₀, m₁₄), w'xy (m₇). The PI wy' is not essential: its real 1s are 9 and 12, and 9 is also in x'y', 12 is also in z'.

Minimal SOP: f = z' + x'y' + w'xy. It uses X at 0, 6, 8, 13 only where they make the groups larger (0, 6, 8 are inside the chosen groups; 13 is left uncovered, which is allowed).

2018–19 Q1(c) · 12 marks

Using a K-map minimise f(w,x,y,z) = Σ(1, 2, 3, 7, 10, 12, 14) + d(0, 6, 8, 13). Find the PIs and EPIs, and write the responsible minterm for each EPI.

Answer

All PIs (six): w'x' (0, 1, 2, 3), w'y (2, 3, 6, 7), x'z' (0, 2, 8, 10), yz' (2, 6, 10, 14), wz' (8, 10, 12, 14), wxy' (12, 13).

EPIs: w'x' (responsible minterm m₁) and w'y (responsible minterm m₇). After these, 10, 12 and 14 are uncovered, and wz' covers all three at once.

Minimal SOP: f = w'x' + w'y + wz'.

4. POS from a K-map

  1. Mark the 0-squares (X may be taken as 0 if it helps).
  2. Group the 0s exactly as you group 1s. Read the groups as products: this gives F' as an SOP.
  3. Complement with De Morgan: complement every literal and swap · with +. Each group becomes one sum term; F = product of the sum terms.

Shortcut: while reading a 0-group, write each variable complemented when it is 1 in the group and plain when it is 0. That gives the sum term directly.

2022–23 Q3(b) · 14 marks · also 2011–12 style

Simplify F(A,B,C,D) = Σ(0,1,2,5,8,9,10) into (a) sum-of-products and (b) product-of-sums.

Answer

(a) SOP: groups B'D' (0, 2, 8, 10), B'C' (0, 1, 8, 9), A'C'D (1, 5). All three are essential (responsible minterms 2/10, 9, 5). F = B'D' + B'C' + A'C'D.

(b) POS: group the 0s. The zeros are 3, 4, 6, 7, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, which give the groups CD (3, 7, 11, 15), BD' (4, 6, 12, 14) and AB (12, 13, 14, 15). So F' = CD + BD' + AB. De Morgan: F = (C' + D')(B' + D)(A' + B').

2024–25 Q2(c) · 15 marks (with the tabulation theory part)

Simplify F = Σ(3, 4, 6, 7, 9, 11, 12, 14, 15) to product-of-sums. What is the main purpose of the tabulation (Quine–McCluskey) method and how does it help compared to a K-map?

Answer

Zeros of F: 0, 1, 2, 5, 8, 10, 13. Groups: B'D' (0, 2, 8, 10), and then 1 and 5 and 13 need A'B'C' (0, 1) or A'C'D (1, 5), and BC'D (5, 13). So F' = B'D' + A'B'C' + BC'D (or B'D' + A'C'D + BC'D), 8 literals either way.

F = (B + D)(A + B + C)(B' + C + D'), or equivalently (B + D)(A + C + D')(B' + C + D'). 8 literals is the minimum. For the tabulation part see chapter 5.

5. Five-variable maps

Use the two-map layout above. Groups can sit in one map or span both maps in the same position. A group of 4 inside one map, or a group of 4 formed by 2 + 2 squares in matching positions across the two maps, drops 2 variables; a group of 8 can span the two maps too. The variable that selects the map (A, or v) is dropped from a PI exactly when the group appears in both maps.

CT2 2026 Q1 · 12 marks

F(A,B,C,D,E) = Σ(0, 2, 5, 7, 8, 16, 18, 21) with don't-cares d = Σ(10, 31). Using the 5-variable K-map (two 4-variable maps, A = 0 on the left, A = 1 on the right, each BC \ DE): (i) find all prime implicants; (ii) find the minimum SOP.

Answer

(i) Prime implicants (four, all essential):

  • A'C'E' = {0, 2, 8, 10}: m₁₀ is a don't-care used to complete the group of four. Responsible minterm: m₈.
  • B'C'E' = {0, 2, 16, 18}: m₀, m₂ in the left map and m₁₆, m₁₈ in the same positions of the right map. Responsible minterms: m₁₆, m₁₈.
  • A'B'CE = {5, 7}. Responsible minterm: m₇.
  • B'CD'E = {5, 21}: m₅ and m₂₁ in the same position of the two maps. Responsible minterm: m₂₁.

The don't-care m₃₁ has no neighbour that is a 1 or an X, so it makes no useful group (its only PI is ABCDE, which covers no 1 and is dropped). (5, 7, 21, 23) is not a group because m₂₃ is neither a 1 nor an X.

(ii) F = A'C'E' + B'C'E' + A'B'CE + B'CD'E. All four PIs are essential, so this is the only minimal sum. The 1s 0, 2 and 5 are each covered twice, which is allowed. (It factors as C'E'(A' + B') + B'CE(A' + D'), but the question asks for the SOP.)

6. K-map result to a circuit

Once you have the minimal SOP, drawing it as AND-OR or NAND-NAND is routine (chapter 4). For 2023–24 Q2c (Σ(0,1,2,4,5,6,8,9,12,13,14) with NAND gates only) the minimisation is F = y' + w'z' + xz' above; the NAND drawing, including the inverters, is in chapter 4 section 2.

Traps that cost marks

  • Writing the axes in binary order (00 01 10 11). They must be Gray order, 00 01 11 10.
  • Grouping diagonally, or in 3s or 6s.
  • Forgetting wrap-around groups (corners, left–right edges).
  • Stopping at a group of 2 when a group of 4 or 8 exists. A smaller group is not a prime implicant.
  • Adding a redundant group (all its 1s already covered). It is a PI but not needed; do not put it in the answer.
  • Naming a don't-care as a responsible minterm, or listing "essential" for a PI whose real 1s are all shared.
  • For POS: reading the 0-groups and forgetting to complement the literals.
  • 5 variables: treating the right map as a mirror image. Adjacent means the same position in the other map.
  • Listing only the EPIs when the question says "all prime implicants".
