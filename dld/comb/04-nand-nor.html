1. Why NAND and NOR are universal

A set of gates is universal if any Boolean function can be built from it. AND, OR and NOT are enough (every function is a sum of products). So it is enough to show that NAND alone, and NOR alone, can make NOT, AND and OR. NAND(x, y) = (xy)'; NOR(x, y) = (x + y)'.

NAND only: NOT: tie the inputs, (xx)' = x'. AND: NAND followed by a NOT. OR: NOT both inputs, then NAND: (x'y')' = x + y (De Morgan). NOR only: NOT: tie the inputs. OR: NOR followed by a NOT. AND: NOT both inputs, then NOR: (x' + y')' = xy. Each figure below checks itself against its truth table.

2022–23 Q3(a) 14 marks · 2019–20 Q5(b) 7 marks · 2013–14 · 2015–16

What do you mean by a universal gate? Show that both the NAND gate and the NOR gate are universal gates. (2019–20: "Which gates are called universal gates and why?")

Answer

Definition (2 marks). A gate that can implement every Boolean function, by itself, with no other gate type. NAND and NOR are universal.

Proof (10 marks). Any function can be written with AND, OR and NOT only. So show NOT, AND and OR using one gate type: the six circuits above, with the equations (xx)' = x'; ((xy)')' = xy; (x'y')' = x + y for NAND, and (x+x)' = x'; ((x+y)')' = x + y; (x'+y')' = xy for NOR. Draw each. Because NOT, AND, OR are realised, every function is realisable.

Why we use them (2 marks). NAND and NOR are the cheapest gates in CMOS and TTL (fewer transistors than AND/OR, which need an extra inverting stage), they are faster, and a design needs one gate type, which simplifies manufacture and spares.

Corollary: an SOP maps directly to NAND-NAND and a POS to NOR-NOR (section 2).

What to write (universal gates, 7–14 marks)

  1. One-line definition, naming NAND and NOR.
  2. "AND, OR, NOT are sufficient, so show those three."
  3. Six small drawings (NOT, AND, OR for each gate) with the algebra beside each.
  4. One sentence of conclusion and one practical reason (fewer transistors, one gate type).

Related theory (old CT 2 and old finals)

  • If one input of a NAND gate is 1: NAND(x, 1) = (x·1)' = x'. The gate becomes an inverter of the other input. (If one input is 0 the output is always 1.) Same for NOR with 0: NOR(x, 0) = x'.
  • Why XOR is not universal. Any circuit built only from XOR gates computes the XOR (parity) of some subset of its inputs, i.e. an affine function. It can never produce AND (not affine). Also, with no constant input, all-zero inputs always give 0, so even a NOT is impossible. (With a constant 1 you get NOT, but still no AND.)
  • NAND and NOR are not associative (2011–12, 2015–16): (x NAND y) NAND z = xy + z' but x NAND (y NAND z) = x' + yz. At x = y = 0, z = 1 the first is 0 and the second is 1. For NOR: (x NOR y) NOR z = (x + y)z' and x NOR (y NOR z) = x'(y + z); at x = 1, y = z = 0 they are 1 and 0. This is why a 3-input NAND is not two cascaded 2-input NANDs (not "input extendable", ma'am L4); AND, OR and XOR are.
  • Why NAND/NOR in fault-tolerant systems (old CT 2, 8 marks, not in ma'am's notes; the usual textbook reasons): (a) they are universal, so the whole system uses a single gate type: any module or spare gate can replace any other, which makes redundancy such as triple modular redundancy and spare-gate replacement simple; (b) they are single-stage CMOS gates, fewer components means fewer places to fail, lower power and delay; (c) a regular two-level NAND-NAND structure is easy to test and replicate.

Hardware cost and "how do I know it is minimal?" (2024–25 Q2a, 10 marks)

2024–25 Q2(a) · 10 marks

Why are NAND and NOR called universal gates? How is hardware cost related to Boolean simplification? If a function is already minimal, how can you verify it?

Answer

(i) Universal: section 1 above (NOT, AND, OR from either gate; any function is a sum of products).

(ii) Cost and simplification. The hardware cost of a two-level circuit is the number of gates plus the number of gate inputs (equivalently, the number of product terms and the number of literals). Each literal is one gate input; each product term is one gate. Simplifying the Boolean expression removes literals and terms, so it removes gates and gate inputs directly, and also shortens delay and lowers power. Example: Σ(0,1,2,4,5,6,8,9,12,13,14) as a sum of minterms needs 11 AND gates with 4 inputs; the simplified F = y' + w'z' + xz' needs 2 AND gates, 1 OR gate and 5 literals.

(iii) Verifying a function is minimal.

  1. Plot it on a K-map (or run the tabulation). Check that every group is a prime implicant: no group can be enlarged to 2×, i.e. no literal can be dropped.
  2. Check that no group is redundant: removing any term leaves some 1 uncovered (every term has a responsible minterm).
  3. Check the number of terms cannot be reduced: the essential PIs are all present and the remaining 1s cannot be covered by fewer PIs. If it is not an SOP already, also try consensus/absorption.
  4. Confirm with the truth table that the simplified function equals the original.

Caveat: this proves minimality for two-level SOP/POS. A multi-level (factored) form can still have fewer literals, so say "minimal two-level form".

2. NAND-NAND and NOR-NOR (two-level, complements available)

  1. Simplify: minimal SOP for NAND-NAND, minimal POS for NOR-NOR (chapter 3).
  2. NAND-NAND: one NAND gate per product term (first level); one NAND gate collecting them (second level). A term with a single literal has no first-level gate: feed the complement of that literal straight into the second-level NAND. (Reason: the second NAND complements each input.)
  3. NOR-NOR: one NOR gate per sum term; one NOR collecting them. A single-literal sum term goes in complemented.
  4. State the assumption "both x and x' are available as inputs". If they are not, add inverters (a NAND or NOR with tied inputs).

Why it works: NAND-NAND = ((xy)'(zw)')' = xy + zw. The two inversions cancel. It is AND-OR with all gates replaced by NAND (De Morgan: the second-level NAND is an OR with inverted inputs).

2020–21 Q3(c) · 5 marks

Implement f(A,B,C) = Π(0,2,3,5,7) with NOR gates.

Answer

Zeros are 0, 2, 3, 5, 7 (so F = Σ(1, 4, 6)). Group the zeros: (0, 2) → A + C; (3, 7) → B' + C'; (5, 7) → A' + C'. F = (A + C)(B' + C')(A' + C'): NOR-NOR, three 2-input NORs into one 3-input NOR (complements of the inputs assumed available). Another equally small POS is (A + C)(A + B')(A' + C').

older · 6 marks

Implement f = xy + yz using as few NOR gates as possible.

Answer

Factor first: f = y(x + z). Then f = NOR(y', NOR(x, z)): the inner NOR gives (x + z)', the outer gives y·(x+z). Two NOR gates (plus one inverter if y' is not available).

3. Multi-level NAND or NOR (non-standard forms)

If the expression is not SOP or POS (it has three or more levels), use ma'am's bubble-balancing method:

  1. Draw the circuit with AND and OR gates, level by level.
  2. Replace every AND by a NAND and every OR by a NAND in its invert-input OR form (bubbles on the inputs), in alternate levels: the output bubble of one level meets the input bubbles of the next, and the pair cancels.
  3. Check each wire: a bubble at one end and none at the other changes the polarity. Fix it by inserting an inverter, or by complementing the literal if the wire comes from a primary input.
  4. Count gates and levels, then verify with the truth table or by algebra.

(a) F = A(CD + B) + BC' with NAND gates only. (b) F = (AB' + A'B)(C + D') with NAND, and with NOR gates. (Complemented literals available.)

(a) Work from the output: F = X + Y with X = A(CD + B), Y = BC'. F = (X'·Y')' = NAND(X', Y'). Y' = NAND(B, C'). X' = NAND(A, W) with W = CD + B = NAND(NAND(C,D), B'). Five NAND gates, 4 levels:

4. The sixteen two-level combinations and the eight non-degenerate forms

With four gate types (AND, OR, NAND, NOR) there are 4 × 4 = 16 ways to pick a first-level and a second-level gate. Eight are degenerate: the two levels collapse into a single operation, so they do nothing a bigger single gate could not (for example AND-AND is just a wider AND, AND-NAND is just a wider NAND). The other eight are non-degenerate and can implement useful functions:

2017–18 Q1(b) 8 marks · 2018–19 Q1(b) 6 marks · 2011–12 (6 + 6)

Express f(A,B,C,D) = Σ(0,4,8,9,10,11,12,14) with the two-level forms (2017–18) NOR-OR and AND-NOR; (2018–19) NAND-AND and OR-NAND; (2011–12) NOR-OR and OR-NAND. Both normal and complemented inputs are available.

Answer

K-map (the engine's groups): F = Σ(0,4,8,9,10,11,12,14). 1-groups: C'D' (0, 4, 8, 12), AB' (8, 9, 10, 11), AD' (8, 10, 12, 14). So F = C'D' + AB' + AD'. 0-groups (the zeros are 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 13, 15): A'D (1, 3, 5, 7), A'C (2, 3, 6, 7), BD (5, 7, 13, 15). So F' = A'D + A'C + BD (SOP of F').

AND-NOR and NAND-AND use F' as an SOP: F = (A'D + A'C + BD)'.

OR-NAND and NOR-OR use F' as a POS. From the 1-groups: F' = (C + D)(A' + B)(A' + D) (the sum terms are the complemented literals of C'D', AB', AD'). So F = [(C + D)(A' + B)(A' + D)]' for OR-NAND. For NOR-OR the same three terms appear as NOR gates giving back the products: NOR(C, D) = C'D', NOR(A', B) = AB', NOR(A', D) = AD'.

2023–24 Q4(b) · 14 marks

Implement F(x,y,z) = x'y'z' + xyz' with two-level NAND-AND. Complements are available.

Answer

F = Σ(0, 6). The 1s do not combine (0 = 000 and 6 = 110 are not adjacent). NAND-AND needs F' as an SOP: group the zeros: F' = xy' + x'y + z (this is the AOI example in ma'am's notes). Then F = (xy' + x'y + z)' = (xy')'(x'y)'z'. Two NANDs for the products xy' and x'y; the single literal z enters the final AND as z' (complemented) because the AND has no inverting input:

Left: NAND-AND. Right: the same function as OR-NAND (the OAI form, from F' as a POS: F' = (x + y + z)(x' + y' + z)). In section 5 the AND-NOR twin of the left circuit is drawn too.

What to write (two-level forms, 6–14 marks)

  1. The K-map (or reference to it) and the minimal expression you need for that form: F (SOP), F (POS), F' (SOP, for AOI) or F' (POS, for OAI).
  2. One line identifying the form: "AND-NOR = (SOP of F')'".
  3. The circuit with every gate labelled and every input named (x, x'), the output named F. Say "complements assumed available".
  4. Bubbles drawn on NAND/NOR outputs. For single-literal terms, show which polarity enters.
  5. Optional but safe: gates and gate-inputs count, and a spot-check row of the truth table.

5. AOI and OAI from her notes

AND-OR-INVERT (AOI) = complement of an SOP. Her #1: F = (AB + CD + E)' is AND-NOR: two ANDs and the single literal E into a NOR. Moving bubbles left gives NAND-AND (E enters complemented). OR-AND-INVERT (OAI) = complement of a POS. Her #2: F = ((A + B)(C + D)E)' is OR-NAND (NOR-OR is the bubble-moved twin).

Her example #3 (L18)

Express F(x,y,z) = Σ(0, 6) in AOI and OAI form.

Answer

AOI: group the zeros: F' = xy' + x'y + z, so F = (xy' + x'y + z)' (AND-NOR; also NAND-AND). OAI: from the ones, F = x'y'z' + xyz', F' = (x + y + z)(x' + y' + z), so F = [(x + y + z)(x' + y' + z)]' (OR-NAND; also NOR-OR).

Traps that cost marks

  • A single-literal term must enter the second-level NAND/NOR complemented (NAND-NAND, NOR-NOR) or as given (AND-OR). Feeding it the wrong polarity changes the function.
  • Using the F SOP for AND-NOR. AND-NOR gives the complement, so you need F' as the SOP.
  • Calling a 3-input NAND "two 2-input NANDs": NAND is not associative.
  • Forgetting that NAND/NOR with tied inputs are inverters, and not counting them when complements are not available.
  • Drawing AND-OR and writing "NAND" in the label. Draw bubbles on the NAND gates.
  • Using the minimal SOP of F for a POS-type form. Choose the expression first, then the gate form.
  • Claiming a circuit is "minimal" without saying it is the minimal two-level form.
