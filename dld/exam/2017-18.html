Section A · combinational

Q1(a) · 7 marks · subtraction by complement, hexadecimal

Write the rules of subtraction M − N for two n-digit numbers in base r, for the cases M ≥ N and M < N. Find 9 − 6.9 − 9.8 using the complement rule in hexadecimal.

Answer

Time: about 6 min

Rules (2 marks)

  1. Add M to the r's complement of N: M + (rn − N).
  2. M ≥ N: an end carry appears; discard it. The rest is M − N.
  3. M < N: no end carry. The sum is the r's complement of (N − M): take the r's complement of the sum and put a minus sign.

Assumption. The printed "9 − 6.9 − 9.8" is read as the two subtractions of the rule, 9.0 − 6.9 (M ≥ N) and 6.9 − 9.8 (M < N), written as two-digit hex numbers (one fractional digit: 90, 69, 98). The left-to-right chain is also given.

  1. 9.0 − 6.9: 16's complement of 69 = 97 (FF − 69 + 1). 90 + 97 = 127: end carry 1, discard → 2.7 (hex).
  2. 6.9 − 9.8: 16's complement of 98 = 68. 69 + 68 = D1, no end carry, so negative. 16's complement of D1 = 2F → −2.F (hex).
  3. Chain 9 − 6.9 − 9.8 = 2.7 − 9.8: 27 + 68 = 8F, no carry → −71 = −7.1 (hex).

Complements from the engine; checked against ordinary subtraction (0x90−0x69 = 0x27, 0x69−0x98 = −0x2F, 0x27−0x98 = −0x71).

Q1(b) · 8 marks · NOR-OR and AND-NOR

Express f(A,B,C,D) = Σ(0,4,8,9,10,11,12,14) in the two-level forms NOR-OR and AND-NOR.

Answer

Time: about 7 min

Both forms are "degenerate": they compute the complement of a normal circuit, so feed them the simplified F'. Zeros: 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 13, 15.

  1. AND-NOR: F' as SOP: F' = A'D + A'C + BD. AND gate per product term, one NOR: (ΣP)' = F.
  2. NOR-OR: F' as POS: F' = (C + D)(A' + B)(A' + D). NOR gate per sum, one OR: Σ(sum)' = (Π sums)' = F.

Both circuits checked against all 16 minterms.

Q2(b) · 9 + 4 marks · BCD adder-subtractor

Design a BCD adder-subtractor for BCD inputs A and B; selector x = 0 gives A + B, x = 1 gives A − B. Explain 9 + 7 and 9 − 7 with your circuit.

Answer

Time: about 11 min

What to write (13 marks)

  1. Subtract by adding the 10's complement of B = 9's complement + 1; the +1 is the carry-in x.

9 − 7 (x = 1): B' = 9 − 7 = 2 = 0010; 1001 + 0010 + 1 = 01100: Z = 1100, K = 1 → + 0110 = 10010: digit 0010, K = 1 (no borrow): +2.

Q5(b) · 5 marks · why master-slave flip-flops

Why do we use master-slave flip-flops?

Answer

Time: about 4 min

  • A level-triggered J-K latch with J = K = 1 toggles repeatedly while the clock is high, because the changing output feeds back to the inputs: race-around, final state unpredictable (the output delay is shorter than the clock width).
  • A master-slave flip-flop uses two latches on opposite clock levels: the master follows the inputs while CLK = 1, the slave is closed; when CLK falls the master closes and the slave copies it.
  • The output changes only once per clock, at the falling edge, so J = K = 1 toggles exactly once. Inputs sampled at one instant: edge-like behaviour from level latches.
Q5(c) · 10 marks · clock division with D flip-flops

Given a 100 MHz clock, derive with D flip-flops 50 MHz and 25 MHz signals. Draw a timing diagram for the three signals.

Answer

Time: about 9 min

  1. Each D flip-flop wired as a toggle (D = Q') halves the frequency of its clock input.
  2. FF1: clock = 100 MHz, D₁ = Q₁' → Q₁ = 50 MHz.
  3. FF2: clock = Q₁, D₂ = Q₂' → Q₂ = 25 MHz.
  4. Rising-edge triggered; Q₁ changes just after each rising edge of CLK, Q₂ just after each rising edge of Q₁ (a ripple delay).

Q6(c) · 5 marks · Mealy and Moore machines

Define (i) a Mealy machine and (ii) a Moore machine.

Answer

Time: about 4 min

  1. Mealy machine: a sequential circuit whose outputs depend on the present state and the present inputs, z = λ(S, x). Output written on the arrows of the state diagram (input/output).
  2. Moore machine: outputs depend only on the present state, z = λ(S). Output written inside the state circle (state/output).
  3. Moore usually needs more states and its output changes only at clock edges; Mealy responds in the same period and can glitch with the input.

Q7(c) · 10 marks · 5-bit ring counter

Design a 5-bit ring counter using a shift register and illustrate its operation using a timing diagram.

Answer

Time: about 9 min
