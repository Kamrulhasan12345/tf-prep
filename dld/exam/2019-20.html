Q3(a) · 10 marks · MOD-12 ripple counter, JK

A MOD-12 counter counts 0000 to 1011. Design it as a ripple counter with positive edge-triggered J-K flip-flops and basic gates.

Answer

Time: about 7 min

  1. 4 flip-flops, J = K = 1 (toggle). Ripple up counter with + edge triggers: the clock of each stage is the Q' of the stage before.
  2. A binary counter would reach 1100 (12). Detect 1100 with NAND(Q₃,Q₂) and use it to clear all flip-flops asynchronously (CLR' = 0), so 1100 lasts only a moment and the sequence is 0 … 11, 0.
  3. Sequence: 0000, 0001, …, 1011, then (1100) → 0000.

Q5(b) · 7 marks · universal gates

Which gates are called universal gates and why?

Answer

Time: about 5 min

  • NAND and NOR are universal: every Boolean function (AND, OR, NOT, hence any SOP/POS) can be built from only one of them.
  • NAND: NOT = NAND(x,x); AND = NOT of NAND; OR = NAND(x',y').
  • NOR: NOT = NOR(x,x); OR = NOT of NOR; AND = NOR(x',y').
  • Also: they cost less in hardware and one gate type simplifies fabrication.

Q6(a) · 15 marks · tabulation vs K-map

Compare the tabulation method and the Karnaugh map for minimising Boolean functions, with merits and demerits.

Answer

Time: about 11 min

K-mapTabulation (Quine-McCluskey)
MethodVisual grouping of adjacent cellsSystematic pairing of minterms differing in one bit, then a PI chart
VariablesPractical up to 4–5 (6 is clumsy)Any number
MeritsFast, intuitive, shows EPIs at a glanceMechanical, no mistakes from pattern-spotting, programmable, handles many variables
DemeritsError-prone beyond 5 variables; relies on human pattern recognition; hard to automateSlow by hand, long tables, many comparisons
Don't-caresTreat as 1 or 0 as helpfulInclude in pairing, exclude from the PI chart
ResultMinimal cover by inspectionSame PIs; minimum cover from the PI chart (Petrick if cyclic)

Both give the same prime implicants; choose K-map for ≤ 4 variables, tabulation for more or for computer programs.

Q7(b) · 7 marks · dual of XOR

Show that the dual of the exclusive-OR equals its complement.

Answer

Time: about 5 min

x ⊕ y = xy' + x'y. Dual (swap AND/OR, keep literals): (x + y')(x' + y) = xx' + xy + y'x' + y'y = xy + x'y' = x ⊙ y = (x ⊕ y)'.

Last two columns match, so dual(XOR) = XNOR = complement of XOR.
