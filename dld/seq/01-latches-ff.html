Adnan sir's words, and the textbook word next to each.
  • Excitation table (his, first row of his summary on p.16) = the characteristic table (function table) of the book: inputs and Qn give Qn+1.
  • Excitation requirement (his) = the excitation table of the book: for a wanted Qn → Qn+1, which inputs are needed. It is used for design and for flip-flop conversion (next chapter).
  • Transition table (his) = present state to next state in binary. Not needed in this chapter.
  • Lower case y = present state, capital Y = next state. For one flip-flop, Qn = y and Qn+1 = Y.

1. Latch or flip-flop (his definition)

A sequential circuit has memory: its output depends on the inputs and on the stored state. A clock is a train of pulses used to synchronise the memory elements. He names four things on a clock: positive level, negative level, positive edge, negative edge.

Latch
A memory element with no clock, or a level-triggered clock (called enable or C). While the level is active, the output follows the input.
Flip-flop (FF)
A memory element with an edge-triggered clock. It samples its inputs at one instant per clock cycle.
Types
D (Delay), SR (Set-Reset), JK, T (Toggle). JK and T latches are rarely used. Synchronous circuit: all FFs change together on the clock. Asynchronous: no common clock.
Positive-level D latch against positive-edge D flip-flop. The latch copies D for the whole time CLK = 1 (it caught the glitch at t = 4 to 7). The flip-flop looks at D only at the rising edges: it saw 0 at t = 2 and 1 at t = 14.

2. SR latch (NOR) and the forbidden input

Two NOR gates, each output fed to the other. Q' is the complement of Q in every allowed state.

Why do 00 and 01 hold the 0-state and 00 and 10 hold the 1-state? In the 0-state, R = 1 does nothing new (it asks for reset, which is already true). That is why the state diagram has two labels on each loop.

Why S = R = 1 is not allowed (2018–19 Q7(c), 7 marks). Follow his reasoning in order:
  1. S = R = 1 forces both NOR outputs to 0. This state is stable, but now Q = Q' = 0, which breaks the rule that Q' is the complement of Q.
  2. Now make S and R both 0 together. Both NOR gates see 0, 0 and each tries to output 1.
  3. Each output is fed to the other gate. Whichever gate responds first wins, forces the other to 0, and the latch lands in the state of the winner. The loop can also oscillate or hang between levels (metastable).
  4. Which one wins depends on gate delays, which we cannot control. The next state is unpredictable, so S = R = 1 is a forbidden input: race condition.
  5. Cures: use a design that makes S·R = 0 impossible, or use a JK (next section), which feeds Q back so that J = K = 1 means toggle.

Use the bench above: press S and R together, then release them together.

3. SR latch with NAND, gated SR, D latch

SR latch with NAND gates, with the two input inverters (so S and R are active-high). Remove the inverters and S' and R' are active-low.

With NAND gates the inputs are active-low (S' and R'). He draws NOT gates in front, so the input is active-high again and the excitation table is the same as for NOR. Rule he writes next to the gate: NOR, any 1 gives 0. NAND, any 0 gives 1. For the NAND latch with active-low inputs the forbidden case is S' = R' = 0 (both outputs 1).

Gated (clocked) SR latch: add an enable C. C = 0 forces the front gates to their inactive output, so the latch holds. C = 1 behaves like the plain SR latch.

4. JK, and the race-around problem

The JK is the SR with the forbidden state fixed: J is set, K is reset, and J = K = 1 means toggle. In the gate circuit Q' is fed back to the set gate (J·C·Q') and Q to the reset gate (K·C·Q). Only one of the two gates can be on, so S = R = 1 never reaches the latch.

Level-triggered JK latch: Q' and Q are fed back into the input gates. While C = 1 and J = K = 1 the output keeps flipping: race-around.

Check one row with the equation: J = K = 1 gives Qn' + 0 = Qn' for Qn = 0 (→ 1) and 0 + Qn for Qn = 1 (→ 0). So the next state is the complement: toggle.

The problem in the construction of a JK flip-flop (2023–24 Q6(b), 6 marks).
  1. Problem: with J = K = 1 the output toggles. In a level-triggered JK the toggle changes Q, the changed Q comes back through the feedback gates, and if C is still high it toggles again. Q flips again and again for as long as C = 1: the "infinite toggle", also called race-around. At the end of the pulse, Q is unpredictable.
  2. When it happens: if the clock pulse width tp is longer than the propagation delay around the loop, tpd (about twice a gate delay).
  3. Cures (his order): (a) make the clock pulse shorter than the gate delay (he marks this with a doubt: the delay is nanoseconds and varies, so it is not practical); (b) change the construction to an edge-triggered flip-flop; (c) use a master-slave flip-flop (two latches).

On the JK bench, set J = 1, K = 1, then raise clk. Watch Q toggle on its own until you lower the clock.

J = K = 1. Level-triggered JK: Q flips once per gate-delay loop for as long as CLK = 1 (race-around). Master-slave: Q changes once per clock, at the falling edge.

5. Master-slave JK, and why it works

Why master-slave (2017–18 Q5(b), 5 marks): the JK latch races when J = K = 1. A master-slave flip-flop is two latches in series with opposite clock levels. The master is open (copies J, K) while C = 1 and the slave is closed. At the falling edge the master closes and the slave opens and copies the master. So Q can change only once per clock pulse, at the edge: no race-around, and the output is the value that was present at the end of the pulse.

Break before make. The two latches are never open together. Falling edge: the master closes first, then the slave opens. Rising edge: the slave closes first, then the master opens. The inverter on the clock gives exactly this order.

2018–19 Q5(b) · 8 marks · same family 2017–18, 2021–22, 2022–23 (see below)

Draw the circuit diagram of a master-slave J-K flip-flop using only NAND gates.

Answer
Master-slave JK flip-flop with nine NAND gates. The clock inverter is a NAND with both inputs tied. Master is open while C = 1, slave while C = 0.

Nine NAND gates (count them: 2 + 2 master, 2 + 2 slave, 1 inverter).

  1. Master input gates: N1 = (J·C·Q')', N2 = (K·C·Q)'. These are 3-input NANDs. Q and Q' come back from the slave outputs.
  2. Master latch: two cross-coupled NANDs give Qm and Qm'.
  3. Clock inverter: a NAND with its two inputs tied together gives C'.
  4. Slave input gates: (Qm·C')' and (Qm'·C')'. Slave latch: two cross-coupled NANDs give Q and Q'.
  5. Master works at C = 1, slave at C = 0, so Q changes after the falling edge.

Check with J = K = 1 and Q = 0: master sets (Qm = 1) while C = 1. Falling edge: slave copies Qm, so Q = 1. Next pulse: Q' = 0 blocks N1, Q = 1 enables N2, master resets, Q = 0. One toggle per pulse.

2021–22 Q5(c) · 5 marks

Draw a master-slave JK flip-flop with asynchronous preset and clear using only NOR gates.

Answer
Master-slave JK, NOR gates only, asynchronous PRE and CLR (active high here) wired to both the master and the slave latch. The three inverters are NORs with tied inputs.
  1. Master input gates are 3-input NORs: S₁ = (J' + C' + Q)' = J·C·Q' and R₁ = (K' + C' + Q')' = K·C·Q. The NOR needs inverted J, K and C, so three NORs with tied inputs act as inverters.
  2. Master latch: two cross-coupled NORs. Slave input gates: S₂ = (Qm' + C)' = Qm·C' and R₂ = (Qm + C)' = Qm'·C'. They are open when C = 0. Slave latch: two cross-coupled NORs.
  3. PRE (preset) is a third input to the NOR that makes Q' (so Q' is forced to 0 and Q to 1). CLR (clear) is a third input to the NOR that makes Q (Q forced to 0). Both go to the master and the slave, so the value is not overwritten when the clock next goes low.
  4. PRE = CLR = 0 in normal operation. PRE = CLR = 1 together is forbidden (same as S = R = 1). They work at any time, whatever the clock does: asynchronous.

Total gates: 3 inverters + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 11 NOR gates. With NAND gates the PRE and CLR would be active-low (drawn with a bubble). Because the question says NOR, they are active-high here: say so on the script.

2022–23 Q6(a) · 10 marks · older papers: 12 marks

Draw the block diagram of a negative-edge triggered master-slave D flip-flop using D latches.

Answer
Negative-edge D flip-flop: master latch open while CLK = 1, slave latch open while CLK = 0 (through the inverter). Q takes D at the falling edge.
Master open while CLK = 1 (follows D). Slave opens at the falling edge and copies Qm. Q changes only at the falling edges, t = 8 and t = 20.
  1. Master D latch: enable = CLK (open when CLK = 1). Its D input is the flip-flop's D.
  2. Slave D latch: enable = CLK' (a NOT gate), open when CLK = 0. Its D input is Qm.
  3. When CLK falls, the master closes, the slave opens, and Q takes the D value that the master held at the edge. The output changes only at the falling edge: negative-edge triggered.
  4. For a positive-edge D flip-flop swap the two: master enable CLK', slave enable CLK.

The equation: Qn+1 = D, sampled at the falling edge.

6. Master-slave under real clock edges (2024–25 Q4(b), 12 marks)

2024–25 Q4(b) · 12 marks · compulsory half of Q4

A clocked sequential circuit uses master-slave configuration. (i) Explain how signal propagation through master and slave stages affects timing. (ii) Analyse and justify the possibility of race-around under a non-ideal clock (finite rise and fall time).

Answer

(i) Propagation and timing.

  1. While C = 1 the master follows J, K (and the frozen Q, Q'). The slave is closed, so Q does not move even when J and K change. Only the master output Qm moves.
  2. At the falling edge the master closes after its gate delay tpd,m, holding the value at the edge. The slave opens through the inverter (one more tpd) and Q follows Qm after another tpd,s.
  3. So the output delay from the falling clock edge is about tinv + tpd,s. The inputs must be stable for a set-up time before the master closes and for a hold time after it.
  4. Q cannot reach the master while the master is open (the slave is closed), so no loop runs with C high: the structure has no race-around if the two latches are never open together.

(ii) Finite rise and fall time.

  1. An ideal clock switches in zero time. A real clock falls through a transition region, taking tf. The master closes when C drops below its input-gate threshold. The slave opens when C' (the inverter output) rises above its own threshold.
  2. If tf is large, or the inverter is fast and the master gates have a low threshold, there is a short window tov in which the master is still open and the slave is already open (or the other way round at the rising edge).
  3. In that window the master follows J, K, Q; the slave copies the master; Q changes; and the changed Q feeds back to the master, which is still open. With J = K = 1 the loop toggles. If tov is longer than the loop delay (about 2·tpd) Q toggles again: race-around returns and the output is uncertain. Exactly the failure of the single latch, reduced to the length of the overlap.
  4. Justification: the design is safe only if tov < loop delay, i.e. break before make is kept. A slowly changing clock breaks that assumption.
  5. Remedies: keep clock edges sharp (clock buffer or Schmitt trigger), make the slave's inverter slower than the master's gates, or use a genuinely edge-triggered flip-flop (data sampled in a very small window) in place of master-slave.

A second known weakness of the master-slave JK (not in his notes, one line is enough): ones-catching. A glitch on J while C = 1 can set the master, and the feedback from Q then blocks K from clearing it again, so a false 1 reaches Q.

  2. Draw both feedback wires from the slave outputs to the master input gates.
  3. Write: "Master works at C = 1, slave at C = 0. Q changes once, after the falling edge, so there is no race-around."
  4. Write the gate count (nine NANDs, or 11 NORs with PRE/CLR) at the side of the figure.

What to write ("problem of JK, how to overcome", 6 marks)

  1. Problem: J = K = 1 with level clock gives repeated toggling (race-around) when tp > tpd.
  2. The waveform of Q toggling during C = 1.
  3. Three cures: narrow pulse (impractical), edge-triggered FF, master-slave FF.
  4. A sentence on the master-slave: one change per clock cycle, at the edge.

Traps that cost marks

  • NOR latch inputs are active-high; the bare NAND latch inputs are active-low. Say which one you drew.
  • Master-slave: the feedback goes from the slave Q and Q' to the master gates. Do not wire it from the master.
  • Do not forget the inverter on the clock. Without it both latches open together and the whole thing is just a long latch.
  • Do not say the master-slave JK is "edge triggered by the clock edge itself". It changes at the edge, but only because of the two latch levels.
  • The 2021–22 question says NOR only: draw the NOR inverters for J, K, C. Do not drop in a NOT symbol.
  • In the gated SR equation keep (C' + R')Qn. Dropping C'Qn makes the latch lose its state when C = 0.
  • A latch is not a flip-flop. Use the two words as he defines them.
