Elementary functions: exp, trig, log

Her last two lectures. Two question types: solve \(\cos z=k\), \(\cosh z=k\), \(\sin z=k\) by equating real and imaginary parts, and show that a principal-Log identity fails. Both came in the 2022-23 and 2024-25 papers, so expect one of them.

Types. (A) Solve a trig or hyperbolic equation 2 of 3 years. (B) Principal Log counterexample, and powers \(z^c\) repeated 2015-16, 2016-17, 2022-23. Mandatory: 3 cards (the three TF questions). Everything else is optional extra.

The definitions you must be able to write

Exponential
\(e^z=e^x(\cos y+i\sin y)\) for \(z=x+iy\). So \(|e^z|=e^x\) and \(\arg e^z=y\). For \(a>0\): \(a^z=e^{z\ln a}\).
Trig
\(\sin z=\dfrac{e^{iz}-e^{-iz}}{2i}\), \(\cos z=\dfrac{e^{iz}+e^{-iz}}{2}\). Then \(\tan,\sec,\csc,\cot\) as usual.
Hyperbolic
\(\sinh z=\dfrac{e^{z}-e^{-z}}{2}\), \(\cosh z=\dfrac{e^{z}+e^{-z}}{2}\). Then \(\tanh,\operatorname{sech},\operatorname{csch},\coth\).
The starred identities
\(\sin iz=i\sinh z\), \(\cos iz=\cosh z\), \(\tan iz=i\tanh z\), \(\sinh iz=i\sin z\), \(\cosh iz=\cos z\), \(\tanh iz=i\tan z\).
Expansion formulas (write the one you use)
\(\sin(x+iy)=\sin x\cosh y+i\cos x\sinh y\)
\(\cos(x+iy)=\cos x\cosh y-i\sin x\sinh y\)
\(\sinh(x+iy)=\sinh x\cos y+i\cosh x\sin y\)
\(\cosh(x+iy)=\cosh x\cos y+i\sinh x\sin y\)

Where these come from: \(\cos(x+iy)=\cos x\cos iy-\sin x\sin iy\), and \(\cos iy=\cosh y\), \(\sin iy=i\sinh y\). That is why \(\cosh y\) sits next to \(\cos x\) and a minus sign appears.

Type A · Solve \(\cos z=k\), \(\cosh z=k\), \(\sin z=k\)

The idea is always the same: put \(z=x+iy\), expand with the formula above, then match real part with real part and imaginary part with imaginary part. The imaginary part is a product, so it gives two cases. One case is always impossible.

Recipe · equation by equating parts

  1. Write \(z=x+iy\) and the expansion formula, e.g. \(\cos(x+iy)=\cos x\cosh y-i\sin x\sinh y\).
  2. Equate real parts and imaginary parts with the right-hand side (a real \(k\) has imaginary part 0).
  3. The imaginary part is \(\sin x\sinh y=0\) (or similar). Two cases: one factor is 0 or the other is.
  4. Reject the case that forces something impossible: \(|\cos x|>1\), \(|\sin|>1\) or \(\cosh<1\).
  5. In the surviving case, the sign of the real part fixes the parity of \(n\) (even or odd).
  6. Solve \(\cosh t=a\): \(e^{2t}-2ae^{t}+1=0\), so \(e^t=a\pm\sqrt{a^2-1}\), so \(t=\ln(a\pm\sqrt{a^2-1})=\pm\ln(a+\sqrt{a^2-1})\). The \(\pm\) is automatic because \((a+\sqrt{a^2-1})(a-\sqrt{a^2-1})=1\).
  7. Write the general solution with \(n\in\mathbb Z\).

What to write

(1) The expansion formula. (2) The two equations from equating parts. (3) The case you reject and the one-line reason. (4) The quadratic in \(e^{t}\). (5) The general solution, with \(n\in\mathbb Z\) and the \(\pm\), then a boxed line.

Traps

  • Losing the \(\pm\). The seniors' solutions did this for \(\sin z=5\) and \(\sin z=\cosh 4\). There are two families of roots, above and below the real axis.
  • Wrong parity. \(\cosh z=-2\) needs \(\cos y=-1\), so \(y=(2n+1)\pi\), not \(2n\pi\). \(\sin z=5\) needs \(\sin x=1\), so \(x=2n\pi+\frac\pi2\), not \((2n+1)\frac\pi2\) (that would give \(\sin x=-1\) for half of the \(n\)).
  • Solving \(\cosh z=2\) instead of \(-2\). The seniors' PDF did exactly this (answer \(\ln(2+\sqrt3)+2n\pi i\), wrong). The sign of \(k\) decides the parity of \(n\).
  • Writing \(i\sin(\cdot)\) for \(i\ln(\cdot)\) in the last line (a slip in one set of class notes).

Type B · Principal Log, and \(z^c\)

Multivalued log
If \(z=e^{w}\) then \(w=\ln z=\ln r+i(\theta+2k\pi)\), \(k\in\mathbb Z\), where \(z=re^{i\theta}\). Infinitely many values.
Principal value \(\mathrm{Log}\,z\)
\(\mathrm{Log}\,z=\ln r+i\theta\) with \(-\pi<\theta\le\pi\). Her notes also write \(0\le\theta<2\pi\) once, but her only worked example uses \((-\pi,\pi]\). Use \((-\pi,\pi]\) in the exam (with \([0,2\pi)\) the counterexample below would fail).
Other bases and powers
\(\log_a z=\dfrac{\ln z}{\ln a}\). For complex \(c\): \(z^c=e^{c\log z}\). The principal value uses \(\mathrm{Log}\).

The point of the identity questions: \(\log z_1z_2=\log z_1+\log z_2\) holds for the multivalued log (as sets), but for the principal value the argument of the product can leave \((-\pi,\pi]\), and then it must be shifted back by \(2\pi\). So \(\mathrm{Log}(z^2)\ne2\,\mathrm{Log}\,z\) whenever \(2\,\mathrm{Arg}\,z\) is outside \((-\pi,\pi]\), i.e. whenever \(|\mathrm{Arg}\,z|>\pi/2\).

Recipe · show \(\mathrm{Log}(w^2)\ne2\,\mathrm{Log}\,w\)

  1. Write \(w=re^{i\theta}\) with \(\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]\). (For \(-1+i\sqrt3\): \(r=2\), \(\theta=\frac{2\pi}3\).)
  2. Right side: \(2\,\mathrm{Log}\,w=2\ln r+2i\theta\). Do not reduce this.
  3. Left side: \(w^2=r^2e^{2i\theta}\). The angle \(2\theta\) is outside \((-\pi,\pi]\), so subtract \(2\pi\): \(\mathrm{Log}\,w^2=2\ln r+i(2\theta-2\pi)\).
  4. The imaginary parts differ by \(2\pi\), so the two sides are not equal.

What to write

The polar form of \(w\) with the principal angle, the polar form of \(w^2\) with the reduced angle, both sides evaluated, and the sentence "the imaginary parts differ by \(2\pi\), hence not equal". Say that \(\mathrm{Log}\) uses \(-\pi<\theta\le\pi\).

Traps

  • Forgetting to reduce \(\frac{4\pi}3\) to \(-\frac{2\pi}3\). Without that step LHS = RHS and there is nothing to show.
  • Using the \([0,2\pi)\) branch. Then \(\theta\) for \(-1+i\sqrt3\) is still \(\frac{2\pi}3\) and \(2\theta=\frac{4\pi}3<2\pi\) needs no reduction: no counterexample.
  • Treating Log as analytic on \(\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}\). It is analytic only off the cut \((-\infty,0]\).

One-minute summary

  • \(\cos(x+iy)=\cos x\cosh y-i\sin x\sinh y\); \(\sin(x+iy)=\sin x\cosh y+i\cos x\sinh y\); \(\cosh(x+iy)=\cosh x\cos y+i\sinh x\sin y\).
  • \(\cosh t=a\) gives \(t=\pm\ln(a+\sqrt{a^2-1})\). Do not lose the \(\pm\).
  • \(\cosh z=-2\): \(z=\pm\ln(2+\sqrt3)+(2n+1)\pi i\). \(\cos z=4\): \(z=2n\pi\pm i\ln(4+\sqrt{15})\). \(\sin z=5\): \(z=(2n+\frac12)\pi\pm i\ln(5+2\sqrt6)\).
  • Principal \(\mathrm{Log}\,z=\ln|z|+i\theta\), \(-\pi<\theta\le\pi\). Multivalued \(\ln z=\ln r+i(\theta+2k\pi)\).
  • \(\mathrm{Log}(-1+i\sqrt3)^2=\ln4-\frac{2\pi}3i\ne2\,\mathrm{Log}(-1+i\sqrt3)=\ln4+\frac{4\pi}3i\). Always reduce the doubled angle.
  • \(z^c=e^{c\log z}\); \(|\cos z|^2=\cos^2x+\sinh^2y\); \(|\sin z|^2=\sin^2x+\sinh^2y\).
