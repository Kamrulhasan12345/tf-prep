Complex numbers, roots, functions and mappings

The first three lectures: the Argand diagram and polar form, De Moivre roots, a function \(w=u+iv\), and what \(w=z^2\) does to a point, a line and a square. The paper has asked for roots (2023-24), \(u\) and \(v\) of a given \(f\) (2023-24) and, in two of three years, a mapping question that she never taught (bilinear maps, last section).

Types: (1) polar form · (2) roots of a complex number repeat · (3) find \(u\) and \(v\) · (4) images under \(z^2\) (class only) · (5) images under Möbius maps came in papers, not taught. Mandatory: 6 cards (4 from papers, 1 CT, 1 class example), the rest are optional extras.

1. Complex numbers and polar form

Cartesian and polar form
\(z=a+ib\), \(|z|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}=r\). \(z=r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)=re^{i\theta}\), where \(\theta=\arg z\).
Principal argument
In class the polar-form examples use \(-\pi\lt\theta\le\pi\), so \(-3i=3e^{-i\pi/2}\) (not \(3e^{i3\pi/2}\)). Use this range everywhere in the exam.

Recipe · polar form

  1. Plot the point first and note the quadrant.
  2. \(r=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\).
  3. \(\theta=\tan^{-1}(b/a)\), then add or subtract \(\pi\) if the point is in quadrant II or III. Put \(\theta\) in \((-\pi,\pi]\).
  4. Write \(z=re^{i\theta}\).

What to write

The line \(z=r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)=re^{i\theta}\), the values of \(r\) and \(\theta\) with the quadrant remarked, then the answer. For a sum or difference of vectors, write the components first and add; draw the parallelogram or the head-to-tail polygon if the question says "graphically".

Traps

  • \(\tan^{-1}\) alone gives an angle in \((-\pi/2,\pi/2)\). For \(-5+5i\), \(\tan^{-1}(-1)=-\pi/4\) is wrong; the point is in quadrant II, so \(\theta=3\pi/4\).
  • Direction problems: say what axis is 0°. In the man-travels problem E is the \(x\)-axis and N the \(y\)-axis; "30° west of north" is \(90^\circ+30^\circ=120^\circ\).

2. Roots of a complex number (De Moivre)

De Moivre's theorem for roots
If \(z=r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)\), then \[z^{1/n}=r^{1/n}\left[\cos\frac{\theta+2k\pi}{n}+i\sin\frac{\theta+2k\pi}{n}\right],\quad k=0,1,\dots,n-1.\] The \(n\) roots are the vertices of a regular \(n\)-gon of radius \(r^{1/n}\). The principal root is \(k=0\) with the principal \(\theta\).

Recipe · \(z^n=w\)

  1. Write \(w=Re^{i(\Theta+2k\pi)}\) with the general argument.
  2. Radius of each root: \(R^{1/n}\) (a positive real \(n\)th root).
  3. Angles: \(\dfrac{\Theta+2k\pi}{n}\) for \(k=0,\dots,n-1\). Stop at \(k=n-1\); the next one repeats \(k=0\).
  4. Give rectangular form only if asked (then use \(\cos,\sin\) of the angle).
  5. Sketch: a regular polygon on the circle of radius \(R^{1/n}\), one vertex at \(k=0\).

What to write

"\(-32=32\,e^{i(\pi+2k\pi)}\), so \(z=32^{1/5}e^{i(2k+1)\pi/5}\), \(k=0,1,2,3,4\)", then the five values, then the sketch.

Traps

  • Writing \(-32=32e^{i\pi}\) and stopping: without \(+2k\pi\) you only get one root.
  • Dividing the radius too: it is \(R^{1/n}\), not \(R/n\).
  • Principal argument of \(-2\sqrt3-2i\) is \(-5\pi/6\), not \(7\pi/6\). Both give the same four roots, but the principal one is \(k=0\) of the principal angle.

3. A function of \(z\) as \(u+iv\)

\(w=f(z)=u(x,y)+iv(x,y)\) sends the point \(P=(x,y)\) of the \(z\)-plane to the point \(P'=(u,v)\) of the \(w\)-plane (the image of \(P\)). For \(w=z^2\): \(u=x^2-y^2\), \(v=2xy\).

Recipe · find \(u\) and \(v\)

  1. Replace \(z\) by \(x+iy\) everywhere.
  2. Expand powers; write \(e^{a+ib}=e^a(\cos b+i\sin b)\) (and the same for \(e^{2z}=e^{2x}e^{2iy}\)).
  3. Multiply out and collect: real part \(=u\), imaginary part \(=v\).
  4. For a quotient, multiply top and bottom by the conjugate of the denominator.

What to write

"Put \(z=x+iy\)", the expanded product, and the two lines \(u=\dots\), \(v=\dots\). Quote \(e^{iy}=\cos y+i\sin y\) when it is used.

Traps

  • The 2023-24 paper has \(e^{2z}\); the question bank prints \(e^{2x}\) by mistake.
  • Forgetting \(i^2=-1\) when expanding \((x+iy)^2\): \(u\) is \(x^2-y^2\), not \(x^2+y^2\).

4. Mappings taught in class: \(w=z^2\)

She only drew images under \(w=z^2\) (Schaum 2.51, 2.52). Method: write \(u=x^2-y^2\), \(v=2xy\), put in the equation of the curve, and eliminate. Remember edges of a square are mapped to parabolas, not to straight lines: all three sets of class notes drew a straight-sided triangle, which is wrong.

Lines parallel to the axes under \(w=z^2\)
The line \(x=c\) (\(c\ne0\)): \(u=c^2-y^2\), \(v=2cy\), so \(u=c^2-\dfrac{v^2}{4c^2}\), a parabola opening to the left. The line \(y=c\): \(u=x^2-c^2\), \(v=2cx\), so \(u=\dfrac{v^2}{4c^2}-c^2\), a parabola opening to the right. The axes \(x=0\) and \(y=0\) go to the real axis (\(x=0\): \(u=-y^2\le0\); \(y=0\): \(u=x^2\ge0\)).

Recipe · image of a region under \(w=z^2\)

  1. Map the vertices with \(u=x^2-y^2\), \(v=2xy\).
  2. Map each edge by eliminating the free variable (edge \(x=c\) gives \(u=c^2-v^2/4c^2\) and so on).
  3. Draw the curved arcs between the image vertices. Check one interior point (for the unit square, \(\tfrac12+\tfrac12i\mapsto\tfrac12 i\)) lies inside the sketch.
  4. If \(z\) and \(-z\) are both in the region (square centred at 0), the image is covered twice.

What to write

The substitutions \(u=x^2-y^2\), \(v=2xy\), a table "vertex \(\to\) image vertex", the equation of the image of each edge, and a labelled sketch of both planes.

Traps

  • Joining image vertices by straight lines.
  • Square \(\pm1\pm i\): the picture is the lens between the two parabolas, not just the segment between \(\pm2i\).
  • \(z^2\) doubles angles: a region touching the origin with angle \(\alpha\) there is mapped to angle \(2\alpha\).

5. Came in papers, not taught: images under Möbius (bilinear) maps

Not taught this term. Ma'am's mapping work stops at \(z^2\), but 2022-23 Q3(c) (8 marks) and 2024-25 Q3(b) (10 marks) both asked for a bilinear map or its image of a half-plane or line. These are standard once you know the two recipes below. Treat them as the lowest-priority mandatory cards of the chapter: do them last, but do them.

Recipe · image of a line, circle or half-plane under \(w=\dfrac{az+b}{cz+d}\)

  1. Invert. Solve for \(z\): \(z=\dfrac{dw-b}{-cw+a}\).
  2. Put \(w=u+iv\) and write \(z=x+iy\) as fractions with the real denominator \(|{-cw}+a|^2\) (multiply top and bottom by the conjugate).
  3. Substitute the condition (e.g. \(\operatorname{Im}z\ge0\), \(\operatorname{Re}z\ge0\), or \(|z|=1\)) and simplify to a circle, line, disc or half-plane in \((u,v)\).
  4. Shortcut for a circle or line: it goes to a circle or line. Map three points of it, or compute \(|w|\) directly if the curve is \(|z|=\text{const}\) or the map has the form \(|{\cdot}|/|{\cdot}|\).
  5. Choose the correct side: map one interior point (\(z=i\) for the upper half-plane) and see if it lands inside or outside the image curve.

Recipe · bilinear map sending \(z_1,z_2,z_3\) to \(w_1,w_2,w_3\)

  1. Write the cross-ratio equation \[\frac{(w-w_1)(w_2-w_3)}{(w-w_3)(w_2-w_1)}=\frac{(z-z_1)(z_2-z_3)}{(z-z_3)(z_2-z_1)}.\]
  2. Put in the numbers, simplify each side (find the constant factors), set \(K=\) the \(z\)-side.
  3. Solve the \(w\)-side \(\dfrac{w-w_1}{w-w_3}\cdot\text{const}=K\) for \(w\) as a function of \(z\).
  4. Check all three points go where they should. Then to find the image of a curve use the first recipe.

What to write

The recipe line you use (cross-ratio, or "\(z=\dots\) in terms of \(w\)"), the simplified relation in \(u,v\), then the sentence "Hence the image is the closed unit disc \(|w|\le1\)" (or circle, half-plane...) with a sketch of both planes. For a three-point map, a line "check: \(z=-i\mapsto-1\), ..." at the end.

Traps

  • A Möbius map sends circles and lines to circles or lines. The image of a line is a line only if the line passes through the pole \(z=-d/c\).
  • The image of \(z=\infty\) is \(w=a/c\): a boundary point such as \(w=-i\) in the first card is the image of \(\infty\), not of any finite \(z\). Mention it, do not lose marks for a missing point.
  • An arithmetic slip in the cross-ratio constants. Always check the three points.

One-minute summary

  • \(z=re^{i\theta}\), \(\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]\); add \(\pm\pi\) to \(\tan^{-1}(b/a)\) in quadrants II, III.
  • \(z^{1/n}=r^{1/n}\operatorname{cis}\dfrac{\theta+2k\pi}{n}\), \(k=0..n-1\): regular \(n\)-gon of radius \(r^{1/n}\). \(z^5=-32\): \(2\operatorname{cis}\dfrac{(2k+1)\pi}5\).
  • \(w=u+iv\): put \(z=x+iy\), expand, separate; \(e^{iy}=\cos y+i\sin y\). For \(z^2\): \(u=x^2-y^2\), \(v=2xy\).
  • Under \(w=z^2\): \(x=c\mapsto u=c^2-\dfrac{v^2}{4c^2}\); \(y=c\mapsto u=\dfrac{v^2}{4c^2}-c^2\). Edges become parabolas.
  • Möbius image of a region: invert \(z=\dfrac{dw-b}{-cw+a}\), impose the condition, simplify, test one point. Three points to three points: cross-ratio.
  • \(\dfrac{z-i}{iz-1}\): \(\operatorname{Im}z\ge0\mapsto|w|\le1\). \(w=\dfrac{i(1-z)}{1+z}\): \(-i,0,i\mapsto-1,i,1\), the \(y\)-axis \(\mapsto|w|=1\).
