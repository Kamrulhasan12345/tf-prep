Stokes' theorem

The last lecture of the term (19 Sep). No MATH 241 paper has asked it yet, but it came in MATH 147 four times (2015-16, 2018-19, 2019-20, 2020-21), and this is the very last topic Jasim sir taught. The class notes end at Schaum 6.32, the hemisphere verification below.

One type, two flavours. (A) Verify Stokes: compute the line integral round the boundary and the surface integral of the curl, show they are equal. (B) Evaluate using Stokes: replace a hard surface (or line) integral by an easy one. weak spot 7 mandatory cards (all class or suggestion examples, so marked must), 2 optional extras.

1. The theorem

Stokes' theorem (write this word for word)
If \(S\) is an open, two-sided surface bounded by a simple closed curve \(C\), and \(\vec F\) has continuous first partial derivatives on \(S\) and \(C\), then \[\oint_C\vec F\cdot d\vec r=\iint_S(\nabla\times\vec F)\cdot\hat n\,dS,\] where \(C\) is traversed in the positive direction relative to \(\hat n\): counter-clockwise when seen from the tip of \(\hat n\), so that the surface is on your left as you walk round \(C\).
Curl
\(\nabla\times\vec F=\begin{vmatrix}\hat i&\hat j&\hat k\\\partial_x&\partial_y&\partial_z\\F_1&F_2&F_3\end{vmatrix}=\hat i(\partial_yF_3-\partial_zF_2)-\hat j(\partial_xF_3-\partial_zF_1)+\hat k(\partial_xF_2-\partial_yF_1)\).
Link to Green
If \(S\) is flat in the \(xy\)-plane, \(\hat n=\hat k\), \((\nabla\times\vec F)\cdot\hat k=N_x-M_y\) and Stokes is Green's theorem.

In words: the circulation of \(\vec F\) round the edge of a surface equals the total "twist" (curl) passing through the surface. Key consequence: the right-hand side depends only on the boundary \(C\), not on the shape of \(S\). So any surface with the same boundary gives the same value, and you may swap a curved surface for the flat disc.

2. Orientation: pick \(\hat n\), then walk round \(C\)

  • Choose \(\hat n\) first (the question says "upward", "outward", or it is \(\hat k\) for a surface lying in the \(xy\)-plane).
  • Walk round \(C\) so that, seen from the tip of \(\hat n\), you go counter-clockwise (right-hand rule: thumb along \(\hat n\), fingers curl along \(C\)).
  • For an upward normal on a surface above the \(xy\)-plane whose boundary lies in \(z=0\): \(C\) is counter-clockwise seen from above, i.e. \(x=r\cos t,\ y=r\sin t\), \(t:0\to2\pi\).

3. Type A: verify Stokes' theorem weak spot

Recipe · verify Stokes

  1. Sketch \(S\) and its boundary \(C\); mark \(\hat n\) and the counter-clockwise direction of \(C\) seen from the tip of \(\hat n\). Name the pieces \(C_1,C_2,\dots\) if \(C\) has several.
  2. Line side. On each piece of \(C\) parametrise, compute \(\vec F\cdot d\vec r=F_1dx+F_2dy+F_3dz\), integrate in the direction of travel, add. (A boundary in \(z=0\) has \(dz=0\) and \(z=0\): the integrand simplifies a lot.)
  3. Surface side. Compute \(\nabla\times\vec F\) from the determinant.
  4. Find \(\hat n\,dS\):
    • surface \(z=f(x,y)\), upward: \(\hat n\,dS=(-f_x,\,-f_y,\,1)\,dx\,dy\) (no square roots to fight);
    • sphere of radius \(a\): \(\hat n=\vec r/a\) and \(dS=\frac az\,dx\,dy\) (projection on the \(xy\)-plane, \(z\ge0\));
    • flat surface in a plane \(z=\)const: \(\hat n=\pm\hat k\), \(dS=dx\,dy\).
  5. Compute \((\nabla\times\vec F)\cdot\hat n\,dS\), integrate over the shadow region \(R\) in the \(xy\)-plane (polar coordinates for discs; odd terms vanish by symmetry).
  6. Compare. Write "L.H.S. \(=\) R.H.S., Stokes' theorem verified."

Recipe · evaluate using Stokes

  1. Either turn a surface integral of a curl into the line integral round the boundary, or turn a line integral into the flux of the curl.
  2. Find \(C\) (the boundary, e.g. the trace \(z=0\) of a hemisphere or paraboloid is a circle).
  3. Pick the easiest of the two sides. For a line integral round a plane curve, use the flat region it bounds (\(\hat n=\hat k\), \(dS=dx\,dy\)).

What to write

The statement of Stokes' theorem. A sketch showing \(S,\ C,\ \hat n\) and the direction of \(C\). (L.H.S.) the parametrisation of \(C\), \(\vec F\cdot d\vec r\), the integral. (R.H.S.) \(\nabla\times\vec F\), \(\hat n\,dS\), the integrand, the region and limits, the integral. "L.H.S. \(=\) R.H.S., verified."

Traps

  • Curl determinant: the \(\hat j\) term has a minus sign. Check with a simple field before trusting your curl.
  • \(C\) traversed clockwise gives the wrong sign. Seen from above the tip of \(\hat n=\hat k\), go counter-clockwise.
  • Using \(dS=dx\,dy\) on a curved surface. You need \(\hat n\,dS=(-f_x,-f_y,1)\,dx\,dy\) or \(dS=\frac az\,dx\,dy\) on the sphere.
  • Boundary of a hemisphere or paraboloid: it is the circle in the plane \(z=0\); do not try to integrate over the dome's edge.
  • Rectangle in the plane: four pieces, each with its own direction (bottom left to right, right side up, top right to left, left side down).

One-minute summary

  • \(\oint_C\vec F\cdot d\vec r=\iint_S(\nabla\times\vec F)\cdot\hat n\,dS\); \(C\) counter-clockwise seen from the tip of \(\hat n\) (surface on your left).
  • Curl \(=\hat i(\partial_yF_3-\partial_zF_2)-\hat j(\partial_xF_3-\partial_zF_1)+\hat k(\partial_xF_2-\partial_yF_1)\).
  • \(z=f(x,y)\), upward: \(\hat n\,dS=(-f_x,-f_y,1)\,dx\,dy\). Sphere: \(\hat n=\vec r/a\), \(dS=\frac az\,dx\,dy\). Flat in \(xy\): \(\hat n=\hat k\).
  • Same boundary, same value: replace a curved \(S\) by the flat disc.
  • Hemisphere \(\vec F=(2x-y,-yz^2,-y^2z)\): curl \(=\hat k\); both sides \(\pi\). Paraboloid \(\vec F=(2z,x,y^2)\): \(4\pi\). Rectangle \(\vec F=(x^2+y^2,-2xy,0)\): \(-4ab^2\).
  • Stokes flat in the plane is Green's theorem: \((\nabla\times\vec F)\cdot\hat k=N_x-M_y\).
