0. Vectors from zero

A vector in space is a bracket of three numbers: \(\vec A=(A_1,A_2,A_3)=A_1\hat i+A_2\hat j+A_3\hat k\), where \(\hat i=(1,0,0)\), \(\hat j=(0,1,0)\), \(\hat k=(0,0,1)\). It has a direction and a length.

Length (magnitude)
\(|\vec A|=\sqrt{A_1^2+A_2^2+A_3^2}\).
Unit vector
\(\hat A=\vec A/|\vec A|\): the same direction, length 1. Example: \(\vec A=(1,2,2)\), \(|\vec A|=3\), \(\hat A=(\tfrac13,\tfrac23,\tfrac23)\).
Dot product (a number)
\(\vec A\cdot\vec B=A_1B_1+A_2B_2+A_3B_3=|\vec A||\vec B|\cos\theta\). It is 0 exactly when the vectors are perpendicular.
Cross product (a vector)
\(\vec A\times\vec B=\begin{vmatrix}\hat i&\hat j&\hat k\\A_1&A_2&A_3\\B_1&B_2&B_3\end{vmatrix}\). It is perpendicular to both \(\vec A\) and \(\vec B\); \(|\vec A\times\vec B|=|\vec A||\vec B|\sin\theta\); \(\vec B\times\vec A=-\vec A\times\vec B\).
Scalar triple product
\([\vec A\,\vec B\,\vec C]=\vec A\cdot(\vec B\times\vec C)=\det\begin{pmatrix}A_1&A_2&A_3\\B_1&B_2&B_3\\C_1&C_2&C_3\end{pmatrix}\). Volume of the box on the three vectors. Zero if and only if they are coplanar (linearly dependent).
Vector triple product
\(\vec A\times(\vec B\times\vec C)=(\vec A\cdot\vec C)\vec B-(\vec A\cdot\vec B)\vec C\).
Component of \(\vec A\) along \(\vec p\)
\(\dfrac{\vec A\cdot\vec p}{|\vec p|}\) (a number). The vector component is \(\dfrac{\vec A\cdot\vec p}{|\vec p|^2}\vec p\). The perpendicular part is \(\vec A\) minus that.

The teacher's two vector-algebra examples (Lec 2). First: write a given vector as a combination of three others by comparing components. Second: find a unit vector perpendicular to two given vectors without the cross product, by solving two equations with \(c_3\) left free.

1. Differentiating a vector function

If \(\vec R(u)=R_1(u)\hat i+R_2(u)\hat j+R_3(u)\hat k\), then \[\frac{d\vec R}{du}=\lim_{\Delta u\to0}\frac{\vec R(u+\Delta u)-\vec R(u)}{\Delta u}=\frac{dR_1}{du}\hat i+\frac{dR_2}{du}\hat j+\frac{dR_3}{du}\hat k.\] Rule 1: differentiate each component, nothing else. The unit vectors \(\hat i,\hat j,\hat k\) are constants.

Product rules (keep the order in the cross product)
\(\dfrac{d}{du}(\vec A\cdot\vec B)=\vec A\cdot\dfrac{d\vec B}{du}+\dfrac{d\vec A}{du}\cdot\vec B\)
\(\dfrac{d}{du}(\vec A\times\vec B)=\vec A\times\dfrac{d\vec B}{du}+\dfrac{d\vec A}{du}\times\vec B\)
\(\dfrac{d}{du}(\phi\vec A)=\phi\dfrac{d\vec A}{du}+\dfrac{d\phi}{du}\vec A\)
\(\dfrac{d}{du}(\vec A\cdot\vec B\times\vec C)=\vec A\cdot\vec B\times\dfrac{d\vec C}{du}+\vec A\cdot\dfrac{d\vec B}{du}\times\vec C+\dfrac{d\vec A}{du}\cdot\vec B\times\vec C\)

Traps

  • Swapping the order in the cross-product rule gives the wrong sign. Write \(\vec A\times\vec B'\) first, \(\vec A'\times\vec B\) second.
  • In a dot-product rule, each term has one dot between two vectors. Never leave \(\hat i,\hat j\) hanging on a dot.

Worked examples

Partial derivatives of vector fields. For \(\vec A(x,y)\) the same rule: differentiate each component with respect to one variable, holding the others fixed. \(\vec A_x=\partial\vec A/\partial x\); \(\vec A_{xy}\) means differentiate in \(x\) then \(y\).

2. Velocity, acceleration, components in a direction

A particle at position \(\vec r(t)\) has velocity \(\vec v=\dfrac{d\vec r}{dt}\), speed \(|\vec v|=\dfrac{ds}{dt}\) and acceleration \(\vec a=\dfrac{d\vec v}{dt}=\dfrac{d^2\vec r}{dt^2}\).

Recipe · component of velocity or acceleration in a given direction

  1. Differentiate \(\vec r\) once for \(\vec v\), twice for \(\vec a\); put in the time asked.
  2. Take the direction \(\vec p\) given (not necessarily unit).
  3. Component \(=\dfrac{\vec v\cdot\vec p}{|\vec p|}\). The teacher struck out a version dividing by \(|\vec p|^2\): that gives the vector, not the number.

3. Space curves: tangent vector, unit tangent, tangent line

A curve in space is \(\vec r(t)=(x(t),y(t),z(t))\). Its derivative \(\vec r'(t)\) points along the curve; it is the velocity. So:

  • tangent vector at \(t_0\): \(\vec r'(t_0)\);
  • unit tangent: \(\vec T=\dfrac{\vec r'}{|\vec r'|}\);
  • tangent line at \(t_0\): \(\vec r(t_0)+s\,\vec r'(t_0)\), i.e. \(x=x_0+sx'\), \(y=y_0+sy'\), \(z=z_0+sz'\);
  • arc length: \(\dfrac{ds}{dt}=|\vec r'(t)|\).

Recipe · tangent line to a curve

  1. Compute \(\vec r'(t)\).
  2. Put in \(t_0\): the point \((x_0,y_0,z_0)=\vec r(t_0)\) and the direction \(\vec r'(t_0)\).
  3. Write the line \(\frac{x-x_0}{x'}=\frac{y-y_0}{y'}=\frac{z-z_0}{z'}\) or the parametric form.
  4. Unit tangent: divide \(\vec r'\) by its length. (A vector like \((3,4,5)\) is not a unit vector; its length is \(5\sqrt2\).)

4. The Frenet frame: T, N, B, curvature, torsion (one idea at a time)

Step 1: the unit tangent \(\vec T\). Already done: \(\vec T=\vec r'/|\vec r'|\), length 1.

Step 2: how fast the direction turns, curvature \(\kappa\). If a curve is a straight line, \(\vec T\) never changes. If it bends, \(\vec T\) turns. Measure the turning per unit arc length: \[\frac{d\vec T}{ds}=\kappa\vec N,\qquad \kappa=\left|\frac{d\vec T}{ds}\right|=\frac{|d\vec T/dt|}{|d\vec r/dt|}.\] Radius of curvature \(\rho=1/\kappa\). Because \(\vec T\) has constant length, \(d\vec T/ds\) is perpendicular to \(\vec T\).

Step 3: the principal normal \(\vec N\). It is the unit vector in the direction of \(d\vec T/ds\): \(\vec N=\dfrac1\kappa\dfrac{d\vec T}{ds}\). It points toward the inside of the bend.

Step 4: the binormal \(\vec B\). \(\vec B=\vec T\times\vec N\). Now \(\vec T,\vec N,\vec B\) are three mutually perpendicular unit vectors, a moving frame along the curve.

Step 5: torsion \(\tau\). How fast the curve twists out of its plane: \(\dfrac{d\vec B}{ds}=-\tau\vec N\). A plane curve has \(\tau=0\).

Planes and a shortcut
Osculating plane at \(P\): the plane containing \(\vec T\) and \(\vec N\) (the plane that fits the curve best). Its normal is \(\vec B\). Normal plane: perpendicular to \(\vec T\). Rectifying plane: perpendicular to \(\vec N\).
Shortcut formulas (use when \(\vec T\) is messy): \[\kappa=\frac{|\vec r'\times\vec r''|}{|\vec r'|^3},\qquad \vec B=\frac{\vec r'\times\vec r''}{|\vec r'\times\vec r''|},\qquad \tau=\frac{(\vec r'\times\vec r'')\cdot\vec r'''}{|\vec r'\times\vec r''|^2}.\]

Recipe · T, N, B, κ, τ the long way

  1. \(\vec r'\), \(|\vec r'|=ds/dt\).
  2. \(\vec T=\vec r'/|\vec r'|\).
  3. \(\dfrac{d\vec T}{dt}\); then \(\kappa=\dfrac{|d\vec T/dt|}{|\vec r'|}\) and \(\vec N=\dfrac{d\vec T/dt}{|d\vec T/dt|}\).
  4. \(\vec B=\vec T\times\vec N\).
  5. \(\tau\) from \(d\vec B/ds=-\tau\vec N\), i.e. \(\tau=-\dfrac{1}{|\vec r'|}\dfrac{d\vec B}{dt}\cdot\vec N\), or by the shortcut.

What to write

The definitions you use (\(\vec T\), \(\kappa\), \(\vec N\), \(\vec B\)), then each vector in turn, then the boxed result. For "define the osculating plane": the plane through \(P\) spanned by \(\vec T\) and \(\vec N\), with normal \(\vec B\).

Traps

  • Differentiating \(\vec T\) with respect to \(t\) and calling that \(\kappa\vec N\). You must divide by \(ds/dt=|\vec r'|\).
  • Forgetting that \(\vec N\) is a unit vector: divide \(d\vec T/ds\) by \(\kappa\).
  • Writing the \(\hat j\) term of \(\vec B\) with \(\hat i\) (a class-note slip). Check \(\vec B\cdot\vec T=0\) and \(\vec B\cdot\vec N=0\).

5. Tangential and normal components of acceleration

Write the velocity as speed times direction, \(\vec v=v\vec T\) with \(v=ds/dt\). Differentiate: \[\vec a=\frac{dv}{dt}\vec T+v\frac{d\vec T}{dt}=\frac{dv}{dt}\vec T+\kappa v^2\vec N.\] So the acceleration has a part along the path (speeding up) and a part toward the centre of the bend (turning).

Formulas to use
\(a_T=\dfrac{\vec v\cdot\vec a}{|\vec v|}\) (component along the tangent; vector \(\vec a_T=a_T\vec T\))
\(\vec a_N=\vec a-\vec a_T\), \(a_N=|\vec a_N|=\sqrt{|\vec a|^2-a_T^2}=\dfrac{|\vec v\times\vec a|}{|\vec v|}=\kappa v^2\).

Recipe · a_T and a_N at t = t₀

  1. \(\vec v(t_0)\) and \(\vec a(t_0)\).
  2. \(\vec v\cdot\vec a\) and \(|\vec v|\). Then \(a_T=\vec v\cdot\vec a/|\vec v|\).
  3. \(\vec a_T=a_T\,\vec v/|\vec v|\) (that is \(\dfrac{\vec v\cdot\vec a}{|\vec v|^2}\vec v\)).
  4. \(\vec a_N=\vec a-\vec a_T\); \(a_N=|\vec a_N|\). Cross-check with \(|\vec v\times\vec a|/|\vec v|\).

Traps

  • The \(\hat k\) component of \(\vec a_N\) in the 2023-24 question is \(+\tfrac97\); a senior's solution printed \(-\tfrac{12}7\).
  • \(a_T\) can be negative (slowing down). \(a_N\ge0\) always.

One-minute summary

  • Differentiate vectors componentwise. \((\vec A\times\vec B)'=\vec A\times\vec B'+\vec A'\times\vec B\) (order kept).
  • \(\vec v=\vec r'\), \(\vec a=\vec r''\), component along \(\vec p\) \(=\vec v\cdot\vec p/|\vec p|\).
  • Unit tangent \(\vec T=\vec r'/|\vec r'|\); tangent line \(\vec r_0+s\vec r'(t_0)\); \(ds/dt=|\vec r'|\).
  • \(\dfrac{d\vec T}{ds}=\kappa\vec N\), \(\vec B=\vec T\times\vec N\), \(\dfrac{d\vec B}{ds}=-\tau\vec N\); \(\kappa=\dfrac{|\vec r'\times\vec r''|}{|\vec r'|^3}\), \(\tau=\dfrac{(\vec r'\times\vec r'')\cdot\vec r'''}{|\vec r'\times\vec r''|^2}\).
  • Helix \((a\cos t,a\sin t,bt)\): \(\kappa=\dfrac a{a^2+b^2}\), \(\tau=\dfrac b{a^2+b^2}\). Circle: \(\kappa=1/a\), \(\tau=0\).
  • Osculating plane: through \(P\) with normal \(\vec r'\times\vec r''\).
  • \(a_T=\dfrac{\vec v\cdot\vec a}{|\vec v|}\), \(a_N=\dfrac{|\vec v\times\vec a|}{|\vec v|}=\sqrt{|\vec a|^2-a_T^2}=\kappa v^2\).
