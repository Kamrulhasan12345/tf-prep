Gradient: tangent plane, directional derivative, angle between surfaces

Part (a), (b) and (c) of Q1 are almost always gradient questions: tangent plane and normal line, directional derivative, and once the angle between two surfaces. Tangent plane and directional derivative came in 2022-23 and 2024-25.

Types: 6 tangent plane and normal line (2 of 3 years) · 7 directional derivative, including the maximum (2 of 3 years) · 9 angle between surfaces and applications of the gradient (2023-24). Mandatory: 5 cards.

1. The gradient and the one fact behind everything

Gradient
For a scalar function \(\phi(x,y,z)\): \(\nabla\phi=\dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}\hat i+\dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}\hat j+\dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial z}\hat k\). It is a vector. At each point it points in the direction in which \(\phi\) increases fastest, and its length is that fastest rate.

The one fact: \(\nabla\phi\) at a point is perpendicular (normal) to the level surface \(\phi=c\) through that point.

Why: take any curve \(\vec r(t)\) lying on the surface, so \(\phi(\vec r(t))=c\). Differentiate: \(\nabla\phi\cdot\dfrac{d\vec r}{dt}=0\). The tangent vector \(\vec r'\) of every such curve is perpendicular to \(\nabla\phi\), so \(\nabla\phi\) is normal to the surface.

Tangent plane, normal line, unit normal, angle between surfaces all follow from this. Directional derivative and maximum rate follow from the dot product.

2. Type 6: tangent plane and normal line

The teacher's table (all four rows are the same idea):

GivenTangent at \((x_0,y_0,z_0)\)
curve \(y=f(x)\)line \(y-y_0=m(x-x_0)\)
curve \(f(x,y)=0\)line \(f_x(x-x_0)+f_y(y-y_0)=0\)
surface \(z=f(x,y)\)plane \(f_x(x-x_0)+f_y(y-y_0)=z-z_0\)
surface \(f(x,y,z)=0\)plane \(f_x(x-x_0)+f_y(y-y_0)+f_z(z-z_0)=0\)

Recipe · tangent plane and normal line

  1. Move everything to one side: surface \(F(x,y,z)=0\). If the surface is \(z=f(x,y)\), use \(F=f-z\). (A constant on the right does not change \(\nabla F\).)
  2. Check the point lies on the surface: put it into \(F\).
  3. Compute \(\nabla F\) at the point. Call it \(\vec n=(a,b,c)\).
  4. Tangent plane: \(a(x-x_0)+b(y-y_0)+c(z-z_0)=0\). Simplify to \(ax+by+cz=d\).
  5. Normal line: \(\dfrac{x-x_0}{a}=\dfrac{y-y_0}{b}=\dfrac{z-z_0}{c}\). If one of \(a,b,c\) is 0, that coordinate stays constant (e.g. \(y=y_0\)).
  6. Unit normal: \(\pm\vec n/|\vec n|\).

What to write

\(F=\dots\), \(\nabla F=(\dots)\), \(\nabla F\) at the point, "normal vector \(\vec n\)", the plane equation (point-normal form first, then simplified) and the symmetric equations of the line.

Traps

  • Not checking the point is on the surface (the 2023-24 angle question is exactly such a misprint; see section 4).
  • Differentiating with respect to the wrong variable when a factor like \(z^3\) appears: the \(z\)-derivative of \(-yz^3+yz^2\) is \(-3yz^2+2yz\).
  • For \(4z=x^2-y^2\) the \(z\)-component of the normal is \(-4\), not \(-8\) (a senior's solution was wrong).
  • Writing the line as a ratio with a zero in the denominator.

3. Type 7: directional derivative

Directional derivative
The rate of change of \(\phi\) at \(P\) in the direction of the unit vector \(\hat a\): \(D_{\hat a}\phi=\nabla\phi\cdot\hat a\), with \(\hat a=\vec a/|\vec a|\). Largest value: \(|\nabla\phi|\), in the direction of \(\nabla\phi\). Smallest: \(-|\nabla\phi|\). It is 0 along directions tangent to the level surface.

Recipe · directional derivative

  1. \(\nabla\phi\), then put the point in.
  2. Make the direction a unit vector: \(\hat a=\vec a/|\vec a|\). (A direction given as "toward \(Q\)" means \(\vec a=\overrightarrow{PQ}\).)
  3. Answer \(=\nabla\phi\cdot\hat a\).
  4. Maximum rate \(=|\nabla\phi|\) in the direction \(\nabla\phi\).
  5. "Maximum \(=k\) in the direction of the \(z\)-axis": then \(\nabla\phi(P)=(0,0,\pm k)\). Solve for the unknown constants.

What to write

\(\nabla\phi\), its value at the point, \(\hat a\) with \(|\vec a|\) computed, the dot product, a boxed number.

Traps

  • Forgetting to divide by \(|\vec a|\). This is the most common slip.
  • Putting the point in before differentiating a product (substitute only after you have \(\nabla\phi\)).
  • For the "unknown constants" question: the gradient components give three equations, but the sign (\(+64\) or \(-64\)) changes all three constants.

4. Type 9: angle between surfaces and applications of the gradient

The angle between two surfaces at a common point is the angle between their normals:

\[ \cos\theta=\frac{\nabla\phi_1\cdot\nabla\phi_2}{|\nabla\phi_1|\,|\nabla\phi_2|}. \]

Take the absolute value of the dot product for the acute angle. The surfaces are orthogonal (cut at right angles) when \(\nabla\phi_1\cdot\nabla\phi_2=0\).

Recipe · angle between surfaces

  1. Write each surface as \(\phi_i=0\). Check the point lies on both.
  2. \(\vec n_i=\nabla\phi_i\) at the point.
  3. \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{|\vec n_1\cdot\vec n_2|}{|\vec n_1||\vec n_2|}\); \(\theta=\cos^{-1}(\cdot)\).

Applications of the gradient (a standard 2 to 4 mark answer): (1) normal to level surfaces; (2) tangent plane and normal line; (3) direction and value of the maximum rate of change; (4) directional derivatives; (5) angle between surfaces; (6) a conservative field is a gradient, \(\vec F=\nabla\phi\) (and \(\vec E=-\nabla V\) in physics); (7) steepest ascent and descent in optimisation.

The 2023-24 Q1(c) misprint

The point \((1,-1,2)\) printed in the question lies on neither surface: \(xy^2z-3x-z^2=2-3-4=-5\ne0\) and \(3x^2-y^2+2z=6\ne1\). The question is Schaum 4.66, where the point is \((1,-2,1)\); there both surfaces check out. Every solution in circulation just used the printed point and got about \(53.75^\circ\). In the exam: compute with the printed point (that is what is marked), and add one line "the point is not on the surfaces; with \((1,-2,1)\), \(\theta\approx79.92^\circ\)". One-minute summary

  • \(\nabla\phi\perp\) level surface \(\phi=c\). Normal vector \(\vec n=\nabla F(P)\).
  • Tangent plane: \(F_x(x-x_0)+F_y(y-y_0)+F_z(z-z_0)=0\). Normal line: \(\frac{x-x_0}{F_x}=\frac{y-y_0}{F_y}=\frac{z-z_0}{F_z}\).
  • For \(z=f(x,y)\): plane \(f_x(x-x_0)+f_y(y-y_0)=z-z_0\).
  • \(D_{\hat a}\phi=\nabla\phi\cdot\hat a\), \(\hat a=\vec a/|\vec a|\). Max rate \(=|\nabla\phi|\) along \(\nabla\phi\).
  • \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{\nabla\phi_1\cdot\nabla\phi_2}{|\nabla\phi_1||\nabla\phi_2|}\); orthogonal if the dot product is 0.
  • Always check the point is on the surface first.
