Divergence, curl, potentials and identities

Two of the most reliable marks in the paper: "show the field is irrotational and find \(\phi\)" and "prove an identity with \(r\)". Each has come in every MATH 241 paper.

Types: 1 irrotational field and scalar potential every year · 4 \(\vec r,r\) identities and operator identities (curl curl, \(r^n\vec r\) solenoidal) every year. Mandatory: 10 cards (6 MATH 241 TF, 4 CT-2 2023).

1. The operators, slowly

Del
\(\nabla=\hat i\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}+\hat j\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y}+\hat k\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}\). It is an instruction to differentiate, not a vector you can reorder.
Gradient (scalar to vector)
\(\nabla\phi=\phi_x\hat i+\phi_y\hat j+\phi_z\hat k\).
Divergence (vector to scalar)
For \(\vec F=F_1\hat i+F_2\hat j+F_3\hat k\): \(\nabla\cdot\vec F=\dfrac{\partial F_1}{\partial x}+\dfrac{\partial F_2}{\partial y}+\dfrac{\partial F_3}{\partial z}\).
Curl (vector to vector)
The determinant, expanded \(\hat i(\cdot)-\hat j(\cdot)+\hat k(\cdot)\): \[\nabla\times\vec F=\begin{vmatrix}\hat i&\hat j&\hat k\\ \partial_x&\partial_y&\partial_z\\ F_1&F_2&F_3\end{vmatrix}=(\partial_yF_3-\partial_zF_2)\hat i+(\partial_zF_1-\partial_xF_3)\hat j+(\partial_xF_2-\partial_yF_1)\hat k.\]
Laplacian
\(\nabla^2\phi=\nabla\cdot\nabla\phi=\phi_{xx}+\phi_{yy}+\phi_{zz}\).
Solenoidal
\(\nabla\cdot\vec F=0\) everywhere (no sources or sinks).
Irrotational
\(\nabla\times\vec F=\vec0\) everywhere. Then \(\vec F=\nabla\phi\) for some scalar \(\phi\) (a "conservative field"), and \(\phi\) is the scalar potential.

Physical meaning (a 2 to 5 mark Part A question). Divergence is the net outflow of the field per unit volume at a point: positive at a source, negative at a sink. Curl measures rotation: a tiny paddle wheel placed in the field spins, and for a rigid rotation \(\vec V=\vec\omega\times\vec r\) we get \(\nabla\times\vec V=2\vec\omega\), so the curl is twice the angular velocity. Curl zero means no rotation (irrotational).

Two identities you may quote: \(\nabla\times(\nabla\phi)=\vec0\) and \(\nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\vec F)=0\). (Mixed partials commute, so each component cancels in pairs.)

Worked examples: computing div and curl

2. Type 1: irrotational field and scalar potential every year

Recipe · find the potential

  1. Write \(\vec F=F_1\hat i+F_2\hat j+F_3\hat k\). Compute \(\nabla\times\vec F\) by the determinant. Show all three components are 0, so \(\vec F\) is irrotational.
  2. Set \(\vec F=\nabla\phi\): \(\phi_x=F_1,\ \phi_y=F_2,\ \phi_z=F_3\).
  3. Integrate each: \(\phi=\int F_1\,dx+f(y,z)=\int F_2\,dy+g(x,z)=\int F_3\,dz+h(x,y)\).
  4. Collect every distinct term once and add \(C\). (Do not add the three integrals.)
  5. Check by differentiating: \(\nabla\phi\) must give back \(\vec F\).
  6. If \(\phi(P)=k\) is given, solve for \(C\). If work is asked, work \(=\phi(B)-\phi(A)\).

What to write

The determinant, the three curl components each equal to 0, the statement "so \(\vec F\) is irrotational (conservative) and \(\vec F=\nabla\phi\)", the three integrations, the combined \(\phi\), and the value of \(C\) if asked.

Traps

  • Adding the three partial integrals instead of taking the union of terms.
  • "Rotational or not?" means compute the curl; do not guess.
  • For \(\varphi(a)=0\) with \(\varphi=\ln r+C\): \(C=-\ln a\), so \(\varphi=\ln(r/a)\).
  • "Find \(a,b,c\) so that \(\vec V\) is irrotational": set each curl component to 0.
  • For \(f(r)\vec r\) fields, the curl is always \(\vec 0\) (see Type 4); guess \(\phi=\int rf(r)\,dr\).

3. Type 4: identities with \(\vec r\) and \(r\) every year

Notation: \(\vec r=x\hat i+y\hat j+z\hat k\), \(r=|\vec r|=\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}\). The one fact everything rests on:

\[ \frac{\partial r}{\partial x}=\frac{x}{r},\quad\frac{\partial r}{\partial y}=\frac yr,\quad\frac{\partial r}{\partial z}=\frac zr\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \nabla f(r)=f'(r)\,\frac{\vec r}{r}. \]

(Differentiate \(r^2=x^2+y^2+z^2\): \(2r\,r_x=2x\).) Also \(\nabla\cdot\vec r=3\) and \(\nabla\times\vec r=\vec0\).

Key results (learn these)
\(\nabla r^n=nr^{n-2}\vec r\) · \(\nabla\ln r=\vec r/r^2\) · \(\nabla(1/r)=-\vec r/r^3\)
\(\nabla\cdot(f(r)\vec r)=rf'(r)+3f(r)\) · \(\nabla\cdot(r^n\vec r)=(n+3)r^n\), solenoidal iff \(n=-3\)
\(\nabla^2r^n=n(n+1)r^{n-2}\), so \(\nabla^2(1/r)=0\) · \(\nabla\times(f(r)\vec r)=\vec0\) · \(\nabla\cdot\hat r=2/r\)
\(\nabla\cdot(\vec r\times\vec a)=0,\ \nabla\times(\vec r\times\vec a)=-2\vec a,\ \nabla(\vec r\cdot\vec a)=\vec a\) for constant \(\vec a\).

Recipe · prove an r-identity

  1. State \(r^2=x^2+y^2+z^2\), so \(\partial r/\partial x=x/r\) (and \(y,z\)).
  2. Differentiate one component with the chain rule; the other two are the same with \(y,z\).
  3. Use the product rule \(\nabla\cdot(\phi\vec A)=\nabla\phi\cdot\vec A+\phi\,\nabla\cdot\vec A\).
  4. Use \(\vec r\cdot\vec r=r^2\) to simplify. State the final line and the condition (\(r\ne0\)).

Recipe · prove an operator identity (curl curl)

  1. Write the vector in components \(\vec A=(A_1,A_2,A_3)\).
  2. Expand the left side for the \(\hat i\) component only.
  3. Expand the right side for the \(\hat i\) component only.
  4. Show they agree; say "similarly for \(\hat j\) and \(\hat k\)". The BAC-CAB memory trick is not a proof and seniors lost marks for it.

What to write

Every identity: the starting fact \(\partial r/\partial x=x/r\), one displayed chain of equalities, and the conclusion line. For curl curl: both \(\hat i\) components written out in full.

Traps

  • \(\nabla^2(1/r)=0\), not \(-1/r^3\) (one class note ends wrongly). The sum \(3r^{-3}-3r^{-3}\) must be shown.
  • Quotient rule: the denominator of \(x/r^3\) differentiates to \(r^6\), not \(r^2\).
  • \(\nabla\cdot\vec r=3\), not 1. \(\nabla\cdot(r^n\vec r)=(n+3)r^n\), not \(nr^{n-1}\).
  • "Solenoidal for \(r^n\vec r\)" gives \(n=-3\), never \(n=3\).

One-minute summary

  • \(\nabla\cdot\vec F=\sum\partial_iF_i\); curl by the determinant \(\hat i(\partial_yF_3-\partial_zF_2)-\hat j(\partial_xF_3-\partial_zF_1)+\hat k(\partial_xF_2-\partial_yF_1)\).
  • Solenoidal: div \(=0\). Irrotational: curl \(=\vec0\), then \(\vec F=\nabla\phi\).
  • Potential: integrate \(F_1\) in \(x\), \(F_2\) in \(y\), \(F_3\) in \(z\); union of distinct terms plus \(C\); check; work \(=\phi(B)-\phi(A)\).
  • \(\partial r/\partial x=x/r\); \(\nabla r^n=nr^{n-2}\vec r\); \(\nabla\cdot\vec r=3\); \(\nabla\times\vec r=\vec0\).
  • \(\nabla\cdot(r^n\vec r)=(n+3)r^n\): solenoidal iff \(n=-3\); \(f(r)\vec r\) solenoidal gives \(f=C/r^3\).
  • \(\nabla^2r^n=n(n+1)r^{n-2}\); \(\nabla^2(1/r)=0\); \(\vec E=\vec r/r^2\) has \(\varphi=\ln(r/a)\).
  • \(\nabla\times(\nabla\times\vec A)=-\nabla^2\vec A+\nabla(\nabla\cdot\vec A)\): compare \(\hat i\) components, \(\partial_{xy}A_2-\partial_{yy}A_1-\partial_{zz}A_1+\partial_{xz}A_3\).
