1. Surface integral (flux)

Flux of \(\vec F\) through a surface \(S\)
\[\iint_S\vec F\cdot\hat n\,dS,\] where \(\hat n\) is the unit normal to \(S\) (outward for a closed surface, as the question says otherwise) and \(dS\) is a small piece of area of \(S\). It counts how much of the field passes through \(S\).
Expanded form
\(\iint_S\vec F\cdot\hat n\,dS=\iint_SF_1\,dy\,dz+F_2\,dz\,dx+F_3\,dx\,dy\).
Projection formula
Project \(S\) onto the \(xy\)-plane (region \(R\)): \(\ dS=\dfrac{dx\,dy}{|\hat n\cdot\hat k|}\). Onto the \(yz\)-plane: \(dS=\dfrac{dy\,dz}{|\hat n\cdot\hat i|}\). Onto the \(zx\)-plane: \(dS=\dfrac{dx\,dz}{|\hat n\cdot\hat j|}\).
Unit normal of \(\phi(x,y,z)=\text{const}\)
\(\hat n=\pm\dfrac{\nabla\phi}{|\nabla\phi|}\). Choose the sign (outward / upward) the question wants.

Why the projection. The tilted surface piece \(dS\) has a shadow \(dx\,dy\) on the \(xy\)-plane, and shadow \(=dS\cdot|\hat n\cdot\hat k|\) (the tilt shrinks it). So \(dS=dx\,dy/|\hat n\cdot\hat k|\). You may only project onto a plane the surface is not perpendicular to, i.e. where the denominator is not 0.

Recipe · flux by projection

  1. Write the surface as \(\phi(x,y,z)=\text{const}\). Compute \(\nabla\phi\) and \(\hat n=\nabla\phi/|\nabla\phi|\) with the sign the question wants.
  2. Choose the projection plane. If \(\hat n\cdot\hat k\neq0\), the \(xy\)-plane works. If \(\hat n\cdot\hat k=0\) (a vertical wall, like a cylinder about the \(z\)-axis), use a plane the wall is not perpendicular to (\(xz\) or \(yz\)).
  3. \(dS=\dfrac{dx\,dy}{|\hat n\cdot\hat k|}\) (or the matching formula). For a plane \(ax+by+cz=d\) the shortcut is \(\hat n\,dS=\dfrac{(a,b,c)}{c}\,dx\,dy\).
  4. Compute \(\vec F\cdot\hat n\) and use the surface equation to remove the third variable (\(z\) in terms of \(x,y\), etc.).
  5. Find the shadow \(R\) of \(S\) on the projection plane (a triangle for a first-octant plane, a rectangle for a cylinder piece). Set the limits and integrate \(\iint_R\vec F\cdot\hat n\,\frac{dx\,dy}{|\hat n\cdot\hat k|}\).
  6. Closed surface (cube, box)? Do each face with its own outward normal and add, or use the divergence theorem.

What to write

The surface equation, \(\hat n\), the projection plane and \(dS\), \(\vec F\cdot\hat n\) reduced to two variables, the region \(R\) with its limits, the integral and the boxed value. A small sketch of \(S\) and \(R\) earns the method marks.

Traps

  • Projecting a vertical wall onto the \(xy\)-plane: \(\hat n\cdot\hat k=0\), so \(dS\) is infinite. Choose \(xz\) or \(yz\) (our class note tried \(xy\) first, then fixed it).
  • Forgetting the absolute value, or the \(1/|\hat n\cdot\hat k|\) altogether.
  • Wrong limits: the region is the shadow, not the surface. For \(2x+3y+6z=12\) in the first octant the shadow is the triangle \(2x+3y\le12\) (set \(z=0\)).
  • Normal pointing the wrong way (inward instead of outward) flips the sign.

2. Volume integrals

\(\iiint_V\vec F\,dV\) (vector-valued: integrate each component) or \(\iiint_V\phi\,dV\). It is three ordinary integrals, with limits from the bounding surfaces.

Recipe · volume integral

  1. Sketch the solid. Name the surfaces that bound it.
  2. Choose the order: innermost variable runs between two surfaces (e.g. \(z\) from \(x^2\) to 4), then the next between two curves, the last between two numbers.
  3. Integrate component by component (\(\hat i\), \(\hat j\), \(\hat k\) separately for a vector \(\vec F\)).
  4. Symmetry: an odd integrand over a region symmetric about that variable gives 0.
  5. Cylinder: \(dV=r\,dr\,d\theta\,dz\). Sphere: \(dV=\rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta\).

3. The divergence theorem (Gauss) weak spot

Divergence theorem
If \(V\) is a volume bounded by a closed surface \(S\), \(\hat n\) is the outward unit normal, and \(\vec F\) has continuous first partial derivatives in \(V\), then \[\iint_S\vec F\cdot\hat n\,dS=\iiint_V\nabla\cdot\vec F\,dV,\qquad \nabla\cdot\vec F=\frac{\partial F_1}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial F_2}{\partial y}+\frac{\partial F_3}{\partial z}.\] Expanded form: \(\iint_SF_1\,dy\,dz+F_2\,dz\,dx+F_3\,dx\,dy=\iiint_V\nabla\cdot\vec F\,dV\).
Useful special case
\(\nabla\cdot\vec r=3\), so \(\iint_S\vec r\cdot\hat n\,dS=3V\). And \(\nabla\cdot(r^n\vec r)=(n+3)r^n\).

Meaning: the total outflow through the closed surface equals the total "source strength" inside. It lets you trade a surface integral (several faces) for a volume integral (one integral).

Recipe · evaluate a surface integral using Gauss

  1. Check \(S\) is closed. If the question gives only the curved part, add the flat lid or base as part of \(S\) (e.g. a hemisphere plus its base disc) when the question says "the closed region".
  2. Write the integral as \(\iint\vec F\cdot\hat n\,dS\). If given as \(\iint F_1dy\,dz+F_2dz\,dx+F_3dx\,dy\), read off \(\vec F=(F_1,F_2,F_3)\).
  3. Compute \(\nabla\cdot\vec F\).
  4. Integrate it over \(V\): cylinder coordinates for cylinders, spherical for spheres, plain limits for boxes. Use symmetry.
  5. Box the value.

Recipe · verify the divergence theorem

  1. Volume side: \(\nabla\cdot\vec F\), then \(\iiint_V\nabla\cdot\vec F\,dV\).
  2. Surface side: split \(S\) into faces; for each give \(\hat n\) (outward) and \(dS\), compute \(\vec F\cdot\hat n\), integrate:
    • plane \(x=\)const: \(\hat n=\pm\hat i\), \(dS=dy\,dz\) (likewise for \(y,z\));
    • cylinder wall \(x^2+y^2=a^2\): \(\hat n=\frac{(x,y,0)}a\), \(dS=a\,d\theta\,dz\);
    • sphere radius \(a\): \(\hat n=\vec r/a\), \(dS=a^2\sin\phi\,d\phi\,d\theta\);
    • slanted plane: \(dS=dx\,dy/|\hat n\cdot\hat k|\) (the projection recipe).
  3. Add the faces. Compare with the volume side. Write "L.H.S. \(=\) R.H.S., verified".

What to write

Statement of the divergence theorem (with outward \(\hat n\)). Sketch of the solid with faces named. \(\nabla\cdot\vec F\). Volume integral and value. One line per face with \(\hat n\), \(\vec F\cdot\hat n\), the value. The sum. "Verified."

Traps

  • Never replace \(x^2+y^2+z^2\) by \(a^2\) inside the volume: \(a^2\) holds only on the surface of the sphere. The suggestion book and our class note did this for the hemisphere and got \(\frac{2\pi a^5}3\); the correct value is \(\mathbf{\frac{2\pi a^5}{5}}\) (use the corrected value).
  • Bottom face \(z=0\): the outward normal is \(-\hat k\), so \(\vec F\cdot\hat n=-F_3\).
  • Closed means every face counts: faces where \(\vec F\cdot\hat n=0\) still need a line saying so.
  • A term odd in \(y\) over a region symmetric in \(y\) integrates to 0 (spot it before integrating, e.g. \(4y\) over a disc).
  • Each face integral carries \(dS\), not just \(dx\,dy\): on the cylinder wall it is \(a\,d\theta\,dz\).

One-minute summary

  • Flux \(=\iint_S\vec F\cdot\hat n\,dS\), \(dS=\dfrac{dx\,dy}{|\hat n\cdot\hat k|}\) (or \(dy\,dz/|\hat n\cdot\hat i|\), \(dx\,dz/|\hat n\cdot\hat j|\)). Plane \(ax+by+cz=d\): \(\hat n\,dS=(a,b,c)\,dx\,dy/c\).
  • Vertical wall (cylinder): project onto \(xz\) or \(yz\), never \(xy\). Plane \(2x+3y+6z=12\), \(\vec A=(18z,-12,3y)\): 24. Cylinder \(x^2+y^2=16\): 90.
  • Gauss: \(\iint_S\vec F\cdot\hat n\,dS=\iiint_V\nabla\cdot\vec F\,dV\), \(\hat n\) outward, \(S\) closed.
  • \(\iint_S\vec r\cdot\hat n\,dS=3V\); \(\nabla\cdot(r^n\vec r)=(n+3)r^n\).
  • Verify: volume side plus one line per face (\(\pm\hat i,\pm\hat j,\pm\hat k\); cylinder wall \(dS=a\,d\theta\,dz\)). Bottom face has \(\hat n=-\hat k\).
  • Hemisphere \(\vec F\) with div \(=x^2+y^2+z^2\): \(\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^{\pi/2}\int_0^a\rho^4\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta=\frac{2\pi a^5}5\) (not \(\frac{2\pi a^5}3\)). Cylinder verify: \(84\pi\). Box verify: \(abc(a+b+c)\).
