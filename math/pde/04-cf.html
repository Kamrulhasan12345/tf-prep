Linear PDEs with constant coefficients: the complementary function

Every MATH 241 paper has a Q7 of "solve these higher-order PDEs". Each part needs a complementary function (CF) first, and the CF is the half you can get completely right by following a fixed procedure. This page does the CF only, slowly; the next page (particular integrals) finishes the solutions.

Types: 1 homogeneous operator every year · 2 non-homogeneous but reducible (Cases 1–3) every year · 3 irreducible, \(\sum Ae^{hx+ky}\) 22-23 and 23-24. weak spot Mandatory: 14 cards (the CF parts of every slide example and of the 2022-23 to 2024-25 Q7 parts).

0. Notation, slowly

The operators
\(D_x=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}\), \(D_y=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y}\). A product means "apply twice": \(D_x^2z=z_{xx}\), \(D_xD_yz=z_{xy}\), \(D_y^2z=z_{yy}\). Old notation: \(p=D_xz,\ q=D_yz,\ r=D_x^2z,\ s=D_xD_yz,\ t=D_y^2z\). So \(2r+5s+2t=0\) is the same thing as \((2D_x^2+5D_xD_y+2D_y^2)z=0\).
The equation
\(F(D_x,D_y)\,z=f(x,y)\) where \(F\) is a polynomial in \(D_x,D_y\) with constant coefficients. Its order is the highest total power.
Homogeneous operator
Every term of \(F\) has the same total degree \(n\), e.g. \(D_x^2+3D_xD_y+2D_y^2\) (all degree 2). This is Farid sir's §4.1. Note it only talks about \(F\); \(f\) can be anything.
Non-homogeneous operator
Terms of mixed degree, e.g. \(D_x^2+D_xD_y+D_y-1\) (degrees 2, 2, 1, 0). This is §4.2. It is reducible if it splits into linear factors \((\alpha D_x+\beta D_y+\gamma)\), otherwise irreducible.
Complementary function
\(z_c\) = the general solution of \(F(D_x,D_y)z=0\) (right side replaced by 0). It has as many arbitrary functions as the order.
Particular integral
\(z_p\) = any one solution of \(F\,z=f\). The full solution is \(z=z_c+z_p\) (because the equation is linear: \(F(z_c+z_p)=0+f\)).

1. Type 1: CF of a homogeneous operator every year

Why the rule works (so you can reconstruct it)

Try \(z=\phi(y+mx)\), where \(\phi\) is any twice-differentiable function and \(m\) a constant. Put \(w=y+mx\). Then \(D_xz=m\phi'(w)\) and \(D_yz=\phi'(w)\). Every \(D_x\) brings a factor \(m\) and every \(D_y\) a factor \(1\), so a term \(D_x^iD_y^j\) (total degree \(i+j=n\)) gives \(m^i\phi^{(n)}(w)\). Hence \[F(D_x,D_y)\,\phi(y+mx)=F(m,1)\,\phi^{(n)}(y+mx).\] This is zero for every \(\phi\) exactly when \(F(m,1)=0\). That is the whole rule: replace \(D_x\to m,\ D_y\to1\), solve for \(m\).

Recipe · CF of a homogeneous operator

  1. Write the equation as \(F(D_x,D_y)z=\dots\). (If it is given with \(r,s,t\): \(r\to D_x^2,\ s\to D_xD_y,\ t\to D_y^2\).)
  2. Auxiliary equation: replace \(D_x\to m,\ D_y\to1\). Solve \(F(m,1)=0\). (Try \(m=\pm1,\pm2,\pm\frac12\) first, then divide by \((m-\text{root})\).)
  3. Each distinct root \(m\) gives a term \(\phi(y+mx)\).
  4. A root repeated \(k\) times gives \(\phi_1(y+mx)+x\phi_2(y+mx)+\dots+x^{k-1}\phi_k(y+mx)\). Each repeat adds one more power of \(x\) outside, never inside the bracket.
  5. Complex roots \(m=\pm i\): keep them as \(\phi(y+ix)+\phi(y-ix)\). Do not convert to \(\sin,\cos\) as in an ODE.
  6. \(m=0\) gives \(\phi(y)\). If the \(D_x^n\) term is missing (e.g. a factor \(D_y\)), the polynomial \(F(m,1)\) has lower degree than \(n\); each missing root contributes a factor \(D_y\), which gives \(\phi(x)\).
  7. Fractions are allowed to be cleared: \(\phi(y+\frac34x)=\psi(4y+3x)\), because \(\phi\) is arbitrary.

Factor form shortcut. If the operator is already factored as \(\prod(b_iD_x-a_iD_y)\): put \(D_x\to m,\ D_y\to1\) in one factor, \(bm-a=0\), so \(m=\frac ab\) and the term is \(\phi(y+\frac abx)=\psi(ax+by)\). Example: \((2D_x-D_y)\) has \(b=2,\ a=1\), giving \(\phi(x+2y)\).

What to write

"Auxiliary equation: put \(D_x=m,\ D_y=1\): \(\dots=0\), so \(m=\dots\)." Then the factorisation, the list of roots, and \(z_c=\dots\) with a separate arbitrary function for each root. State \(z=z_c+z_p\) once.

Traps

  • Sign: root \(m=2\) gives \(\phi(y+2x)\), not \(\phi(y-2x)\). Root \(m=-1\) gives \(\phi(y-x)\).
  • Repeated root: \(\phi_1(y+mx)+x\phi_2(y+mx)\). The second function is multiplied by \(x\); it is not \(\phi_2(y+mx^2)\) and it is not \(\phi_1(y+mx)\) twice.
  • Complex roots stay as \(\phi(y\pm ix)\) (Raiyan p.47).
  • When one factor is only \(D_y\) the auxiliary equation has fewer roots than the order. Count roots against the order: a third-order operator must give three functions.
  • Do not put \(D_x\to1,\ D_y\to m\) and then write \(\phi(x+my)\) unless you carry the convention through; the course convention is \(D_x\to m,\ D_y\to1\), \(\phi(y+mx)\).

2. Type 2: CF of a non-homogeneous reducible operator every year

Why \(e^{kx}\phi(y+mx)\) appears

Take the simplest linear factor, \((D_x-mD_y-k)z=0\), i.e. \(p-mq=kz\). This is a Lagrange equation \(P p+Qq=R\) with \(P=1,\ Q=-m,\ R=kz\). The auxiliary system is \(\dfrac{dx}1=\dfrac{dy}{-m}=\dfrac{dz}{kz}\). From the first two, \(y+mx=c_1\). From the first and third, \(\dfrac{dz}z=k\,dx\), so \(z=c_2e^{kx}\). Combine with \(c_2=\phi(c_1)\): \[(D_x-mD_y-k)z=0\ \Longrightarrow\ z=e^{kx}\phi(y+mx).\] Farid sir's three cases are just this, written for a general factor \((\alpha D_x+\beta D_y+\gamma)\):

FactorCF termWhy
Case 1: \((\alpha D_x+\beta D_y+\gamma)\), \(\alpha\ne0\)\(e^{-\gamma x/\alpha}\,\phi(\beta x-\alpha y)\)divide by \(\alpha\): \(m=-\beta/\alpha\), \(k=-\gamma/\alpha\); \(y+mx\propto\alpha y-\beta x\)
Case 2: \((\alpha D_x+\gamma)\)\(e^{-\gamma x/\alpha}\,\phi(y)\)Case 1 with \(\beta=0\)
Case 3: \((\beta D_y+\gamma)\)\(e^{-\gamma y/\beta}\,\phi(x)\)\(z_y=-\frac\gamma\beta z\), so \(z=e^{-\gamma y/\beta}\times\)(function of \(x\))
Repeated \((D_x-mD_y-k)^r\)\(e^{kx}\,[\phi_1+x\phi_2+\dots+x^{r-1}\phi_r](y+mx)\)same extra-powers-of-\(x\) rule as Type 1 (from the seniors; not in Farid sir's notes but correct)

The CF of a product of distinct factors is the sum of the CF terms of the factors (each factor kills its own term, and the factors commute).

How to factor a non-homogeneous operator

Recipe · factorise

  1. Take the highest-degree part of \(F\) (all terms of top degree) and factor it exactly as in Type 1: \(L_1L_2\), where \(L_i\) are linear in \(D_x,D_y\).
  2. Assume \(F=(L_1+a)(L_2+b)\) with unknown constants \(a,b\). Expand: \(L_1L_2+aL_2+bL_1+ab\).
  3. Match the degree-1 terms (this gives two equations for \(a,b\)) and then the constant term (this must come out equal to \(ab\)).
  4. If the equations are inconsistent, \(F\) is irreducible: go to Type 3.

Traps

  • Check the constant term \(ab\) last; students stop after matching the linear terms and trust a wrong factorisation.
  • Bring each factor to the form of a Case before reading off the CF: \((2D_x-5D_y+6)\) is Case 1 with \(\alpha=2,\beta=-5,\gamma=6\), so \(e^{-3x}\phi(-5x-2y)\), i.e. \(e^{-3x}\phi(5x+2y)\).
  • \(e^{-\gamma x/\alpha}\) has a minus sign: \((D_x+1)\) gives \(e^{-x}\), \((D_x-1)\) gives \(e^{x}\).

3. Type 3: CF of an irreducible operator 22-23 7c, 23-24 7b

Examples of irreducible operators: \(D_x^2-D_y\), \(3D_x^2-2D_y^2+D_x-1\), \(D_x^2D_y+D_y^2-2\), \(D_x^2+3D_y-2D_x\). No choice of constants \(a,b\) in the factorisation recipe works for them. Instead try an exponential, which works because \[D_x^iD_y^j\,e^{hx+ky}=h^ik^j\,e^{hx+ky}\ \Longrightarrow\ F(D_x,D_y)\,e^{hx+ky}=F(h,k)\,e^{hx+ky}.\] So \(e^{hx+ky}\) is a solution of \(Fz=0\) whenever \(F(h,k)=0\). That is one equation in two unknowns, so there is a whole family of \((h,k)\), and any sum of them is a solution: \[z_c=\sum_{h,k}A\,e^{hx+ky},\qquad F(h,k)=0.\]

Recipe · irreducible CF

  1. Show \(F\) does not factor (the matching in section 2 fails), or just say "\(F\) is irreducible".
  2. Write \(z_c=\sum A\,e^{hx+ky}\), where \(A\) is an arbitrary constant and the sum runs over all \((h,k)\) satisfying \(F(h,k)=0\).
  3. Write \(F(h,k)=0\) explicitly (replace \(D_x\to h,\ D_y\to k\)). If it is easy, solve for \(k\) in terms of \(h\) and put it in: \(z_c=\sum Ae^{hx+k(h)y}\). If not, leave the relation as it is.
  4. If \(F\) is a product of a reducible part and an irreducible part, add the two CFs (see the example \((D_x^2-D_y)(D_x-2D_y)\)).

What to write

"\(F\) is irreducible, so \(z_c=\sum A\,e^{hx+ky}\) where \(F(h,k)=0\)", then the relation. The answer in this form is complete; no arbitrary functions appear.

Traps

  • Do not write \(\phi(y+mx)\) forms for an irreducible factor; they do not solve it.
  • Replace \(D_x\to h\), \(D_y\to k\) including constants: \(3D_x^2-2D_y^2+D_x-1\) becomes \(3h^2-2k^2+h-1\).
  • Farid sir did teach this (Lec 10, slide §4.2 "irreducible"). Seniors wrote "not taught in class"; that was their year.

Worked examples

4. How to get the CF: decision list

Recipe · flowchart as an ordered list

  1. Put the equation in operator form \(F(D_x,D_y)z=f\). Convert \(r,s,t,p,q\) if needed. Ignore \(f\) for the CF.
  2. Is \(F\) homogeneous? (every term the same total degree)
    • Yes: Type 1. Put \(D_x\to m,\ D_y\to1\), solve \(F(m,1)=0\). Roots distinct: \(\phi(y+mx)\). Root repeated \(k\) times: add \(x\phi_2,\dots,x^{k-1}\phi_k\). Complex root: \(\phi(y\pm ix)\). Missing root (no \(D_x^n\) term, factor \(D_y\)): \(\phi(x)\).
  3. No: try to factor into \((\alpha D_x+\beta D_y+\gamma)\) factors (top-degree part first, then match \(a,b\)).
    • Factors found (reducible): Type 2. For each factor use Case 1 (\(\alpha,\beta\) both non-zero), Case 2 (only \(D_x\)) or Case 3 (only \(D_y\)). A factor repeated \(r\) times gets \(1,x,\dots,x^{r-1}\) in front of the functions.
    • The matching fails (irreducible): Type 3. \(z_c=\sum Ae^{hx+ky}\), \(F(h,k)=0\).
    • Partly factors (a linear factor times an irreducible one): CF is the sum of the two parts.
  4. Count and check. A reducible operator of order \(n\) must produce \(n\) arbitrary functions. Substitute one term back if in doubt (e.g. \(e^{kx}\phi(y+mx)\) into the factor).

One-minute summary

  • Homogeneous \(F\): \(D_x\to m,\ D_y\to1\), \(F(m,1)=0\); root \(m\) gives \(\phi(y+mx)\); a root repeated \(k\) times gives \(\phi_1+x\phi_2+\dots+x^{k-1}\phi_k\) (same argument); complex roots stay \(\phi(y\pm ix)\); factor \(D_y\) gives \(\phi(x)\).
  • Factor \((bD_x-aD_y)\) gives \(\phi(ax+by)\).
  • Non-homogeneous reducible: factor as \((L_1+a)(L_2+b)\) after matching degree-1 and constant terms.
  • Case 1: \((\alpha D_x+\beta D_y+\gamma)\to e^{-\gamma x/\alpha}\phi(\beta x-\alpha y)\). Case 2: \((\alpha D_x+\gamma)\to e^{-\gamma x/\alpha}\phi(y)\). Case 3: \((\beta D_y+\gamma)\to e^{-\gamma y/\beta}\phi(x)\).
  • \((D_x-mD_y-k)^r\to e^{kx}[\phi_1+x\phi_2+\dots+x^{r-1}\phi_r](y+mx)\).
  • Irreducible: \(z_c=\sum Ae^{hx+ky}\) with \(F(h,k)=0\) (\(D_x\to h,\ D_y\to k\)).
  • Number of arbitrary functions = order, for reducible operators.
