Classification of second-order PDEs

Is a second-order linear PDE hyperbolic, parabolic or elliptic? The test is the sign of \(B^2-4AC\). Farid sir's notes end exactly here (wave is hyperbolic, heat is parabolic). No MATH 241 paper has asked it yet, but it is the natural end of the PDE syllabus, so it is short enough to learn in ten minutes.

Types. (1) classify a given equation; (2) find where the type changes when \(A,B,C\) depend on \(x,y\); (3) reduce to canonical form (in syllabus, not taught in class: Raisinghania Part II Ch 8, S. L. Ross Ch 12). No paper has asked it, so only the two class-level cards are mandatory.

1. The test

Write the equation as

\[A\,u_{xx}+B\,u_{xy}+C\,u_{yy}+D\,u_x+E\,u_y+F\,u=f,\]

where \(B\) is the full coefficient of \(u_{xy}\) (if the equation has \(2u_{xy}\), then \(B=2\)). Only \(A,B,C\) matter; \(D,E,F,f\) do not.

\(B^2-4AC\)TypeArchetype
\(\gt0\)hyperbolicwave \(u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}\)
\(=0\)parabolicheat \(u_t=c^2u_{xx}\)
\(\lt0\)ellipticLaplace \(u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0\)

Recipe · classify

  1. Identify the two independent variables (\(x,y\), or \(x,t\)) and read off \(A,B,C\) as the coefficients of \(u_{xx},u_{xy},u_{yy}\) (for \(x,t\): \(u_{xx},u_{xt},u_{tt}\)). A missing term has coefficient 0.
  2. Compute \(B^2-4AC\).
  3. If \(A,B,C\) are constants, one answer. If they depend on \(x,y\), the type depends on the point: solve \(B^2-4AC\gt0\), \(=0\), \(\lt0\) and say which region is which.

What to write

"Here \(A=\dots,\ B=\dots,\ C=\dots\), so \(B^2-4AC=\dots\), hence the equation is hyperbolic / parabolic / elliptic" and, for variable coefficients, "in the region \(\dots\), on the curve \(\dots\), in the region \(\dots\)".

Traps

  • The equation \(u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}\) must be moved to one side first: \(c^2u_{xx}-u_{tt}=0\), so \(A=c^2,\ C=-1\), not \(+1\). Sign errors here flip hyperbolic to elliptic.
  • \(B\) is the coefficient of \(u_{xy}\), not half of it. Some books write \(Au_{xx}+2Bu_{xy}+Cu_{yy}\) and test \(B^2-AC\). Farid sir's form is \(B^2-4AC\) with the full coefficient. Stay with his.
  • First-order terms (\(u_t\) in the heat equation) never enter the test. Heat: \(c^2u_{xx}-u_t=0\) has \(B=0,\ C=0\), so \(B^2-4AC=0\).
  • With variable coefficients, say where the type changes (usually a curve) and what the type is on each side. Just writing "\(-4y\)" earns no marks.

2. Canonical forms in syllabus, not taught in class

Idea: find new variables \(\xi,\eta\) in which the second-order part becomes as simple as possible, so the equation can be integrated. For constant coefficients this is the same method as chapter 4 in disguise.

Characteristic equation
\(A\lambda^2-B\lambda+C=0\) with \(\lambda=\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) (for constant \(A,B,C\), \(\lambda\) is the slope of the characteristic lines \(y-\lambda x=\text{const}\)).
Why this equation
Try \(u=f(y-\lambda x)\): \(u_{xx}=\lambda^2f'',\ u_{xy}=-\lambda f'',\ u_{yy}=f''\), so \(Au_{xx}+Bu_{xy}+Cu_{yy}=(A\lambda^2-B\lambda+C)f''\), which is 0 exactly when \(\lambda\) is a root. (Compare chapter 4: \(m=-\lambda\).)
TypeRoots \(\lambda\)New variablesCanonical form
hyperbolictwo real, \(\lambda_1\ne\lambda_2\)\(\xi=y-\lambda_1x,\ \eta=y-\lambda_2x\)\(u_{\xi\eta}=\dots\)
parabolicone double root \(\lambda\)\(\xi=y-\lambda x\), \(\eta=x\) (anything independent of \(\xi\))\(u_{\eta\eta}=\dots\)
elliptic\(\lambda=\alpha\pm i\beta\)\(\xi=y-\alpha x,\ \eta=\beta x\)\(u_{\xi\xi}+u_{\eta\eta}=\dots\)

For variable coefficients, \(\lambda\) is a function; solve the ODE \(dy/dx=\lambda(x,y)\) for each root, write each solution as \(\varphi(x,y)=c\), and take \(\xi,\eta\) to be those \(\varphi\)'s (see the last card).

Recipe · reduce to canonical form

  1. Classify with \(B^2-4AC\).
  2. Solve \(A\lambda^2-B\lambda+C=0\). Choose \(\xi,\eta\) from the table.
  3. Chain rule: \(u_x=u_\xi\xi_x+u_\eta\eta_x\), \(u_y=u_\xi\xi_y+u_\eta\eta_y\), and again for \(u_{xx},u_{xy},u_{yy}\). Write all three out.
  4. Substitute into the equation and collect \(u_{\xi\xi},u_{\xi\eta},u_{\eta\eta}\). Check that the coefficients you wanted to vanish really do.
  5. If it reduces to \(u_{\xi\eta}=0\) or \(u_{\eta\eta}=0\), integrate (arbitrary functions), then go back to \(x,y\).

What to write

Type, characteristic equation and its roots, the choice of \(\xi,\eta\), the chain-rule derivatives, the substituted equation, and the canonical form. Say that the Jacobian \(\xi_x\eta_y-\xi_y\eta_x\ne0\) (the change of variables is legitimate).

Traps

  • Roots of \(A\lambda^2-B\lambda+C=0\) have the sign of \(B\) flipped compared with \(A\lambda^2+B\lambda+C\). For \(u_{xx}+4u_{xy}+3u_{yy}\) it is \(\lambda^2-4\lambda+3\), roots 1, 3 (not \(-1,-3\)).
  • Forgetting a cross term in \(u_{xx}\): \(u_{xx}=u_{\xi\xi}\xi_x^2+2u_{\xi\eta}\xi_x\eta_x+u_{\eta\eta}\eta_x^2+u_\xi\xi_{xx}+u_\eta\eta_{xx}\). With linear \(\xi,\eta\) the last two vanish; with \(\xi=xy\) they do not.
  • Parabolic: \(\eta\) must be independent of \(\xi\). Taking \(\eta=y-\lambda x\) again gives a zero Jacobian.

One-minute summary

  • \(Au_{xx}+Bu_{xy}+Cu_{yy}+\dots\): \(B^2-4AC\gt0\) hyperbolic, \(=0\) parabolic, \(\lt0\) elliptic. \(B\) is the full coefficient of \(u_{xy}\).
  • Wave \(c^2u_{xx}-u_{tt}\): \(4c^2\gt0\) hyperbolic. Heat \(c^2u_{xx}-u_t\): 0 parabolic. Laplace: \(-4\) elliptic.
  • Variable coefficients: solve the inequality and name the regions (curve where it is 0 is parabolic).
  • Characteristic equation \(A\lambda^2-B\lambda+C=0\), \(\lambda=dy/dx\).
  • Hyperbolic \(\xi=y-\lambda_1x,\ \eta=y-\lambda_2x\to u_{\xi\eta}=\dots\); parabolic \(\xi=y-\lambda x,\ \eta=x\to u_{\eta\eta}=\dots\); elliptic \(\lambda=\alpha\pm i\beta\), \(\xi=y-\alpha x,\ \eta=\beta x\to u_{\xi\xi}+u_{\eta\eta}=\dots\).
  • Wave: \(\xi=x-ct,\ \eta=x+ct\to u_{\xi\eta}=0\), \(u=f(x-ct)+g(x+ct)\) (d'Alembert).
