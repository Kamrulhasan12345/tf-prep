Wave equation the O'Neil way

Farid sir told the class: for the wave equation, see Peter V. O'Neil, Advanced Engineering Mathematics, and no more practice sheets will be given. So a wave question can be worded, and its answer written, in O'Neil's style and notation. This chapter is that book's §16.1, §16.2 (string on an interval) and §16.6 (d'Alembert) turned into types, recipes and solved cards.

Types. (1) string released from rest, \(g=0\) (16.2.1); (2) string struck, \(f=0\) (16.2.2); (3) both \(f\) and \(g\) (16.2.3); (4) what changing \(c\), \(L\) or the data does (16.2.4); (5) forcing term, \(y_{tt}=c^2y_{xx}+F\) (16.2.5); (6) d'Alembert on the whole line, with and without forcing (16.6). Types 1 to 3 are the exam type (Q8 string). every year Mandatory: 10 cards, all fixed-end strings. Types 5 and 6 are optional extras. weak spot Section 3 below lists four misprints in the book, so check your own working against the cards, not against the printed answers.

1. O'Neil's setup and notation

Problem (O'Neil 16.1)
\(y_{tt}=c^2y_{xx}\) for \(0\lt x\lt L,\ t\gt0\); \(y(0,t)=y(L,t)=0\) for \(t\ge0\); \(y(x,0)=f(x)\) (initial position); \(y_t(x,0)=g(x)\) (initial velocity). Here \(y(x,t)\) is the displacement of the string and \(c^2=h/\rho\) (horizontal tension over mass per unit length). Compatibility: \(f(0)=f(L)=0\).
Answer
\(\displaystyle y(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big[a_n\cos\frac{n\pi ct}{L}+b_n\sin\frac{n\pi ct}{L}\Big]\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}\), with \(a_n=\dfrac2L\displaystyle\int_0^Lf(x)\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}dx\) and \(b_n=\dfrac2{n\pi c}\displaystyle\int_0^Lg(x)\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}dx\).

Where the equation comes from (O'Neil 16.1, in short). Take a small piece of string between \(x\) and \(x+\Delta x\). The tension pulls along the string at both ends; only the vertical components matter, and they give \(\rho\,\Delta x\,y_{tt}\) (Newton). The horizontal component \(h\) of tension is the same everywhere (the string does not move sideways), and the vertical component is \(h\,y_x\), so \(\rho y_{tt}=h\,y_{xx}\). Put \(c^2=h/\rho\). A forcing term \(F(x,t)\) (an outside load) is simply added: \(y_{tt}=c^2y_{xx}+F\).

His separation of variables, step by step

Recipe · O'Neil's separation (write it in this order)

  1. Try \(y=X(x)T(t)\). Then \(XT''=c^2X''T\), so \(\dfrac{X''}{X}=\dfrac{T''}{c^2T}\). The left side depends on \(x\) only, the right on \(t\) only, so both equal one constant, which O'Neil calls \(-\lambda\): \[\frac{X''}{X}=\frac{T''}{c^2T}=-\lambda\quad\Longrightarrow\quad X''+\lambda X=0,\qquad T''+\lambda c^2T=0.\]
  2. Boundary conditions: \(y(0,t)=X(0)T(t)=0\) for all \(t\) forces \(X(0)=0\); likewise \(X(L)=0\). This is a Sturm-Liouville problem: \(X''+\lambda X=0,\ X(0)=X(L)=0\).
  3. Eigenvalue argument (three cases for \(\lambda\)):
    • \(\lambda\lt0\), say \(\lambda=-k^2\): \(X=Ae^{kx}+Be^{-kx}\); \(X(0)=0\) gives \(B=-A\), \(X(L)=A(e^{kL}-e^{-kL})=0\) gives \(A=0\). Only \(X\equiv0\).
    • \(\lambda=0\): \(X=Ax+B\); \(X(0)=0\) gives \(B=0\), \(X(L)=AL=0\) gives \(A=0\). Only \(X\equiv0\).
    • \(\lambda\gt0\), say \(\lambda=k^2\): \(X=A\cos kx+B\sin kx\); \(X(0)=0\) gives \(A=0\); \(X(L)=B\sin kL=0\) with \(B\ne0\) gives \(kL=n\pi\).
    Eigenvalues and eigenfunctions: \(\boxed{\lambda_n=\dfrac{n^2\pi^2}{L^2},\quad X_n(x)=\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}{L},\quad n=1,2,3,\dots}\)
  4. Time equation: \(T''+\dfrac{n^2\pi^2c^2}{L^2}T=0\), so \(T_n=\alpha_n\cos\dfrac{n\pi ct}{L}+\beta_n\sin\dfrac{n\pi ct}{L}\). Each product \(y_n=X_nT_n\) satisfies the PDE and the boundary conditions.
  5. Superpose: \(y=\sum y_n\). Initial position: \(y(x,0)=\sum a_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}L=f\), the Fourier sine series of \(f\) on \([0,L]\), so \(a_n=\frac2L\int_0^Lf\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx\).
  6. Initial velocity: \(y_t(x,0)=\sum\frac{n\pi c}{L}b_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}L=g\), so \(\frac{n\pi c}{L}b_n=\frac2L\int_0^Lg\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx\), i.e. \(b_n=\frac2{n\pi c}\int_0^Lg\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx\).

Compared with 08-separation.html (Farid sir's lecture notation). The notes write \(u\) for the displacement, the separation constant as \(\mu\) with \(X''/X=\mu\) (so \(\mu=-\lambda^2\) there is O'Neil's \(-\lambda=-k^2\), and O'Neil's \(\lambda_n=n^2\pi^2/L^2\) is the notes' \(\lambda_n^2\)), and the coefficients as \(E_n\) (cosine in time) and \(F_n\) (sine in time). O'Neil: \(y\), \(-\lambda\), \(a_n\), \(b_n\). The formulas are identical: \(E_n=a_n\), \(F_n=b_n\). In the exam, state which letters you use ("let \(y=XT\) and \(X''/X=T''/c^2T=-\lambda\)"); both are accepted.

What the answer means (O'Neil's style of interpretation)

  • Every term \(\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\cos\frac{n\pi ct}L\) (or with \(\sin\) in time) is a normal mode: a standing wave whose shape never changes, it only scales up and down in time.
  • Fundamental frequency (\(n=1\)): angular frequency \(\omega_1=\dfrac{\pi c}{L}\), frequency \(\dfrac{c}{2L}\), period \(\dfrac{2L}{c}\). Mode \(n\) vibrates at \(n\) times this (the overtones), so the whole motion repeats with period \(2L/c\).
  • A longer or looser string (larger \(L\), smaller \(c\)) has a lower pitch; a tighter string (larger tension, so larger \(c\)) a higher one.
  • If the string is plucked at a point that is a node of mode \(n\), that mode is absent from the motion (its coefficient is zero): pluck at the middle and all even modes disappear.

2. The five subsections as types

16.2.1 · Zero initial velocity: string released from rest every year

Problem
\(g\equiv0\), \(y(x,0)=f(x)\). Then \(b_n=0\) (the time factor must have zero slope at \(t=0\), so only the cosine survives, because \(T'(0)=0\)).
Answer
\(y=\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge1}a_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\cos\frac{n\pi ct}L\), \(a_n=\dfrac2L\displaystyle\int_0^Lf(\xi)\sin\frac{n\pi\xi}L\,d\xi\).  (O'Neil 16.5)

Recipe · released from rest

  1. Identify \(L\), \(c\) (square root of the number in front of \(y_{xx}\); \(y_{tt}=8y_{xx}\) means \(c=2\sqrt2\)), and \(f\).
  2. If \(f\) is already a sine \(K\sin\frac{m\pi x}L\): match terms, \(a_m=K\), all other \(a_n=0\). No integral.
  3. Otherwise integrate \(a_n=\frac2L\int_0^Lf\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx\) by parts (split the integral at the corner for a piecewise \(f\)). Use \(\sin n\pi=0\), \(\cos n\pi=(-1)^n\).
  4. Write \(y=\sum a_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\cos\frac{n\pi ct}L\). Write the first few terms.
  5. Interpret.

What to write

The four conditions with \(g=0\); "let \(y=X(x)T(t)\)"; \(X''+\lambda X=0\), \(T''+\lambda c^2T=0\); \(X(0)=X(L)=0\); the three \(\lambda\) cases and \(\lambda_n,X_n\); "\(y_t(x,0)=X\,T'(0)=0\) so \(T'(0)=0\) and \(T_n=\cos\frac{n\pi ct}{L}\)"; superposition; "\(y(x,0)=f\) is the Fourier sine series of \(f\) on \([0,L]\)" and \(a_n\); the integral in full; the final series. Interpretation: a plucked/released string vibrates as a sum of normal modes; fundamental frequency \(c/2L\); the motion has period \(2L/c\); nodes of mode \(n\) at \(kL/n\).

Traps

  • The time factor is \(\cos\frac{n\pi ct}{L}\): the \(c\) is inside. For \(c=3,\ L=4\) it is \(\cos\frac{3n\pi t}{4}\).
  • A triangular (plucked) \(f\) needs two integrals, one on each side of the corner, each with its own formula for \(f\).
  • \(\sin\pi x\) on \(0\lt x\lt4\) is the mode \(n=4\) (\(\sin\frac{4\pi x}4\)), not \(n=1\). Convert before matching.
  • For a symmetric plucked shape, \(a_n=0\) for even \(n\): it is not an error. Write the sum over odd \(n\) or keep \(\sin\frac{n\pi}2\).

Practice: string released from rest, solve every one

16.2.2 · Zero initial displacement: string struck every year

Problem
\(f\equiv0\), \(y_t(x,0)=g(x)\). Then \(a_n=0\) (from \(T(0)=0\), only the sine in time survives).
Answer
\(y=\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge1}b_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\sin\frac{n\pi ct}L\), \(b_n=\dfrac2{n\pi c}\displaystyle\int_0^Lg(\xi)\sin\frac{n\pi\xi}L\,d\xi\).  (O'Neil 16.6)

Recipe · struck string

  1. Identify \(L\), \(c\), \(g\). Zero displacement means \(a_n=0\).
  2. Compute the sine coefficient of \(g\): \(\beta_n=\frac2L\int_0^Lg\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx\).
  3. Divide: \(b_n=\dfrac{L}{n\pi c}\beta_n=\dfrac2{n\pi c}\int_0^Lg\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx\). (The division comes from differentiating \(\sin\frac{n\pi ct}{L}\).)
  4. Write \(y=\sum b_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\sin\frac{n\pi ct}L\) and a few terms.

What to write

"\(y(x,0)=X\,T(0)=0\) so \(T(0)=0\) and \(T_n=\sin\frac{n\pi ct}L\)"; superposition; "\(y_t(x,0)=\sum\frac{n\pi c}Lb_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}L=g\)"; the formula for \(b_n\); the integral in full. Interpretation: the string starts flat and the blow gives it energy; the same normal modes, now starting as \(\sin\) in time (modes start at zero displacement, with maximum speed).

Traps

  • The extra factor. \(b_n\) has \(\frac{1}{n\pi c}\) in front, not \(\frac{1}{L}\)-only: forgetting the \(n\) (so that \(b_n\) decays only as the sine coefficient does) is the usual error. Check: \(b_n\) must decay faster than \(\beta_n\).
  • A piecewise \(g\) (nonzero on a sub-interval, as in a hammer blow) integrates only over that sub-interval: the other part contributes zero.
  • \(\sin\) in time: \(\sin\frac{n\pi ct}L\), again with \(c\).

Practice: struck string, solve every one

16.2.3 · Both nonzero: add the two solutions every year

Idea
The problem is linear. If \(y_f\) solves the problem with \((f,\,0)\) and \(y_g\) the one with \((0,\,g)\), then \(y=y_f+y_g\) solves \((f,g)\): both satisfy the PDE and boundary conditions, and \(y(x,0)=f+0\), \(y_t(x,0)=0+g\).
Answer
\(y=\displaystyle\sum_{n\ge1}\Big[a_n\cos\frac{n\pi ct}L+b_n\sin\frac{n\pi ct}L\Big]\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\), with \(a_n\) from \(f\) and \(b_n\) from \(g\) (the formulas of Section 1).

Recipe · both

  1. Compute \(a_n\) from \(f\) alone, and \(b_n\) from \(g\) alone. Two independent integrals.
  2. If \(f\) (or \(g\)) is already a sum of sines, read its coefficients off instead.
  3. Put both in the series; keep the \(n\) with an exceptional value (such as \(n=1\) or \(n=2\)) as a separate term if the general formula fails there.

What to write

"By linearity \(y=y_f+y_g\)", then the two coefficient integrals and the combined series. Interpretation: each mode oscillates as \(a_n\cos+b_n\sin=\sqrt{a_n^2+b_n^2}\cos(\frac{n\pi ct}L-\varphi_n)\): amplitude from both data, a phase shift from the ratio.

Traps

  • Different integrals for \(f\) and \(g\); do not reuse \(a_n\) as \(b_n\).
  • A formula valid for \(n\ge2\) may fail at \(n=1\) (denominator \(n^2-1\) in Example 16.3): do \(n=1\) separately.

Practice: both \(f\) and \(g\), solve every one

16.2.4 · Influence of constants and initial conditions weak spot

O'Neil uses this subsection to look at how the constants change the motion (his figures are plots). The words you can write in an exam:

  • The speed \(c\). \(c\) enters only through \(ct\). With data \(f\) only, \(y(x,t)=\Phi(x,ct)\): changing \(c\) just makes the same motion faster or slower, with the same amplitudes. With data \(g\), each term has \(\frac{1}{c}\) in \(b_n\): the same initial speed profile \(g\) with a larger \(c\) gives a smaller amplitude (the string oscillates faster, so it travels less far) but a higher frequency.
  • The length \(L\). The fundamental frequency is \(\frac{c}{2L}\): longer string, lower note.
  • The initial data. A sharp corner in \(f\) (pluck) gives slowly decaying \(a_n\sim1/n^2\) (many modes, a bright sound); a smooth \(f\) gives rapidly decaying \(a_n\) (few modes). The position of the pluck decides which modes vanish.

Traps

  • O'Neil's Example 16.6 prints the time factor as \(\sin(1.2t)\); it must be \(\sin(1.2\,nt)\) (see Section 3).
  • When \(\epsilon\) is an integer \(m\), the "general" coefficients are \(0/0\) at \(n=m\); use the match-the-terms rule instead: \(y=\frac{1}{cm}\sin mx\sin cmt\).

16.2.5 · Forcing term (an outside load) weak spot

Problem
\(y_{tt}=c^2y_{xx}+F(x)\), \(y(0,t)=y(L,t)=0\), \(y(x,0)=f\), \(y_t(x,0)=g\). The term \(F\) makes separation fail (\(XT''=c^2X''T+F\) does not split).
Idea
Write \(y=Y(x,t)+\psi(x)\), with \(\psi\) chosen to soak up the forcing so that \(Y\) solves the plain wave problem.

Recipe · \(y=Y+\psi\) (\(F\) depends on \(x\) only)

  1. Choose \(\psi\) with \(c^2\psi''+F(x)=0\), \(\psi(0)=\psi(L)=0\): integrate twice, fix the two constants from the two end conditions.
  2. Then \(Y_{tt}=c^2Y_{xx}\) (the \(\psi''\) and \(F\) cancel), \(Y(0,t)=Y(L,t)=0\).
  3. New initial data: \(Y(x,0)=f-\psi\), \(Y_t(x,0)=g\) (since \(\psi\) does not depend on \(t\)).
  4. Solve for \(Y\) by 16.2.3: \(a_n=\frac2L\int_0^L(f-\psi)\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx\), \(b_n\) from \(g\).
  5. Answer \(y=\psi+Y\). Check \(y(x,0)=f\).

What to write

"Separation fails because of \(F\)", "put \(y=Y+\psi\)", the equation for \(\psi\) and its boundary values, the problem for \(Y\) with its initial data, then the series and \(y=\psi+Y\). Interpretation: \(\psi\) is the shape the string would hold under a constant load (the static deflection); the motion is vibration about that shape.

Traps

  • The initial displacement of \(Y\) is \(f-\psi\), not \(f\). With \(f=0\) it is \(-\psi\). Forgetting this is the commonest slip.
  • \(\psi\) must vanish at both ends; check \(\psi(L)=0\) after finding the constants.
  • If \(F\) also depends on \(t\) this trick fails; use Duhamel / d'Alembert (16.6 forced, below).

16.6 · Characteristics and d'Alembert's solution (whole line)

No boundary: \(u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}\) for \(-\infty\lt x\lt\infty\), \(u(x,0)=f\), \(u_t(x,0)=g\) (O'Neil calls this the Cauchy problem). Same formula as 08-separation.html (type 6) but O'Neil shows where it comes from.

Recipe · d'Alembert

  1. Change variables \(\xi=x-ct\), \(\eta=x+ct\) (the lines \(x\mp ct=\) const are the characteristics, of slope \(\pm1/c\) in the \(x,t\)-plane). By the chain rule \(u_{xx}=U_{\xi\xi}+2U_{\xi\eta}+U_{\eta\eta}\), \(u_{tt}=c^2(U_{\xi\xi}-2U_{\xi\eta}+U_{\eta\eta})\), so the PDE becomes \(U_{\xi\eta}=0\).
  2. \(U_{\xi\eta}=0\) means \(U=F(\xi)+G(\eta)\): general solution \(u=F(x-ct)+G(x+ct)\) (a right-moving and a left-moving wave).
  3. Impose the data: \(F+G=f\), \(-cF'+cG'=g\). Integrate the second, solve for \(F\) and \(G\), and add.
  4. Result: \(\boxed{u(x,t)=\tfrac12\big[f(x-ct)+f(x+ct)\big]+\dfrac1{2c}\displaystyle\int_{x-ct}^{x+ct}g(w)\,dw}\).
  5. With forcing \(F(x,t)\): add \(\dfrac1{2c}\displaystyle\iint_\Delta F(X,T)\,dX\,dT\) over the characteristic triangle \(\Delta\) with vertices \((x-ct,0)\), \((x+ct,0)\), \((x,t)\). As an iterated integral: \[\frac1{2c}\int_0^t\int_{x-c(t-T)}^{x+c(t-T)}F(X,T)\,dX\,dT.\]

What to write

The problem (Cauchy), the formula (state it), the substitution of \(c,f,g\) (and \(F\)), the integrals in full, the simplified \(u\). Interpretation (forward and backward waves): \(u=\varphi(x-ct)+\beta(x+ct)\) with \(\varphi=\frac12f-\frac1{2c}\int_0^xg\), \(\beta=\frac12f+\frac1{2c}\int_0^xg\): the profile is a wave moving right at speed \(c\) plus one moving left; if \(g=0\), each is half of \(f\), and they separate when \(f\) has bounded support.

Traps

  • The integral of \(g\) has the factor \(\frac1{2c}\), not \(\frac12\); \(f\) has \(\frac12\) in front of both shifts.
  • Forced term: the inner limits are \(x\mp c(t-T)\) (they shrink to the point \(x\) as \(T\to t\)); the outer limits are \(0\) to \(t\).
  • If \(F\) is linear in \(X\) (say \(F=x+t\) or \(2xT\)), the inner integral is just \(2c(t-T)\) times \(F\) evaluated at \(X=x\): the whole forced term collapses to \(\int_0^t(t-T)\,F(x,T)\,dT\). (True because the average of \(X\) over a symmetric interval is its midpoint.)

3. Misprints found in the book (checked by computer algebra)

Do not copy these

  • Example 16.2 (\(f=x\cos\frac{5x}2\) on \([0,\pi]\)): the printed coefficient has 8 in the numerator; the correct one is \(\dfrac{160\,n(-1)^{n+1}}{\pi(5+2n)^2(5-2n)^2}\) (numerical integration for \(n=1,2,3\) agrees with 160).
  • Example 16.6: the series is printed with \(\sin(1.2t)\) after \(\sin nx\). It must be \(\sin(1.2\,nt)\), since \(\frac{n\pi ct}{L}=1.2nt\) for \(L=\pi\).
  • Example 16.12: the last term is printed \(\frac18\sin4x\cos8t\). The integral \(\frac14\int_{x-2t}^{x+2t}\cos4w\,dw\) gives \(\frac18\cos4x\sin8t\) (the printed one would give \(u_t(x,0)=0\), not \(\cos4x\)).
  • Example 16.14: the displayed integral of the \(g\)-term is written \(\int e^{w}dw\); it should be \(\int e^{-w}dw\) (the next line uses the right one).
  • Section 16.2, problem 1 says "graph with \(c=1\) if \(c\) is unspecified": take \(c=1\).

One-minute summary

  • Problem: \(y_{tt}=c^2y_{xx}\), \(y(0,t)=y(L,t)=0\), \(y(x,0)=f\), \(y_t(x,0)=g\). Separate with \(X''/X=T''/c^2T=-\lambda\); \(\lambda_n=\frac{n^2\pi^2}{L^2}\), \(X_n=\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\).
  • \(y=\sum\big[a_n\cos\frac{n\pi ct}L+b_n\sin\frac{n\pi ct}L\big]\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\), \(a_n=\frac2L\int_0^Lf\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\), \(b_n=\frac2{n\pi c}\int_0^Lg\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\).
  • Rest: \(g=0\), only \(a_n\). Struck: \(f=0\), only \(b_n\) (with the \(\frac1{n\pi c}\)). Both: add. Finite sum of sines: match terms (\(\sin\pi x\) on \((0,4)\) is \(n=4\)).
  • Useful integrals: \(\int_0^\pi(\pi-x)\sin nx\,dx=\frac\pi n\); \(\int_0^L(Lx-x^2)\sin\frac{n\pi x}L=\frac{2L^3(1-(-1)^n)}{n^3\pi^3}\); pluck at \(a\), height \(h\): \(a_n=\frac{2hL^2\sin(n\pi a/L)}{\pi^2n^2a(L-a)}\).
  • Meaning: normal modes; fundamental frequency \(\frac c{2L}\); nodes at \(\frac{kL}n\); period \(\frac{2L}c\).
  • Forcing \(F(x)\): \(y=Y+\psi\), \(c^2\psi''+F=0\), \(\psi(0)=\psi(L)=0\); \(Y(x,0)=f-\psi\).
  • d'Alembert: \(u=\frac12[f(x-ct)+f(x+ct)]+\frac1{2c}\int_{x-ct}^{x+ct}g\,dw\) \(\big(+\frac1{2c}\int_0^t\int_{x-c(t-T)}^{x+c(t-T)}F\,dX\,dT\big)\).
