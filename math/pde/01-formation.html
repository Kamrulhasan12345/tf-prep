Forming a PDE: eliminate constants, eliminate functions

Farid sir taught this straight from his slides, so every slide example and exercise is here. It is the easiest mark in the PDE part: it came in the 2022-23 final (Q6a, a two-function elimination) and in CT-1 2023, and the same questions were asked in 2017-18.

Types. (A) Eliminate arbitrary constants (one or two give first order, three give second order). (B) Eliminate one arbitrary function, \(z=f(u)\) or \(\phi(u,v)=0\). (C) Eliminate two functions \(f\) and \(g\) (second order). (D) Derive \(Pp+Qq=R\) from \(\phi(u,v)=0\) CT-1 2023. Mandatory: 5 cards (final papers and CT). The rest of his slide exercises are optional extra.

Notation and the one idea

Derivatives
\(p=z_x,\ q=z_y,\ r=z_{xx},\ s=z_{xy},\ t=z_{yy}\). Farid sir writes exactly these letters, so write them too.
Order, degree, linear
Order is the highest derivative that appears. Degree is the power of that highest derivative. Linear means first degree in \(z\) and its derivatives.
The idea
A relation with \(n\) arbitrary constants is a family of surfaces. Differentiate until you have as many equations as unknowns to remove (the constants, or the functions), then eliminate. The result is the PDE that every member of the family satisfies.

Type A · Eliminate arbitrary constants

Start from \(F(x,y,z,a,b)=0\). Differentiate with \(z\) a function of \(x,y\): \[F_x+pF_z=0,\qquad F_y+qF_z=0.\] These three equations (the relation and its two derivatives) contain \(a,b\). Eliminate \(a,b\) and you get \(G(x,y,z,p,q)=0\). If there are three constants, two derivatives are not enough: go on to second order (\(r,s,t\)) and use whichever combinations are free of the constants.

Recipe · constants

  1. Count the constants. Two or fewer: first order. Three: second order.
  2. Differentiate with respect to \(x\) (get \(p\)) and with respect to \(y\) (get \(q\)). Remember \(z\) depends on \(x,y\), so \(\partial_x(z^2)=2zp\).
  3. Solve the derivative equations for the constants (or for combinations of them, like \(ax\) and \(by\)) and substitute back into the original.
  4. For three constants, differentiate again. Pick out the second-order equation(s) that no longer contain \(a,b,c\).
  5. Write the PDE in \(p,q\) (and \(r,s,t\)) and box it.

What to write

The relation, the two derivative lines with \(p\) and \(q\), the line where you solve for the constants, and the boxed PDE. When the answer is second order, show all the second-order relations that come out, not only one. His note on \(z=ax+by+cxy\): "when the answer is in 2nd order, we have to show other 2nd order derivatives as well", so write \(xp+yq=z+xys\) and \(r=0,\ t=0\).

Traps

  • Forgetting the chain rule: \(\partial_x z^2=2zp\), not \(2z\).
  • His slide says "squaring and adding" for \(ax^2+by^2+z^2=1\). What it really does is multiply the two derivative equations by \(x\) and \(y\) and add. Do not copy the words, copy the algebra.
  • A quadric surface has three constants (\(a,b,c\)), so the answer is second order. Ellipsoid, hyperboloid of one sheet and hyperboloid of two sheets all give the same PDEs (his boxed note).
  • Several correct forms exist for the same family (for example \(pq+zs=0\) is equivalent to the pair \(x(p^2+zr)=zp,\ y(q^2+zt)=zq\)). Any correct one is accepted, but say which you derived.

Type B · Eliminate one arbitrary function

If \(z=f(u(x,y))\), then \(p=f'u_x\) and \(q=f'u_y\). Eliminate \(f'\) by dividing: \(\dfrac pq=\dfrac{u_x}{u_y}\), that is \(u_y\,p-u_x\,q=0\). If the relation is \(\phi(u,v)=0\) with \(u,v\) functions of \(x,y,z\), there is a ready-made determinant (next section).

Recipe · one function \(z=f(u)\) or \(g(z)=f(u)\)

  1. Differentiate both sides with respect to \(x\), then with respect to \(y\). The unknown \(f'\) (or \(\phi'\)) appears in both lines.
  2. Eliminate it: divide the two lines, or cross-multiply. Never try to find \(f\).
  3. Tidy to \(Pp+Qq=R\) and box.

What to write

The two differentiated lines with \(f'\) written out, the line where \(f'\) cancels, and the boxed first-order PDE.

Traps

  • A relation like \(z=y^2+2\phi(\tfrac1x+\log y)\) has an extra term outside \(\phi\). Isolate it: the derivative of \(y^2\) is \(2y\) in \(q\) and zero in \(p\).
  • The inner function of \(\phi\) is a combination, so differentiate it with the chain rule (\(\partial_x\tfrac1x=-\tfrac1{x^2}\)).

Type D · Derive \(Pp+Qq=R\) from \(\phi(u,v)=0\)

Here \(u,v\) are functions of \(x,y,z\) and \(z=z(x,y)\). Differentiate \(\phi(u,v)=0\) with respect to \(x\) and \(y\): \[\phi_u(u_x+pu_z)+\phi_v(v_x+pv_z)=0,\qquad \phi_u(u_y+qu_z)+\phi_v(v_y+qv_z)=0.\] These are two linear equations in \((\phi_u,\phi_v)\), not both zero. So the determinant vanishes: \[\begin{vmatrix}u_x+pu_z & v_x+pv_z\\ u_y+qu_z & v_y+qv_z\end{vmatrix}=0 .\] Expanding gives \(Pp+Qq=R\) with \[P=\frac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial(y,z)}=u_yv_z-u_zv_y,\quad Q=\frac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial(z,x)}=u_zv_x-u_xv_z,\quad R=\frac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial(x,y)}=u_xv_y-u_yv_x .\] The slide prints the same equation as \((u_zv_y-u_yv_z)p+(u_xv_z-v_xu_z)q=v_xu_y-u_xv_y\), which is this one multiplied by \(-1\).

Recipe · \(F(u,v)=0\) by the determinant

  1. Write \(u\) and \(v\) as functions of \(x,y,z\) only (no \(p,q\)).
  2. List the six partials \(u_x,u_y,u_z,v_x,v_y,v_z\).
  3. Compute \(P=u_yv_z-u_zv_y\), \(Q=u_zv_x-u_xv_z\), \(R=u_xv_y-u_yv_x\).
  4. Write \(Pp+Qq=R\). Cancel any common factor and box.

Traps

  • Sign of \(R\): it is \(u_xv_y-u_yv_x\) on the right of the equation, with \(Pp+Qq\) on the left.
  • Check the answer by testing one member: \(z=\) any function of \(u\) alone (take \(v\) fixed) should satisfy the PDE.

Type C · Two arbitrary functions \(f\) and \(g\) (second order)

With two arbitrary functions you need second derivatives. Differentiate twice with respect to \(x\), \(y\) (and once mixed if needed) and hunt for the combination in which the \(f',f'',g',g''\) all cancel. The usual shapes:

  • \(z=f(x-at)+g(x+at)\): the two second derivatives are \(f''+g''\) and \(a^2(f''+g'')\), so they are proportional. This is the wave equation.
  • \(z=f(x^2-y)+g(x^2+y)\): \(p\) mixes \(f'+g'\) and \(r\) contains \(f''+g''\); the combination \(xr-p\) kills \(f'+g'\).

Recipe · two functions

  1. Compute \(p\) and \(q\). Name the inner derivatives \(f',g'\).
  2. Compute \(r,s,t\). Write each in terms of \(f',f'',g',g''\).
  3. Look for a combination of \(r,s,t,p,q\) in which every \(f',g',f'',g''\) cancels. Often \(f''+g''\) appears in two of them and \(f'+g'\) in \(p\).
  4. Box the PDE. Write \(f'\) for the derivative of \(f\) with respect to its own argument.

What to write

\(p,q,r,s,t\) all written out in terms of \(f',f'',g',g''\), the combination that cancels, and the boxed result. Examiners want to see the five lines.

Traps

  • The seniors' claim that \(z=f(x^2+y)+g(x^2-y)\) gives \(r-t=0\) is wrong. The correct answer is \(xr-p=4x^3t\) (because \(\partial_x\) of the inner function is \(2x\), not 1).
  • For \(f(x-at)+g(x+at)\) the roles are \((x,t)\), so the answer is \(a^2z_{xx}=z_{tt}\), not \(z_{xx}=a^2z_{yy}\) unless you rename \(t\to y\).

One-minute summary

  • Constants: \(F_x+pF_z=0,\ F_y+qF_z=0\), eliminate \(a,b\). Three constants: go to second order and show all the second-order relations.
  • Ellipsoid-type \(\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}+\frac{z^2}{c^2}=1\) (and \(ax^2+by^2+cz^2=1\), hyper- and two-sheet versions): \(x(p^2+zr)=zp,\ y(q^2+zt)=zq\), equivalently \(pq+zs=0\).
  • Quadrics homogeneous in \(x,y\) of degree \(k\) (cone, paraboloids, \(z=ax^2+bxy+cy^2\)): \(xp+yq=kz\).
  • \(\phi(u,v)=0\): \(Pp+Qq=R\), \(P=u_yv_z-u_zv_y,\ Q=u_zv_x-u_xv_z,\ R=u_xv_y-u_yv_x\).
  • \(z=f(u)\): eliminate \(f'\) by dividing \(p\) by \(q\). \(z=f(x-at)+g(x+at)\): \(a^2z_{xx}=z_{tt}\). \(z=f(x^2-y)+g(x^2+y)\): \(xr-p=4x^3t\).
