Non-linear first order: Charpit's method and the standard forms

Integrals of a first-order PDE

Complete integral
A solution \(f(x,y,z,a,b)=0\) with two arbitrary constants (as many as independent variables), where \(a_xb_y-a_yb_x\ne0\) (the constants are genuinely independent).
Particular integral
Give \(a,b\) particular values.
Singular integral
The envelope of the complete integral: eliminate \(a,b\) from \(f=0,\ f_a=0,\ f_b=0\). The result must itself satisfy the PDE, and it does not always exist (his note, Raiyan p.34). Questions say "singular integral if it exists".
General integral
Take \(b=\phi(a)\), \(\phi\) arbitrary, and form the envelope: eliminate \(a\) between \(f(x,y,z,a,\phi(a))=0\) and \(\dfrac{\partial}{\partial a}f(x,y,z,a,\phi(a))=0\).

Type A · Charpit's method

For \(F(x,y,z,p,q)=0\), Charpit's auxiliary equations are \[\frac{dp}{F_x+pF_z}=\frac{dq}{F_y+qF_z}=\frac{dz}{-pF_p-qF_q}=\frac{dx}{-F_p}=\frac{dy}{-F_q}.\] Pick the simplest integral that contains \(p\) or \(q\), say \(G(x,y,z,p,q)=a\) (often \(p=a\), \(q=a\), \(p=aq\) or \(p^2+q^2=a^2\)). Solve \(F=0\) and \(G=a\) for \(p\) and \(q\), then integrate \(dz=p\,dx+q\,dy\) (it is exact because of how \(G\) was chosen).

Recipe · Charpit

  1. Write \(F(x,y,z,p,q)=0\) and compute \(F_x,F_y,F_z,F_p,F_q\). Write the full Charpit equations (this alone carries marks).
  2. Look for a zero or a simple pair. If \(F_x+pF_z=0\), then \(dp=0\) and \(p=a\). If \(F_y+qF_z=0\), then \(q=a\). If two ratios give \(\dfrac{dp}p=\dfrac{dq}q\), then \(p=aq\). If \(p\,dp+q\,dq=0\), then \(p^2+q^2=a^2\).
  3. Solve the PDE together with that relation for \(p\) and \(q\) (in terms of \(x,y,z,a\)).
  4. Integrate \(dz=p\,dx+q\,dy\). Make it exact (a substitution like \(u=ax+y\), or multiplying by \(z\) or \(y\)) and add the second constant \(b\).
  5. Singular integral: differentiate the complete integral \(f(x,y,z,a,b)=0\) with respect to \(a\) and \(b\); eliminate. Then check it satisfies the PDE.
  6. General integral: put \(b=\phi(a)\), differentiate with respect to \(a\), eliminate \(a\).

What to write

The Charpit equations in full with the partials substituted. The relation you extract (\(p=aq\), etc.) and where it came from. The complete integral with \(a,b\) arbitrary, in a boxed line. For the singular integral: the lines \(f_a=0\), \(f_b=0\), the elimination, and "this satisfies the PDE" (or "\(f_b\ne0\) for every \(a,b\), so there is no singular integral"). For the general integral: \(b=\phi(a)\) and the envelope condition.

Traps

  • Writing \(dz=p\,dx+q\,dy\) with \(p,q\) still containing \(z\): the integral must be exact. Try \(u=ax+y\), or divide by a power of \(z\).
  • Calling \(z=0\) a singular integral without checking it satisfies the PDE. (For \(z^2(p^2z^2+q^2)=1\), \(z=0\) gives \(0=1\), so there is none.)
  • Stopping at the complete integral when the question says "complete, singular and general". Give all three.
  • Arbitrary constant \(b\) added in the wrong place: in \(z=\frac{(ax+y)^2}{2a}+\frac ba\) the constant divides by \(a\), that is fine, but keep \(b\) additive after integrating.

Recipe · Form III \(f(z,p,q)=0\)

  1. Put \(q=ap\). Substitute into the PDE: it becomes an equation between \(z\) and \(p\) only. Solve for \(p\) in terms of \(z\) and \(a\).
  2. Let \(u=x+ay\). Then \(dz=p\,dx+q\,dy=p(dx+a\,dy)=p\,du\), so \(\dfrac{dz}{du}=p(z)\).
  3. Separate and integrate: \(\int\dfrac{dz}{p(z)}=u+b=x+ay+b\).

Recipe · Form IV and Clairaut

  1. Form IV: separate the PDE as a function of \((x,p)\) equal to a function of \((y,q)\). Set both to \(a\). Solve \(p=p(x,a)\), \(q=q(y,a)\). Then \(z=\int p\,dx+\int q\,dy+b\).
  2. Clairaut: replace \(p,q\) by \(a,b\) in \(f\). Singular: \(x+f_a=0\), \(y+f_b=0\), then substitute \(a,b\) back into the complete integral.
  3. A PDE that is only Form IV after a substitution (like \(Z=z^2/2\) for \(2x(z^2q^2+1)=pz\)) is also fine: say what you substituted.

Useful integrals. \(\displaystyle\int\sqrt{u^2-a^2}\,du=\frac u2\sqrt{u^2-a^2}-\frac{a^2}2\ln\big(u+\sqrt{u^2-a^2}\big)+c\). Also \(\displaystyle\int\sqrt{u^2+a^2}\,du=\frac u2\sqrt{u^2+a^2}+\frac{a^2}2\ln\big(u+\sqrt{u^2+a^2}\big)+c\).

One-minute summary

  • Charpit: \(\dfrac{dp}{F_x+pF_z}=\dfrac{dq}{F_y+qF_z}=\dfrac{dz}{-pF_p-qF_q}=\dfrac{dx}{-F_p}=\dfrac{dy}{-F_q}\). Take the easiest integral \(G=a\), solve for \(p,q\), integrate \(dz=p\,dx+q\,dy\).
  • Quick shortcuts: \(F_x+pF_z=0\Rightarrow p=a\); \(\frac{dp}p=\frac{dq}q\Rightarrow p=aq\); \(p\,dp+q\,dq=0\Rightarrow p^2+q^2=a^2\).
  • Clairaut \(z=px+qy+f(p,q)\): \(z=ax+by+f(a,b)\); singular from \(x+f_a=0,\ y+f_b=0\).
  • \(f(z,p,q)=0\): \(q=ap\), \(u=x+ay\), \(dz/du=p(z)\); then \(\int dz/p(z)=x+ay+b\).
  • \(f_1(x,p)=f_2(y,q)=a\): \(z=\int p\,dx+\int q\,dy+b\). \(f(p,q)=0\): \(z=ax+\phi(a)y+c\), no singular.
  • Singular: eliminate \(a,b\) from \(f=f_a=f_b=0\) and check it satisfies the PDE. General: \(b=\phi(a)\), envelope in \(a\).
  • Past answers to know: \(px+qy=pq\): \(z=\frac{(ax+y)^2}{2a}+\frac ba\). \(2(z+px+qy)=yp^2\): \(z=\frac{ax}{y^2}+\frac by-\frac{a^2}{4y^3}\), no singular. \(2x(z^2q^2+1)=pz\): \(z^2=2(1+a^2)x^2+2ay+2b\).
