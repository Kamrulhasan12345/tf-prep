Splay Trees

Short but worth the 30 minutes: asked in 2017–18 (Q6(a) splay a key, 10 marks; Q6(d) main objective, 5), 2018–19 (Q3(b), 15), 2020–21 (Q6(a) rank and potential after insertion, 5+10), 2021–22 (Q2(a) splay steps, 8), 2022–23 (Q3(a) potential function, 8) and 2023–24 (Q13(b) insert 30 and delete 35, 13). Not asked in 2019–20 or 2024–25. Syllabus note: the notes and slides do not teach splay trees, and Bayzid sir's page marks it "not in syllabus", but the papers above did ask it, so learn the three steps and the potential idea.

1. The idea

A splay tree is an ordinary BST with no colours and no heights. After every access (search, insert, delete) the touched node is moved to the root by a sequence of rotations called splaying. Recently used keys stay near the top.

2017–18 Q6(d): "main objective? does it explicitly balance height?" The objective is good amortised time: any sequence of m operations on n keys costs O(m log n) in total, and frequently accessed keys become cheap. It does not explicitly balance the height. One operation can still cost O(n) (a stick of 5 keys, access the deepest). The cost is paid back by later operations.

2. The three splay steps

Let x be the node being splayed, p its parent, g its grandparent. Repeat the right step until x is the root. Greek letters are whole subtrees.

Method · choose by the shape x, p, g

  1. Zig (p is the root): one rotation at p.
  2. Zig-zig (x and p are children on the same side, a straight line): rotate at g first, then at p.
  3. Zig-zag (x and p are on opposite sides, a bend): rotate at p, then at g (this is x rotated up twice).

Where marks go

  • In zig-zig, rotating at p first (that is, rotating x up twice) is wrong. It does not give the amortised bound and the final tree is different.
  • Splaying until x is the root, not just one step.
  • Showing only the final tree when the question says "show the intermediate steps". Draw the tree after every step and name it (zig, zig-zig, zig-zag).

Watch zig-zig twice on a stick: access 1 in 5(4(3(2(1)))).

3. Operations

Method · search, insert, delete

  1. Access / search k: walk down as in a BST, then splay the node found. If k is not there, splay the last node you touched.
  2. Insert k: BST insert as a leaf, then splay the new node to the root.
  3. Delete k: splay k to the root and remove it. You now have a left tree L and a right tree R. Splay the largest key of L to the root of L (it then has no right child). Attach R as its right child.

Insert 30 goes under 35 (30 < 35, so it is the left child of 35). Splay: zig-zig (30, 35, 40), then zig-zag (30, 45, 26), then zig at the root 13. 5. Rank and potential, in brief

Needed for 2022–23 Q3(a) (8) and 2020–21 Q6(a) (5+10). Amortised analysis uses a potential function Φ, a number stored with the tree that goes up when the tree gets worse and down when it gets better.

  • Size s(v) = number of nodes in the subtree rooted at v (including v).
  • Rank r(v) = lg s(v). A leaf has rank 0.
  • Potential Φ(T) = sum of r(v) over all nodes. A stick has a large potential, a balanced tree has a small one.
  • Amortised cost of an operation = actual cost + ΔΦ.

Access lemma (state it, do not re-prove it unless asked): splaying x in a tree with root t has amortised cost at most 3(r(t) − r(x)) + 1. Summed over all steps it telescopes, so a splay costs O(log n) amortised. The idea in words: an expensive access walks down a long path, which means the tree was unbalanced; splaying that path rebalances it and the potential drops by about as much as the work done.

Method · 2020–21 Q6(a): ΔΦ from an insertion (before the splay) is O(log n), 10 marks

  1. The tree had n nodes. Insert x as a leaf. Its rank is lg 1 = 0. Only the ancestors change: let them be u₀ (root), u₁, …, u_k (the parent of x), with sizes s₀, …, s_k before the insertion. Each size grows by 1.
  2. \[ \Delta\Phi = \sum_{i=0}^{k} \left[\lg(s_i + 1) - \lg s_i\right]. \]
  3. u_{i−1} is the parent of u_i, so s_{i−1} ≥ s_i + 1. Hence for i ≥ 1: \( \lg(s_i+1) \le \lg s_{i-1} \).
  4. So for i ≥ 1 each term is at most \( \lg s_{i-1} - \lg s_i \), and these telescope: \[ \sum_{i=1}^{k} \le \lg s_0 - \lg s_k \le \lg n. \]
  5. The root term: \( \lg(s_0+1) - \lg s_0 \le 1 \), since s₀ ≥ 1.
  6. Total: \( \Delta\Phi \le \lg n + 1 = O(\log n) \).

Skip lists (not in the notes or slides; one short note). A skip list is a sorted linked list with extra "express" levels. Each key is promoted one level for every consecutive head in a coin toss, so level i holds about n/2i keys. Expected space: n(1 + 1/2 + 1/4 + ...) is at most 2n, so O(n). Search starts at the top-left and moves right while the next key is not larger, else down: about log n levels and about 2 steps per level, so expected O(log n). Insert and delete search first, then add or remove the key on its levels. Worked answers: 2018–19 Q4(b) and 2020–21 Q5(b).
