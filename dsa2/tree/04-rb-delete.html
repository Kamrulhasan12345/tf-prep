RB Deletion from Zero: the 4 Cases

2024–25 Q5(a) (25 marks with insertion) asked deletions of 8, 12 and 19 from the tree you build. 2023–24 Q12(b), 10 marks: delete 50 from a given tree. 2020–21 Q6(b), 5+15: explain whether deletion can be fixed without rotation, then delete 150 from a given tree. The notes and slides stop before deletion, so this chapter teaches it completely. It follows CLRS. Read it in order.

1. Plain BST deletion first

Deleting node z from a normal BST has three structural cases. You already know them. Red-black adds colour bookkeeping on top.

  1. z has no children (a leaf): remove it. Its place is now a NIL.
  2. z has one child: the child takes z's place.
  3. z has two children: find the successor y, the smallest key in z's right subtree (go right once, then left as far as you can). y has no left child. Move y into z's place. If y was deeper, y's right child takes y's old place. Use the successor. (The predecessor also works, but CLRS and this book use the successor, and the engine does too. If you use the predecessor, say so.)

2. Why deleting a black node is the only problem

The colours of the five rules (see the properties chapter): red node has black children, every path has the same number of blacks.

  • Removing a red node breaks nothing. A red node adds no black to any path, so black counts stay equal. It had black children and a black parent, so no red-red pair appears. Done, no fix.
  • Removing a black node breaks the black count. Every path that went through it now has one black fewer. It can also create a red-red pair (a red child now sits under a red parent) or leave a red root.

Which colour counts? The colour of the node that is physically removed from its position. In cases 1 and 2 of section 1 that is z. In the two-child case it is the successor y: y moves up and takes z's colour, so z's colour is preserved and the position that really loses a node is y's old position, with y's original colour. Rule: if the original colour of y (or z) was red, stop. If it was black, fix up.

3. The "extra black" idea

Call x the node that moves into the place that lost a black. It is either the child that took the removed node's place, or a NIL if there was none. Every path through x lost one black. Instead of repairing all paths at once, we pretend x carries an extra black to make up for it. Then the counts are correct again, but x is now "doubly black", which breaks the rule that a node is red or black.

The whole fix-up is: push this extra black up the tree, or absorb it, until it disappears.

  • If x is red: it is "red and extra black", which is just one black. Colour x black. Done.
  • If x is the root: the extra black can simply be dropped. Every path loses the same one black, so counts stay equal.
  • Otherwise x is black with an extra black. Look at its sibling w. w is never NIL here: x's side is one black short, so the sibling side has black height at least 1 and w is a real node.

How to draw a NIL that is double black

NIL is normally drawn as a small black square. For double black, draw a second square around it (or write "2B" or put two dots on it) and name it "x". In the trace tool it appears as a box with a double ring. Do this on paper too: the examiner needs to see where the extra black sits. When the extra black moves up to a real node, draw that node with a double circle.

4. The four cases

Everything below is for x being the left child. If x is the right child, mirror left and right. w is x's sibling. The far child of w is the one on the side away from x. The near child is the one on x's side. Dashed grey nodes mean "any colour, copy it exactly". Amber ring: the node being fixed. Double ring: extra black.

Method · pick the case from w and its children

  1. w is red → Case 1. Make w black, make the parent p red, rotate left at p. x's new sibling is black. Go to case 2, 3 or 4. (Never ends here.)
  2. w is black and both of its children are black → Case 2. Colour w red. Move the extra black from x up to p. If p was red, p becomes black and you stop. If p was black, p is now the double-black x: repeat. (If p is the root, drop it and stop.)
  3. w is black, near child red, far child black → Case 3. Make the near child black, w red, rotate right at w. Now the far child is red: you are in case 4.
  4. w is black, far child red → Case 4. w takes p's colour. p becomes black, far child becomes black. Rotate left at p. The extra black is gone. Stop.

One-line memory: sibling red: case 1 (recolour, rotate, look again). Sibling black, both nephews black: case 2 (recolour, climb). Far nephew red: case 4 (finish). Near nephew red and far black: case 3 (make far red, then case 4). Look at the far nephew first.

Case 1: sibling is red

p must be black because w is red. After the rotation x has a black sibling (c) and the same extra black. The tree is not finished; you continue with 2, 3 or 4 at the same x. Trace: delete 5 from 10B(5B,20R(15B,30B)), case 1 then case 2:

Case 2: sibling black, both nephews black

Colouring w red removes one black from w's side, which matches x's side. The extra black now belongs to p. This is the only case that repeats, and it only recolours (no rotation). Trace A: p is red, so it stops. Trace B: p is black and the extra black climbs twice, to the root, where it is dropped.

Case 3: sibling black, near nephew red, far nephew black

This case does not fix anything yet. It only turns the picture into case 4 (a red far nephew). Trace: delete 5 from 10B(5B,15B(12R,)).

Case 4: sibling black, far nephew red

Why it works: the left rotation moves one black (p) to x's side. The path through x now has p (black) plus x. The far nephew turns black to replace the black that w's side lost to the rotation. Colour check: w takes p's old colour so the colour above the piece is unchanged. Trace: delete 5 from 10B(5B,15B(,20R)).

5. When does it stop?

SituationWhat happens
Removed node was redStop at once, no fix-up
x is red (red and extra black)Colour x black, stop
x is the rootDrop the extra black, stop
Case 4Stop, always
Case 2 and p is redp becomes black, stop
Case 2 and p is blackp is the new x: repeat one level up
Case 1Not an end. Always continues into case 2, 3 or 4
Case 3Not an end. Always continues into case 4

So deletion does at most 3 rotations (case 1, case 3, case 4 once each) and any amount of recolouring up the tree, O(log n) in total. Insertion needs at most 2 rotations.

6. Pseudocode

RB-DELETE(T, z)
 y = z; y-orig = y.color
 if z.left == NIL:  x = z.right; TRANSPLANT(T, z, z.right)
 elif z.right == NIL: x = z.left; TRANSPLANT(T, z, z.left)
 else
   y = TREE-MINIMUM(z.right); y-orig = y.color; x = y.right
   if y.p == z: x.p = y
   else TRANSPLANT(T, y, y.right); y.right = z.right; y.right.p = y
   TRANSPLANT(T, z, y); y.left = z.left; y.left.p = y
   y.color = z.color                       // y takes z's colour
 if y-orig == BLACK: RB-DELETE-FIXUP(T, x)

RB-DELETE-FIXUP(T, x)
 while x != T.root and x.color == BLACK
   if x == x.p.left
     w = x.p.right
     if w.color == RED                                    // case 1
       w.color = BLACK; x.p.color = RED
       LEFT-ROTATE(T, x.p); w = x.p.right
     if w.left.color == BLACK and w.right.color == BLACK  // case 2
       w.color = RED; x = x.p
     else
       if w.right.color == BLACK                          // case 3
         w.left.color = BLACK; w.color = RED
         RIGHT-ROTATE(T, w); w = x.p.right
       w.color = x.p.color; x.p.color = BLACK             // case 4
       w.right.color = BLACK
       LEFT-ROTATE(T, x.p); x = T.root
   else (same with left and right exchanged)
 x.color = BLACK

TRANSPLANT(T, u, v) replaces the subtree at u by the subtree at v (fixes the parent link). The last line colours a red x black, and also handles the root.

7. 2024–25 Q5(a): the three deletions

After inserting 41, 38, 31, 12, 19, 8, 25, 32 (previous chapter) the tree is 38B(19R(12B(8R,),31B(25R,32R)),41B). Then delete 8, 12, 19 in order.

  • Delete 8. 8 is a red leaf. Remove it. Nothing else changes.
  • Delete 12. 12 is a black leaf. Its place is a NIL with an extra black (x). Its sibling is 31 (black). 31's far child is 32, red. So case 4: 31 takes the parent's colour (19 was red, so 31 becomes red), 19 and 32 become black, rotate left at 19.
  • Delete 19. 19 now has only a right child, 25 (red). 25 moves up into 19's place. x is the red 25, so colour it black. Done.

Final tree: 38B(31R(25B,32B),41B). Check black counts: every path from 38 down has 38, one more black, NIL.

On paper: draw the tree after each of the three deletions (three trees, colours on every node). After deleting 12, draw the intermediate tree with the NIL x in a double box, write "sibling 31 black, far child 32 red: case 4", and then the rotated tree. A marker gives marks per tree, so never skip an intermediate tree.

"Is it possible to fix without any rotation?" (5 marks)

Answer in two lines. Sometimes yes, not always. Recolouring alone is enough when the removed node is red, when x is red, or when you only hit case 2 (sibling and both nephews black). But when the sibling is red (case 1), or a nephew is red (cases 3 and 4), a rotation is unavoidable, because recolouring cannot change which side holds the extra black. 10. Traps

Where marks go

  • Using the wrong colour to decide. The fix-up depends on the original colour of the node that moved (the successor), not on the colour of the key you deleted.
  • Forgetting that the successor takes the deleted node's colour as well as its place.
  • Stopping after case 1 or case 3. They always continue.
  • Case 2: recolouring w red but forgetting to check whether p is red (stop) or black (continue).
  • Mirror mistakes. When x is the right child, rotate right in case 1 and 4, left in case 3, and "far" is the left nephew.
  • Treating a missing sibling child as no child. It is a black NIL.
  • Not saying whether you used the successor or the predecessor.
