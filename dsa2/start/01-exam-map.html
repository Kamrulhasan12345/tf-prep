What the Exam Asks, and How to Answer It

CSE 207 is a drawing and proving exam. Most marks are for showing every step of an algorithm by hand, writing pseudocode, and writing short proofs from memory. This page shows what appeared in the last 8 papers so you spend time where the marks are.

1. The paper

  • 210 marks, 3 hours, two sections, separate script for each (2018–19 was 180 marks and 2 hours per the paper header).
  • Most years: 4 questions per section, answer 3, about 35 marks each.
  • A compulsory question is common in recent papers: 2024–25 Q5 (RB insert and delete, plus AVL), 2023–24 Q10, 2022–23 Q1 and Q5.
  • 2023–24 was unusual: Section A was 9 short questions, answer 7. Do not rely on one format.
  • Sections are not fixed to one teacher: in 2024–25 Section A had NP and heaps, and Section B had trees and graphs. Prepare both parts.

3. Notes versus exam

Your notes and slides leave gaps that the exam does not.

  • RB deletion is not in the slides or notes, but 2024–25 made it part of a compulsory 25-mark question. Full chapter here.
  • Reductions: Bayzid sir's "reduction won't come" was written about the CT. Term finals keep asking them.
  • Vertex cover 2-approximation: the notes say "not in syllabus", yet it was asked in 2022–23 and 2024–25 for 15 marks each. It takes 20 minutes, so learn it.
  • Splay: asked in 6 of 8 older papers, but out of the syllabus for this exam. Skip it.
  • Prim, Bellman-Ford code, AVL operations: blank in the notes. Taught here.

Ask a classmate or the teacher if the syllabus for this exam excludes anything. Until you hear otherwise, treat this table as the syllabus.

4. How to write answers

Method · what earns marks

  1. Simulations: draw every intermediate tree, table or residual graph. Name the case or rule used next to each step.
  2. Trees: write R or B on every node, even when you use coloured pens.
  3. Tables: Floyd-Warshall needs every D(k) and Π(k) unless told otherwise. Dijkstra needs the extraction order and d values.
  4. Proofs: state what you prove, the method (induction, contradiction), base case, step, and a one-line conclusion.
  5. Design questions: define the graph (vertices, edges, weights), name the algorithm, give pseudocode, state the complexity.
  6. Choosing: spend the first 5 minutes picking questions. Budget about 1 minute per 2 marks; a 35-mark question gets about 50 minutes at most.

5. How this book makes you practise

  • Tracers: type any input and step through the algorithm. Each step names the case.
  • Mandatory practice ends every chapter. Random instances are marked automatically, and steps open only after you attempt it. Proofs use a self-marking checklist.
