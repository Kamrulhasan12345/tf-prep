Cuts, Max-Flow Min-Cut, Edmonds-Karp

The max-flow min-cut proof is asked almost every year: 2023–24 Q11(c) (15 marks, "derive a proof"), 2022–23 Q4(a) (8). Edmonds-Karp: 2022–23 Q3(c) (5 of 15 marks), 2017–18 Q2, CT2 Q1 (simulate it and check that distances only grow). 2023–24 Q11 also asked the runtimes. 2017–18 Q2(b) and 2018–19 (b) both end with "find the min cut".

Read chapter 06 first. You need residual networks and the augmentation lemma.

1. Cuts

Cut (S, T)
Split the vertices into two sets \(S\) and \(T=V-S\) with \(s\in S\) and \(t\in T\).
Capacity of a cut
\(c(S,T)=\sum_{u\in S,\,v\in T}c(u,v)\). Only edges going from S to T count.
Flow across a cut
\(f(S,T)=\sum_{u\in S,\,v\in T}f(u,v)-\sum_{u\in S,\,v\in T}f(v,u)\). Flow forward minus flow coming back.
Min cut
A cut of smallest capacity.

On the example network, take \(S=\{s,a,b,d\}\), \(T=\{c,t\}\). The edges from S to T are a→c (12), d→c (7), d→t (4). So \(c(S,T)=23\). Edge c→b goes T to S, so it does not count toward the capacity.

Cut \(S=\{s\}\) has capacity 16 + 13 = 29. Different cuts, different capacities. The smallest one limits everything.

2. Two lemmas

Lemma 1: for every cut, \(f(S,T)=|f|\)

The teacher's version: "flow of any cut is constant and equals the flow of the network". Proof:

  1. \(|f|\) is the net outflow of s. For every other \(u\in S\) (so \(u\ne s,t\)) the net outflow is 0 by conservation. So \(|f|=\sum_{u\in S}\Big(\sum_{v\in V}f(u,v)-\sum_{v\in V}f(v,u)\Big)\).
  2. Split each inner sum by where v lies: \(v\in S\) or \(v\in T\).
  3. The terms with both ends in S cancel in pairs: the edge (x,y) with x,y in S appears as \(+f(x,y)\) in the line for x and as \(-f(x,y)\) in the line for y.
  4. What remains is \(\sum_{u\in S,v\in T}f(u,v)-\sum_{u\in S,v\in T}f(v,u)=f(S,T)\). \(\blacksquare\)

Lemma 2 (weak duality): \(|f|\le c(S,T)\) for every flow and every cut

  1. \(|f|=f(S,T)\) by Lemma 1.
  2. \(f(S,T)\le\sum_{u\in S,v\in T}f(u,v)\) because the subtracted part is not negative.
  3. \(\sum_{u\in S,v\in T}f(u,v)\le\sum_{u\in S,v\in T}c(u,v)=c(S,T)\) by the capacity constraint. \(\blacksquare\)

Consequence: max flow \(\le\) min cut. Every flow is below every cut. The theorem says they actually meet.

3. The max-flow min-cut theorem

Theorem
For a flow f in G these are equivalent:
(1) f is a maximum flow;
(2) the residual network \(G_f\) has no augmenting path;
(3) \(|f|=c(S,T)\) for some cut (S,T).

The proof goes around the circle (1) ⇒ (2) ⇒ (3) ⇒ (1). Write it exactly like this in the exam.

Method · the 15-mark proof, in order

  1. Define flow, cut, c(S,T), f(S,T), residual network (two lines each).
  2. Lemma 1 (flow across a cut = |f|) and Lemma 2 (|f| ≤ c(S,T)). Proofs are in section 2.
  3. (1) ⇒ (2). Suppose \(G_f\) had an augmenting path p. By the augmentation lemma, \(f\uparrow f_p\) is a flow of value \(|f|+c_f(p)>|f|\). So f was not maximum, a contradiction.
  4. (2) ⇒ (3). No path from s to t in \(G_f\). Let \(S=\{v: v\text{ is reachable from }s\text{ in }G_f\}\) and \(T=V-S\). Then \(s\in S\), \(t\in T\). Now look at the edges across the cut:
    • If \(u\in S\), \(v\in T\) and \((u,v)\in E\): if \(f(u,v)0\), and v would be reachable. So \(f(u,v)=c(u,v)\): saturated.
    • If \(u\in S\), \(v\in T\) and \((v,u)\in E\): if \(f(v,u)>0\) then \(c_f(u,v)=f(v,u)>0\), and v would be reachable. So \(f(v,u)=0\): empty.
    Therefore \(f(S,T)=\sum_{S\to T}c(u,v)-0=c(S,T)\). By Lemma 1, \(|f|=f(S,T)=c(S,T)\).
  5. (3) ⇒ (1). Let f' be any flow. By Lemma 2, \(|f'|\le c(S,T)=|f|\). So no flow beats f, and f is maximum.
  6. Conclusion. Max flow value = min cut capacity. The last two sentences matter: the flow is a maximum and the cut S found in step 4 is a minimum cut (by Lemma 2 every cut has capacity ≥ |f|, and this one equals it).

Where marks go

  • The step most answers skip: edges from S to T are saturated and edges from T to S carry zero flow. Say both. Without them \(f(S,T)=c(S,T)\) is not justified.
  • Defining S as "some set". It must be the vertices reachable from s in the residual network.
  • Proving only (1) ⇒ (3). The examiner wants the three-way equivalence, or at least max flow = min cut with Lemma 2 for the other direction.
  • Counting T→S edges in the capacity of the cut.

Integrality. If all capacities are integers, every augmentation pushes an integer, so Ford-Fulkerson ends with an integer flow. So an integer max flow always exists. Chapter 08 uses this for matching.

4. Finding the min cut from the answer

Method · min cut after the last augmentation (2017–18 Q2(b), 2018–19 (b))

  1. Draw the final residual network.
  2. Mark every vertex reachable from s (follow residual edges). That set is S. The rest is T.
  3. The cut edges are the original edges that go from S to T. They should all be full (f = c).
  4. Add their capacities. The sum must equal the max flow. If not, you have an arithmetic or drawing mistake.

The trace below ends by listing S and the cut edges. For the example network the answer is \(S=\{s,a,b,d\}\), cut edges a→c, d→c, d→t, capacity \(12+7+4=23\) which is the max flow.

On paper: draw the final flow network and draw a dashed curve separating S from T. Label S and T. List the edges the curve cuts from S to T with their capacities and add them: "12 + 7 + 4 = 23 = max flow". Edges crossing from T to S are not part of the sum; you may mention that they carry 0.

A network can have more than one min cut. Vertices reachable from s give the one with the smallest S. If a question asks for "the" min cut, give this one.

5. Edmonds-Karp

Edmonds-Karp is Ford-Fulkerson where the augmenting path is chosen by BFS: a path with the fewest edges (ignoring capacities). The notes call this the "minimum path length augmentation". Nothing else changes.

EDMONDS-KARP(G, s, t)
 for each edge (u,v): f(u,v) = 0
 while BFS from s in G_f reaches t
   p = the BFS (fewest-edges) path from s to t
   push c_f(p) along p exactly as in Ford-Fulkerson
 return f

Why it helps: plain FF depends on the flow value (section 6 of chapter 06), which can be huge. EK needs at most \(O(VE)\) augmentations whatever the capacities, so it is polynomial and works with real numbers too.

The same network, with BFS paths:

CT2 Q1 asks you to show that "the shortest path distance of each vertex from s only increases". After each augmentation, run BFS from s on the new residual network and write the distance (number of edges) beside each vertex. The numbers never go down. Lemma 1 below is the reason.

6. Why Edmonds-Karp makes \(O(VE)\) augmentations: the proof

Notation: \(\delta_f(s,v)\) is the number of edges on a shortest path from s to v in \(G_f\). In EK every augmenting path is a shortest path.

Lemma 1: during EK, \(\delta_f(s,v)\) never decreases for any v

  1. Suppose, for contradiction, an augmentation \(f\to f'\) gives \(\delta_{f'}(s,v)<\delta_f(s,v)\) for some v. Among all such v, pick the one with the smallest \(\delta_{f'}(s,v)\). Then \(v\ne s\) (\(\delta(s,s)=0\) always) and \(v\) is reachable in \(G_{f'}\) (its distance is finite).
  2. Take a shortest path to v in \(G_{f'}\), and let (u,v) be its last edge. Then \(\delta_{f'}(s,u)=\delta_{f'}(s,v)-1\). By the choice of v, u did not get closer: \(\delta_{f}(s,u)\le\delta_{f'}(s,u)\).
  3. Case A: (u,v) is an edge of \(G_f\). Then \(\delta_f(s,v)\le\delta_f(s,u)+1\le\delta_{f'}(s,u)+1=\delta_{f'}(s,v)\). So v did not get closer. Contradiction.
  4. Case B: (u,v) is not in \(G_f\) but is in \(G_{f'}\). The augmentation created it, so the augmenting path used the edge (v,u). That path was a shortest path in \(G_f\), so \(\delta_f(s,v)=\delta_f(s,u)-1\le\delta_{f'}(s,u)-1=\delta_{f'}(s,v)-2\). Again v did not get closer. Contradiction. \(\blacksquare\)

Lemma 2: each edge becomes critical at most about V/2 times

An edge of \(G_f\) is critical if it is a bottleneck of the augmenting path (it has the smallest residual capacity on p). It disappears from the residual network after the push.

  1. Let (u,v) be critical in \(G_f\). It lies on a shortest path, so \(\delta_f(s,v)=\delta_f(s,u)+1\).
  2. For (u,v) to become critical again, it must first reappear. It only reappears when some later augmenting path (in \(G_{f''}\)) uses the opposite edge (v,u). On a shortest path that gives \(\delta_{f''}(s,u)=\delta_{f''}(s,v)+1\).
  3. By Lemma 1, \(\delta_{f''}(s,v)\ge\delta_f(s,v)=\delta_f(s,u)+1\). So \(\delta_{f''}(s,u)\ge\delta_f(s,u)+2\).
  4. So between two times that (u,v) is critical, the distance of u from s grows by at least 2. When (u,v) is critical, u is on a shortest s-t path and is followed by at least the edge (u,v), so \(u\ne t\) and \(\delta(s,u)\le\delta(s,t)-1\le V-2\). The first time \(\delta(s,u)\ge0\), and it rises by 2 each further time, so (u,v) is critical at most \(\frac{V-2}{2}+1=\frac V2\) times. (If u becomes unreachable, (u,v) is never on an augmenting path again.)

Counting

  1. The residual network has at most \(2E\) edges (each edge of G gives (u,v) or (v,u)).
  2. Each is critical \(O(V)\) times, so there are \(O(VE)\) critical events in total.
  3. Every augmentation makes at least one edge critical. So there are \(O(VE)\) augmentations.
  4. Each augmentation costs one BFS, \(O(E)\). Total \(O(VE\cdot E)=O(VE^2)\). \(\blacksquare\)

Slide and CLRS labels for the Edmonds-Karp proof. The two lemmas above are Lemma 26.7 (\(\delta_f(s,v)\) increases monotonically, slides 17 p.27-40) and the counting step is Theorem 26.8 (\(O(VE)\) augmentations, slides 17 p.60-61, CLRS p.729). The slide proof of Theorem 26.8 shows the same argument as the notes: the longest shortest path has length \(|V|-2\), an edge that is critical again must have its tail farther from \(s\) by at least 2, so the reappearance count is \((|V|-2)/2\).

Lectures

  • Monirul Islam sir, Lec 16 (Jul 28, 2026), notes pp.36-37. p.36: \(|f\uparrow f_p|>|f|\), so augmentation always increases the flow. A cut \((S,T)\) splits the vertices into two disjoint sets with \(s\in S\), \(t\in T\). Capacity \(c(S,T)\) is the total capacity of edges going from \(S\) to \(T\). The flow of a cut \(f(S,T)\) is the flow on edges from \(S\) to \(T\) minus the flow on edges from \(T\) to \(S\). "The flow of any cut is constant and equals the flow of the network." p.37: max-flow min-cut as three equivalent statements: (1) \(f\) is a maximum flow, (2) no augmenting path in the residue network, (3) \(|f|=c(S,T)\) for some cut. His reasons: (2) otherwise we could increase the flow; (3) if a cut has capacity equal to the flow, there is no room to push more through it, so \(f\) is maximum.
