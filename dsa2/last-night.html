Red-black

PropertiesEvery node red or black; root black; NIL black; red node has black children; same black count on every path. \(h \le 2\lg(n+1)\).
Insert (z red, look at uncle)Case 1, uncle red: recolour p, u black, g red, z = g. Case 2, uncle black, bent: rotate at p to straighten, then case 3. Case 3, uncle black, straight: p black, g red, rotate at g. Root black at the end.
Delete (only removing black is a problem; look at sibling w)Case 1, w red: swap colours of w and parent, rotate at parent toward x; continue. Case 2, w black with two black children: w red, move extra black up (a red parent just becomes black). Case 3, w black, near child red, far black: swap colours of w and near child, rotate at w away from x; go to 4. Case 4, w black, far child red: w takes parent's colour, parent and far child black, rotate at parent toward x. Stop.

AVL (bf = left height − right height)

bf +2, left child ≥ 0LL: right rotate at the node
bf +2, left child −1LR: left rotate at child, then right rotate at the node
bf −2, right child ≤ 0RR: left rotate at the node
bf −2, right child +1RL: right rotate at child, then left rotate at the node
Height\(N(h) = N(h-1) + N(h-2) + 1\), \(N(0)=1, N(1)=2\): 1, 2, 4, 7, 12, 20. So \(h = O(\log n)\). Insert: at most one rebalance. Delete: may rebalance at many levels.

Hashing

Load \(\alpha = n/m\). Chaining: unsuccessful \(O(1+\alpha)\), successful \(O(1+\alpha/2)\). Linear \((h+i)\bmod m\) clusters. Quadratic \((h+i^2)\bmod m\) may not reach all slots. Double \((h_1 + ih_2)\bmod m\). Deleting from open addressing needs a deleted marker, or later searches stop early.

Flow

Residual: forward \(c-f\), backward \(f\). Max flow = min cut. Min cut: \(S\) = vertices reachable from \(s\) in the final residual graph; count capacities of edges from \(S\) to \(T\) only. Theorem chain: \(f\) is maximum ⇒ no augmenting path ⇒ \(|f| = c(S,T)\) for some cut ⇒ \(f\) is maximum. Matching: \(s\) to left, left to right, right to \(t\), all capacity 1.

Hard problems

  • P: solvable in polynomial time. NP: YES certificate verifiable in polynomial time. co-NP: NO certificate verifiable. NP-hard: every NP problem reduces to it. NP-complete: in NP and NP-hard.
  • \(A \le_p B\): hardness flows from A to B. To prove X hard, reduce a known hard problem to X.
  • NP-complete template: (1) X is in NP: certificate and checker. (2) Construction from known hard Y, polynomial. (3) YES of Y ⇒ YES of X. (4) YES of X ⇒ YES of Y.
  • 3-SAT → IS: triangle per clause, edge between each literal and its negation, \(k\) = clauses. IS ↔ VC: \(S\) independent iff \(V-S\) is a cover, so map \((G,k)\) to \((G,n-k)\) both ways. IS ↔ Clique: complement graph.
  • P = NP ⇒ NP = co-NP: use "P is closed under complement" both ways.
  • Vertex cover 2-approx: pick any uncovered edge, take both endpoints. Picked edges form a matching, OPT needs one endpoint each, so ALG ≤ 2 OPT.
  • Randomized quicksort: \(\Pr[z_i, z_j \text{ compared}] = 2/(j-i+1)\), by linearity of expectation \(E[X] = \sum_{i<j} 2/(j-i+1) < 2n\ln n = O(n\lg n)\). Boost Monte Carlo: failure \((1-p)^t \le e^{-pt}\).
  • Knapsack bound: fractional knapsack on remaining items, expand largest bound, prune when bound ≤ best.
